# The Household Roster — the women around Gael (servant cards)

*Counterfactual-life library, companion to `the-household.md`. Where that doc defines the servant
**rings and functions**, this doc gives the **reasonable profile** for each body/nursery category and
indexes the **named individuals** built as cards in `and-experiment/warehouse/`. Read the INDEX
naming/era note: this is the **Gael / Jaehaerys-as-father** layer (the protagonist's card slug is
`saerys-targaryen`; in card bodies she is "Gael").*

> **What "a reasonable profile" means here.** A royal nursery + body-servant establishment in the
> Conciliator's peace is a real, structured thing. Each category has a *type* — origin, age band,
> status, what they do for/to the child, when they are present, how their loyalty is held, and the
> plot-seam they sit on. Below: the type, then 2–3 concrete people who fit it, each a card. They are
> built so a scene can cast a *specific* watcher, and so the principal has a **menu** to pick the
> Book II maid from (OQ-CL2) without that choice being pre-made.

All cards: `class: persona · scope: project · quality: full · tier: minor` (background — Haiku-spawned),
referencing `westeros-smallfolk` + `westeros-grrm-mannerisms` (register) + `saerys-targaryen` +
`loc-maegors-holdfast`. Names are era-plausible smallfolk names chosen not to collide with existing
cards; they remain overridable (INDEX OQ-CL3).

> **⚠ Era-casting hazard.** These servant cards are correctly **Jaehaerys-era (~84–95 AC)** and
> era-clean. But the **family/court cards already in `warehouse/` are still stamped 122 AC / Viserys-I**
> (e.g. `helaena-targaryen-122ac` says b.109, daughter of Viserys I, Gael's *elder* sister — wrong era
> AND wrong sibling-order vs GUARD-4). **Do not co-cast a servant here with an un-migrated family card
> as-is** (you'd put an 84 AC nursery next to a sister not born until 109). Read the family cards
> through GUARD-4 + `design/time-anchor.md`, or wait for the run-03 migration to re-derive them. See
> `time-anchor.md` §5.

## A. Wet-nurses — the infant's whole diet (phase 0, age 0–2)

**Reasonable profile.** A healthy smallfolk woman recently delivered of her own living child, chosen
for clean health, abundant steady milk, and a biddable temperament. She lives in or beside the nursery
and feeds the princess on a ~2–3-hour cycle, day and night, for roughly two years. **Milk-kin
politics** (canon device, [canon-uncertain #2]): her own babe is nursed alongside or nearby and becomes
Gael's *milk-sibling* — a tie meant to bind a smallfolk family to the crown for life. Her loyalty is
bought with coin, a position, and that tie. A royal infant usually had a **primary** nurse and at
least one **backup/successor** (milk fails, a babe weans, a nurse falls ill). She is the relentless
presence of I.1–I.2 — the feeding clock the infant must cultivate *between* — and, because she knows
babies, often the **first to sense the child is wrong**.

| Card | One-line | Plot-seam |
|---|---|---|
| `mella-wet-nurse` | Primary nurse; a fisherman's widow; warm, superstitious, milk-kin (her son Pate = Gael's milk-brother). | The I.1 worried-watcher; first to feel the infant's stillness is *off*; the founding below-stairs tie (her family owes the crown / will be owed by Gael). |
| `bessa-wet-nurse` | Second/successor nurse; a baker's wife; brisk, milk-proud, faintly resentful of Mella's primacy. | On the night-watch during a key I.1 cultivation gap; the nurse who'd *report* an oddity rather than soothe it (a sharper hazard than Mella). |

## B. Nursemaids & nannies — all the not-feeding care (phases 0–3, age 0–~6)

**Reasonable profile.** The establishment that does everything the wet-nurse doesn't: swaddling,
bathing, soothing, dressing, the day's small decisions, and the night watch. It has a shape:
- **The head nursemaid ("the nan")** — middle-aged, has raised other highborn children, runs the
  room, directs the under-maids and the rocker, answers to the septa and the Queen. The nursery's
  *institutional memory* — what is normal, what is not. Doting; therefore often **wrong about Gael**
  in exactly the way the comedy needs.
- **Under-nursemaids** — younger hands: carry water, change linen, tend the fire, fetch and bear,
  sleep in the room on rotating watch so the child is covered through the night.
- **The rocker / cradle-girl** — youngest, lowest status (a junior maid or a poor relation); rocks the
  cradle and watches the sleeping infant. The boring, human, night-time obstacle.

They are the literal answer to *"who is in the room"* in phases 0–2, and the watch a heist must remove.

| Card | One-line | Plot-seam |
|---|---|---|
| `mistress-bryony` | The head nan; capable, fond, set in her ways; reads Gael as "a good, easy, solemn baby." | The doting-watcher who gives the infant the hard toy (I.1 step 4, the egg-chipping tool); the household memory the comedy needs to be *wrong*. |
| `cissa-nursemaid` | Under-nursemaid; young, warm, soft-handed, incurious; the gentlest watcher. | The easy gap (she doesn't cross-reference times); ages plausibly toward a chamber-maid post — a *low-arc* maid-candidate. |
| `wenda-the-rocker` | The cradle-girl; ~12, a laundress's daughter, half-asleep on the night watch. | The night-watcher the infant must wait out (I.1); the lowest below-stairs node — a child Gael could *be owed by* cheaply. |

## C. Chamber-maids & body-servants — the maid-candidate pool (phases 2–3, age ~6–9)

**Reasonable profile.** As Gael ages out of the nursery (~6), a personal **chamber-maid** takes over:
she dresses her, runs her errands, sleeps on the **truckle-bed** in or beside the chamber, and —
crucially — **goes outside** on those errands, making her the child's one practical channel to the
world. Young, low-status, mobile. **This is the pool the Book II maid is drawn from** (II.1: "a new
maid is assigned to her household"). The three below are deliberately a *menu*: different temperaments,
different distances-to-the-world, different arc-lengths. **None is committed as the maid** — that is a
principal call (OQ-CL2). They are also the never-alone enforcers, the supply-line legs, and (per II.5)
the figures a faction can squeeze to reach the princess.

| Card | One-line | Maid-candidate read | Plot-seam |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nona-chambermaid` | Steady, plain, incurious, devout-by-rote; does her work and sees nothing. | **Low** — the *safe* maid; little arc; the one Gael keeps *because* she doesn't look. | The watcher who is easy because she's dull; a foil for whoever becomes the real maid. |
| `pella-chambermaid` | Sharp, quick, ambitious, well-liked below-stairs; the one who *does* go outside and *does* notice. | **High** — the strongest candidate; her wit + reach + want-of-betterment are exactly the Book II maid's engine (face/hands/legs). | The natural channel to the outside; the one a faction would target (II.5) *and* the one who could become co-conspirator (II–III). |
| `marra-chambermaid` | Timid, anxious, intensely pious; loyal but breakable; frightened of the odd princess. | **Medium/cautionary** — a maid whose *fear* makes her the faction's lever (II.5 "the maid comes back different"). | The compromised-channel risk made flesh; if she's the maid, the II.5 squeeze is built in. |

## D. Tiring-women & wardrobe — the body's presentation (phases 2–3, age ~6–9)

**Reasonable profile.** The people who manage how the princess *looks*: the silver-gold Valyrian hair
(a daily labour), dressing, mending, laundering, and the wardrobe itself. Two tiers:
- **The wardrobe mistress** — senior, skilled, proprietary about her domain and her charge's
  appearance; the person with the most sustained, close, *appraising* contact with Gael's body. The
  one most likely to **notice** when cultivation makes that body uncanny (I.4 beauty; the healed scar).
- **The tiring-girl / hairdresser** — young, deft, a gossip conduit; below-stairs information moves
  through her hands as surely as the combs.

The seam: **the body is handled, never private.** A change Gael can hide from the septa she cannot
hide from the women who dress her every morning.

| Card | One-line | Plot-seam |
|---|---|---|
| `mistress-orla-wardrobe` | The wardrobe mistress; exacting, proud, observant; loves the hair, frets at the strangeness. | The watcher of the *body*; first to clock the uncanny perfection (I.4) and the impossibly-healed scar (I.1→I.2); a hazard the compliance mask can't cover with stillness. |
| `nesta-tiring-girl` | Young hairdresser; chatty, deft, plugged into below-stairs gossip. | The information channel (court gossip in, household news out); a faction conduit and a patronage node Gael can buy with kindness. |

## Casting notes & seams (quick reference)

- **The watch to subtract (I.1–I.2):** Mella + Bessa (feeding), Bryony (day), Cissa + Wenda (night).
  A heist is the math of removing exactly these.
- **The first to *know* something's wrong:** Mella (feel it), Bessa (report it), Bryony (be sweetly
  wrong about it), Mistress Orla (see it on the body). Different alarm-types for different beats.
- **The maid-candidate menu (OQ-CL2):** Pella (high), Marra (medium/cautionary), Nona (low). Pick one;
  the others become the household texture around her. Pella + Marra each pre-build a different II.5
  ("the maid compromised") shape.
- **Below-stairs faction seeds (loophole 5):** Wenda (cheap), Nesta (gossip), Mella's milk-kin family
  (inherited tie). The people Gael becomes *owed by*.
- **What this batch does NOT cover (future cards):** the **maester** + the **masters of
  accomplishments** (Ring 2 handlers — male, higher status, a different build), and the **companion
  girls / ladies** (highborn peers, not servants — a Hightower kinswoman is the natural first). Flagged
  for a follow-on batch; out of scope for the maids/nannies/wet-nurses the principal asked for here.
