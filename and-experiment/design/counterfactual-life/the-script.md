# The Script — expectations, behaviors, the rules spoken and unspoken

*Counterfactual-life library, doc 3 of 3. What the household wants Gael to **be**: the "good princess"
rubric, the Faith's gender-script, the dynasty/marriage machine and its clock, the social meaning of
dragonlessness, and the calibrated ladders of reward and punishment that enforce all of it. Read the
INDEX first (Gael / Jaehaerys-as-father layer).*

> **The deepest cage is the script, not the schedule.** `the-household` is *who* watches; `the-day` is
> *when*; this doc is *what for*. The expectations below are the standard the **compliance mask**
> performs against (I.3 "outward compliance; internally: absolute rejection"). Every line here is
> something Gael either mimics flawlessly or refuses internally. The comedy is the gap between the
> performance and the rejection; the horror is what the performance slowly costs.

## The "good princess" rubric — what she is graded on

A highborn girl is raised to be an **ornament and an instrument**, never an actor. The household
measures her, continuously and mostly silently, against a standard with five strands. Think of it as
the rubric the septa files her reports against (candidate card: `cond-good-princess-rubric`).

1. **Stillness & deportment.** Sit straight, move smoothly, speak softly, take up no more space than
   granted. The body composed and *legible* at all times. (Helaena fails this charmingly; it is read
   as the eccentric-royal budget.)
2. **Obedience.** To the septa below, to mother and father above, to the Faith over all. A good
   princess does not *want* things on her own account; she is *given* things and is grateful.
3. **Piety.** Genuine, visible devotion to the Seven. Faith is not private belief but public
   practice; the modest, prayerful girl is the creditable one.
4. **Accomplishment.** Needlework (the cardinal proof — "application of the hands to useful work"),
   then music, dancing, letters, courtesy. Accomplishments are *displayed*; they raise her market
   value as a bride.
5. **Modesty & chastity.** The maidenhead is dynastic property. Her reputation is an asset of the
   house. Nothing about her body or conduct may put a future match in question.

**The seam:** Gael scores *perfectly* on strands 1, 2, and 4 as a **performance** — the trained
stillness is the meditation cover; the obedience is the mask; the accomplishment (needlework, sums) is
the double-entry bookkeeping no one audits. She scores *zero* internally on 3 and 5 (anti-Faith,
lesbian, body-autonomy revulsion) while presenting flawless compliance. The rubric does not measure
interiority — which is the exact blind spot the whole Book I–II operation lives in.

## The Faith's gender-script — the Seven as role-enforcer

The Faith is not just devotion; it is the **ideological frame** that names what a woman is for (I.3:
"The Seven as gender-role enforcer"). The catechism a septa drills (canon-uncertain #3 on the exact
day-to-day) teaches:

- **The Maiden → the Mother.** A woman's arc is maidenhood (modesty, purity, waiting) into motherhood
  (bearing, nurturing, yielding). These two aspects *are* the script of her life.
- **Submission as virtue.** Obedience to father, then husband, framed as piety. The good woman yields;
  the willful one is corrected.
- **The body as obligation.** Her body belongs to the dynasty: to be kept chaste for a husband, then
  spent on heirs. Bodily autonomy is not a concept the script contains.
- **Marriage as sacrament + alliance.** Wedded union sanctified by the Seven; for a Targaryen,
  brother-sister marriage *tolerated* by the Faith per Jaehaerys's compromise (acknowledgment, not
  blessing).

**The seam (the curdle):** I.3's "visceral anti-Faith — gut revulsion from an adult who knew bodily
autonomy, not theology." The lesbian element "compounds it: doesn't want a husband at ALL, and that's
her entire purpose." The Faith's script is the precise inverse of everything the reincarnated adult
is. She performs the devotions (the-day §weekly) while rejecting the doctrine wholesale — "seeds
planted, not sprouted; smart enough not to fight power she can't beat yet."

## The dynasty machine — marriage, betrothal, and the clock

This is the engine that ultimately drives the whole series (the betrothal-clock the ship races). The
ordinary expectation, stated plainly:

- **A princess is a piece.** Her value to the house is her *bloodline* and her *marriageability* — an
  alliance to be made, a loyalty to be bought, a womb to continue a line. She is wealth the house
  spends.
- **Betrothed young, wed young.** Betrothal in early childhood is normal (instances: Baela & Rhaena
  betrothed ~age 2); marriage around **13** (Helaena wed her brother at ~13). The clock starts almost
  at birth.
- **How a match is made (candidate card: `cond-betrothal-machinery`):** the King (with the Queen, the
  Hand, advisors) chooses; it is a *political* decision — who needs binding, who pays best in loyalty.
  The girl is informed, not consulted. Negotiation, betrothal, a long engagement, then the wedding.
- **The discount problem.** A *dragonless* princess is a weaker piece (see below) — betrothed later,
  cheaper, to someone worse: a pacification match for a grumpy minor lord (the Northern betrothal,
  Alicent interlude + II.4). "It reveals what Gael is worth to Jaehaerys: not much."
- **Children as duty.** The expected sequel to marriage — heirs are the point; the body spent on the
  line.

**The seam:** the entire SOLITUDE desire is the refusal of this script, and the entire plot-clock is
this machine ticking. The betrothal is "the maximum violation of *leave me alone*" (II.4). The lesbian
fact makes the husband-future not merely unwanted but *impossible* to her — and it is her *whole
assigned purpose*. The North as destination doubles the horror (cold/death + the marriage). She can't
refuse ("she's a princess, this is how dynasties work") — so she races a different clock (the ship)
instead.

## The dragon mystique & the meaning of dragonlessness

The load-bearing social fact (constraints.md §1). A Targaryen's prestige is **dragon-blood made
visible**. The cradle-egg is placed to bond and grow with the child; *it often fails*.

- **What a dragon means:** magic that "took" in *you*; power, prestige, a strong bride/groom, a piece
  with teeth. Dragonriders are the apex of the family.
- **What dragonlessness means:** the magic "didn't take in you." Socially: **pity or mockery** (the
  canon precedents — Rhaena praying nightly over a replacement egg while her twin flew; Aemond mocked,
  given a pig as his "dragon," until he seized Vhagar at 10). Politically: **value collapses to pure
  bloodline** — all the constraints of a princess, none of the payoff. *That is the engine.*
- **The expected response to a dud egg:** *pray over it.* Keep it, sleep with it, beseech the gods
  for a dragon to match your kin's (Rhaena's documented behavior). Patient, pious, hopeful, passive.

**The seam (the inciting inversion, GUARD-2/3):** Gael is given the sacred egg to *pray over* — and
she **grinds and eats it** (I.1). She doesn't accept the dragonless-girl script (pity, prayer,
waiting); she converts the symbol of her failure into fuel. The household reads the dead egg as *a
dud* (her dragonlessness confirmed) — the horror is retroactive. And the epithet "Dragonbane" (I.1)
is the dragonless-pity-script turning to *fear*: not "poor girl, no dragon" but "the girl dragons die
around."

## The calibrated ladders — how the script is enforced

Cost of defiance is **calibrated, not catastrophic** (constraints.md §2: "not the dungeon, but
married off faster, cheaper, worse, financially de-fanged — or, the Westeros ceiling, shipped to a
sept"). Reward and punishment are the two hands that hold the script in place.

### The punishment ladder (low → high)
1. **Correction** — the septa's reproof; a missed pleasure; extra devotions.
2. **Doubled septa-hours** — pure punishment by schedule (I.3: "doubled septa training"). The cage
   tightens by adding *watcher-hours*, not bars.
3. **Withdrawn favor** — the King's doting withdrawn; the transactional cold (I.3: "withdraws when she
   doesn't [perform]"). Cheaper, colder treatment; gifts and indulgences stopped.
4. **The faster/worse/cheaper match** — the marriage market as discipline: spent sooner, on someone
   lesser, with her finances de-fanged. (The Northern pacification match carries this flavour.)
5. **The sept as disposal** — the ceiling for an inconvenient royal woman: shipped to a sept,
   removed. (This is what happens to *the septa herself* — `warehouse/saerys-septa.card.md` — when she
   is right without the right paper. The disposal mechanism, turned on the handler.)

### The reward ladder (the carrot, equally calibrated)
1. **The septa's approval; the afternoon nothing-alarming-happens.**
2. **Indulgences** — the doting King's gifts, latitude, the sanctioned "collection," the
   eccentric-royal budget extended.
3. **Display & presentation** — brought to court, dressed fine, shown as the creditable miracle child;
   public favor.
4. **The King's-hand note** — the apex indulgence and the **master key** (loophole 1): a note in the
   King's hand overrules every chaperone below him. *His love is the cage and the skeleton key at
   once, and he never understands either fact.*

**The seam:** Gael games **both** ladders. She performs hard enough to keep the carrots (the
indulgence, the collection, the note) and to avoid the sticks (she's "smart enough not to fight power
she can't beat yet" — I.3), while doing the opposite of everything the ladders are meant to enforce.
The King's-hand note is the prize at the top of the reward ladder *and* the instrument that defeats
the punishment ladder (the septa's true accusation, II/Lock I). She wins the game by its own rules.

## The unsaid rules — the script that is never spoken aloud

Some expectations are enforced precisely by *never being stated*. A child absorbs them by the shape of
what is permitted and what produces silence.

- **The incest norm is not explained; it is assumed.** No one sits a child down and explains
  brother-sister marriage. She discovers it by being *appraised* (Daemon, I.4) — and the
  modern-revulsion horror is sharpened by its having been ambient, unspoken, normal-to-everyone.
- **The body is discussed only in code.** Maidenhood, "purity," "her future" — the dynastic facts of
  her flesh are handled in euphemism. Bluntness about the body is itself a transgression.
- **Servants are furniture.** Never stated, always enforced: a maid is interchangeable, movable,
  invisible. "Your maid can go ahead to your new household — isn't that thoughtful?" (II.7) is the
  unsaid rule made audible. The whole plot is Gael breaking this one rule, for exactly one person.
- **Faction is real but unspeakable.** Who is Hightower, who is the King's, who owes whom — the court
  runs on it and names it never. (Otto as ambient authority; the II.5 faction-play on the maid.)
- **A girl is not running anything.** The deepest unsaid rule, and Gael's best cover: "she doesn't
  need to launder her intelligence — the world launders it by refusing to take her seriously" (II
  sexism-as-cover). The rule that erases her is the rule that hides her.

## The eccentric-royal latitude — the one slack in the script

The script has a single sanctioned exception: **royals are *allowed* to be odd** — "it's the dragon
blood." A deviance budget the merely-noble don't get (loophole 4). Helaena already spends hers on
insects and riddles; the smallfolk love her for it.

**The seam:** this is the slack Gael lives in. "Collecting," reclusion, the studious-strange-princess
cover (II.1 "scholar-princess") — all of it spends the eccentric-royal budget. The script that cages
her also grants her *one* license to be incomprehensible, and she pours the whole operation through
it. The risk: the budget has a ceiling, and a child who is *too* odd (the poison-eater, the
dragon-killer, the silent withdrawn girl of the Alicent interlude) starts to read not as eccentric but
as *wrong* — and wrongness re-engages the punishment ladder.

## Using this doc

1. **Find the expectation the scene touches** (a rubric strand, a Faith beat, the betrothal clock, the
   dragonless meaning, a ladder rung, an unsaid rule).
2. **Decide the mask state.** Is Gael *performing* compliance (mask on — the gap is dramatic irony),
   *failing* it (mask slips — danger, the punishment ladder arms), or *exploiting* it (the rubric/
   latitude as cover — the comedy of competence hidden by contempt)?
3. **Track the cost.** The script is enforced by the ladders; every performance banks favor or spends
   it. A scene where she games the ladders should make the *banking* legible — the note she's earning,
   the suspicion she's spending down.
