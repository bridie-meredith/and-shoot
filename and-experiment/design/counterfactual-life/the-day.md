# The Day — routines, patterns, the calendar

*Counterfactual-life library, doc 2 of 3. The shape of an ordinary princess's time: the hour-by-hour
day across four age phases, the weekly Faith rhythm, the annual court calendar, and the standing
routines of meals, dress, the body, and sleep. Read the INDEX first (Gael / Jaehaerys-as-father
layer; Book I ≈ 84–93 AC).*

> **Why time matters more than space.** The cage is the attendant, not the door — so the real map of
> Gael's life is not the floor-plan, it's the **timetable**. Each routine below is a *surveillance
> schedule*: it tells you who is watching at the third hour after dawn and where the gap opens at the
> seventh. A deviation (a heist, a meditation, an excursion) is an hour subtracted from this table
> and accounted for. The household's certainty about what the missing hour *contained* is the comedy.

Westerosi time is reckoned loosely — by the bells of the sept and the hours of light, not clocks. The
horaria below use "hour after dawn" / "midday" / "dusk" as the court would.

## The four age phases

The household's grip changes shape as she grows. Four phases span Book I:

| Phase | Age | The grip | The room | The watcher who owns the day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — the swaddled infant | ~0–1 | total; the feeding cycle is the clock | the nursery | the wet-nurse + the nan (+ Alicent) |
| 1 — the toddler | ~2–3 | constant but mobile; leading-strings | the nursery floor | the nan + under-maids + the rocker |
| 2 — the little princess | ~4–6 | the timetable hardens; the septa takes the day | nursery → her chamber | **the septa** |
| 3 — the schoolroom child | ~6–9 | a full lesson-block day; more rope, same watch | her chamber + the maester's tower | the septa + the masters; the maid arrives |

---

### Phase 0 — the swaddled infant (~0–1)

The infant has no schedule of her own; she lives inside the **feeding cycle** and the **watch**.

- **The feeding clock:** roughly every 2–3 hours, day and night, the wet-nurse (Mella) feeds her.
  Between feeds: changing, swaddling, rocking, sleep. There is no two-hour window in this phase that
  is not owned by someone — which is exactly the I.1 constraint (cultivation happens in the *seams*,
  and stops the moment she is "monitored by worried nanny + Alicent").
- **Swaddling:** the infant is wrapped tightly, immobilised — the literal version of the trapped-mind
  premise (I.1: "adult mind, zero mobility"). Unwrapped only for changing and bathing.
- **The cradle & the egg:** the cradle-egg sits in or by the cradle, to be bonded-with as she grows
  (canon custom). She is meant to be *near* it, prayed over it. This is the most intimate furniture of
  her station — and her first reagent (I.1).
- **The watch:** the rocker by day-naps; an under-maid through the night; the nan directing; the
  wet-nurse on the feeding cycle; Alicent "almost never leaves her sight." The room is never empty.
- **Christening / naming:** a Targaryen infant is named and shown — a christening-spoon, a presenting
  at court (cf. `warehouse/prop-christening-spoon.card.md`). The first time the household *measures*
  her against the standard.

**The gap:** the only unwatched minutes are the changeovers — when a feed ends and the next watcher
hasn't fully settled, when the nan steps out and the rocker is half-asleep. Minutes, not hours. I.1's
cultivation is built out of exactly these.

### Phase 1 — the toddler (~2–3)

Mobile now, which means *more* watching, not less. The leading-strings phase.

- **Weaning:** off the wet-nurse onto pap, then soft foods at the nursery board. Mella's intimate role
  recedes (her milk-kin tie remains).
- **Leading-strings:** a toddling child is kept on strings or by the hand — physically tethered to a
  maid whenever she moves. The body is *steered*.
- **The nursery floor:** days are spent in the nursery and the small courtyard (loc-maegors-holdfast:
  "enough for a child to run in, not large enough for real exercise"). Supervised movement only.
- **First "lessons":** not yet the septa's schoolroom — but the start of the deportment drip
  (don't grab, don't shout, kiss the hand, sit when told). The expectations begin as soft corrections.
- **First words & the family tongue:** High Valyrian heard at home (a *family* tongue for Targaryens),
  the Common Tongue around her. A toddler princess is *displayed* — brought out, made to greet, then
  taken back.
- **Name-day:** the annual marker; gifts, a small observance (see calendar below).

**The seam:** the I.2 toddler-heist exploits *exactly this phase's* assumption — that a 2–3-year-old
is harmless and underestimated. The leading-strings world has minders who can be slipped because no
one believes a toddler is *planning*. "Exploit toddler-underestimation to escape minders" (I.2) is a
deviation from the leading-strings norm.

### Phase 2 — the little princess (~4–6)

The septa takes the day. The soft corrections become a **timetable**. This is where ordinary girlhood
acquires its rigid daily shape — the shape Gael learns to perform and hide inside.

**A representative day (Phase 2):**

| When | What | Who watches |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn | Wake; the chamber-maid dresses her; wash | chamber-maid (Cissa/Nona) |
| 1st hour | **Morning devotions** in the family sept | the septa |
| 2nd hour | Breakfast (nursery/schoolroom board) | the septa / a maid |
| 2nd–4th hour | **Lesson-block I:** letters, sums, reading | the septa |
| Midday | Dinner (the main meal); sometimes at a family board | the septa; sometimes Alicent |
| Early afternoon | **Needlework** (heavily emphasised) | the septa |
| Mid-afternoon | An "accomplishment": music *or* dancing (by day) | the relevant master + septa |
| Late afternoon | Chaperoned leisure — the courtyard, the garden, companion girls | the septa / a maid |
| Dusk | **Evening devotions** | the septa |
| Evening | Supper (light); quiet time | a maid |
| Night | Bed; a body-servant on the truckle bed | chamber-maid |

The day is **lesson-block + chaperoned leisure, supervised throughout** (constraints.md §1). Nothing
in it is unwatched by design.

**The sanctioned still-room / "collection":** in this phase Alicent permits a studious hobby — a
girl's collection, a place in the household still-room to make preserves and simple remedies (the
sanctioned domestic craft the housekeeper already runs). This is the *cover* Gael's real work grows
inside. To the household it is a sweet, harmless, even creditable thing for an odd child to do.

**The seam:** I.3 is the cage *revealing its full shape* through these lessons (marriage, children,
the Faith, submission — see `the-script`). The needlework-and-stillness curriculum trains the exact
skill the compliance mask needs (I.3 "outward compliance"). The devotions she "attends with
appropriate piety" while counting acoustics (loc-maegors-holdfast). The timetable is the **alibi
frame**: every escaped hour has a slot it's *supposed* to be in.

### Phase 3 — the schoolroom child (~6–9)

A fuller, harder day, with marginally more rope (she can be sent to the maester's tower, can have
unattended-feeling study time) but the same watch underneath. The years the Book II machine is built.

**A representative day (Phase 3):**

| When | What | Watcher / the gap |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn | Wake; dressed; the hair (see §dress — a long labour) | chamber-maid |
| 1st hour | Morning devotions | the septa |
| 2nd–4th hour | **Lesson-block I** (letters/sums/history; High Valyrian) | the septa / the master of letters |
| Late morning | Time with the **maester** — health check, "study" in his tower/library | the maester (**a looser watch — he's busy; correspondence and records are *right there*; II.3 world-map material**) |
| Midday | Dinner | the septa / family board |
| Early afternoon | **Needlework / deportment** | the septa |
| Mid-afternoon | Accomplishment (music/dancing) **or** "the collection"/still-room | a master, **or the still-room (the productive gap — sanctioned, semi-private; II.2 startup work)** |
| Late afternoon | Chaperoned leisure; companions; the garden | a maid (**overlap-window: shift changes here; loc-maegors-holdfast 3–5 min**) |
| Dusk | Evening devotions | the septa |
| Evening | Supper; quiet study or sewing | a maid / the truckle-bed begins |
| Night | Bed; body-servant on the truckle bed | **the maid (III.2: who later performs "she is meditating, do not disturb")** |

**Where the day leaks:** three productive gaps, all *sanctioned*, all exploited —
1. **The maester's tower** (loose watch + records + reagents),
2. **The still-room / collection** (semi-private, expected, the startup's workshop and the assay
   bench in one),
3. **The shift-change overlap** in late afternoon (the 3–5 minute seam for moving things).
The performed-illness loophole adds a fourth on demand: a "delicate" princess "not to be disturbed"
converts a whole afternoon into unmonitored hours, enforced by her own staff (loophole 3).

## The weekly rhythm — the Faith's seven

The week is shaped by the **Seven**. Each day leans toward one aspect (the Father, the Mother, the
Warrior, the Smith, the Maiden, the Crone, the Stranger), and devotions inflect accordingly. For a
princess this means:

- **Daily:** morning and evening devotions in the family sept (above).
- **Weekly:** a fuller observance / a visit to a greater sept on the day the household keeps most
  solemnly; readings from *The Seven-Pointed Star*; the septa's catechism intensifies.
- **The Maiden's day** is freighted for a girl: the aspect of maidenhood, modesty, and the
  marriage-future. The day the gender-script is loudest (see `the-script`).
- **Fasting / penance days** as the Faith and season require.

**The seam:** Gael's gut-revulsion at the Faith (I.3 "visceral anti-Faith") plays out *weekly*, every
devotion a small performance of submission she does not feel. The acoustically-counted prayers
(loc-maegors-holdfast) are this rhythm, subverted.

## The annual calendar — the court year in the long peace

The year a princess lives against. Beats should place themselves on this wheel (candidate card:
`cond-court-calendar-jaehaerys-peace`).

- **Her name-day** — the personal annual marker; gifts, an observance scaled to her standing. A
  doting King makes it much (loophole-1 weather); a withdrawn one makes it little (I.3
  transactional). A *dragonless* princess's name-day quietly carries the question of the egg that
  never hatched.
- **The seven feast-days of the Faith** — the great festivals; fuller devotions, feasting, alms (the
  charity duty — loophole 2 keystone — is most visible and most expected here).
- **The King's court occasions** — name-days of the King and Queen, royal anniversaries, the arrival
  of great lords, tourneys and feasts in the peace. The princess is *displayed*: dressed, presented,
  made to be ornamental and correct. High-surveillance, high-stakes-for-deportment days.
- **The seasons** (Westerosi long seasons): provisioning and the household's logistical year — the
  steward's busiest work, the supply deliveries, the preserving (the still-room's sanctioned purpose
  peaks at harvest). Winter tightens the Holdfast; summer opens the gardens and yards.
- **Betrothal / marriage occasions** at court — other children betrothed and wed around her, each a
  reminder of her own clock (`the-script` §the dynasty machine). Helaena's eventual wedding is one
  such marker.
- **Deaths & mournings** — the court's solemn punctuation; Jaehaerys's own death (103 AC) falls after
  Book I but looms as the era's terminus.

**The seam:** the calendar is a *recurring surveillance and display schedule*. Feast-days and court
occasions are when she is most watched and most performed; the seasons and the harvest are when the
still-room cover is most legitimate and the supply lines move most. The charity expected at festivals
is the seed of the whole sick-house network (II keystone).

## Standing routines (cut across all phases)

### Meals
- **Where:** the nursery/schoolroom board for a child; the family high table on occasion; great
  feasts at court when displayed.
- **What:** rich by smallfolk standards — bread, meat, fish, fruit, sweets, watered wine as she ages.
  A princess eats *well* and *correctly* (deportment at table is itself a lesson).
- **The comedy seam:** the child who eats *rocks, poison, and Valyrian steel* (I.2–I.4) is doing it
  *around* a life of plentiful proper food. Her appetite is not hunger — which is the II.6
  source-not-pool reframe in miniature ("greed, not survival") staged at the dinner board.

### Dress & the body
- **The hair:** Valyrian silver-gold hair is a daily *production* — washed, combed, braided, dressed
  by the tiring-women. Cultivation makes it uncannily perfect (I.4 "super-silver hair") — an asset
  that becomes a target.
- **Dressing:** she is dressed *by* others, every day, her whole childhood. Layers, laces, the
  correct gown for the day's occasions. The body is **handled, never private**.
- **Bathing:** drawn and tended by maids; another handled-body routine.
- **The seam:** privacy is an achievement, not a default. The uncanny beauty cultivation produces
  (I.4) is read by others *on her body, which they handle* — she cannot hide a change that the
  tiring-women dress every morning. The one body she ever acts *on* rather than is acted-upon is the
  maid's, tempered (III.5) — the inverse of a life spent being handled.

### Sleep
- **Where:** the nursery, then her own chamber.
- **Never alone:** a body-servant on the truckle bed, every night (Ring 1). The deepest cage.
- **The seam:** the night is the only time the watch *thins* — one sleeping maid instead of a daytime
  crowd. So the night is where the body-tempering shows itself to *herself* (III.2 "superhuman in the
  dark") and where the excursions live — but only because the maid agrees to *perform the sleeping
  princess* (III.2). The truckle-bed rule is the last thing the escape defeats.

## Using this doc

1. **Place the scene on the timetable.** What age phase? What hour? That tells you who is present and
   what she is *supposed* to be doing.
2. **Find the gap.** The right-hand columns name where the watch thins (changeover, the maester's
   tower, the still-room, the shift-overlap, the night, the performed illness). A deviation lives in a
   gap and is accounted-for against the slot it left.
3. **Keep the baseline legible under the deviation.** The reader should feel the ordinary day the
   strange day is wearing as a disguise — the needlework she's skipping, the prayer she's
   miscounting, the maid who should be watching.
