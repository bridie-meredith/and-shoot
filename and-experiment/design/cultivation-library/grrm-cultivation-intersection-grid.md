**Dragonglass × Void-attribute elemental cultivation / reagent grade (A1 × B1+B4)**

She theorizes: A Tier-2 void-attribute crystal whose qi-negation effect is consistent with its fire-origin suppressing ice-attribute qi structures. She wants to eat it for void-attribute body tempering. She has not yet done this. It is on the list.

Really happening: Dragonglass kills Others through a mechanism ASOIAF does not explain. It is volcanic glass with one extraordinary property unrelated to any conventional chemistry or the cultivation framework. The maester files it as geological curiosity and folk weapon.

Comedic potential: She has correctly identified it as Tier-2 significant and put "eat powdered dragonglass" on a list alongside "eat powdered weirwood" and "eat Valyrian steel filings." The list itself is a gag.

Curdle/horror potential: In Book III she is adjacent to a supply of actual anti-Other weapons — dragonglass is lying around the Dragonstone storerooms — and her primary interest is in it as a reagent for personal body-tempering, not as a weapon against an existential threat. The broken clock has its priorities.

Status: ALREADY-EXPLOITED (cited in `westeros-alchemy-substances-mystica.md` Part II). The "wants to eat it" detail is noted; the Dragonstone-supply adjacency is not yet a discrete scene. **NET-NEW angle:** the scene where she catalogues the dragonglass inventory at Dragonstone and enters "void-crystal stockpile: Tier-2 earth-grade, abundant; cauldron consumption schedule pending" in the log, while actual Others are a continent away, is not yet in the outline. A bones-level beat.

**Weirwood paste × Soul cultivation / pill elixir (A1 × B2+B3)**

She theorizes: A soul-cultivation elixir produced by the weirwood network's Ancestor (the Three-Eyed Crow in his tree-merger seclusion). She classifies it as a Tier-2 wood-attribute spirit-elixir and badly wants to acquire some for testing. The bitter alkaloid content is consistent with a soul-cultivation catalyst that works through the consciousness rather than the body — which is outside her primary path, making it theoretically dangerous to her cauldron but irresistible for documentation purposes.

Really happening: Weirwood paste does something real. It facilitates greensight access. A maester would call it a bitter psychoactive alkaloid preparation. What it does pharmacologically vs. magically is the canon's standing ambiguity. It likely does both.

Comedic potential: She runs the most precisely wrong supply-chain analysis for acquiring weirwood paste: the northern forests are beyond her current factor network, the Children of the Forest are not a known trade contact, the godswood in the Red Keep contains a heart tree but extraction of paste from a heart tree is not available through the maester's still-room. Her procurement-planning monologue for a magically-active psychedelic would be meticulous and wrong about every single logistical node.

Curdle/horror potential: If she ever obtained and consumed weirwood paste, the question of what a non-greenseer — specifically this one, who carries cultivation-novel knowledge as her internal frame — would experience in a genuine greensight session is unsettling. She would not interpret the vision correctly. She might interpret it as the cauldron achieving a soul-cultivation breakthrough. What the vision showed her would be filtered through the wrong map.

Status: **NET-NEW.** The "wants weirwood" note appears in `westeros-alchemy-substances-mystica.md` but no scene or procurement subplot is in the outline. Candidate beat for Book II — the Yi Ti expedition's return to King's Landing could pass near a weirwood source. Could be a procurement subplot: attempting to extract paste from the Red Keep's godswood tree and being stopped by the very structure of the weirwood (it does not simply yield samples to someone with a knife and a jar).

**Shade of the evening × Soul cultivation / pill elixir (A1 × B1+B3)**

She theorizes: A Tier-2–3 soul-cultivation elixir calibrated for the soul-cultivation path. She would be cautious — body cultivators cannot safely consume soul-cultivation reagents without risk of cultivation deviation. She wants a small sample for documentation purposes.

Really happening: Shade of the evening is a genuinely magical substance. The visions it produces have real content; it appears to enhance warlock-type magic. A maester calls it a dangerous psychoactive drug with addictive potential and the alarming property of permanently staining the consumer's lips blue-black.

Comedic potential: The blue-lip side effect is the project's body-horror comedy gift. If Saerys samples shade of the evening in Book II (Essos expedition), she would spend several chapters with blue-black lips explaining that "the soul-cultivation elixir has begun integrating at the mercury-principle level, manifesting via the oral cavity interface." No one would accept this explanation. The septa would have additional material for her ninth report.

Curdle/horror potential: If the visions are real and she experiences one under the cultivation frame, she would produce a detailed incorrect log entry about what she saw. The log entry would have some accuracy despite the wrong frame, which is worse than total error.

Status: **NET-NEW.** The substance is catalogued in `westeros-alchemy-substances-mystica.md` but no consumption scene exists in the outline. The blue-lip gag is a ready-made comedic set-piece for Book II. Candidate card: could warrant a discrete scene-beat card if it becomes a running body-horror motif.

**The oily black stone × Devouring/gluttony path + the horror of the correct appetite (A1 × B2+B7)**

She theorizes: Tier-4 supreme reagent; ancient; not of this world's qi-stratum; the correct next tier for the Cauldron-Belly. The register begins to falter here — no further gloss arrives.

Really happening: Unknown. The oily black stone is associated with pre-human builders, with locations of historical terror (Yeen, the Five Forts), with the deepest unresolved horror in ASOIAF. The cultivation register's "not from this world's qi-stratum" may be the closest any framework gets to the truth.

Comedic potential: Zero. This is the one item on the reagent list where the comedy does not fire. The register's silence is itself the signal. A maestra would file it as an unusual geological material; Saerys files it as the apex reagent and for once does not explain further.

Curdle/horror potential: Maximum. The Cauldron-Belly's appetite for the oily black stone is the gluttony-path at its terminal expression: a mechanism that began consuming an eggshell has escalated, through everything the book throws at it, to wanting to eat something that the narrative itself treats as potentially catastrophic. The devouring-cultivation path (B2 §13) ends in this shape: the practitioner standing before the one thing that might not be safe to devour, and wanting to anyway.

Status: ALREADY-EXPLOITED (fully treated in `westeros-alchemy-substances-mystica.md`, `cultivation-cosmology-dao-law-and-dark-paths.md`, and the fusion outline Book III M2). **NET-NEW angle:** the cultivation-genre framing of this specifically as the gluttony-path's terminal arc — the B2 §21 gluttony-dao reading — has not been formally linked to the oily stone appetite in a single place. The two-path convergence (gluttony-path + the genre's devourer archetype = the Cauldron-Belly reaching for Tier-4) warrants a formal annotation.

**Dragons (dead; the Dance battlefield) × Corpse cultivation / death-qi environments (A2 × B2)**

She theorizes: The largest reagent windfall in recorded history; Tier-3 to Tier-4 materials; systematic harvest is the correct response. The cultivation log notes the advancement opportunity without processing the catastrophe.

Really happening: The field of dead dragons is a historical civilizational catastrophe. The death of these creatures ends Targaryen magical supremacy, freezes the wildfire production capacity, and stills the glass candles. The maester would call it an irreplaceable ecological loss. Everyone calls it grief.

Comedic potential: None at this stage — the corpse-cultivation frame arrives at Book III, past the farce horizon.

Curdle/horror potential: The cultivation genre's corpse-cultivator thrives in death-qi environments — battlefields, mass-death sites, places where the powerful died suddenly. The Dance's battlefield IS the cultivation genre's premium corpse-cultivation site, translated into the Westerosi register. Saerys walking it with a refiner's calm maps exactly onto the corpse-cultivator's standard scene: the figure moving through the dead, absorbing what remains. The genre-literate reader sees the corpse-cultivator's posture; the general reader sees something monstrous; both readings are correct.

Status: ALREADY-EXPLOITED (the identical-shot progression, the harvest scene, are load-bearing in the fusion outline). **NET-NEW angle:** the corpse-cultivation frame (B2 §17 in the library's taxonomy) is not explicitly linked to this scene in any existing document. The connection — a death-qi environment populated by the highest-rank dead, a practitioner who advances through processing remains — is a formal cultivation-type match that would give the bones author a precise vocabulary register for the harvest scene. Candidate annotation rather than a new scene.

**The Others / White Walkers × Ice-demonic-sect + heavenly tribulation (A2 + A7 × B2+B7)**

She theorizes: An ice-attribute demonic sect of Nascent Soul tier or above, running a devouring-cultivation program using wights as their sect-army. The Long Night was their last heavenly-tribulation-equivalent deployment against the mortal realm. The ice/fire axis conflict between R'hllor and the Great Other is a righteous-vs-demonic cultivation sect war at civilizational scale.

Really happening: The Others are real; their nature is ASOIAF's deepest open mystery. The ice/fire framing may not be wrong as a structural description; the cultivation mechanism is completely wrong. Wights are not devouring-cultivation fodder; they are reanimated dead under the Others' power. The maester would file the Long Night as folklore. It is not.

Comedic potential: Limited — the Others are outside Saerys's operational range in the main story. The comedy is the precision of her analysis: "The Inferior Path is not yet qualified to engage this sect directly" is an entirely accurate survival assessment delivered in the entirely wrong register.

Curdle/horror potential: If Saerys ever encountered a wight or an Other directly, her cultivation-register would attempt to classify it. The classification would be correct in structure (ice-demonic-sect practitioner at Nascent Soul+) and wrong in every useful way for survival. She would want to harvest the body.

Status: ALREADY-EXPLOITED (covered in `westeros-alchemy-substances-mystica.md` Part III bestiary). **NET-NEW angle:** the Long Night × the cultivation genre's concept of a "civilizational qi-deviation event" — the idea that the Long Night was a realm-scale breakthrough tribulation dispatched against the entire mortal world — has not been explored as a discrete intersection. See the Net-New section for fuller treatment.

The Iron Bank's institutional durability — the way its paper outlasts the governments that issued it — is exactly what makes the orphaned king's-hand license useful. She has turned a dead king's paper into a spirit stone: it carries value because it is old and correct, regardless of whether the issuing authority still exists.

Status: **NET-NEW.** The Iron Bank × spirit stones intersection is mentioned briefly in `cultivation-genre-reference.md` Part IV.B (she classifies coin as "false currency") but never developed into a specific analysis of the Iron Bank as a spirit-stone-analog institution. This is a Book II candidate scene (the Braavos visit) where she formally evaluates the Iron Bank as a cultivation-economic institution and reaches the partially-correct conclusion above.

**The Doom of Valyria × Cultivation deviation (zouhuo rumo) at civilizational scale (A7 × B1+B7)**

She theorizes: The Valyrian civilization experienced a collective cultivation deviation — the entire dragonlord civilization was engaged in a form of mass-cultivation whose qi-deviation event destroyed the civilization from within. The Smoking Sea is the residual evidence of uncontrolled fire-attribute qi running wild through the geological substrate.

Really happening: The Doom is unexplained. The most common fan speculation is volcanic; some theories involve magical experiments gone wrong; the text leaves it open. The "dragonlords did something catastrophic to themselves" interpretation is textually supported even if the mechanism isn't specified.

Comedic potential: Her formal geological analysis of the Smoking Sea as "residual qi-contamination from a civilizational-scale cultivation deviation event" is exactly the kind of misreading that is approximately right. She would submit this analysis to the Iron Bank as a risk-assessment report for the Valyrian recovery zone.

Curdle/horror potential: She draws the inference explicitly: "A civilization that over-cultivated without paying the karmic cost was destroyed by its own accumulated qi. The Dance of the Dragons is a smaller-scale version of the same event: the Targaryen cultivation-civilization is experiencing a qi-deviation event at the dynasty level." The inference is approximately correct and she adjusts her strategy accordingly: she does not accumulate; she liquidates; she converts everything into portable, non-accumulation-dependent assets (paper, immunity, a ship). The Doom teaches her to exit before the deviation fires. That is the ship-heist's deepest root.

Status: **NET-NEW.** This is one of the most important NET-NEW intersections. The Doom-as-cultivation-deviation analysis directly informs her strategic logic in a way no existing document has articulated. It is not just a gag; it is a character-logic beat that explains why the ship-heist is the correct move in cultivation-genre terms. A practitioner who has correctly diagnosed a civilizational qi-deviation does not stay for the explosion. She leaves with a ship.

**Daenys the Dreamer / the flight from Valyria × Dao-favored protagonist + fortuitous encounter (A7 × B7+B9)**

She theorizes: Daenys the Dreamer was a fate-dao practitioner who received a prophetic inheritance — a high-tier fortuitous encounter with the fate-dao itself — and used it to execute a sect-preservation strategy twelve years before the civilizational qi-deviation event. The Targaryen sect's survival was the direct result of a successful fate-dao cultivation achievement. The five dragons they brought to Dragonstone were the sect's most valuable cultivation resources.

Really happening: Daenys the Dreamer prophetically foresaw the Doom and the Targaryens fled to Dragonstone twelve years before it happened. This is genuine Targaryen magical heritage — the dream-blood runs in the family.

Comedic potential: Her analysis of Daenys the Dreamer as "the highest-tier fate-dao practitioner confirmed in the historical record" is genuine admiration expressed in wrong vocabulary. She has enormous respect for this ancestor. She also notes that Daenys's survival-optimization strategy is exactly what she herself is attempting: use foreknowledge (hers comes from cultivation novels rather than prophecy) to survive a civilizational event.

Curdle/horror potential: Saerys IS Daenys the Dreamer's analog in this generation — the Targaryen who sees what is coming and prepares to survive it, not through martial power but through knowledge and preparation. Daenys used fate-dao cultivation. Saerys uses cultivation-novel knowledge and mithridatism. Both are forms of the same thing: having read the pattern before it fires. The horror is that she succeeds in the same way Daenys did — she survives. The question is what she leaves behind.

Status: **NET-NEW.** The Daenys × dao-favored-protagonist parallel is not explicitly drawn in any library document. It is structurally load-bearing: it positions Saerys as the latest in a family line of Targaryens who survive civilizational collapses through unconventional foreknowledge, and gives her cultivation-register logic for why she should escape rather than stay. This is a candidate annotation for the existing character card or a condition card entry.

**The Kingsguard / knightly tournament × Formation master / deployment (A3+A8 × B8)**

She theorizes: The Kingsguard is a sword-sect formation unit deployed around the Targaryen sect's ancestral compound. The tourney formation — the lists, the spectators, the judge's box — is a deployment formation that channels "face" (qi-fortune) from the victor's performance to the sponsor-sect's reputation. The herald is the formation activator.

Really happening: The Kingsguard is an elite guard order with formal oaths and rankings. The tourney is a regulated competition.

Comedic potential: She views every Kingsguard drill as a formation-training exercise and would be genuinely interested in their deployment patterns purely from a formation-master's analytical standpoint. She studies the Kingsguard the way a formation master studies an enemy sect's defensive array: looking for the formation eye (the most critical node to disable).

Curdle/horror potential: She identifies the Kingsguard's formation eye as the individual whose absence would most destabilize the defensive array. She is right. She has never told anyone this. She files it as "formation-vulnerability analysis; no current application."

Status: **NET-NEW.** Not in any library document. The formation-eye analysis of the Kingsguard's defensive role is a set-piece: a scene where she watches a Kingsguard training drill and produces a formal formation-vulnerability report in her log. It would be correct in structure and wrong in vocabulary. It could fire as foreshadowing: the Kingsguard formation eye she identifies is the one that fails during the Dance.

## Part 4 — Hot Zones and Barren Zones

### Hot Zones (where the genres rhyme richest)

**Zone 1: Substances × Alchemy/Pills (A1 × B3)**  
The project's foundational hot zone. Every Westerosi magical or pharmacological substance gets a tier assignment, a cauldron-processing event, and a broken-clock gloss. The three-register collision (cultivation / maester / ASOIAF truth) is the production tool. Already comprehensively mined.

**Zone 2: Creatures/Beasts × Beast cultivation (A2 × B5)**  
Dragons as spirit-beasts at Rank-7–9, the failed taming contract, the egg-as-reagent, the Dance-battlefield-as-windfall. Already comprehensively mined. Residual net-new: dead-dragon blood as bloodline-resonance material.

**Zone 3: Magic-phenomena × Dao/Karma/Tribulation (A4 × B7)**  
The Dance as Dynasty-class tribulation; killing karma Saerys doesn't run; the heartless-dao as grief-armor; the Heavenly Dao as the Crown-equivalent. Already mined. Residual net-new: Daenys's death as "insufficient-realm soul-reforging" beat.

**Zone 4: Institutions × Sect social-order (A3 × B6)**  
Maesters, Guild, Faith, Kingsguard as sect types. Already mined. Residual net-new: the Alchemists' Guild apprentice-without-joining scene; the Iron Bank as spirit-stone analog.

**Zone 5: History/Legend × Cultivation deviation + Dao-favored (A7 × B1+B7+B9)**  
UNDER-MINED. The Doom-as-qi-deviation, the Long Night as civilizational sect-war, Daenys the Dreamer as dao-favored foreknowledge protagonist — these are the most productive NET-NEW intersections and none is in the outline. The hot zone for the next development pass.

**Zone 6: Social/Cultural mechanisms × Sect social order + Metagenre (A8 × B6+B9)**  
The septa, the below-stairs faction, the paper empire as cultivation structures. Already mined. Residual net-new: knighthood/tourney as face-slapping arena; betrothal as commodified dual-cultivation; Faith-as-Heavenly-Dao alignment.

**Zone 7: Geography × Spirit-vein + Cultivation cosmology (A6 × B1+B4)**  
UNDER-MINED. Dragonstone as spirit-vein / spiritual ground; Yi Ti as high-density zone; Valyria as qi-deviation ruin; Asshai as shadow-attribute ground. These are geography-specific comedy-horror beats with correct-structure misreadings. The library has treated these at the general level; scene-level treatment is missing.

---

### Barren Zones (where the genres genuinely do not map)

**Music / sound cultivation × almost any Westerosi element (B2 §14 × most of A)**  
The Westerosi court music tradition (harps, drums, songs) does not rhyme productively with the cultivation genre's music-dao path. There is no Westerosi entity whose power is expressed through vibration in a way that maps cleanly. The Helaena/insect-vibration note is the one exception and it is more thin than rich.

**Puppet / mechanism masters × Westerosi elements (B2 §15 × most of A)**  
No puppet-master analog in Westeros. The Faceless Men's faces might stretch to "flesh puppets" but that is a thin gag at best.

**Talisman / rune mastery × most Westerosi elements (B2 §11 × most of A)**  
The one exception (maester's chain as talisman-array) is already mined. The rest of the talisman-master vocabulary does not map onto anything in Westeros with productive results.

**The divine realm / Nine Heavens / immortal realm cosmology × most Westerosi elements (B1 × A6+A7)**  
The "ascending to the upper immortal realm" structure has no Westerosi correlate. Westeros does not have a confirmed afterlife or a Heaven-equivalent. The cultivation genre's aspirational cosmological destination has nothing to land on. Her "ascension" is horizontal (she sails off the edge of the map) not vertical. This is correct for the project — she is ascending out of the story's world, not to a higher plane — but it means the Nine Heavens vocabulary stays dormant.

**Flying swords / divine abilities × most Westerosi elements (B8 × most of A)**  
No one in Westeros has a flying sword. No one cultivates divine abilities in the genre's sense. These categories are barren for the specific setting.

**The arrogant young master as a plot-driving antagonist (B9 × the project's structure)**  
Aemond is classified in this slot but he is emphatically not the antagonist. He is a foil. The face-slapping payoff the genre promises against the arrogant young master does not fire. The trope's barrenness in the project is itself a load-bearing structural choice — documented in `cultivation-metagenre-roots-and-archetypes.md`. The barren zone is deliberately barren.

**1. Betrothal / marriage politics × Dual cultivation / dao companion (NET-NEW; HIGH)**  
*How it fires:* Her resistance to betrothals is reframed as refusing an inadequate cultivation-partner assignment. Her classification of the betrothal system as "a society-wide commodified dao-companion protocol" is the project's most devastating cultural critique delivered in wrong vocabulary. Daenys is her actual dao companion — the one who fulfills the genre's function and dies to catalyze the protagonist's advancement. The cultivation-genre term for what happens to Daenys in Book III is "the dao companion's sacrifice." The blank line is what happens when the protagonist cannot process the dao companion's sacrifice through the register.  
*Candidate card:* `cond-betrothal-dual-cultivation-frame.card.md`  
*Candidate scene:* Book I — her formal analysis of why she is being offered an inferior betrothal (deficient spiritual roots = deficient dao-companion market value).

**2. Doom of Valyria × Civilizational cultivation deviation — strategic inference (NET-NEW; HIGH)**  
*How it fires:* She reads the Doom as a qi-deviation event and draws the inference: the Dance is the same thing at smaller scale. The correct response to a civilizational qi-deviation is to liquidate, minimize accumulation, and exit before the deviation fires. The ship-heist is therefore the cultivation-genre's correct strategic response to the Doom-pattern she has identified. This makes her escape not cowardice or grief-avoidance but a technically correct cultivation-strategy move — which is both a gag and a genuine vindication of her framework in the worst possible context.  
*Candidate scene:* Book II — she reads the historical record of the Doom and produces the formal deviation-analysis. Book III — she explicitly cross-references the Doom → Dance pattern as her strategic justification for the heist.

**3. The Long Night × Ice-demonic-sect's civilizational incursion + the Wall as containment formation (NET-NEW; HIGH)**  
*How it fires:* Her historical-cultivation analysis of the Age of Heroes as a "civilization-scale righteous-vs-demonic sect war" and the Wall as a "containment formation" is both a precision misreading and approximately correct. The scene where she reads Long Night legends and produces the analysis — filing the Others as ice-attribute cultivators, the Wall as a grand formation, the heroes of the Age of Heroes as righteous-path cultivators armed with fire-attribute materials — is a set-piece comedy-horror beat. The horror: she correctly identifies the threat, correctly identifies the countermeasures, and returns to her advancement schedule because the ice-attribute demonic sect is "outside the cauldron's current operational range."  
*Candidate scene:* Book I or II — she reads the maester's Long Night history records and produces a cultivation-register analysis that the maester finds baffling.

**4. Daenys the Dreamer × Dao-favored foreknowledge protagonist (NET-NEW; HIGH)**  
*How it fires:* Saerys IS Daenys the Dreamer's structural analog: the Targaryen in each generation who uses unconventional foreknowledge (prophecy / cultivation-novel knowledge) to survive a civilizational collapse while everyone else stays. This parallel, once stated, makes her escape feel like family destiny rather than cowardice. She is the latest in a bloodline that produces one person per catastrophe who reads the pattern correctly and gets out. The cultivation frame does this: she classifies Daenys as "the highest-tier fate-dao practitioner in the historical record" and herself as "the current generation's attempt at the same achievement, using a different foreknowledge source." She does not realize this is an accurate self-assessment.  
*Candidate annotation:* The character card or a condition card.

**5. Helaena's prophetic riddles × Fate-dao cultivation intelligence-transmission (NET-NEW; HIGH)**  
*How it fires:* A specific scene where Helaena says something about the Dance before it happens — one of her characteristic prophetic riddles, correctly interpreted as a warning — and Saerys processes it as "sect intelligence from the fate-dao cultivation sister, requiring further analysis." She files it. She does not act on it. The warning was correct; the frame was wrong; the correct intelligence reached the wrong receiver.  
*Candidate scene:* Book II or early Book III — this is a tragedy beat with enormous compression: the cultivation register demonstrably prevented her from using correct information because it required the wrong interpretation step.

**6. Knighthood / tourney × The sword-cultivation path and the face-slap arena (NET-NEW; MEDIUM)**  
*How it fires:* A set-piece scene where she attends a tourney (compelled by the septa or by court obligation) and produces a formal cultivation-analysis: the lists as face-slapping deployment zone, the herald as formation activator, the mounted joust as "face-slapping via material-weapon manifestation of sword-intent." She declines to participate ("the Inferior Path does not compete in sword-cultivation formats"). The scene clarifies that her refusal of the face-slap payoff is not just structural — it is a formal decision she has made and logged.  
*Candidate scene:* Book I.

**7. Iron Bank of Braavos × Spirit-stone analog institution (NET-NEW; MEDIUM)**  
*How it fires:* Book II Braavos visit. She formally evaluates the Iron Bank as a cultivation-economic institution: "The Iron Bank has replicated spirit-stone exchange protocols through longevity of obligation rather than qi-crystallization. This is impressive. A mortal institution that has achieved spirit-stone equivalence through sheer institutional durability deserves professional respect." The Iron Bank would have no idea what she was writing in her analysis. The gag has the additional layer: she is about to use Iron Bank-adjacent instruments (bills of exchange) for the ship-heist. She is studying the institution she is about to exploit.

**8. Dragonstone × Spirit-vein geography / spiritual ground survey (NET-NEW; MEDIUM)**

**9. Shade of the evening × Blue-lip body-horror comedy (NET-NEW; MEDIUM)**

**11. Faceless Men × Intelligence-sharing factor relationship (NET-NEW; LOWER)**

**12. Dragon blood (post-Dance) × Bloodline-resonance material (NET-NEW; LOWER)**
