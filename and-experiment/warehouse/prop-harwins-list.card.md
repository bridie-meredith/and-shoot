# Prop — Harwin's List

> 🅿️ **PARKED for the Dance installment (2026-06-11)** — like its owner `ser-harwin-the-patient` (`scope:
> project`), this artifact is held for the later Dance book, **not used in span-A this series**. Ser Harwin
> Strong does not appear in the escape series; his eat-list parks with him. The eat-list running-gag, if
> wanted for span-A, is a NEW card with a span-A keeper (the maester / a nanny), not this one.

Ser Harwin Strong's running list of items Gael Targaryen is no longer permitted to put in her mouth. It began as a practical safety measure. It became, over the course of Book II, the project's primary running-gag instrument. It is also, in structural fact, a love letter written in the form of an incident log.

---

## Physical Description

A piece of folded parchment, initially, that became two pieces, then a folded booklet that Harwin sewed with waxed thread himself (he has never sewn anything else in his life; the stitching is militarily competent and aesthetically disastrous). He keeps it in his left boot, next to the spare knife — a placement that tells you something about how he ranks relative threats.

The handwriting on the list is Harwin's: large, careful letters that suggest a man taught to read late and who takes the task seriously. He adds items in his own hand. He does not cross items off. The list is additive-only because "we tried that, it went badly" is the structure of each entry, and he has no interest in discovering whether she has reconsidered in the intervening period.

Each entry is numbered. As of the opening of Book II, the list is at item 31. By Book II's end it has reached item 44. It does not grow in Book III because Harwin dies in Book II, item 44 ("the green jar, label in unknown script, acquired in Braavos, smells like the sea and something worse") being his final addition.

The list survives him. She finds it on his effects. She does not read it. It is the first item in the account-book closed to zero — an entry that was never going to balance. She keeps the list in the account-book afterward, between two pages, where it functions as a bookmark.

---

## Affordances / Uses

**As a practical safety list:** Partially effective. It stops her from ingesting items she has *already been stopped from ingesting*. It does not anticipate new categories. He adds to it as new categories are discovered. This is not an efficient system.

**As a running-gag instrument:** The list's comedy is structural — each new item is evidence of a specific incident that required a specific prohibition. Item 7 ("the maester's samples, any of them, labeled or unlabeled") implies an incident. Item 19 ("nothing from any jar purchased at a market without showing it to me first") implies a pattern. Item 27 ("the interesting green rock") is one of seven items describing geological specimens she has attempted to consume. Item 31 ("any preparation described by the vendor as 'medicinal for the very robust'") implies that at some point she specifically sought out substances sold under this description. The list is a comedic archaeology of all her worst ideas, in the order they occurred to him.

**As the behavior card's memory monument made physical:** Per `saerys-targaryen-behavior.card.md`, Harwin's memory monument is registered as "the list" — the object by which his care for her is indexed. The behavior card notes that he never says "I love you" directly; he adds to the list. The list is how he talks about the relationship. Each item is the evidence of an occasion when something frightened him and he responded by writing it down so it would not happen again. That is what parents do. He was not her parent. He was the person who filled the space.

**As a narrative weight-bearing object (Bk II close):** When Harwin dies (indirectly attributable to her vanity, per the persona card), the list is the object that carries his absence. Not his sword, not his armor — the list. She does not grieve him. The cultivation register closes the account. The list goes into the account-book and the account-book does not open to that page again. The reader understands what the register has filed and what it cost.

## Sensory Hooks

- The parchment has been refolded so many times that the fold-lines are permanent; it opens in exactly two ways, neither of which is flat
- Boot-leather smell, faint, under the parchment smell; it has lived in the boot since item 3
- His handwriting presses firmly — he holds a quill like a weapon; the letters are slightly indented on the other side
- The wax-thread stitching of the booklet binding has been redone once, with different thread; she can see the line where he repaired it
- The unfinished entry has a small dried ink-mark where the quill stopped

## Portability

Harwin's (his left boot). After his death: between two pages of the account-book, functioning as a bookmark. She carries it without registering she is carrying it.

---

## Carry State

While Harwin lives: in his boot, always. After: in the account-book, which is always with her. The list does not take up much space. It weighs less than she expects every time she notices it is there.

---

## Functional State

The list does not grow after Harwin's death. It has 44 items and one unfinished entry. Its function as a safety instrument is suspended; its function as a weight-bearing object takes over. It is complete. The comedy engine it powered is extinguished with him.

Item 44 is the last item he had time to think about. Item 44.5 (the unfinished black stone entry) is what he was worried about when he died.
