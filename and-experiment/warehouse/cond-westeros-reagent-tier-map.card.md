# Condition — Westeros Reagent Tier Map

The Tier 0–4 material classification table as Saerys Targaryen uses it. Her primary reference system for every substance she ingests, compounds, or assigns to the network. Needed by bones authors for any scene involving her relationship to a material — she cannot encounter a substance without immediately assigning it a tier.

**Purpose of this card:** bones-author lookup. Tier assignments are the most frequently reached-for element of her cultivation register — every still-room scene, every ingestion beat, every "I noted the reagent at such-and-such grade" line requires this table. Too long to reload from the source doc inline; this is the dispatch-loadable condensation.

**Hard fence:** The broken clock stays broken. Her tier classifications are belief, not truth. The real-state column is the authoritative column for prose consistency. The pharmacological and magical facts of each tier level are what the scene must honor; her cultivation gloss is what she narrates.

**Cross-reference for depth:** `and-experiment/design/cultivation-library/cultivation-genre-reference.md` § Part IV.B (the full reagent-grade taxonomy with all broken-clock mappings and notes on spirit herbs, beast materials, and the resource-gradient cosmology). `cond-westerosi-poison-pharmacology` for the substance-by-substance pharmacological detail on Tier 1 items. `cond-maesters-cabinet` for the maester-register counterpart — the same substances seen from the Citadel's vocabulary.

## Description

A five-level classification system Saerys applies to every material in her operational scope. In cultivation-genre terms this maps to the standard reagent grade ladder (mortal → spirit → earth → heaven → saint/divine). In Westerosi terms it maps to: common herbs, medicinal/poisonous plants, magically-resonant materials, genuinely magical Westeros materials, and the frontier-class materials where the broken clock may have genuine charge.

## The Tier Table

| Her tier | Her grade name | Westeros material | Real property | Broken-clock note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 0 | Mortal-grade | Common herbs, outer weirwood bark, dried fungi, kitchen spices | Standard medicine; the maester uses these daily; pharmacologically inert or mildly active | Correct that these are low-potency; wrong about why |
| Tier 1 (early) | Spirit-grade early | Nightshade, hemlock, monkshood, milk-of-the-poppy | Real medicinal/poisonous plants; controlled by maesters; genuine dose-response lethality curves | Correct that these are pharmacologically significant; the "qi-disruptor" framing is the wrong register on a correct observation |
| Tier 1 (peak) | Spirit-grade peak | Concentrated Lysene tears, oil of poppy at high grade, basilisk venom | Potent-to-lethal poisons; the upper range of her mithridatism program | Her "Tier 1 peak" classification corresponds to the substances where her documented tolerance is real and hard-won |
| Tier 2 | Earth-grade | Dragonglass / obsidian (raw), weirwood heartwood and sap, Valyrian steel filings | Materials with confirmed magical properties in Westeros canon (dragonglass kills Others; weirwood has genuine magical resonance; Valyrian steel is magically forged) | Her instinct that these are categorically different from pharmacological plants is correct; her explanation is wrong in almost every detail |
| Tier 3 | Earth-grade high | Powdered dragon egg, dragon bone fragment, wildfire concentrate | Genuinely magical Westeros materials; uncontrolled ingestion produces real effects — the broken clock's first genuine ring zone | The wildfire-shard fever (Bk I M3) is the canonical confirmed real effect; dragon egg powder's effects are real but less controlled; this tier is where her methodology intersects actual magic |
| Tier 4 | Heaven-grade | The oily black stone; dragon-fire essence; blood of a living dragon | Materials at or beyond the frontier of Westerosi magical understanding; unpredictable effects | The register begins to falter at this tier — the comedy-farce volume drops; the oily black stone's appetite in Yi Ti (Bk II) reads as wrong and hungry in a way her other appetites do not |

## Acquisition Source Map

Her supply-line is structured around tier:

- **Tier 0–1 (early):** the maester's still-room stock and the charity sick-house supply; the maesters' restricted-items list is a Tier 1 (early) problem, not a Tier 1 (peak) problem
- **Tier 1 (peak):** the Braavosi factor network; some substances require Essosi import; appears on the trade manifests as "medicinal preparations"
- **Tier 2:** the Alchemists' Guild (for wildfire-adjacent materials); weirwood is rare and requires strategic acquisition; Valyrian steel filings are a long-term project
- **Tier 3:** the dragon egg was a one-time event; wildfire shard was a one-time event; dragon bone fragments require the Dance's aftermath (Bk III windfall)
- **Tier 4:** the oily black stone is in Yi Ti; she knows this; it is the stated goal of the Bk II expedition's cultivation-register framing

## Her Classification Vocabulary

When she assigns a tier, she uses characteristic phrasings the impersonator should replicate:

- Tier 0: "base-grade; suitable for early tempering; no appreciable qi-density"
- Tier 1 (early): "spirit-grade; detectable qi-resonance; correct attribute classification required before consumption"
- Tier 1 (peak): "peak spirit-grade; the cauldron must be confirmed stable at the previous threshold before advancement"
- Tier 2: "earth-grade; genuine magical resonance confirmed by observation; extreme caution indicated"
- Tier 3: "earth-grade high; the cauldron enters a real tribulation at this tier; the advancement is genuine if the tribulation is survived"
- Tier 4: "heaven-grade; the Inferior Path notes the tier assignment with less certainty than at lower tiers; the dose-log protocol does not yet cover this range"

The transition from Tier 3 to Tier 4 is the only point where her usually-flat register admits uncertainty. She does not use the phrase "I don't know" — she uses "the protocol does not yet cover this range." Same thing, her vocabulary.

---

## The "Immortality Herb" Note

The expensive Yi Ti tea she encounters in Bk II (Ch II.5) is placed, by her, at Tier 2 ("ancient cultivation heritage; earth-grade minimum"). Her assessment after consumption: "the herb contains no spiritual density; its qi-coherence is below threshold." She is correct by accident — there is no qi to detect. It is expensive tea and has no potency. The tier mis-assignment (then correction) is a comedy beat: she diagnosed a Tier 2 item and then documented it as Tier 0 when it did nothing. Her protocol is more honest than her initial classification.

## Sensory Impact

This condition operates as a mental overlay in any scene where Saerys encounters a material. Its behavioral signature: she goes still for one breath when she picks up or sees a new substance; she categorizes before she does anything else; she can be distracted from social obligations by an interesting reagent mid-conversation. These are Tier-assignment behaviors, not tier outcomes.

---

## Duration

Persistent across all three books. Tier assignments are stable (she does not re-classify downward); she adds to the upper tiers as she acquires new source knowledge in Bk II–III.
