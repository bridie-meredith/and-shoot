# Prop — The Christening Spoon

The grinding instrument of the Inferior Path's founding act. Named in the chapter slug for I.1 ("the-christening-spoon"). Small, silver, ceremonial — and the physical anchor of the Cauldron-Belly's declaration. Recurs as the comedy-of-the-belly's founding callback: the one object that is always somewhere in the nursery and always implicates her.

---

## Physical Description

A silver christening spoon of the sort presented to noble and royal children at name-day or other ceremonial occasions. In form: shallow bowl, long handle, the silver worked into a simple but high-quality decorative shape — likely with a heraldic element (dragon motif, Targaryen styling, or the symbol of the gift-giver) on the handle's terminus. The bowl is small — the size appropriate for feeding a baby, not an adult.

Hallmarks of use: the bowl is scratched. The scratches are fine, distributed across the bowl's interior and lower rim in a pattern consistent with repeated hard contact with a rough mineral surface. They were not there before I.1. They are never explained to the nursery staff, who assume the child ate something off a stone floor. The spoon is not cleaned of the grey powder residue before the nursery staff find it. This is how they know.

Weight: negligible; lighter than it feels like it should be; the silver is thin-gauge ornamental, not working cutlery. It was never meant to grind a dead dragon egg. It performed the function anyway.

Temperature: silver takes the temperature of its environment quickly; on the day of I.1 it was room-temperature, then body-temperature as Saerys held it for an extended period.

---

## The Physical Anchor

The christening spoon is the founding *object* of the Cauldron-Belly in a way the cradle-egg is not. The egg was a given — placed in her cradle before she could make choices; she transformed it. The spoon she reached for. It was the nearest implement. It was not given to her for this purpose. The selection was practical and three years old and entirely correct: the bowl is the right shape for grinding, the handle is the right length for leverage, and the silver is soft enough to work against the egg's harder mineral surface without destroying the tool.

She kept it. After I.1, the christening spoon lives in the nursery as a seemingly-normal object with an abnormal history. It is her first functional tool. It is also the comedy prop for the belly-as-cultivation gag: subsequent appearances (a toddler reaching for it when something else needs to be "processed," a nurse finding it where it should not be, the spoon's presence in a scene as a single low-comedy signal that Saerys is about to eat something inadvisable) play off the founding act.

The chapter slug "the-christening-spoon" (I.1) names it rather than the egg. This is intentional: the egg was given; the spoon was chosen; the choice is the act.

## Affordances / Uses

**Primary (established at I.1):** grinding implement for reducing solid material to powder for consumption. Used on the cradle-egg; the technique (bowl-on-mineral surface, short strokes, collecting powder in the bowl for transfer to mouth) is established here and would generalize to other grinding tasks if required.

**Secondary (recurrent comedy prop):** the christening spoon's presence in a scene signals the comedy-of-the-belly. Bones authors staging any eating-antics scene (I.4's montage, any subsequent cauldron-tempering beat) can use the spoon as a low-cost physical comedy anchor. It does not need to appear in every scene, but its presence makes any of those scenes denser.

**As cultivator's first instrument:** In her register, the spoon is a Tier 1 cultivation instrument — a mortal-grade tool that performed a legitimate refining task on a Tier 2 ingredient. She has not formally classified it as a fabao. It has not rated that assessment. It is the first tool the Inferior Path ever used, which she notes once in the dose-log under a margin annotation ("initial refining instrument: mortal-grade silver implement, function confirmed, no spiritual contamination detected"). This may be the single most absurd entry in the dose-log.

## Sensory Hooks

- Silver's clean metallic taste when held between the teeth, which is the wrong way to hold it and which Saerys does briefly during the grinding task
- Weight: lighter than it looks; the balance tips toward the bowl when held at the handle's end
- Surface: the engraved decoration on the handle has a raised-edge texture that catches under a fingernail
- Sound of grinding: the fine high-pitched mineral scrape, as described on the cradle-egg card — the spoon transmits this upward through the handle as a faint vibration; Saerys found this uninteresting; the nursery staff found it appalling
- The grey powder residue in the bowl's scratches, when the spoon is found after I.1: fine, pale, chalky; it does not clean out fully; the ghost of the founding act stays in the metal

---

## Portability

Portable. Small enough to fit in a pocket, a sleeve, or a cupped hand. Weight: negligible. After I.1 it stays in the nursery — it is too obviously a baby's spoon to carry on a person at court; it is also too obviously connected to the founding act to discard.

---

## Carry State

Post-I.1 location: the nursery of Maegor's Holdfast, in or near Saerys's things. Not carried on the body outside the nursery context. Present as a background prop in any nursery-set scene. Its location is stable until the move out of Maegor's Holdfast at Book I's close; after that, its custody is unspecified but Saerys would not discard it.

Weight and concealment: small, light, easy to conceal if needed, but not deliberately concealed — it is a christening spoon; nobody looks for it.

---

## Functional State

**Single-use tool:** The spoon performed its grinding function in I.1 and was not destroyed by the use. It remains functional as a spoon and as a grinding implement. The scratches are cosmetic damage, not structural.

**Comedy-trigger state:** from I.1 forward, the spoon's appearance in a scene implies the comedy-of-the-belly. It does not need dialogue or action to carry this implication; its presence is sufficient signal for an attentive reader.

**Dose-log footnote:** annotated once in the margin of an early dose-log entry as "initial refining instrument." It has no further functional state in the cultivation record because she did not continue to use it as a grinding instrument after the cradle-egg — subsequent eating antics do not require grinding (she moved to liquids and direct ingestion). The spoon's record closes at entry 1-adjacent.
