# Condition — The Trade-Network Formation (Real-State)

The factor-and-medicine empire as a world-state condition: its nodes, how it operates, its legal underpinning, what it carries, and why the same manifests work for both cargoes. This is the real-state card — the network as it actually exists in the world, not Gael's cultivation reading of it. Her cultivation reading (the network-as-formation, with the account-book as its formation eye) is a separate P2 card (`cond-grand-formation-trade-network`). Do NOT build that here.

**Purpose:** The world-state Gael discovers she owns in Bk II.1. The operational context for Bk I.2–I.8 plant beats and the Bk II Essosi operation. Bones authors for any scene involving the supply chain — bills of exchange, the factor, the still-room receiving a delivery, the below-stairs faction running an import — need to know what this network actually is.

---

## Description

A medicine-and-specialty-compound import network operating on the Narrow Sea under a standing royal-household authorization in the name of the apothecary works of the Princess Gael Targaryen. Founded in Bk I.2 when she wrote the first bill of exchange to Moro Tessavik. Built by: six years of accurate orders, a factor who expanded his routes because the license permitted it and the demand existed, and a below-stairs faction that handled King's Landing logistics without asking too many questions about the provenance of specific items.

By Bk II.1, when the network's full scale becomes visible in the account-book, it consists of:
- **The factor** (Moro Tessavik) — primary import agent; 17 bills filled, 11 signed purchase agreements, 3 additional supply routes built under the standing authorization
- **9 downstream vendors** — Tessavik's network of suppliers; they know him as the conduit; they do not know the account's ultimate origin
- **The below-stairs faction** — King's Landing-side logistics; receives and distributes deliveries to the still-room receiving point; the faction's contacts run the import-side errand chain
- **The still-room / soap-and-apothecary front** — the public-facing receiving point and trade operation; legitimate deliveries surface through the apothecary commerce; the legitimate apothecary-and-soap trade is the legal cover
- **The account-book** — the ledger; the only place where the network's full shape is visible; only she can read it

The network carries two cargoes. The manifest reads: *medical supplies and compound preparations.* Both cargoes appear on this manifest. The manifest is accurate. The factor noted on the second order that both were present; he has never commented further. The below-stairs faction receives both; some items go to the apothecary front's legitimate stores; others go to the still-room's locked cabinet or to her personal dose-log supply. The manifest does not distinguish.

## Sensory Impact

The network has no direct sensory presence — it is a paper-and-relationship structure. Its sensory signature comes through its nodes:

- **The account-book** (prop-account-book): the smell of old ink and good paper; the cramped notation only she can read; the weight of six years of operations in compact form
- **The still-room receiving point**: a back door, irregular delivery hours, the factor's couriers (Braavosi or Pentoshi, well-dressed in a commercial way, not military); the smell of compound preparations temporarily unsealed
- **The bills of exchange** (prop-bill-of-exchange): ink and good paper; the handwriting that is identifiably hers and identifiably precise; the standing-license notation in the upper corner
- **The factor himself** (at Bk II.1): a man who addresses himself to the paper; who does not look at her as a person; who has been the human axis of her network for six years without knowing what he was building

## Duration

**Bk I (I.2–I.5):** Formation phase. The network is being built through successive bills, each more sophisticated than the last. The factor expands his routes; the below-stairs faction builds the King's Landing logistics chain; the still-room / apothecary front establishes its receiving identity. The Bk I audience does not know the full network exists — it is staged through glimpses (the bill written, the delivery received, the account-book entry).

**Bk II (II.1–II.10):** Operational maturity. The network's full scale is revealed at II.1 when she meets the factor in person and reviews the account-book with him. This is the "the thing she discovers she owns" beat. The network then operates at full capacity through Bk II — the Essosi expeditions use its infrastructure; the Yi Ti sourcing goes through its supply-chain extensions; the Braavosi banker relationship develops in its shadow.

**Bk III (the escape):** The network stays live. The King lives; the standing authorization is never orphaned; the factor keeps filling orders under live royal-household authority. The supply chain is **not** the escape mechanism — the getaway ship is commissioned through cut-out paper and paid out of the off-books dice-coin (`OFFBOOKS-ACCOUNT`), kept deliberately clear of this network's signature-bearing account. Two separate paper systems: the apothecary network (open, under authorization) and the escape (laundered, off-books). *(The Dance's later disruption of the Narrow Sea belongs to the parked installment, decades after she has sailed.)*

## Legal Underpinning (Real-State)

**The standing authorization:** A document issued under the Queen's household authority, authorizing apothecary and medical-supply purchases charged to the Princess Gael Targaryen's works account. It is genuine, stamped, and indexed in the household's records. It was obtained across Bk I.2–I.3 via **Alicent's patronage** (the Queen, doting and indulgent of her daughter's scholarly-apothecary works, found the framing charming and appropriate to a princess of her station; the cold King, who files her as useless, never looked).

**What the authorization permits:** Apothecary and medical-supply purchases in quantities appropriate to a princess's household apothecary. In practice: it has been interpreted expansively by the factor (who built three additional supply routes under it) and by the below-stairs faction (who have used it as authority for import arrangements well beyond the original scope). No one has challenged the interpretation because the authorization is genuine, the apothecary commerce is real, the Queen does not audit her daughter's works, and the cold King has filed her useless and does not look.

**The manifests:** All deliveries are covered by a manifest reading *medical supplies and compound preparations.* The manifest form is accurate; both the medicine column and the compound-preparations column are real products with real sources. The manifest does not specify which items fall in which column. This ambiguity is structural, not accidental — the factor noted on the second order that both were present and filed it accordingly.

**Through the escape (Book III):** The King lives; the authorization is never orphaned. The network's legal anchor stays live and unchallenged. The escape is **not** run through it: the getaway is laundered off-books (cut-out paper + the dice-coin), kept clear of this signature-bearing account. The supply network simply keeps operating — open, authorized, and innocent-looking — right up to and past the morning she sails.

## Both-Cargoes-One-Manifest (Operational Logic)

This is the network's central operational fact and its founding moral ambiguity.

**Medicine column:** Herbal preparations, poisons-as-medicines (low-dose analgesics, tisane bases, wound treatments), specialty compounds for the apothecary front's legitimate trade. These are real. The apothecary-and-soap operation sells genuine remedies and goods. The maester-register name on the manifest covers these correctly.

**Compound preparations column:** Higher-concentration compounds in her mithridatism regimen; substances that read as medicine in low doses and as poisons in higher doses; the Braavosi drug-agent specialty items that exist in both columns depending on concentration and application. These are also real. They are also correctly manifested — the manifest does not lie; it categorizes both accurately under the broader *compound preparations* heading.

**The founding ambiguity:** The factor did not create this structure; he found it on the second order and filed it correctly. She did not explain which column was which; he did not ask. Six years of operations have normalized a manifest that covers two fundamentally different use-cases without distinguishing between them. This is the trade network's structural condition. It is not the factor's fault. It is not her fault in the sense of a specific lie. The ambiguity preceded the network and is now the network's operating logic.

The factor's summary of this (delivered, without affect, when Gael at age nine first referenced the compound column explicitly): "Both cargoes have been on this manifest since the second order. Nothing has changed." He said this very quietly. He has never said it again. Nothing changed after he said it either.
