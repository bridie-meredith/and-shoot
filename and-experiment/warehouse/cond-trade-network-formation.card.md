# Condition — The Trade-Network Formation (Real-State)

The factor-and-medicine empire as a world-state condition: its nodes, how it operates, its legal underpinning, what it carries, and why the same manifests work for both cargoes. This is the real-state card — the network as it actually exists in the world, not Saerys's cultivation reading of it. Her cultivation reading (the network-as-formation, with the account-book as its formation eye) is a separate P2 card (`cond-grand-formation-trade-network`). Do NOT build that here.

**Purpose:** The world-state Saerys discovers she owns in Bk II.1. The operational context for Bk I.2–I.8 plant beats and the Bk II Essosi operation. Bones authors for any scene involving the supply chain — bills of exchange, the factor, the sick-house receiving a delivery, the below-stairs faction running an import — need to know what this network actually is.

---

## Description

A medicine-and-specialty-compound import network operating on the Narrow Sea under a standing royal license issued in the name of the charitable works of Princess Saerys Targaryen. Founded in Bk I.2 when she wrote the first bill of exchange to Moro Tessavik. Built by: six years of accurate orders, a factor who expanded his routes because the license permitted it and the demand existed, and a below-stairs faction that handled King's Landing logistics without asking too many questions about the provenance of specific items.

By Bk II.1, when the network's full scale becomes visible in the account-book, it consists of:
- **The factor** (Moro Tessavik) — primary import agent; 17 bills filled, 11 signed purchase agreements, 3 additional supply routes built under the King's-hand license
- **9 downstream vendors** — Tessavik's network of suppliers; they know him as the conduit; they do not know the account's ultimate origin
- **The below-stairs faction** — King's Landing-side logistics; receives and distributes deliveries to the sick-house; the faction's contacts run the import-side errand chain
- **The sick-house** — the public-facing receiving address; all legitimate deliveries arrive here; the charitable operation is the legal cover
- **The account-book** — the ledger; the only place where the network's full shape is visible; only she can read it

The network carries two cargoes. The manifest reads: *medical supplies and compound preparations.* Both cargoes appear on this manifest. The manifest is accurate. The factor noted on the second order that both were present; he has never commented further. The below-stairs faction receives both; some items go to the sick-house's treatment stores; others go to the still-room's locked cabinet or to her personal dose-log supply. The manifest does not distinguish.

## Sensory Impact

The network has no direct sensory presence — it is a paper-and-relationship structure. Its sensory signature comes through its nodes:

- **The account-book** (prop-account-book): the smell of old ink and good paper; the cramped notation only she can read; the weight of six years of operations in compact form
- **The sick-house receiving room**: a back door, irregular delivery hours, the factor's couriers (Braavosi or Pentoshi, well-dressed in a commercial way, not military); the smell of compound preparations temporarily unsealed
- **The bills of exchange** (prop-bill-of-exchange): ink and good paper; the handwriting that is identifiably hers and identifiably precise; the standing-license notation in the upper corner
- **The factor himself** (at Bk II.1): a man who addresses himself to the paper; who does not look at her as a person; who has been the human axis of her network for six years without knowing what he was building

## Duration

**Bk I (I.2–I.10):** Formation phase. The network is being built through successive bills, each more sophisticated than the last. The factor expands his routes; the below-stairs faction builds the King's Landing logistics chain; the sick-house establishes its receiving identity. The Bk I audience does not know the full network exists — it is staged through glimpses (the bill written, the delivery received, the account-book entry).

**Bk II (II.1–II.10):** Operational maturity. The network's full scale is revealed at II.1 when she meets the factor in person and reviews the account-book with him. This is the "the thing she discovers she owns" beat. The network then operates at full capacity through Bk II — the Essosi expeditions use its infrastructure; the Yi Ti sourcing goes through its supply-chain extensions; the Braavosi banker relationship develops in its shadow.

**Bk III (III.1–III.10):** Orphaned license / Dance disruption. Viserys's death at III.1 formally orphans the King's-hand standing license. The network continues to operate on practical relationship grounds — the factor still fills orders because the account has credit and the relationship holds — but the legal anchor is now the orphaned license itself. This is heist mechanic territory: the orphaned license is the paper she cashes in Book III. The Dance disrupts the Narrow Sea supply chain; the network's Braavosi-and-Essosi extensions are affected.

## Legal Underpinning (Real-State)

**The standing license:** A document issued by the King's Hand under Viserys I's authority, authorizing charitable medical supply purchases charged to the Princess Saerys Targaryen charitable works account. The license is genuine, stamped, and indexed in the Tower of the Hand's records. It was obtained in Bk I.2 through Bk I.9 via her father's good offices (Viserys I, indulgent, found the charitable framing charming and appropriate to a princess of her station).

**What the license permits:** Medical supply purchases in quantities appropriate to a functioning charitable sick-house. In practice: it has been interpreted expansively by the factor (who built three additional supply routes under it) and by the below-stairs faction (who have used it as authority for import arrangements well beyond the original scope). No one has challenged the interpretation because the license is genuine, the charitable operation is real, and Viserys is not the kind of king who audits his daughters' piety.

**The manifests:** All deliveries are covered by a manifest reading *medical supplies and compound preparations.* The manifest form is accurate; both the medicine column and the compound-preparations column are real products with real sources. The manifest does not specify which items fall in which column. This ambiguity is structural, not accidental — the factor noted on the second order that both were present and filed it accordingly.

**After Viserys's death (III.1):** The license is formally orphaned — it was issued under an authority that no longer exists. In Westerosi commercial law, such a license remains technically valid until formally revoked; it has not been revoked because no one has moved to revoke it in the chaos of the Dance. The orphaned license is one of the three heist mechanics she cashes in Lock III.

## Both-Cargoes-One-Manifest (Operational Logic)

This is the network's central operational fact and its founding moral ambiguity.

**Medicine column:** Herbal preparations, poisons-as-medicines (low-dose analgesics, tisane bases, wound treatments), specialty compounds for the sick-house's patient population. These are real. The sick-house provides genuine treatment to the poor it serves. The maester-register name on the manifest covers these correctly.

**Compound preparations column:** Higher-concentration compounds in her mithridatism regimen; substances that read as medicine in low doses and as poisons in higher doses; the Braavosi drug-agent specialty items that exist in both columns depending on concentration and application. These are also real. They are also correctly manifested — the manifest does not lie; it categorizes both accurately under the broader *compound preparations* heading.

**The founding ambiguity:** The factor did not create this structure; he found it on the second order and filed it correctly. She did not explain which column was which; he did not ask. Six years of operations have normalized a manifest that covers two fundamentally different use-cases without distinguishing between them. This is the trade network's structural condition. It is not the factor's fault. It is not her fault in the sense of a specific lie. The ambiguity preceded the network and is now the network's operating logic.

The factor's summary of this (delivered, without affect, when Saerys at age nine first referenced the compound column explicitly): "Both cargoes have been on this manifest since the second order. Nothing has changed." He said this very quietly. He has never said it again. Nothing changed after he said it either.
