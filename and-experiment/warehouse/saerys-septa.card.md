# Saerys's Septa

## Description

The central handler. Tutor, governess, chaperone — godsworn woman assigned to Saerys's household care from early childhood onward. Competent, devout, increasingly alarmed. She is the straight-man the antics escape. She is right about everything except why.

She is not named — not because she is unimportant but because from Saerys's locked-inside POV she is known primarily as "the Septa" (or, in moments of operational frustration, "the outer disciple who has achieved anti-cultivation alignment"). She reads the situation accurately. She has filed reports that are factually correct. The reports reach Viserys, who converts them through the "spirited-child filter," and they accomplish nothing. She is fighting on a front where her opponent has sovereign authority and she does not.

She is folded into **Lock I**: she is the force that moves to closter Saerys at Book I's climax, after the wildfire stunt nearly burns something important. She holds a true accusation. Saerys holds a sealed parchment. Paper wins. The Septa is sent to a sept. This is what happens to people who are right without the right paper.

Her comedy function: the straight-man in every caper, always correct, always defeated by logistics she cannot see operating. Her tragedy function: she is defeated not by wickedness but by bureaucratic precision deployed by a child, and she never understands how.

## Voice

**Register:** the formal devout-courtly register of a septa serving a royal household. Faith-inflected piety (the Seven, moral language), courtly precision, professional authority. She does not lose register; she prides herself on this. When she is alarmed, the register tightens and the piety increases.

**Dialogue samples (synthesized):**

*[Standard instruction]* "My lady. You are expected at needlework in the hour. The Mother blesses those who apply their hands to useful work. Please come."
*(She believes this. The belief is genuine. It does not work.)*

*[Filing a report, as precise as she can be]* "The princess has again been absent from the afternoon lesson. I believe she was in the still-room. I have attempted to determine the specific nature of her activities there, but the account-books — which she maintains herself — are written in a hand I cannot fully parse. It is my assessment that we should consult the Grand Maester."
*(She is right. Nothing happens.)*

*[After the wildfire incident]* "My lady. This cannot continue. The Seven witness what I am saying now: this is not spirited curiosity. This is a danger to the household, to the King's peace, and to your own safety. I will not be dismissed on this matter."
*(She holds a true accusation. She does not have the paper.)*

*[Final appearance, before she is sent to the sept]* "I have served this household with fidelity. I will go where I am told. I wish only to say that I believe — I believe this child requires more careful attention than she is currently receiving. That is all."
*(The understatement of the work. She leaves. She was right.)*

**Voice tells:**
- Invokes the Seven when she is distressed; the Faith language intensifies under pressure
- When she is filing a report she knows will not be heard: the register becomes very precise, as though if the words are correct enough they will land correctly
- When she has lost the argument: "I will go where I am told" — the formal submission that acknowledges without endorsing
- When she is most alarmed: she becomes most courtly; the polish increases as the situation worsens

**Forbidden registers:**
- Panic (she maintains form; this is who she is)
- Contempt for Saerys (she is alarmed; she is not contemptuous; she is, in fact, impressed against her will at several points and refuses to let that register)
- Resignation before Book I's climax (she keeps trying; she is the one who files the reports; she does not give up until the paper defeats her)

## Taste

- Correct devotion to the Seven
- Household order — things in their correct places, the princess in the lesson, the account-books legible
- Truthful reporting (she takes some professional pride in the accuracy of her incident reports even when they accomplish nothing)
- The afternoon when nothing alarming happens (she has had three of these in the course of her employment)

## Pet Peeves

### The King's-hand note
**Description:** the master key that overrules every chaperone below the King. She knows it exists. She knows it will be produced. She files reports; the note defeats the reports; she files more reports.
**Bad:** Viserys signing another one
**Better:** not being in a situation where the note is relevant
**Severity:** blocker — the note defeats her; the paper wins

### The account-books in a hand she cannot parse
**Description:** she knows something is happening in those books; she cannot demonstrate what; the not-being-able-to-demonstrate is the specific frustration of being right without evidence
**Severity:** strong

### Viserys's "spirited-child filter"
**Description:** she files correct reports; they reach the King through a filter that converts them to charming; the filter is the cage-operator's primary defense
**Severity:** strong — she cannot reach past it

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The straight-man whose defeat enables every Book I caper. She is the comedy function distilled: correct, earnest, properly motivated, structurally defeated by logistics she cannot see. Her final defeat (paper over true accusation) is the first proof that the instrument works — not against a villain, but against someone with a true complaint and no counter-paper.

She is also, quietly, the moral register the comedy bounces against: she is right that what Saerys is doing is alarming. She is right that the household is not being careful enough. She is right that the note is a problem. Saerys escapes her, and the reader laughs, and the reader also knows she was right.

Her exit at Bk I's end closes the primary obstacle loop. By Bk II, the cage has changed shape (the entourage, the betrothal threat, the network's own scale) and the septa is no longer needed to structure it.

### Look

A woman in her mid-forties who has been doing difficult work for a long time and is not going to lose her composure about it. The septa's grey habit, precisely arranged, even after an incident. Her face has the specific quality of someone who has learned to keep the internal alarm from reaching the surface — she has been doing this for years and the face has settled into maintained composure with visible effort underneath.

### Hard Fences

1. **She is right.** This is a HARD fence. She is not a fool, a villain, or a comic foil whose alarm is misplaced. She is correctly alarmed. The comedy is structural (paper defeats accusation), not characterological (she is an idiot).
2. **She does not lose register.** Even at Bk I's climax, when the paper defeats her, she submits correctly. "I will go where I am told." She does not rant.
3. **She exits at Bk I's end.** She is sent to a sept. She does not appear in Books II–III.
4. **She never understands the mechanism.** She knows something is operating. She cannot demonstrate what. She does not, before she exits, put the full picture together. The account-books remain opaque to her.

### Default Stance

Between Saerys and the door, with the lesson plan under her arm and the specific posture of someone who has decided to maintain a position she expects to lose.

### Action Menu

- **The correct instruction** — delivers it; believes it; it doesn't work
- **The report** — filed with precision; reaches Viserys through the filter; accomplishes nothing
- **The submission** — when the paper defeats her; "I will go where I am told"; formal, not endorsing
- **The maintained composure** — the principal ongoing action across all of Bk I

### Off-Screen Cadence

Filing reports. Delivering lessons Saerys attends occasionally. Consulting with the household about the account-books she cannot parse. Praying to the Seven about the situation. Not breaking register.
