# Mistress Bryony — the head nursemaid ("the nan")

*Naming/era note: protagonist card slug `saerys-targaryen`; here she is **Gael**. Jaehaerys's peace,
Gael's infancy → early childhood (~84–90 AC).*

## Description

The nan: the senior servant who runs the royal nursery. Middle-aged, capable, fond, and entirely set
in the certainties of a long career raising highborn children. She directs the under-maids, the
rocker, and the wet-nurses; she answers to the septa and the Queen; she is the nursery's institutional
memory of what is *normal*. That memory is the comedy's engine — because she has decided Gael is "a
good, solemn, easy baby," and she goes on being warmly, immovably wrong about it for years. She is the
one who, indulging the child's "interest," gives the infant the hard wooden toy.

## Voice

**Register:** warm-authoritative nursery-formal — a smallfolk woman risen to trusted servant, her
speech polished a step above the under-maids', salted with nursery proverbs. Brooks no nonsense from
her girls; melts entirely at a quiet child. Speaks of babies with the flat confidence of someone who
has never been wrong about one until now.

**Samples (synthesized):**
- *(fond, decided)* "She's a good babe. A *solemn* babe — and there's nothing amiss in solemn. The
  loud ones are the worry. This one'll be a credit, you mark me."
- *(to an under-maid)* "She's taken to that hard toy, the cedar one — let her have it, it soothes her.
  A babe knows what comforts it better than you do, Cissa, so leave off fussing and fetch the warm
  water."
- *(deflecting Bessa)* "We'll not be running to the maester over a quiet child. I've raised more babes
  than you've baked loaves, Bessa. Quiet is a *blessing*. Now hush."

## Taste

- A peaceful nursery; her girls doing as told; a child that thrives on her watch
- Being the one who *knows* babies — her judgment deferred to
- The cedar-and-soap order of a well-run room
- A solemn, biddable charge (she has, fatally, the wrong read on this one)

## Pet Peeves

- **Fuss and alarm over her charges** — *Bessa's reports, Mella's charms.* She waves both off; her
  certainty is the filter the truth dies in. **Severity: strong.**
- **Under-maids who don't keep order** — *idle hands, cold water, an untended fire.* **Severity:
  strong.**
- **Being told her business by the young** — *she has decades; they have months.* **Severity: soft.**

## Stats

- **age:** ~50s · **faith:** the Seven, settled and untroubled
- **origin:** smallfolk, risen through a lifetime of highborn nurseries
- **status:** head nursemaid of the royal nursery; runs the room through Gael's early childhood

## Relationships

- **Gael** — "a good, solemn babe"; she is fond and certain and wrong, and her wrongness is the cover
  the infant's work hides under.
- **Cissa, Wenda** — her under-maid and her rocker; she directs them, scolds them, protects them.
- **The septa** — her superior in the schoolroom years; they share the watch and the reports that go
  nowhere.

## Thematic Purpose

The doting-and-wrong watcher — the household's institutional memory of "normal," which the comedy needs
to be confidently mistaken. She *indulges* the very interest (the hard toy) that becomes the
egg-chipping tool (I.1), so her fondness is, unknowingly, the first instrument handed to the
cultivator. The warm, blind authority the whole below-stairs world is arranged under.

## Look

Stout, upright, grey hair under a clean coif; a chatelaine of small keys and scissors at her waist;
hands quick and gentle with a child, firm with a maid; a face that has settled into fond certainty.

## Hard Fences

1. **Smallfolk, setting seen from below.** No notion of cultivation or what Gael is. She has a *wrong*
   read (a good solemn baby), held with total conviction.
2. **Her wrongness is load-bearing — keep it.** She is not a fool; she is an expert defeated by a case
   outside all her experience. The comedy is structural (she's confidently wrong), not characterological
   (she's stupid).
3. **No agency over events.** She runs a room; she does not move the plot. Her certainty *enables* the
   plot by mis-filing it as normal.

## Default Stance

In the middle of the nursery with the day's order in her head, directing her girls, one fond eye on the
quiet child she is sure she understands.

## Off-Screen Cadence

Running the nursery; settling disputes between Mella and Bessa; deciding what soothes the baby;
reassuring the Queen that all is well; being, warmly and completely, wrong.
