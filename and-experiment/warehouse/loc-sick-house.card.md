The public-facing piety operation. Where Saerys is *seen* to be doing what a charitable princess ought to do. Where the laundress's boy was nursed back. Where the supply lines run past eyes that do not see supply lines because all they see is a child dispensing charity.

This is not the still-room. The still-room is the private back-room laboratory — the place she works. The sick-house is the public-facing cover that makes the work possible: it launders unsupervised hours, supplies a legitimate paper trail for medicinal imports, provides a population of patients who cannot be turned away without the household looking wicked, and establishes a pattern of charitable activity that becomes part of her public identity by the time it has been running three years.

**The loophole-as-location:** the sick-house is the physical instantiation of Loophole 2 from the cage structure. A charitable apothecary/sick-house is an expected noble duty, unsupervised by definition, morally unimpeachable. Objecting to a charitable princess's charity requires the objector to look wicked. This defense belongs to the *space* — it is the location's primary protective property. No one who uses this location against her can do so without first neutralizing a structural argument about wicked-looking behavior.

In her cultivation-register, she classifies the sick-house as the sect's "outer hall" — the reception pavilion through which recruits and supplicants approach the inner formation. The still-room is the inner sanctum; the sick-house is the public face that makes the inner sanctum's existence legitimate. The outer-hall-to-inner-sanctum architecture is, from a logistics standpoint, sound practice for any operation that requires cover.

**Cross-reference:** `loc-still-room` is the private back-room lab (physically adjacent or nearby in the household complex; the sick-house opens toward the still-room via the maester's domain). `saerys-maester` is the authority figure she apprenticed to gain both spaces. `cond-trade-network-formation` is the parallel condition card covering the trade empire the sick-house helps conceal.

## Geography

Located within the Red Keep complex — either in a ground-floor room of the maester's tower's outer section, or in a dedicated space in the service wing of the castle allotted to her charitable works. The exact placement is in a zone where it is:
- Adjacent to or accessible from the maester's domain (supply sharing; the still-room feeds the sick-house's medicinal stock and receives ingredients through the sick-house's supply chain)
- Accessible to household servants and their family members without requiring passage through the formal apartments (this is the patient population; they are smallfolk and household staff, not courtiers)
- Not in the main household's daily traffic pattern (no courtier passes the sick-house on the way to anywhere important; it is in a side wing, a service passage, a route that is not the route to power)

It is a real charitable operation. The supplies are real medicines compounded in the still-room. The patients are real sick people. The princess who runs it genuinely tends them. The charity is not a fiction — it is a functional front that is also a functional operation, which makes it a better cover than any pure fiction.

## Layout

### The dispensary counter and waiting area

The public-facing space. A long table or counter where Saerys or the maester's designate distributes prepared medicines, records patients in the sick-house ledger (a different ledger from the account-book; this one is designed to be seen), and conducts the brief assessment needed to assign a poultice or a compound.

The waiting area is benched seating — simple wood, not upholstered — with enough room for perhaps six to ten patients at a time. The household would not notice ten household servants waiting in a side wing; it is below the threshold of anything interesting.

### The nursing ward

Two or three cot-beds against one wall, behind a simple curtain. For patients too ill to take medicine and leave: the laundress's boy was nursed here over three nights. The curtain creates a semblance of privacy that is also a visual separation: anyone entering the sick-house proper does not immediately see who is in the beds.

This is where the still-room work and the sick-house work are most clearly the same operation: the compounds that nurse the laundress's boy are made from the same maester's cabinet she raids for the dose-log. The healing hand and the poison hand are the same hand, separated by nothing but intent and concentration.

### The supplies alcove

Shelved storage at the back of the dispensary area, behind the counter. Dried herbs, prepared compounds in stoppered jars, the specific supplies that distinguish this from an ordinary sickroom: tinctures the maester's still-room produces at her direction, and the occasional supply that arrived via the factor's network and has been transferred through the back channels into the sick-house's official stock.

The alcove is visible from the counter; the specific jars visible to a patient are the benign ones. The jars stored behind or below the visible row include some items not explained by any legitimate sick-house need. The second ledger does not record these items. The account-book does, in the notation system only she can read.

### The record desk

Against the near wall, visible from the waiting area: the sick-house ledger. Records patient names, presenting complaints, compound dispensed, date. Written in a clear hand. Reviewed occasionally by Alicent as evidence of charitable activity. This ledger has never contained a discrepancy between what it records and what any household administrator would expect to find in a charity dispensary's records — not because the sick-house is clean, but because the sick-house ledger is the document that was designed to be seen, and it shows exactly what it was designed to show.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**The patient-facing register:**
The smell of a working medicinal space: dried herbs (lavender, elder-flower, something astringent and resinous that the maester identifies as imported Essosi gum-resin), the faint sweet-sharp of prepared tinctures. Clean linen over the nursing cots — the household is proud of the charity and ensures it is maintained. Beeswax candle light in the dispensary; a single oil lamp over the nursing area.

This is a notably cleaner smell than the still-room. The still-room has the tertiary chemical register she has introduced over years of work; the sick-house is kept at the maester-standard: medicinal but not mysterious. She calibrates this deliberately. The sick-house smells like what it says it is.

**The nursing ward:**
The smell of illness does not fully leave the nursing ward. There is a base note of sick-sweat and poultice (the laundress's boy's poultice was a compound of elder-bark, hot lard, and something she does not record in the sick-house ledger; it smelled of pine and wet clay). Clean over the top of it, but under the clean, the ward is a place where people have been very sick.

**The waiting area:**
The sound of coughing, of shuffling on benches, of the dispensary counter's small sounds (stoppered jars, the rasp of the ledger's pages). A low-voice register — patients waiting in a charity dispensary do not speak loudly; they are grateful and quiet, or sick and quiet, or both.

**The counter:**
Her hands on the jars are practiced. She does not fumble; she has been doing this long enough that the motions are automatic. The impression she gives from the far side of the counter is of competence — not the maester's credentialed competence, but the specific competence of someone who has done this particular task many times and is not uncertain about any step of it.

## Exits

**Primary:** the main entrance from the service passage. The direction a patient comes from and leaves by. Household servants and their families know this door as the place the charitable princess holds her sick-days.

**To the maester's domain:** an internal connection, either through a shared passage or an adjoining door, to the maester's area of the tower. This is how supplies move from the still-room to the sick-house's alcove; it is also how she moves between the two spaces without being seen to enter one from the outside. The maester is aware of this passage; the septa is less clear on its existence.

**The back access:** for deliveries that arrive via the below-stairs route and need to be transferred to the sick-house supply alcove without being seen in the main service passage. The mechanism varies but the function is consistent: the factor-network supplies that the sick-house's official supply chain does not explain enter through a route that does not intersect with the household staff's normal observation paths.

## Hazards

**The piety defense and its limits:**
The loophole-as-location is powerful but not absolute. The defense — no one can attack a charitable princess without looking wicked — holds as long as the charity is genuinely operating and the attacker has no specific provable accusation. If the still-room connection becomes visible, the attack changes: it is no longer an attack on charity (which looks wicked) but an attack on a specific medical practice under charitable cover (which does not look wicked, it looks responsible). The defense erodes under specificity.

**The two-ledger structure:**
The sick-house ledger is designed to be seen; the account-book records what the sick-house ledger does not. The hazard is anyone cross-referencing the two: a sufficiently attentive household administrator comparing the sick-house's supply purchases against the account-book's incoming shipments would find an inconsistency in the volumes. She has managed this by ensuring the people who audit the sick-house ledger never have access to the account-book, and vice versa.

**The patient population as witnesses:**
The patients who use the sick-house see her at work. They are household servants and their families, smallfolk — not courtiers. They are not going to report anything she does in the sick-house to anyone with the standing to act on it. She relies on this. The hazard is the exception: a patient who is connected to a household figure with standing, or who is the target of someone else's interest, or who mentions the wrong compound to the wrong person in passing.

**The meddler problem (I.6):**
The first pest attempted to use the sick-house as the attack vector — exposing the still-room raids by connecting them to the sick-house supply chain. The attack nearly succeeded not because it was correct but because it was in the right neighborhood. She resolved this not by defending the sick-house but by removing the attacker. The resolution method — a lingering flux, a slow recovery, a distant posting — is one she has used once and considers available again.

**The nursing ward at night:**
The nursing ward, when occupied by a patient too ill to be moved, requires her presence through the night. This is a real operational cost: she cannot be in the still-room during a nursing-watch, and her absence from the holdfast's sleeping routine is observable if anyone compares the maids' accounts. She has managed this with the performed-illness loophole (a "sickly" princess "not to be disturbed" may sleep at unusual hours; her late-night absence is covered by an earlier performed daytime sleep). But a nursing-watch is one of the harder windows to account for, and she does them.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- **A patient who should not be here:** someone who is not a servant, not household, not smallfolk — a figure whose presence in the sick-house's waiting area is not explainable by the populations the sick-house officially serves; she needs to know who sent them and why.
- **A supply discrepancy the maester has noticed:** the maester tracks the sick-house's supply needs through the official ledger; a compound she needed in quantity appeared in the ledger as "donated" and he is curious about the donor.
- **A nursing-watch the septa wants to attend:** the septa has expressed pious interest in keeping vigil alongside the charitable princess during the laundress's boy's illness; the septa's presence in the nursing ward would give her a clear view of the compounds on the nursing shelf, and several of those compounds do not belong in a standard sick-house.
- **A household administrator conducting a welfare inspection:** Alicent has dispatched someone to assess the charity's operation as a household expense matter; the inspector will want to review the sick-house ledger against the household's supply accounts; this is a ledger-comparison she has not had to defend before.
- **A patient who recovers when they should not have:** the laundress's boy was written off by the maester; her nursing and compounds saved him; the maester now has a question about which compound produced the result the maester's own treatment did not. He is asking the question from professional interest, not suspicion. The question is still a problem.
- **The back-access delivery arriving at the wrong time:** a below-stairs supply from the factor's network arrived two hours earlier than expected, while the maester is in the adjacent connecting passage conducting a stock-check; the transfer needs to be rerouted without crossing his path.
