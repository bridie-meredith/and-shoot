# Prop — The Account-Book

The ledger only she can read. The Inferior Path's natal artifact. The steering wheel of an empire that is not supposed to exist.

---

## Physical Description

Standard ledger format — larger than the dose-log, roughly the size of a household accounts-register, which it resembles closely enough that anyone inspecting it would conclude they were looking at a household accounts-register before their eyes glazed and they put it down. This is deliberate. The columns, the tabular structure, the occasional official-looking notation — everything reads as tedious and legitimate at the surface level. It is tedious and legitimate. It is also, beneath that, something else.

Bound in dark leather, somewhat worn at the spine from years of handling. No title. No name on the cover. The first two pages are a table of contents written in four languages (the common tongue, High Valyrian, Braavosi trade argot, and one additional language she is still learning as of Book II). The table of contents is accurate. It is also a cipher key that looks like a table of contents.

The columns record: date, entry type (in a category system she devised at age seven and has never shared with anyone), transaction detail (real transaction details for real transactions; the network is run on actual commerce), and a fourth column that appears to be running sums but is not only running sums. The fourth column is where the structural intelligence of the trade network lives — the relationships between factors, the dependency chains, the pressure points, the vulnerabilities of each node she has assessed. A competent reader of ledgers would see numbers. She sees a formation map.

By the end of Book I, the account-book is approximately one-third full. By the end of Book II it is on its second volume; Volume I is in a locked box at a factor's address in Braavos that she controls through correspondence. Volume II travels with her. The transition between volumes is documented: the last entry of Volume I is a single notation — "Volume continues at B1, address encoded per standard protocol, recovery key held in standard location." The first entry of Volume II assumes continuity. There is no gap.

## In-Register Grade and Artifact-Spirit Framing

She has assessed the account-book as a Tier 2 dao-artifact approaching Tier 3 (natal/life-bound) status through extended use, which in the cultivation framework means it has absorbed sufficient "operational intent" to be developing a rudimentary qi-resonance.

The evidence she cites for artifact-spirit emergence: "the ledger sometimes opens to the relevant entry without prompting." She documents this observation neutrally. She adds: "this may be coincidence; the cauldron notes the frequency appears above the baseline for random events; the assessment is ongoing."

The actual explanation: she knows exactly where every entry is. She has used the book so long that her hand finds the right page automatically, without deliberate action. The "artifact-spirit" is her own indexing memory, which she has externalized so completely that its operation feels like the book's. The cultivation-register has correctly classified her own subconscious as an external spirit. This is one of the places where the wrong framework produces an insight the correct framework would have missed.

She would narrate: *"The account-book approaches the threshold of natal-artifact resonance; the life-binding process has not been formalized — I have not bled on it, as blood is a Tier-1 reagent and wasteful in this application — but the qi-integration appears to be occurring naturally through sustained operational use, which the Index of the Inferior Path notes as a theoretically possible but empirically unconfirmed development. The index of the Inferior Path is this account-book. It notes this in its own pages. The circularity is not a problem."*

## What It Actually Is

**A logistics ledger.** The trade-and-medicine network is a real commercial operation running charitable apothecary work as a charity cover and genuine apothecary supply as the commercial core. The account-book is where the commercial operation is recorded. Every factor, every bill of exchange, every cargo manifest, every provisioning order, every letter of credit — they have corresponding entries. The entries are accurate. The network is real. The book makes it legible.

**The network's formation eye.** The account-book is the node at which all information about the network converges. Losing it would not destroy the network — each factor has their own partial record — but it would destroy the *coherence* of the network's view of itself. The formation eye is the component whose destruction collapses the formation's coordination, not its components. She knows this. She does not keep the account-book and the dose-log in the same place.

**The one place a death is ever entered.** The pest-removal poisonings are nowhere in the account-book — they exist only in the cultivation-register as "impurity expulsion" events. But two entries deviate from this. Harwin's death: the account-book has a closed line — a single entry that credits his standing and then debits it to zero, a balance that the column shows as settled. The handwriting is the same as every other entry. The entry took approximately forty seconds to write. She closed the book after and did not open it again that day. The second death entered is Daenys's: a blank line in Book III — entry number without content; the quill stopped after the date; the column fills with white space; the next entry is a week later, date and content both present, as though the book picked up in the middle. The blank line sets up Book III's dramatic accounting: the one debt carried off the edge of the map uncosted.

## Affordances / Uses

- Write: bills of exchange, manifestos, charter-party instructions, correspondence to factors, household stewardship records
- Read: the formation map of the network; the factored relationships; who owes whom, in which direction, across how many intermediaries
- Coordinate: update factor relationships, close old lines, open new ones
- Cover: the account-book legitimizes the scale of transactions because it shows the legitimate transactions that embed the operative ones
- Historical record: the account-book is the evidentiary basis for "she just runs a very large charitable apothecary operation"; without it the operation collapses under scrutiny; with it, the scrutiny slides off

## Sensory Hooks

- Leather cover that has taken on the smell of the still-room — the third chemical register from `loc-still-room` has migrated here; she carries it in the still-room and the book has absorbed years of ambient compound work
- The pages have a slight texture from multiple annotations in different inks — she uses three inks deliberately (standard black for transactions, a brownish-red for factor relationship notes, and a very faint pencil for temporary structural assessments she will either confirm in ink or erase)
- Weight: heavier than it looks; the quality of the ledger stock is high; she bought good paper once and has maintained that standard across volumes
- The spine has a tiny nick at the middle of the binding — where a Kingsguard sword handle caught it during a corridor incident in Book II; the nick is not structural; she noted it in the margin of the nearest entry: "administrative tribulation, minor; record integrity preserved"

## Portability

Carried on her person at all times she is in working mode. Not carried openly at court — tucked inside the formal outer garment, which she wears with additional inner structure for exactly this purpose. At sea, it is the first thing secured to her person and the last thing she would relinquish.

---

## Carry State

Weight: constant companion; she is aware of its absence when separated. Concealment: the formal-garment inner pocket at court; the working-table surface in the still-room; her bunk at sea. Noise: none; the leather binding is soft; the pages do not creak. The account-book makes no sound. This is appropriate.

---

## Functional State

The account-book has three functional states:

**Active:** current-volume entries being written; the network is live; she is adding information. This is the book's primary state from Book I M2 onward.

**Closed:** entries settled; the closed lines show a balance of zero. Closed lines are the book's most significant readings — they record when something is finished. Some closures are commercial (a factor's debt settled). Two are not.

**Blank:** the one blank line in Book III. An entry that begins and does not end. The column shows white space. The date is written. The rest is empty.

The book is currently on its second volume. The first volume is at a factor's address in Braavos.
