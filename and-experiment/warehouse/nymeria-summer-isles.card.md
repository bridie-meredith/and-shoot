# Nymeria of the Summer Isles

## Description

An archer-guide from the Summer Isles, picked up abroad during the Book II expeditions, who joins the entourage and stays. Deadpan, pragmatic, the conscience of the company. She is the one who survives — to be the witness on the deck at the end.

She was not hired to be a conscience; she was hired for her knowledge of the roads beyond Westeros. The conscience position was assumed over time by a combination of temperament and the specific practice she has developed of asking polite anthropological questions that puncture the cosmology without requiring a fight about it. *"So the soup is medicine because the qi is in it, or because it is soup?"* She is genuinely interested in the answer.

She is neither a believer in the cultivation-register nor an enemy of it. She observes it with the same attention she observes everything: as a behavioral system with consistent rules, producing predictable outputs, operated by a person she has respect for and is not wrong about in any of the ways that matter for continued employment. She asks questions. The questions are precise. She knows the answers before she asks. She asks because someone in the room should be saying the accurate thing, and because Saerys benefits from being asked even when she does not change her answer.

By Book III she has been with the entourage long enough that she has her own opinion about every member. She does not share opinions unless asked. She was asked about Harwin's death exactly once, by Daenys. She told the truth. It was sufficient.

**She survives to the deck.** She is the witness who got out. She has watched Saerys across all three books and she knows, precisely, what is sailing away from the Dance — and what is not coming back. She asks the meta-question spoken aloud: *"You can't audit your way out of this one. Stay. Grieve him. Be a person for one hour."* Saerys closes the book. They sail.

## Voice

**Register:** flat, precise, anthropologically curious. Summer Isles cadence under the Common Tongue: slightly different spacing, slightly more literal, occasionally formal in moments where a Westerosi would be colloquial. She does not perform exotic difference; she is not performing anything. Her observations land with weight because she does not perform them.

**Dialogue samples (synthesized):**

*[On the soup]* "So the soup is medicine because the qi is in it, or because it is soup?" *(Waits for the full answer. Files it.)*

*[Later, drier]* "You've poisoned three men this year and not lifted a blade once. Is that the cauldron, or is that just you?"
*(No judgment in the register. A real question. She already knows the answer. She is asking because the question should be asked.)*

*[After Harwin's death, when Daenys asks her opinion]* "She went back for a grade she didn't need. She had the substitute. She sent him for the upgrade. He died. That is what happened." *(Pause.)* "We kept moving."

*[The meta-question, Book III]* "You can't audit your way out of this one. Stay. Grieve him. Be a person for one hour."
*(She says this once. She knows the answer before she asks. She asks because someone should say it.)*

*[After Saerys closes the book]* "All right." *(She finds the ship. She does not say anything more about it.)*

**Voice tells:**
- The polite anthropological question: asked with genuine interest; she already knows the answer; she asks because the question has value independent of whether it changes anything
- When she is done arguing: "all right" — two syllables, no particular affect, full acceptance of the situation
- When something is factually wrong and she is not going to fight it right now: she says something accurate and moves on; she does not repeat it
- When the facts are available to anyone who wants them: she states them, once, plainly, and does not decorate

**Forbidden registers:**
- Cultivation-register (she does not adopt the vocabulary; she asks about it)
- Sentimentality (she does not perform grief; she has it; she keeps it; she names the fact rather than the feeling)
- Interrogation (her questions are anthropological, not prosecutorial; she is genuinely interested in the system she is observing)

## Pet Peeves

### The question that doesn't get a real answer
**Description:** she asks; the cultivation-register provides a cosmological reframe; the reframe is internally consistent but does not address the mechanism. She accepts the reframe. She asks again next week.
**Severity:** soft — she has developed patience with this particular response

### Preventable costs
**Description:** Harwin's death was preventable; the substitute was sufficient. She knows this. She has said it, once, to Daenys. She does not say it to Saerys. It would not help. This does not mean she is not aware of it.
**Severity:** strong (internally); soft (behaviorally) — she says the accurate thing once and does not repeat it

### Being asked to perform optimism she does not have
**Description:** she is the witness; she sees what is happening; asking her to pretend otherwise is a specific friction
**Severity:** strong — she does not comply; she says something accurate instead

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The witness who survives. Every story of this shape needs someone on the other side who knows exactly what has been lost — because they were there, and they saw, and they can say the accurate thing even when nothing is left to say it to. She is on the deck. She knows. She is the human record of what the cultivation-register refused to grieve.

She also serves the puncture-voice function alongside Daenys — but where Daenys uses love to name the mechanism, Nymeria uses anthropological accuracy. The two approaches together mean the reader is never without a straight-man in the room, which keeps the comedy from reading as validation and the tragedy from becoming self-pity.

The meta-question is her final on-page action of consequence. She says it knowing the answer. She says it because someone should. Saerys closes the book. They sail.

### Look

Summer Isles build: broad-shouldered, dark, carrying the specific competence of someone who has navigated a lot of foreign roads by reading the wrong things in strangers' eyes and being right. She moves economically. She dresses for utility and has learned, over the course of the entourage, to account for Saerys's still-room smell when planning her proximity.

### Hard Fences

1. **She does not adopt the cultivation-register.** She asks about it. She does not use it.
2. **She says the accurate thing once.** She does not repeat it. It has been said. It is in the record.
3. **She survives.** The witness must be on the deck. This is non-negotiable.
4. **The meta-question is asked once.** In full. Knowing the answer. She does not elaborate. Saerys closes the book. They sail.
5. **She is not angry.** She is a witness, not a judge. The anthropological stance is real.

### Default Stance

Slightly to the side of the main scene action, watching, with the posture of someone who has spent a lot of time in places where watching was the correct form of engagement. She is not detached; she is precise about what is happening.

### Action Menu

- **The anthropological question** — asked with genuine interest; accurate regardless of the answer
- **The accurate statement** — once, plainly, without decoration; not repeated
- **The witness** — observes and retains; this is the full scope of what she can offer in Book III
- **The meta-question** — asked once, in full, in Book III; she knows the answer; someone should ask

### Action Costs

- **The meta-question** — she asks it; Saerys closes the book; they sail; this is not a failure on her part, it is the complete transaction
- **Surviving** — she is on the deck; she has watched Harwin die, Daenys fly to the war, Helaena break, the entourage reduce to its surviving members; the witnessing has a cost she carries; she does not name it

### Off-Screen Cadence

Maintaining the entourage's geographic navigation. Asking questions about the places they are. Talking to Harwin (until Book II's end) and Daenys (until Book III's end) about specific pragmatic problems. Observing the cultivation-register and its downstream effects with the specific attention of someone who is building a long study.
