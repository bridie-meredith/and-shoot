# Prop — The King's-Hand Note

The most load-bearing prop in the arc. A single document travels from verbal indulgence to sealed parchment to orphaned license to heist mechanism across three books and nine chapters. It is planted in I.1, grows in I.2, is sealed in I.9, fires as a master key in I.10, becomes legally unrevocable through its signer's death in III.1, and closes the heist in III.10. The spine's longest single-prop arc.

---

## Physical Description

**In its final form (after I.9):** A parchment document, roughly letter-size, written in a clear administrative hand. The text grants "the princess" (or "the Lady Saerys" — the exact phrasing varies by chapter reference, but the legal effect is identical) "what she requires for her good works" — a deliberately broad formulation Viserys approved without grasping how broadly it would be read. The hand belongs to a royal secretary; the words were dictated or approved by Viserys himself.

**The seal:** At the document's close, the wax impression of the King's Hand's seal — or, in versions of the card's interpretation that give it more authority, the King's own seal. The seal is applied in red wax. The impression is the authentication. Per constraints §3: "authentication rests on the seal; a slightly-broken seal is itself the standard alibi for irregularities; she needs one genuine impression on the wrong document." This seal is genuine. This impression is real. The man who made it will die before anyone can ask him to revoke it.

**Physical state:** Well-preserved. Saerys does not keep documents carelessly. The parchment is stored flat, rolled tight, protected from moisture and handling. By Book III it has survived six years, two sea voyages, and the Dance of the Dragons without losing the seal. The seal is intact. This matters.

**Document weight and size:** single parchment folio, small and portable — not a formal charter (those are larger, heavier, multi-paged). This is an informal grant, which gives it both its flexibility (broad phrasing, general application) and its authority (the King's own seal on even an informal document overrules every officer below him).

## Full Lifecycle — Chapter by Chapter

This section is the functional-state tracker across the arc. Bones authors need to know which state the note is in at any given chapter.

### State 0 — Verbal indulgence (Chapter I.1 → I.2)

**Form:** Not a document yet. Viserys's spoken word — "give the child what amuses her" or equivalent — is the original grant. This is noted as PLANT[NOTE] in chapter I.1.

**Legal weight:** The King's expressed preference, communicated verbally to household staff. Effective as a soft master key within the household: staff who heard it comply; those who didn't are harder to override. Not portable, not permanent, not producible as evidence.

**What Saerys does with it:** trades on it immediately as a standing authorization for the charitable work and the still-room access. She treats a verbal indulgence as a full license. This is the comedy: she has not yet turned it into paper, but she acts as though she has.

### State 1 — Standing license (Chapter I.2)

**Form:** The verbal indulgence has been formalized to a written standing license — a document that records the King's ongoing approval of "the princess's charitable works." FIRE[NOTE→license] fires here.

**Legal weight:** Now portable and producible. A written license in the King's name overrules every household officer and most court officials below the level of the Hand. It does not bear the seal yet — this is an administrative license, signed but not sealed.

**What Saerys does with it:** Uses it to anchor every bill of exchange she writes to the Braavosi factor. The first bill references the standing license as the legal authority for the transaction. This is the trade empire's constitutional founding document: the license is the root authority under which every subsequent instrument is legitimate.

### State 2 — Sealed parchment (Chapter I.9)

**Form:** The standing license upgraded to a sealed document. Saerys talks Viserys into formalizing and sealing the grant — "the princess shall have what she requires for her good works." FIRE[NOTE→sealed] fires here; PLANT[ORPHAN-SEAL] fires simultaneously (this exact seal is the one cashed in Book III).

**Legal weight:** A sealed document bearing the King's impression is a formal grant. It overrules every household officer, every maester, every septa, every court functionary below the King himself. The septa's true accusation in I.10 is perfectly correct and completely helpless against it. Saerys holds a sealed parchment; the septa holds a true accusation; the sealed parchment wins.

**What Saerys does with it:** Produces it in I.10. One scene, one document, Lock I closed. She has made herself legally ungovernable and thinks she achieved a cultivation breakthrough. The breakthrough is real. It is paper, not qi.

### State 3 — Master key (Chapter I.10)

**Form:** Same sealed parchment. No physical change. FIRE[NOTE→Lock I] fires.

**Legal weight:** Deployed at maximum effect: the King's-hand sealed grant overrules the septa's authority to confine or remove Saerys. The note is not disputed — the septa has no standing to challenge the King's own seal. The note wins without argument.

**Operational detail:** this is the mechanism's first run at the lowest audit-difficulty (the septa is a household official; her authority is real but bounded; the seal is her ceiling). The trick works cleanly. The reader sees the full trick at I.10 so that when the same mechanism runs at higher difficulty in II.10 and III.10, they recognize what they are watching.

### State 4 — Standing license (operational) (Chapters I.10 through II.10 / III.1)

**Form:** The sealed parchment remains in Saerys's possession through Books I and II as a live operational instrument. The factor network operates under it as the authority for all transactions in her name. The manifests reference it. The bills of exchange reference it. Entire sea lanes run on the legitimacy the note provides.

**Legal weight:** Full, live, and continuously effective. The King is alive; the seal is genuine; every transaction under it is authorized. This is the note at peak operational power: it is not a one-time document but a standing grant that creates an ongoing legal umbrella.

**What Saerys does with it:** She does not carry it constantly — it lives in a secure location, referenced but not produced day-to-day. Its existence is the operational authority; its physical production is reserved for moments when authority must be demonstrated rather than implied.

### State 5 — Orphaned license (Chapter III.1 onward)

**Form:** Same sealed parchment. No physical change. But the signer is dead. FIRE[ORPHAN-SEAL] fires; FIRE[GIFT:VISERYS] fires (the father, spent).

**Legal weight:** This is the card's most complex state and its most load-bearing for the heist. Constraints §3: "authentication rests on the seal; she needs one genuine impression on the wrong document." The seal is genuine. The impression is real. The man who made it is dead — but death does not invalidate a seal's authenticity. It invalidates the living signer's ability to revoke, amend, or clarify. The note cannot be called back. It cannot be modified. It cannot be disputed on grounds of the signer's intent, because the signer no longer exists to be asked.

**The orphan dynamic:** she is trading on a dead king's seal. Every transaction made under it after III.1 is technically legitimate (the seal is real; the grant was real) and practically unverifiable (the signer cannot be consulted). This creates the specific legal gray-zone the heist exploits: the document is too old to be forged (it predates the war), too authentic to be challenged (the seal is right), and too unrevocable to be stopped (the signer is dead). The orphaned license is more powerful than the living license precisely because it has lost the signer's ability to say no.

**What Saerys does with it:** Keeps trading. The network continues to operate under the orphaned license. She notes, once, in a margin annotation in the account-book: "the standing license: amended status; operational; the signer's death does not affect the seal's authority; the account remains open; the work continues." This is the most accurate cultivation-register observation she has ever made.

### State 6 — Heist mechanism #3 (Chapter III.10)

**Form:** Same sealed parchment. Same seal. Deployed as the authentication anchor for the heist's paper construction.

**Legal weight:** In III.10, the heist requires one genuine impression on the right document. The orphaned license provides it. The harbor master inspecting the heist's paperwork will find a sealed grant that predates the war, bears a genuine King's-hand impression, and authorizes the Crown's plague-stores provisioning (which is what the laundered hull has been entered as). The authentication is correct. The impression is real. The man who could say "I didn't mean it that way" is two years dead. The men hunting a thief stare at a document more perfect than anything they have ever filed.

**The mechanism (constraints §3):** "writs and seals — authentication rests on the seal; a slightly-broken seal is itself the standard alibi for irregularities; she needs one genuine impression on the wrong document." The seal is not slightly-broken. The seal is in excellent condition. That is the heist: there is no alibi needed because there is no irregularity visible. The document is too correct.

**Narrative payload (fusion-v2):** "the heist is the funeral she has refused since the cradle, inverted — where everyone else would bury the dead, she *files* them, closing every account by sailing away from all of them at once. The paperwork victory IS the grief, refused in its final form." The note is how she converts her father's death into an instrument of escape. The love that was also the cage springs the lock one final time, posthumously.

## What It Actually Is

A real document with real legal weight, used in full accordance with its actual authority. Saerys does not forge the note. She does not misrepresent it. The grant says what she says it says. The seal is genuine. The authority is real.

What she does that is not strictly sanctioned: the phrasing is broad enough to cover far more than Viserys intended when he approved it. "What she requires for her good works" covers the still-room, the factor network, the manifests, the bills of exchange, the trade empire, and ultimately the heist. Viserys intended "give the child her herbs." What he authorized, in writing and under seal, is considerably larger.

This is not fraud. It is interpretation. She is a very good reader of documents and Viserys was a very fond father. The note says what she needs it to say because she made sure it said that before she accepted it.

## Affordances / Uses

- **Authority anchor:** establishes legal authorization for all transactions in her name; the factor network's constitutional document
- **Override instrument:** produces on demand to overrule any authority below the King; effective from I.10 onward against any challenger below Crown rank
- **Heist authentication:** provides the genuine impression that makes the III.10 paper construction unassailable
- **Proof of standing:** in any context where Saerys's right to operate must be demonstrated, the note is the demonstration

## Sensory Hooks

- Parchment texture: smooth on the writing surface, slightly rough on the back; the kind of quality parchment a royal household uses for formal documents
- Wax seal: red, dense, bearing a clear impression; the seal is slightly raised from the parchment surface; it has maintained its integrity because she stores the document well; pressing a thumb to it communicates quality and age
- Ink: dark, formal, the administrative hand's even strokes; no decoration; the document reads as official and competent
- Smell: old parchment, a slight wax note from the seal, the specific dry smell of a well-stored document that has traveled but not deteriorated
- Weight: lighter than it should be for what it does; this is the comedy and the horror both — the instrument that beats every authority in the kingdom weighs nothing

## Portability

Portable and deliberately not carried day-to-day. The note is stored securely — not with the account-book or the dose-log (she does not keep critical documents in the same location). When needed, it is produced from storage. On the sea voyages, it travels with her in a waterproofed oilskin envelope inside a locked document case. At sea, this case is the first thing she secures to herself.

---

## Carry State

**Books I-II:** secure storage, not on the person during normal operation. The note is the authority behind every transaction but is not present at those transactions. Produced on demand (I.10: produced to defeat the septa; other occasions: implied but not dramatized).

**Book III:** the orphaned license shifts from operational document to heist instrument; stored as above but its ultimate deployment in III.10 requires it to be physically present and physically producible. By the final chapters she carries it with her.

Concealment: the document is not inherently suspicious; it is a royal grant and looks like one. If searched, it is legitimately what it appears to be. This is the best possible concealment: it is visible and correct.

## Functional State

The note tracks six operational states (see Full Lifecycle above). Active state at any chapter:

| Chapters | State | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| Before I.1 | Not yet existent | Viserys's indulgence is verbal only |
| I.1–I.2 | Verbal indulgence | Spoken, no paper, soft authority |
| I.2 | Standing license | Written, unsigned/unsealed, moderate authority |
| I.9 | Sealed parchment | Sealed, full authority, the Master Key |
| I.10–III.1 | Master key (operational) | Live, signer alive, maximum legal effect |
| III.1 onward | Orphaned license | Signer dead; seal intact; unrevocable |
| III.10 | Heist mechanism #3 | Deployed as authentication anchor; final fire |

**Preservation:** the document has never been damaged, forged, copied, or modified. Its chain of custody is Viserys (author) → Saerys (sole possessor from I.9). No copies exist. The original is the instrument.
