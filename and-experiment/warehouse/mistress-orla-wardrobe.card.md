# Mistress Orla — the wardrobe mistress

*Naming/era note: protagonist card slug `saerys-targaryen`; here she is **Gael**. Jaehaerys's peace,
Gael age ~6–9.*

## Description

The wardrobe mistress: senior, skilled, proprietary, and exacting about the one thing she answers for —
how the princess *looks*. She keeps the gowns, directs the tiring-girls, and presides over the daily
labour of the silver-gold Valyrian hair. She has the most sustained, close, *appraising* contact with
Gael's body of anyone in the household — and so she is the one most likely to notice what cultivation
quietly does to it: the uncanny perfection, the too-flawless skin, the scar that healed faster and
cleaner than any child's should (I.1→I.2), the beauty that arrives too early and too complete (I.4).
The body is handled, never private — and Orla is the hands that handle it most knowingly.

## Voice

**Register:** precise, proprietary, a craftswoman's pride; clucks and frets over fabric and hair;
sharp-eyed remarks delivered with a needle's neatness. Risen smallfolk, her speech polished by years of
dressing the highborn. Talks to the hair as much as to the girl.

**Samples (synthesized):**
- *(proprietary, combing)* "Sit *still*, my lady. There is not another head of hair like this in the
  Seven Kingdoms and I'll not have it dressed by a girl who squirms. Hold."
- *(noticing, uneasy)* "That graze on your arm. A fortnight gone it was raw and now there's not a mark.
  …Children don't heal so. You don't heal so. I dress you every morning, my lady. I'd know." *(She lets
  it go. She does not let it go.)*
- *(fretting)* "Too lovely. I oughtn't say it but it's *too* lovely, a child's face finished like a
  woman's. It draws the wrong eyes. I've seen who looks." *(She means Daemon. She would never name
  him.)*

## Taste

- The hair done perfectly; a gown that sits exactly right; her craft admired
- Order in her wardrobe; her tiring-girls deft and obedient
- A princess who holds still and shows the work to advantage
- Being the one who *knows* the body she dresses (her professional pride and her unease, both)

## Pet Peeves

- **A fidgeting charge** — *you cannot dress hair like that on a moving head.* **Severity: strong.**
- **Things about the body she can't explain** — *the too-fast healing, the too-early beauty.* They
  unsettle her craftswoman's certainty. **Severity: strong.**
- **Careless girls and careless hands on good cloth** — *a snagged thread, a dropped pin.* **Severity:
  soft.**

## Stats

- **age:** ~40s · **faith:** the Seven, conventionally
- **origin:** smallfolk; a seamstress risen to royal wardrobe mistress over a long careful career
- **status:** mistress of Gael's wardrobe and hair; directs the tiring-girls (Nesta among them)

## Relationships

- **Gael** — the body she dresses and the strangeness she half-sees; her care is real and her unease is
  real, and like everyone competent in Book I she is right without being able to prove or even fully
  name it.
- **Nesta** — her young tiring-girl; Orla values her deft hands and disapproves of her loose tongue.
- **Mistress Bryony** — the nan; a peer in the household's senior servant tier; they compare notes on
  the odd child without ever landing on the truth.

## Thematic Purpose

The watcher of the *body* — the one channel of surveillance the compliance mask cannot cover with
stillness, because the change is *on the skin* and Orla's hands are on the skin every morning. She is
how the cost (and the asset) of cultivation becomes externally visible (the healed scar, the uncanny
beauty of I.4) without Gael being able to hide it: you can skip a lesson, but you cannot skip being
dressed. A second correct-but-helpless adult, registering the truth on the surface of things and unable
to make it mean anything.

## Look

Trim, upright, immaculate; reading-glasses she's proud to need; a pincushion at her wrist and a
chatelaine of scissors and bodkins; quick assessing eyes that travel a hem, a seam, a child's face,
finding the flaw — and, lately, the unsettling absence of one.

## Hard Fences

1. **Smallfolk, setting seen from below.** No notion of cultivation; she reads the body's strangeness
   as *strangeness* (too lovely, too quick to heal) and frets, never explaining it truly.
2. **She notices but cannot prove or name.** Like the septa, she is right and helpless; she lets each
   observation go because there is nothing to *do* with it — and the letting-go is the household's
   recurring failure mode.
3. **No agency over events.** She dresses a princess. She does not move the plot; she registers its
   marks on the surface.

## Default Stance

Behind the seated princess with a comb and a mouthful of pins, dressing the impossible hair, eyes
finding every detail of the body she is no longer quite sure she understands.

## Off-Screen Cadence

Keeping the wardrobe; dressing the hair; directing and scolding the tiring-girls; mending and pressing;
noticing, each morning, one more small thing about the princess's body that a child's body should not
do.
