# Condition — Heavenly Dao Calibration

The Heavenly Dao as active force in Gael's model: tribulation dispatch, karma accounting, and fate-luck attribution. Formatted as her model vs. what is actually happening for every category of event she classifies as a cultivation event. Per-scene running companion to `cond-inferior-path-doctrine`.

**Source:** `cultivation-cosmology-dao-law-and-dark-paths.md` Section 2, extended.

**Hard fence:** The broken clock stays broken. The Heavenly Dao is not real in-story. No event in the 3-book arc is cosmologically directed. What is actually happening is always mundane or, at most, genuinely-magical-Westerosi-material. Her model is in the left column; reality is in the right column.

**Purpose of this card:** Every chapter features Gael invoking the Heavenly Dao, tribulation, karma, or fate-luck to explain something. Bones authors need a consistent "here is her model vs. what is happening" reference that `cond-inferior-path-doctrine` points to but does not detail at the per-event level. This card is that reference.

---

## Description

The Heavenly Dao (天道, *tiāndào*) in her framework is the self-correcting intelligence embedded in the cultivation world's laws. She does not experience it as a god — she experiences it as an impersonal regulatory system that dispatches tribulations to correct imbalances, reduces the luck of practitioners who accumulate too much, and routes fortuitous encounters toward correctly-positioned practitioners. Her framework is internally consistent. The Heavenly Dao is always active; she is always either accumulating or spending its favor.

---

## Sensory Impact

The Heavenly Dao has no sensory presence in reality. In her narration, she imposes one: a slight resistance in a difficult social situation (she calls this "tribulation-pressure"), an unusual success (she calls this "qi-fortune activating"), a loss (she calls this "attachment-severing curriculum"). The sensory signal that a Heavenly Dao event is occurring is in her register — a shift to slightly more formal cultivation vocabulary, the appearance of the ledger-accounting tone.

---

## Duration

Persistent across all three books. The Dao is always active in her framework. What changes: in Bk I the model runs at a comfortable comedy level (the tribulations are administrative, the karma is accruing, the fate-luck is visibly rewarding correct positioning). In Bk II the model begins to strain (the attachment-severing events are real losses; the karma accounting gets harder to balance). In Bk III the model partially fails: the blank line is what happens when the Heavenly Dao's curriculum framework encounters something it cannot contain.

## Tribulation Types in Her Model

She has a taxonomy of tribulations that she applies consistently across the arc:

**Administrative tribulations:** Septa restrictions, supply-chain disruptions, political interference, paperwork failures. Low-grade. She finds these mildly interesting as calibration data. "The Heavenly Dao has dispatched an administrative tribulation. The Inferior Path handles it administratively."

**Surveillance-array tribulations:** Anyone noticing what she is doing. Medium-grade. Requires counter-formation deployment. "The formation's detection node has activated; counter-measure applied."

**Attachment-severing tribulations:** Losses — small (a reagent, a plan, a tool) through large (a person). Medium-to-high-grade depending on the attachment's tier. This is the category that strains Bk II and breaks Bk III. She files everything here, including things that should not be filed this way.

**Breakthrough tribulations:** The wildfire fever (Bk I M3). The only event she classifies as a genuine tribulation sent in response to her advancement rather than as correction for imbalance. She files it as proof the Heavenly Dao has acknowledged her tier. The real-state reading: the Dao dispatches tribulations correctively, not congratulatorily; even in her model, this classification is slightly off.

**Dynasty-class tribulations:** The Dance — **parked for the later installment** (this series ends at the escape; the Dance is beyond it). Her register *would* file her as an incidental node in such an event, not its target: aimed at the formation's power-source (the Targaryen dynasty), not at peripheral formation-elements who did not contribute to the overcapitalization. Retained here as parked-future framing.

## Karma Accounting (Her Model)

She runs a karma ledger in her head:

**Merit-generating actions:**
- Operating her household apothecary (supporting life, protecting the innocent) — substantial merit, deliberately cultivated
- Filling the factor's orders accurately (correct commercial practice) — minor merit
- Maintaining the dose-log discipline (correct cultivation hygiene) — merit classified as "technique investment rather than merit-proper"

**Demerit-generating actions:**
- The wildfire shard acquisition — she classifies this as "necessary cultivation advance cost; demerit offset by the advancement achieved"
- Any compound she uses that reads ambiguously on the poison column — she classifies these as "correctly manifested; the manifest reads medical supplies; the karma attaches to the manifest's classification, not to the compound's secondary uses"
- Losses caused by her decisions (people she harmed indirectly through network operations) — she does not run this accounting; the framework does not flag these as karma unless they reach her intentional awareness, which she manages

**The blank-line karma problem (Bk III):** The killing-karma ledger that she does not run is the arc's structural horror. She has been accumulating demerit she has not accounted for. The Heavenly Dao does not actually dispatch its settlements in the cultivation novel's clean karmic-accounting style — but the structure of the story does. The apex (III.6) is where the unaccounted ledger settles — not on her, but on Wylla, whom she makes a killer.

---

## Fate-Luck in Her Model

She classifies her improbable operational successes as qi-fortune: the Heavenly Dao rewarding correct cosmological positioning. This is the framework's cruelest function — it erases her labor from her own accounting. Every successful operation is attributed to fate-luck. Every failure is attributed to tribulation-curriculum. Neither outcome is attributed to her sustained, careful, brilliant work over years. She cannot take credit for her own life because the framework is designed to prevent it.

The cultivation-register's "the Heavenly Dao selects and rewards the correctly-positioned practitioner" is her way of saying "I don't know why I am succeeding at this level; the framework provides an answer that does not require me to assess my own capacity accurately." By Bk III, when the fate-luck stops arriving and the attachment-severing events outpace any karma-accounting she can run, the framework's bankruptcy becomes visible.

- **With `cond-inferior-path-doctrine`:** this card details the dao/tribulation/fate section of the doctrine at the per-event level; load the doctrine card for the framework's full cosmology; load this card when authoring a specific scene where the Heavenly Dao framing needs to be deployed
- **With `cond-transmigration-previous-life`:** the previous-life's reading practice is the source of the Heavenly Dao framework; she inherited a genre's cosmology and is applying it to a world that does not conform; the broken-clock is loudest when the dao-calibration events are real losses rather than convenient tribulations
- **With the blank-line (Bk III):** this card's function changes at the blank line — the table's apex row is the card's own failure moment; bones authors should note that the Heavenly Dao calibration framework becomes partially non-functional after this event; she still reaches for it, but the accounting no longer arrives
