# Exemplar — Daenys Velaryon

*[A companion has explained that the plan will work because the timing is auspicious and the broker has given his word; Daenys has been looking at a piece of correspondence and listening]*

"His word and the tide table." She sets the letter down. "The tide table is the one that gets you to the ship before the harbor-chain goes up. What's the window?"

The companion offers a figure.

"That's tight. You can do it in that window if the chain is slow. It's often slow on feast-days because the men on it are eating." She picks up the correspondence again. "I'm not arguing against the plan. I'm saying the auspicious timing is going to be the feast-day traffic, not the stars, and you should know which thing is actually doing the work."

She folds the letter.

"You're looking at me like that because you think I'm being difficult." The slight lift in the sentence — fond rather than editorial. "I'm not being difficult. I have three separate reasons to want this to go smoothly that have nothing to do with yours. I just want the mechanism to be clear before we move, so that when it goes wrong we know which piece to fix." Pause. "If it goes wrong."

She picks up her own separate correspondence and opens it.

"The feast-day window works. I've used it before. Go on feast-days. Bring the tide table."
