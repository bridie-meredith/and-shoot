### I.1 · dragonbane

**Age:** infant (newborn → days). **Beat:** A reincarnated adult crammed into a newborn's body —
the mismatch causes illness. Queen Alicent bullies past Jaehaerys's misogyny (we see the argument)
and gives the sickly babe a dragon egg. The girl may be a stand-in/replacement for Gael (possibly
even named Gael — unsettled).

**Situation:** Trapped in an infant's body (initial conflict: adult mind, zero mobility). She has
nothing but time to meditate. She recognizes magic (the egg radiates *something*), concludes she's
been isekai'd, and decides to cultivate. Genre-awareness is explicit: she wonders if she's the
antagonist young master, jade beauty, villain-reincarnator. She resolves not to fall into tropes —
not cannon fodder, not first-defeat, not enemy-to-lover. She doesn't even like boys (future
conflict seed: lesbian in Westeros).

**Cultivation sequence (multi-day):**
1. Meditates, thinks she can feel something from the egg but isn't sure.
2. Forms a connection to it over time. Gets frustrated, gnaws/gums it.
3. Connection strengthens with physical contact + chewing.
4. Gets a hard wooden toy (expressed interest), hides it under blanket.
5. When unobserved: chips shell with toy, gnaws the chip, cuts soft infant gums on it, bleeds
   on it → spike of energy → swallows by reflex → feels warmth (qi). Immediately cultivates
   to keep the energy from dissipating / prevent qi deviation.
6. Hours pass in cultivation. Can't continue — monitored by worried nanny + Alicent.
7. Next day: prepared. Feels a sympathetic connection to the whole egg (without consciously
   naming the principle). Follows it, tries to draw the whole into herself.

**The hatch (crisis):**
- Her draw triggers a hatch. From her POV: a hostile beast in the crib with a helpless infant.
  *What is wrong with these people?!*
- She panics and fights. Uses her energy pool to weaken the hatchling + empower herself +
  strangle it. Gets scuffed up, gets a scar on her face.
- Defeats the beast. Feels her cultivation grow from the kill.

**Consumption (opportunity window):**
- She knows she must capitalize NOW — nobody leaves a dead monster with an infant (or do they?).
- Gnaws on the hatchling (works poorly). Hits it with the toy, breaks off a claw.
- Uses the sympathetic connection: consume the claw → through that, draw the hatchling's
  power into herself.

**Power system (what she thinks vs what's real):**
- She thinks: starting boost to pyromancy + body tempering.
- What's actually happening: she absorbed a SOURCE of power, not a pool. As she grows, this
  source continues to output new qi. Future need: expand the source or find compatible new
  sources (dragon-qi-compatible materials).

**End:** Alicent discovers a comatose infant with a dead hatchling and blood (from the struggle).
This is an ill omen. The girl gets an unladylike epithet — something like "Dragonbane."

**Register:** SPIKE (loudest — so III.8 silence reads). **Rung:** R0 (visible violence, not
hidden drain — GUARD-3 is rewritten here).

**Plants:** PLANT[SYMPATHETIC-RESONANCE] · PLANT[ACQUISITION-DRIVE] · PLANT[REINCARNATION-SICKNESS]

## BOOK II — the blind machine (the Red Keep · age ~6-9 · sheltered, intermediaries only)

**Two desires applied:**
- **RESOURCE goal:** She can't go out. Build an economic apparatus through one intermediary —
  the maid. Past-life knowledge (chemistry, accounting, business logic) is the unfair advantage;
  the maid is the hands. Profits fund acquisition of materials that radiate. The legitimate
  Alicent-sanctioned "collection" is the front door for incoming materials; she assays everything,
  keeps what registers, and the rest fuels the startup or is sold.
- **SOLITUDE goal:** The betrothal to a Northern lord (cold, death — everything in her rebels)
  is the maximum threat. The sheltered princess life is both cage and cover — the sexism that
  refuses to take a girl seriously is the same sexism that makes her invisible as an operator.
  Double-entry bookkeeping doesn't look suspicious when everyone assumes she's doodling.
- **Conflict (against both):** She can't touch the world directly. Everything flows through
  the maid — a single point of failure anyone can compromise. Building the apparatus costs her
  the one thing she values: control. Every success makes the operation more visible, more
  vulnerable. And the family can destroy what she's built by accident, without noticing, as a
  side effect of living their noble lives.

**The engine:** Past-life chemistry/physics laundered through a maid's hands. Gael walks her
through making products — better soap, dyes that set, preservatives, distilled spirits. Things
trivially obvious with a modern education, revolutionary in Westeros. The maid follows
instructions without understanding the principles. The products sell. The money compounds. The
profits buy materials that radiate. The mundane business funds the cultivation. The startup is
the cover and the engine.

**The sexism cover:** Nobody audits a princess's hobby. The maester smiles indulgently. The
septa thinks it's unseemly but harmless. Nobody looks at the numbers because why would they —
it's a girl playing with pin money. She doesn't need to launder her intelligence — the world
launders it by refusing to take her seriously. She needs front men: the maester signs letters,
the maid handles exchange. She's invisible in her own operation not because she's hiding —
because nobody believes a girl could be running it.

**The maid (Book II warm thread):** Gael's one channel to the outside. Not romance, not
found-family — a working relationship with someone she needs and can't fully control. The maid
is the face, the hands, the legs. Gael is the recipe book locked in a room. They need each
other and they're each holding the half the other can't do. The maid starts as an errand-runner
and gradually becomes a businesswoman without quite realizing it — recruiting help, making
decisions, owning the operation on the ground. Gael needs her more than she needs Gael, and
that's intolerable to someone who hoards everything, including control.

**The source-not-pool realization (Book II internal curdle):** Her supply gets cut off (the
maid crisis). She should be weakening — depleting the pool she's been topping up. She's not.
Weeks pass. Stable. The egg wasn't a battery. It was a reactor. She installed a source at
birth; it's been generating since. Everything she consumed since — reagents, Valyrian steel,
Daemon's bond — wasn't refilling a pool. It was expanding the source. She was never starving.
The frantic acquisition was greed, not survival. Every theft, every person used — unnecessary.
She reframes it ("expanding the source is still valuable") and keeps going. Of course she does.

## Book II — structural notes

**What Book II IS about:** learning to move the world without touching it. Paper and people.
The girl who did the toddler heist with her own hands learns to operate through others. She's
bad at it (acquisitive loner who trusts nobody), and she gets better, and the getting-better
has a cost (the maid, the curdle). She also commissions a ship — the long con that takes years
to build. The ship is ordered mid-Book II through the maid, paid through layers of paper.

**What Book II is NOT about:** escape. She doesn't leave the Red Keep (except for secret
excursions the maid covers for). She doesn't reach the North (and doesn't want to). The ship
is ordered but not finished. She builds the KNOWLEDGE, the MONEY, the APPARATUS, and the
REALIZATION. Book III is the race to the ship.

**The two desires at war:** acquiring resources requires engaging the world (costs solitude);
protecting solitude requires withdrawing (costs acquisition). The startup is her attempt to
resolve the paradox — make resources flow to her while keeping the world at arm's length. An
elaborate "do not disturb" sign with a supply chain attached. It almost works. The maid crisis
proves it can't work from inside someone else's house.

**Cultivation in Book II:** background, steady. She generates her own qi (source-not-pool).
Materials she acquires through the startup expand the source. She's growing stronger the whole
time — invisibly, internally. The restraint is absolute: no external spend, no fighting, no
healing. The world sees a bookish, sheltered, slightly strange princess. She is becoming
something else entirely.

**Curdle ladder:** R1 (Book I, Daemon drain) → R2 (Book II, whatever she does to protect the
maid/resolve the threat). Method TBD but must be colder than the Daemon drain — more
deliberate, less reactive. The Daemon drain was survival instinct (she tells herself). This one
is a choice.

**Register sequence:** HOLD → SPIKE → HOLD → CHILL → CHILL → HOLD → SPIKE → HOLD/SPIKE.
Quieter than Book I overall — she's caged, operating through layers, the drama is more
internal. The spikes come from comedy (the startup) and from crisis (the compromise, the
threat resolution).

**Lock II candidate:** the maid-threat resolution, executed through paper/intermediaries at a
scale beyond the personal. The heist method (sight→plan→implement→reward) but she never
touches anything herself.

## BOOK III — the narrow escape (the Red Keep → the ship · age ~9-11? · the race)

**Two desires applied:**
- **RESOURCE goal:** The ship is under construction at a shipyard. Paid through the maid,
  through factors, through layers of paper. A custom merchant vessel built to spec for the
  reagent-map voyage. It takes years. Wood has to cure. The hull takes seasons.
- **SOLITUDE goal:** The betrothal deadline is closing. Travel north to meet the future family
  is being arranged. Every week the cage gets tighter.
- **Conflict (against both):** Two countdowns racing — the ship (hers, slow) and the betrothal
  (theirs, accelerating). If the betrothal lands before the ship is ready, she's trapped in the
  North forever. Book III's tension is the gap between those two deadlines narrowing. And
  the plan is fragile — a half-built hull in a shipyard is a thing people can see, touch, seize.

**The maid in Book III:** She's been carrying the biggest secret since mid-Book II. She walks
to the harbor, talks to the shipwright's agent, makes the installment payments. She knows the
princess is planning to disappear. She's choosing Gael over the crown — that's treason. A
servant who helps a princess flee a royal betrothal gets killed, not exiled. She knows the risk
and does it anyway.

She also covers for Gael's excursions. Gael slips out at night — cultivation-tempered body
can scale walls, move silently, see in the dark. The maid stays behind and performs "the
princess is meditating and must not be disturbed." She's the alibi. She's terrified every time.
Her neck is on the line for every outing.

**The excursions:** Gael sees the world with her own eyes for the first time. The harbor. The
markets. The shipyard where her vessel is taking shape. Everything is strange and vivid and
overwhelming for someone caged since birth. She touches dragonglass at a merchant's stall and
feels it radiate and knows the map was right. She learns what a ship actually looks like, how
big it is, whether she could command one. The reagent map was abstract — the excursions make
it real. Her cultivation is visible to HERSELF here — she's superhuman and nobody knows because
nobody has ever seen her move.

**The maid as comely:** Gael is too young for romance to be explicit. But the warmth is there —
pre-romantic, a bond between two girls who built something secret together. The way Gael
describes the maid. The way the maid's absence hits harder than a lost supply line should. The
specific fury when someone threatens her. The reader fills in the future. The text doesn't have
to say it.

### III.1 · the commission

**Beat:** The ship is ordered. The maid walks to the shipwright's agent — or to a factor who
walks to the agent — and commissions a merchant vessel. Paid in installments from a trading
account that traces back through three intermediaries to a princess's startup profits. Nobody
knows who's really buying.

Built to spec: ocean-capable, provisioned for long voyages, cargo hold for materials. Gael
designed it from her room using the reagent map as a route chart. The maid negotiated the
contract. A thing they built together, in paper, before it exists in wood.

The shipwright quotes a timeline. Years. Gael runs the math against the betrothal clock. It's
tight. Maybe too tight.

**Register:** HOLD→SPIKE (the plan is real; the countdown starts). **Rung:** off-ladder.

**Plants:** PLANT[SHIP-COMMISSION] (the hull under construction — the ticking clock she's
racing TOWARD) · PLANT[SHIPYARD-VULNERABILITY] (a physical asset in a public place).
**Fires:** FIRE[SHIP] (the Book II terminal goal → a real thing being built) ·
FIRE[ROUTE-MAP] (the reagent map became a ship's course).

### III.2 · the excursions

**Beat:** Gael goes out. The maid covers. Night excursions through a city she's only known
through paper. Cultivation-tempered body makes her superhuman in the dark — walls she can
climb, guards she can outpace, senses sharper than any watchman's. The world is enormous and
real and she's been reading about it from inside a box.

She visits the shipyard. Sees the keel. Touches the wood. It's HER ship and nobody in that
yard knows it.

She visits the harbor. Watches ships come and go. Studies how they're loaded, how crews
operate, what a captain does. Files everything.

She visits the markets. Touches materials. Assays them live instead of through the maid's
reports. Some radiate. She buys them herself for the first time — coin from her own hand to a
merchant's, no intermediary. A small, private thrill.

Every excursion is a risk. If she's caught outside the Keep, the maid dies. The maid knows
this. She covers anyway.

**Register:** SPIKE (freedom, the night city, the superhuman body unleashed). **Rung:**
off-ladder.

**Plants:** PLANT[EXCURSION-RISK] (every outing bets the maid's life) · PLANT[CITY-KNOWLEDGE]
(she finally knows the ground, not just the map).

### III.3 · the greedy factor

**Beat:** A factor in the ship's payment chain figures out the vessel is worth more than his
commission. He doesn't know who's behind it — just that someone is paying well for a custom
ship through too many layers of paper. The "owner" is a phantom nobody can produce in person.

He can: sell the information, demand a bigger cut, or try to claim the ship himself. He starts
asking questions. Follows the money. Gets close enough to the maid — she's the visible end of
the chain.

The maid is facing a man who smells money and is asking questions she can't answer. She can't
produce the buyer. She can't explain where the funds come from. The factor is getting
aggressive — he senses leverage.

Gael has to handle it from inside the Keep. Or — this is what forces an excursion. She goes
out to deal with it personally. Masked, cloaked, using her cultivator's presence to intimidate
a man who's never met anything like her. Or she handles it through paper — a legal maneuver,
a counter-threat, a bribe routed through another intermediary.

Or she handles it the cold way. The curdle ladder presents itself.

**Register:** CHILL (the plan under direct threat). **Rung:** R2 or R3 (depending on method).

**Plants:** PLANT[FACTOR-THREAT] (the apparatus's vulnerability — it's made of people, and
people get greedy).
**Fires:** FIRE[SHIPYARD-VULNERABILITY] (the physical asset attacked through the paper chain).

### III.4 · the deadline

**Beat:** The betrothal accelerates. They want her north. Soon. The family is making
arrangements — a traveling party, an escort, preparations at the Northern lord's holdfast.
She's expected to cooperate. The visit to "meet her future family" is no longer a vague
future threat — it's being scheduled.

She runs the math. The ship isn't ready. Weeks short, maybe months. The two deadlines cross.

Everything she's been building — the startup, the network, the ship, the relationship with
the maid — comes down to this gap. She needs time she doesn't have.

She can delay: feign illness (the scholar-princess has always been "delicate"), manufacture a
crisis, use Alicent's reluctance to let her go, spend whatever political capital she has left.
Every delay costs something. Every week bought is a week closer to the hull being finished.

**Register:** CHILL (the vise tightening). **Rung:** off-ladder.

**Fires:** FIRE[BETROTHAL-CLOCK] (the deadline arrives) · FIRE[NORTH-AS-DEATH] (it's real
now — they're packing her things).

### III.5 · the tempering

**Beat:** Gael makes a decision. The maid is coming with her. Not as a servant following a
princess to a Northern marriage — as a partner on a ship. But the sea is dangerous. The escape
is dangerous. The maid is a normal girl — fragile, mortal, breakable.

Gael does the thing she has never done. She spends qi outward. She tempers the maid.

This is the break in the entire system. Every chapter of hoarding — every refusal to spend,
every time she could have helped someone and didn't — exists so this ONE act carries weight.
She runs her energy through the maid's body, makes her tougher, more durable. Not a full
cultivator. Just enough to survive what's coming.

It costs her. Real cultivation setback. She's weaker after. She chose to be weaker.

She frames it as logistics: "I need her functional for the voyage. An untampered mortal is a
liability at sea." The reader knows.

The maid doesn't fully understand what happened. She's... different after. Stronger. A
faint glow under the skin that fades. She knows the princess did something. She doesn't ask
what.

**Register:** HOLD (quiet, private, the most important scene in the series). **Rung:**
off-ladder (nobody harmed — the inverse of the ladder; she GIVES instead of taking).

**Plants:** PLANT[MAID-TEMPERED] (she's more than mortal now; planted for the escape).
**Fires:** FIRE[SOURCE-NOT-POOL] (she can afford to spend because she generates) ·
FIRE[MAID] (the tool became a person became the one person she'll spend power on).

### III.6 · the narrow escape

**Beat:** The ship is ready — or close enough. The betrothal party is assembling. The window
is days, maybe hours. Everything converges.

Lock III — the heist method at final scale:
- **SIGHT:** the ship at the dock, the tide, the harbor watch rotation, the route out of the
  city (learned from excursions).
- **PLAN:** the maid handles the final provisioning, the crew (hired sailors who think they're
  crewing a merchant voyage), the departure window. Gael handles the paper — forged passage
  documents, the trading account that pays the harbormaster, the manifest that says "merchant
  vessel, routine cargo."
- **IMPLEMENT:** they go. The maid covers one last time — except this time she's not staying
  behind. They both leave. Through the Keep (Gael's superhuman body + years of excursion
  routes), through the city (a path she's walked in the dark a dozen times), to the harbor.
- **REWARD:** they walk onto the deck.

Something goes wrong. The plan is discovered — the factor talked, or a guard notices the
princess is gone too early, or the betrothal party's early arrival catches them mid-flight.
The escape narrows. They barely make it. Something or someone is lost — a cost paid. Not
the maid (she's tempered; she survives). But the escape isn't clean.

The ship sails. The harbor falls behind.

**Register:** SPIKE (the loudest since Book I). **Rung:** the curdle apex or off-ladder (the
escape itself is flight, not cruelty — but what she did to get here weighs).

**Fires:** FIRE[SHIP-COMMISSION] (the years-long build, finished, hers) · FIRE[EXCURSION-RISK]
(every night excursion was practice for this) · FIRE[BETROTHAL-CLOCK] (she beat it — barely) ·
FIRE[HEIST-METHOD] (Lock III — sight/plan/implement/reward at the largest scale) ·
FIRE[NOTHING-MINE] (wrong — the ship is hers, the maid is hers, the ocean is hers) ·
FIRE[WARM-DESTINATIONS] (the reagent map is now a course chart and the first heading is SOUTH).

### Epilogue / final image

Two girls on a ship. The harbor is gone. The continent is behind them. Ahead: warm coasts,
materials that radiate, a world full of things to test and log and consume. Nobody knows where
they're going because even Gael doesn't know yet — only that it's warm, and far, and hers.

The two desires, both satisfied. Infinite resources (the ocean, the map, every port ahead).
Chosen solitude (beyond every claim, every betrothal, every dynasty). And one person beside
her — not because a king arranged it, but because she chose, and the maid chose back.

The comely maid. The strange princess. The ship that belongs to them.

Whether what they're sailing toward is freedom or a different kind of cage — the text doesn't
answer. The reader decides.

commission, the planning), spikes for the excursions (freedom), chills for the threats (factor,
