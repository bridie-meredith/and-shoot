# Role 04 — L&D / Instructional Design at a SaaS Company — Persona Analysis

Target role: **Senior Instructional Designer (IC), L&D** at a ~900-person Series-D B2B SaaS company (workflow-automation software — no edtech, no students, no classrooms). Posted band $118k–$152k base + modest equity; hiring manager will stretch to ~$160k for someone who has "clearly done this at a tech company before."

Two gatekeepers screen Catherine in sequence:
- **Tyler Brennan** — Talent Acquisition Partner. Owns the ATS auto-screen and the eight-second human skim. First two gates of three. A "yes" from Tyler = *advance to the hiring manager*.
- **Dana Whitfield** — Director of L&D, the hiring manager. Does her own first pass (distrusts the keyword filter), screens portfolio-first, runs the take-home. A "yes" from Dana = *advance to a recruiter screen, then a Rise-module take-home*.

---

## Persona 1 — Tyler Brennan (saas-talent-recruiter)

### What Tyler is looking for — the bar for a YES

Tyler is not judging instructional-design quality; he knows he is unqualified to. His "yes" means: **the resume parsed clean, the keywords are real, the comp lines up, and the person reads as someone the Director won't bounce back with "why did you send me this."** He is measured on time-to-fill and hiring-manager satisfaction with his slate, so he wants a fast, *no-regret* shortlist.

A YES requires, all together:
- A headline in the top third that literally names the req's job family ("Instructional Designer," "L&D," "Learning Experience Designer," "Training Program Manager").
- At least four of his scorecard keywords (*instructional design, adult learning, ADDIE, Articulate Storyline, Rise, eLearning, LMS, onboarding, sales enablement, stakeholder, SCORM, learning experience*) — ideally sitting inside real accomplishment bullets, not just a stuffed strip.
- Business-resume format: one-to-two pages, reverse-chronological, verb-led bullets that land on a number. No Publications, no Dissertation, no narrative paragraphs.
- Adult/workforce-training *shape* — a company (or client) training its own people, not a classroom of children.
- At least one authoring tool named explicitly.
- On the phone screen: a comp expectation inside/below the $118k–$152k band, a non-ambivalent reason for moving into corporate L&D, and a tenure story that doesn't read as flight risk.

### Instant-reject triggers (any one ends the read)
- Academic-CV format — a Publications section, a Dissertation line, an EdD foregrounded over titles, multi-line narrative role descriptions.
- No recognizable instructional-design title anywhere in the top third.
- Document over three pages.
- A summary leading with student/equity/transformation mission language and no corporate L&D framing.
- (Phone-screen stage) A comp expectation above the band, or quoting consulting day-rates that imply the salary is a step down.

### Top questions Tyler is asking while screening Catherine

1. **"Did this resume even survive the parser?"** — His Greenhouse saved search scores against the twelve-term scorecard; a resume hitting fewer than four terms sinks below the top ~50 and Tyler never sees it. *(Grounded in "ATS auto-screen … keyword scorecard … fewer than four of those keywords sinks in the pile.")*
2. **"In eight seconds, can I find a title that maps to my req?"** — His eyes go to the line under the name first; if it says "EdD, Inclusive Leadership Researcher" he is half-out, if it says "Instructional Designer | Adult Learning & Enablement" he leans in. He does not reconstruct a career or infer. *(Grounded in "Title proximity at the top … He does not infer.")*
3. **"Does the most recent role read as adult/workforce training, not a classroom?"** — He checks the most-recent title + company and the first two bullets for verb, scale, tool, and audience — *are these adults being trained?* *(Grounded in The Skim steps 2–3 and "Adult-learning / corporate framing.")*
4. **"Is at least one authoring tool named — and do the bullets earn the keyword strip, or just assert it?"** — Tools are "the cheapest, fastest legitimacy signal"; he distrusts a Skills strip the bullets never back. *(Grounded in "Keyword density that looks native, not stuffed" and his round-2 caveat that Storyline/Rise/SCORM "appear only in the strip.")*
5. **"Is this a business resume or an academic CV?"** — One-to-two pages, reverse-chronological, no Publications/Dissertation/Selected Presentations. A CV "breaks his pattern-match." *(Grounded in "Business-resume format" + the blocker-severity pet peeve.)*
6. **"Is there mission/identity language that tells me she wants a different job?"** — A summary opening with passion for students, equity, or transforming education reads as a candidate who wants edtech or a mission seat and is settling. *(Grounded in the "passionate about / mission language" pet peeve and the "perceived flight risk / mission-misalignment" screen-out.)*
7. **"Will her comp expectation fit the $118k–$152k band, and can she say so cleanly?"** — A candidate who names $180k+ or quotes day-rates is a decline-risk; Tyler will not advance someone he expects to turn down the offer. *(Grounded in "A comp expectation inside or below the band" and the phone-screen blocker.)*
8. **"Why is this person leaving education entirely — is this a one-year stepping stone?"** — A teaching job still listed as current and an unaddressed career-change signal worsen his flight-risk read; a fast re-quit is a mark against *him*. *(Grounded in "Unexplained gap or a teaching job still listed as current" and "Tenure that doesn't read as flight risk.")*
9. **"Can she walk me through a module she actually built in Rise?"** — His self-flagged phone-screen probe, opened by tool keywords that live only in the strip. *(Grounded in his round-2 caveat: "it becomes a phone-screen question … not a screen-out.")*

## Persona 2 — Dana Whitfield (saas-ld-director)

### What Dana is looking for — the bar for a YES

Dana does her own first pass because the recruiter's keyword filter "surfaces too many K-12 teachers." Her YES means: **"This person has built training for working adults, at scale, with the tools we use, and I believe the portfolio is theirs and not their team's"** — a low-risk hire who can take a half-baked stakeholder ask and turn it into shipped courseware without her babysitting. A YES is *not* "impressive person."

A YES requires:
- A **portfolio link in the header** — and, behind it, 2-3 real sample modules built in Rise or Storyline that are credibly hers and credibly adult-facing. This is "the single highest-signal element"; she will not take design competence on faith.
- Authoring-tool fluency that is **named, specific, and backed by samples** — "built X in Rise," not "familiar with e-learning tools."
- Adult learners as the subject — onboarding cohorts, manager training, sales reps, the word "employees."
- Scale, quantified — headcounts and reach above ~100, plus reuse/adoption.
- Stakeholder partnering with **non-educators** — sales leaders, PMs, eng managers — requirements extraction.
- A named design process (ADDIE / SAM).
- LMS / measurement literacy — Cornerstone/Docebo/Workday Learning; completion rates, adoption, Kirkpatrick levels, ramp-to-quota lift.
- Evidence of **self-paced/asynchronous courseware**, not only live instructor-led facilitation — the req owns onboarding curriculum and sales-enablement courseware that is largely async.
- A clean, business-formatted, one-page resume; no mission/identity language.

### Instant-reject triggers
- The three-strike pattern: **no portfolio link AND a teacher title AND no named authoring tools** — the resume goes to NO without a fourth read.
- A resume that opens with a dissertation and an academic publication list.
- A dead or thin portfolio link behind the URL — *worse than no link*, because it reads as carelessness and flips a BORDERLINE to an instant NO at the click.

### Top questions Dana is asking while screening Catherine

1. **"Is there a portfolio link in the header — and what is actually behind it?"** — Her eyes flick to the top-right of the header; absence is "near-blocker," and the click is "where my verdict actually gets decided." For an instructional designer a missing portfolio is "like a developer with no GitHub." *(Grounded in The Skim step 2 + round-2 "the portfolio is a URL, not yet a proven asset … my BORDERLINE verdict is genuinely suspended on this.")*
2. **"Does the title line say 'instructional designer / L&D / enablement' — or does it say 'teacher'?"** — This single line sets her default verdict before she reads further; "another teacher" ends the read. *(Grounded in The Skim step 1 and the "undisguised K-12 flavor" pet peeve.)*
3. **"Has this person built training for adults who have jobs — not children?"** — She wants onboarding cohorts, managers, sales reps, the word "employees"; she screens out "students," "lesson plans," "grade level." *(Grounded in "Adult learners as the subject" + the K-12-flavor pet peeve.)*
4. **"Does she actually know the tools — named, specific, with a sample to back each?"** — Her real tiebreaker, voiced out loud: "but does she know Storyline?" "Familiar with e-learning tools" is "worthless"; an unbacked strip reads as the "heard the tools matter and is bluffing" pattern. *(Grounded in "Authoring-tool fluency, named and specific" + "Vague tool claims" pet peeve + round-2 "named but unbacked … the seam between BORDERLINE and SCREEN-OUT.")*
5. **"Can she ship *self-paced courseware*, or only run live instructor-led training?"** — The req owns asynchronous onboarding and sales-enablement courseware; a 17-year history that is entirely live facilitation does not prove she can build a Rise module a new hire takes alone. *(Grounded in round-2 "the work is all instructor-led facilitation, not self-paced courseware … the substantive gap.")*
6. **"Is the work framed as program-at-scale, or as depth with a few people?"** — She thinks in reach and reuse; resumes built around intensive 1:1 / small-group work ("case-managed 12 students," "individualized support plans") are "the opposite of the program-at-scale signal." *(Grounded in "Scale, quantified" + "Depth-not-scale framing" pet peeve.)*
7. **"Has she partnered with non-educator stakeholders to extract requirements?"** — Sales leaders, PMs, eng managers — "the daily reality of the job." *(Grounded in "Stakeholder partnering with non-educators.")*
8. **"Are there learning-specific metrics — completion, adoption, Kirkpatrick, ramp lift — not just headcount?"** — L&D "fights to be seen as more than a cost center"; the 60% referral reduction is a program-intervention metric, not a *learning* metric. *(Grounded in "LMS / measurement literacy" + round-2 "no completion/adoption/Kirkpatrick metric.")*
9. **"Is there a named, repeatable design process — ADDIE/SAM?"** — Signals method, not just instincts. *(Grounded in "ADDIE / SAM or any named design process.")*
10. **"Does any 'inclusive leadership / passion for equity' language survive — and will she be restless designing sales-enablement courseware within a year?"** — Mission/identity language "tells her this person's heart is elsewhere." *(Grounded in "Mission/identity language" pet peeve + "identity mismatch bleed-through.")*

## Synthesis — what the two personas jointly demand

Tyler and Dana form a two-gate funnel with a clean division of labor, and the round-2 feedback confirms exactly where each stands today.

**They fully agree on the genre problem — and it is solved.** Both require: a headline that literally names the target role; a business-resume format with the CV apparatus (Publications, Dissertation, Awards header, narrative paragraphs) gone; the consulting practice leading as an adult-facing entry; named scorecard keywords; ADDIE named; and zero mission/identity language. The current resume already clears all of this — Tyler **ADVANCES** it.

**They diverge on what a "yes" costs.** Tyler's job is *genre and keywords* — satisfied by words. Dana's job is *proof* — and proof cannot be supplied by rewriting. Her BORDERLINE verdict is suspended on a single thing: a **live portfolio with 2-3 real, self-built Rise/Storyline modules** behind the header URL, plus at least one **bullet that backs an authoring tool inside a real deliverable** ("built a self-paced onboarding module in Rise 360, packaged to SCORM") and one **self-paced/asynchronous courseware** artifact. A dead or thin portfolio is worse than no link at all.

**The joint bar, stated once:** the document must read in its top third — instantly, to a keyword scorecard and an eight-second skim — exactly like the other strong instructional-design resumes in the pile (Tyler's gate); *and* it must be backed by demonstrable, self-built, adult-facing eLearning artifacts that prove the asserted tool fluency is real, not bluffed (Dana's gate). Words clear Tyler. Only **new build-work** clears Dana. Catherine must also, on the phone screen, name a comp expectation inside the $118k–$152k band and give a non-ambivalent, chose-this-on-purpose reason for moving into corporate L&D — because both personas read ambivalence as one-year flight risk.
