# Role 3 — Edtech Learning Scientist / Instructional Designer: Catherine's Next Steps

Scope: the **SpEd-focused and early-childhood edtech subset** (Goalbook, n2y,
Frontline, IXL's SpEd line, Lovevery, Begin, Khan Kids). These are the two doors
the personas confirm are open. The general-purpose efficacy-research door (Hannah
Brooks) stays closed for now — the only step that touches it is the data-fluency
ramp below, and even that is framed honestly as a nice-to-have, not a learning-
scientist claim.

Every step is achievable in 1-3 months while keeping the teaching job. Each is
tied to the specific persona question or screen-out risk it neutralizes. Drawn
from the opportunity report's Section 4 (Weak spots) and Section 6 (Recommended
next steps).

## Weeks 1-2 — Positioning and foundation

### 1. Lock the "Learning Experience Designer" positioning line
- **Action:** Finalize a one-sentence positioning that works for both Renée and
  Grace and rewrite the LinkedIn headline to match (report Section 4, weak spot
  #3; Section 6 weeks 1-2). Current LinkedIn headline "Education and Administration
  Leadership Expert" must go.
- **Neutralizes:** Renée's "title-chasing leadership language" peeve and Grace's
  "K-12 / administrative framing" instant-reject. Both screeners decide in 8
  seconds off the headline.
- **Realistic:** A writing task, a few hours.

### 2. Scope the age band honestly across all materials
- **Action:** Audit the resume and LinkedIn so the age band reads "ages 3-5 /
  preschool" wherever it is claimed — not a flat "early childhood" implying 0-6
  (critic feedback, Grace fix #1; the resume has no infant/toddler 0-3 signal).
- **Neutralizes:** Grace's credibility ding on the one place the current resume
  oversells. Precise scoping reads as candor, which Grace explicitly rewards.
- **Realistic:** Edit-only, under an hour.

### 3. Start daily AI-tool practice (power-user level)
- **Action:** 5-8 hrs/week using Claude, ChatGPT, NotebookLM, and one AI-for-
  teachers tool — MagicSchool, Brisk, or SchoolAI — on real PD-prep and
  curriculum-drafting work (report Section 4, weak spot #1; Section 6 weeks 1-2).
- **Neutralizes:** Grace's "no AI signal" flag — her product roadmap has an AI
  thread. Upgrades the resume's AI line from "completed a course" to demonstrated
  applied use in early-learning content work.
- **Realistic:** Folds into work she already does; no new time block.

## Weeks 3-6 — Artifact and product-iteration evidence

### 4. Package one existing design artifact as a documented "product spec"
- **Action:** Take the 6-8 week intervention framework (or a PD curriculum) and
  re-document it as a versioned, reusable content artifact: a written scope-and-
  sequence with explicit developmental rationale ("why this skill at this stage"),
  a revision log showing what changed across versions and why, and the
  reach/usage figures (sites, classrooms, educators who ran it).
- **Neutralizes:** Renée's "PD-deck-as-product" peeve and Grace's "no evidence of
  building anything reusable" — both need proof Catherine builds things others use
  at scale, with product verbs and a version history, not one-off delivery.
- **Realistic:** Documentation of work she already did; weekends across 3-4 weeks.

### 5. Take on one product-advisory engagement with a SpEd/early-childhood edtech tool
- **Action:** Secure a short, real cross-functional engagement — a content-advisory
  or curriculum-review contract with a SpEd or early-childhood edtech product, or a
  beta-tester / educator-advisory-board seat (these are widely available and
  low-commitment: Goalbook, n2y, MagicSchool, and early-childhood apps all run
  educator advisory programs). Deliver written content feedback against the
  product's roadmap, in a feedback cycle with its product/design team.
- **Neutralizes:** The single shared screen-out risk — Renée's and Grace's
  **product-iteration gap**. This is the one thing each "most often rejects on."
  It gives the resume a genuine (if modest) entry where Catherine worked with a
  product team, against a roadmap, on iterated content — closing the gap with a
  real artifact instead of an analog.
- **Realistic:** Advisory-board seats are unpaid or stipend-light, asynchronous,
  and 1-2 hrs/week — compatible with a teaching job. A short paid review contract
  is also plausible given her network and OSPI track record.

### 6. Publish one short public artifact on AI + SpEd/early-learning content
- **Action:** Write and publish one LinkedIn article or Substack post — e.g.,
  "What I Wish AI Early-Learning Products Understood About PDA Autism," or a
  product-critic piece on where mainstream edtech fails SpEd kids (report Section
  4, weak spot #2; Section 6 weeks 3-6).
- **Neutralizes:** Renée's "can you name what doesn't work for SpEd kids in
  mainstream edtech" question (proof Catherine thinks like a product critic) and
  Grace's preference for a forward-looking AI signal. Also gives the resume a
  linkable artifact.
- **Realistic:** One post, a few evenings.

## Months 2-3 — Data fluency and targeted applications

### 7. Reach honest, demonstrable basic data fluency
- **Action:** Complete a short, applied R or Python data course (e.g., a DataCamp
  / Coursera intro track) to the level the report explicitly defines as the 1-3
  month target: **"can clean a CSV and run a regression in R or Python."** Do one
  small worked example end-to-end on a real education dataset and keep it as a
  portfolio link.
- **Neutralizes:** Partially addresses Hannah's gap — but framed honestly. This is
  NOT a learning-scientist claim and must not be written as one. On the resume it
  appears as a modest, accurate skills line ("working data fluency: cleans
  datasets and runs basic regression in R; not a causal-inference methodologist").
  For Renée and Grace it is a small plus that signals Catherine can talk to a
  data-heavy leadership team; for Hannah it is a first honest step toward a future
  re-target, not a present qualification.
- **Realistic:** A self-paced course over 6-8 weeks at a few hours/week; the
  report names exactly this scope as a 1-3 month step.

### 8. Apply to 3-5 carefully targeted SpEd / early-childhood edtech roles
- **Action:** Apply to a short list of curriculum / learning-design roles at
  SpEd-focused edtech (Goalbook, n2y, Frontline, IXL's SpEd line) and early-
  childhood edtech (Lovevery, Begin, Khan Kids) — using the projected resume
  (report Section 6, months 2-3). Do NOT apply to general-purpose efficacy-research
  reqs.
- **Neutralizes:** Targets only the doors the personas confirm are open; the
  retarget itself encodes the "don't apply to Hannah" rule.
- **Realistic:** A focused list of 3-5, leveraging the artifact, the advisory
  engagement, and the public post from steps 4-6 as differentiators.

### 9. Disambiguate the consulting record and tighten the pivot bridge
- **Action:** Rework the resume so the two consulting entries (Olver Consulting
  and the OSPI contracts) are clearly distinct engagements, and add one explicit,
  confident pivot-bridge line naming the practice/research origin, the transferable
  spine (curriculum scope-and-sequence, content design, domain-expert
  collaboration), and an understanding of product cadence (critic feedback, Renée
  fixes #2-3).
- **Neutralizes:** Renée's "is this one practice or two?" flicker and her residual
  "pure consultant framing" peeve; reinforces the honest-pivot trust both she and
  Grace reward.
- **Realistic:** Edit-only; done as part of finalizing the projected resume.
