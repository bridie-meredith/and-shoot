# Role 3 — Edtech Learning Scientist / Instructional Designer: Persona Analysis

Three gatekeepers screen Catherine for this category. The opportunity report's verdict:
the **SpEd-focused and early-childhood edtech subset** is her strongest fit; the
general-purpose efficacy-research variant is a no for now. The three personas below
encode exactly that split — two doors that open, one that does not.

---

## Persona 1 — Hannah Brooks, Senior Manager, Efficacy Research (general-purpose edtech)

The Duolingo/Khan-shaped efficacy-research req. One opening, 200+ resumes this cycle,
an 8-15 second read. The resume screen exists only to decide who earns the messy-CSV
take-home. Hannah is a quant cognitive-science PhD; she screens for one trait — *can
this person open a 4-million-row event table and not flinch.*

### Questions Hannah asks while screening
1. Is there a programming language anywhere on this page — R, Python, or SQL? (Her
   8-second hunt; the posting names it as a requirement, not a preference.)
2. Is there a named statistical method attached to a named outcome — mixed-effects
   model, difference-in-differences, RCT, propensity-score matching?
3. Is any single publication empirical and quantitative — a study with an n and a
   design — rather than a framework or a literature synthesis?
4. Does this person reason about *whether the product worked* (retention deltas,
   learning-gain effect sizes, dosage-response) — or only whether students liked it?
5. Has she ever sat with ambiguous data and written "results were null, here's what
   we learned" — i.e., would she survive a deliberately inconclusive take-home?
6. Is the "Skills/Technical" block load-bearing (named packages: tidyverse, lme4) or
   a tell ("proficient in data analysis")?
7. Are the degrees and the dissertation award doing the work the *skill* should be
   doing — the credential shield?
8. Does any impact claim collapse to n-of-one ("improved outcomes for my students")?

### Hannah's bar
- **YES** = "I believe this person can run a regression, read its output skeptically,
  and would survive the CSV take-home." Everyone in the stack knows learning; the
  question is purely the quantitative instrument. One credible quant line (a
  regression, a controlled experiment, a dataset with a row count, a named package)
  buys a full read and moves the resume off the reject pile.
- **Instant rejects:** no programming language anywhere; all research qualitative;
  "data" only as a buzzword; a teaching/leadership CV with a research section bolted
  on; an inflated tool claim ("expert in statistical analysis") that will fold on the
  call.

### Honest verdict for Catherine
**SCREEN-OUT — and correctly so.** Catherine has no R/Python, no large-dataset
analysis, no causal-inference work. That is the instant-reject condition itself, and
no resume edit fixes it. The repositioned tagline ("Learning Experience Designer")
correctly signals this is not an application for Hannah's req. The 1-3 month
data-fluency step (clean a CSV, run a regression in R/Python) does **not** clear
Hannah's bar — her bar is a *shipped quantitative study*. The honest framing
("qualitative and practitioner-research, not causal-inference methods") is what keeps
Catherine from tripping Hannah's inflated-claim blocker. Hannah is the test that
confirms the general-purpose variant is a no; Catherine's effort belongs on the
SpEd/early-childhood subset where her domain depth is the rare thing.

## Persona 2 — Renée Acosta, Director of Learning Design, SpEd-Focused EdTech

A former SpEd teacher turned product-side learning lead at a Goalbook/n2y/Frontline-
shaped company. One curriculum/content req a year, 80-150 applicants — most general-ed
instructional designers who think "differentiation" counts as SpEd. She owns
scope-and-sequence inside a product built specifically for special-education students.

### Questions Renée asks while screening
1. Does the headline name special education explicitly, or hide behind "education
   leadership"? (Her 8-second decision point.)
2. Is the SpEd specific and un-fakeable — complex/PDA autism, IEP/IDEA, disability
   categories, caseload — or general-ed "diverse learners" cosplay?
3. Has this person *built or shaped a product or repeatable artifact* — a goal bank,
   a curriculum used at scale, a framework — or only delivered live PD?
4. Is the compliance work stated as a *craft* ("conducted multi-district IEP/IDEA
   compliance audits") rather than a credential? Compliance is the hard, un-fakeable
   part of every SpEd product.
5. Can she name what doesn't work for SpEd kids in mainstream edtech — has she
   thought like a product critic, not just a user?
6. Is there any product-cadence vocabulary — roadmap, release, sprint, engineers,
   PMs, user feedback loop — or is this all K-12 PD vocabulary?
7. Is the pivot framed honestly — "here's how my work maps to product, here's what
   I'd have to learn" — or does it pretend the gap doesn't exist?
8. Is the most recent role classroom/practitioner, or already adjacent to
   product/content design?
9. Are the EdD and dissertation award present-but-subordinate, or are they the whole
   pitch? (Title-chasing leadership language is a strong peeve.)
10. Is this a pure consultant/speaker with no embedded, accountable, team-based work
    — i.e., can this person take direction inside a product org?

### Renée's bar
- **YES** = this person has *lived the domain* (IEPs, IDEA, disability categories —
  not buzzwords) AND can show they can ship inside a product team's iteration cadence,
  not just deliver a district workshop. An honest one-line acknowledgment of the
  product gap, paired with a credible bridge, earns trust — she pivoted herself.
- **Instant rejects:** general-ed-only resume with no SpEd nouns; "synergy" / "thought
  leader"; a summary that could belong to any educator on earth; SpEd mentioned only
  in a volunteer line.
- **Earns a second pass:** a real SpEd caseload; the phrase "compliance audit"; any
  sign she designed something other humans used at scale; an honest gap-acknowledgment.

### Honest verdict for Catherine
**ADVANCE.** Catherine's SpEd depth is exactly what this screen exists to find and is
rare in the stack: 17 years, a genuine complex/PDA autism niche, current early-
childhood SpEd practice, and — the line that makes Renée stop — multi-site IEP/IDEA
compliance auditing. Live screen-out risks: (1) the **product-iteration gap** — no
roadmap, no release, no engineers, no content revised against product usage data; the
resume foregrounds the closest honest analog (classroom/PD feedback loops) but the
gap is real; (2) **pure-consultant framing** — two of five experience entries are
consulting, with no unambiguously embedded, team-based work. The 1-3 month next-steps
narrow these: a portfolio artifact, a product-vocabulary upskill, and product-cadence
language on the iteration bullets.

## Persona 3 — Grace Tan, Head of Curriculum, Early-Childhood EdTech (ages 0-6)

Trained as a developmental psychologist, ran a lab preschool for six years, now owns
the developmental backbone of a venture-backed play-based product (Lovevery/Begin/
Khan Kids-shaped). One "Head of Learning" req every 18 months; 40-90 resumes; a fast,
ruthless read because the role is coveted.

### Questions Grace asks while screening
1. Does the headline name early childhood or ages 0-6 — or is it K-12 / administrative
   leadership with no early-childhood signal?
2. Is the current role genuinely birth-to-six — does this person work with young
   children *right now, every day*?
3. Does the resume show *real developmental fluency* — milestones, fine vs. gross
   motor, expressive vs. receptive language, the social-emotional ladder, what's
   typical at 18 vs. 36 months — or "developmentally appropriate practice" as
   decoration?
4. "Why this age?" — can this person defend why an activity belongs at month 14 and
   not month 10?
5. Has she built something reusable — a curriculum, a scope-and-sequence, a structured
   set of activities — or is it all 1:1, in-the-moment delivery?
6. Is there any product/iteration signal — designers, engineers, shipped versions,
   content revised on user feedback? (The thing she most often rejects on.)
7. Does she believe children learn through play and child-led pedagogy, not
   worksheets-on-a-screen?
8. Can she write to an anxious parent at 11pm — family-facing, warm, neither
   condescending nor alarming?
9. Does she understand atypical development as developmental *range* (a differentiator)
   — or is autism framed as disability-services credentialing?
10. Is there any AI signal — the product roadmap has an AI thread (personalization,
    parent guidance, content generation)?
11. Does the dissertation/AERA register crowd out concrete craft — is this a
    researcher who's never written a 40-word parent tip?

### Grace's bar
- **YES** = this person could sit in sprint planning tomorrow and defend a scope-and-
  sequence decision to an engineer who wants to cut it, AND write the parent-facing
  copy explaining why tummy time matters — without sounding like a textbook or a
  brand voice that's never met a real toddler.
- **Instant rejects:** headline purely K-12 or administrative leadership with no
  early-childhood signal; the whole resume service delivery with nothing built; an
  obvious district administrator treating an edtech role as a soft landing.
- **Earns a second pass:** a current genuinely-early-childhood role; a concrete
  designed-curriculum artifact; a family-communication line; depth with atypical
  development framed as developmental range.

### Honest verdict for Catherine
**ADVANCE.** The hook is that Catherine's current role *is* birth-to-six developmental
practice, every day. Complex/PDA autism reads to Grace as developmental range — a
genuine YES-pile differentiator. Family-facing IEP work reframed as parent
communication is the 11pm-tip sensibility her product runs on. Live risks: (1) the
**product-iteration gap** (same as Renée's); (2) **age-band honesty** — Catherine's
strongest work is ages 3-5, and Grace's product spans 0-6 including birth-to-3, so
the resume must scope the band precisely ("ages 3-5 / preschool") rather than imply
0-6; (3) **academic register** crowding out craft. The next-steps add a portfolio
artifact, AI-in-early-learning signal, and precise band scoping.

## Synthesis — what the three personas jointly demand

**The split is the whole story.** Hannah is a permanent SCREEN-OUT: her bar is a
shipped quantitative study and Catherine cannot honestly clear it inside 1-3 months.
Renée and Grace both ADVANCE — and they are the two doors Catherine can actually walk
through. The resume must be written for them and must *not* be engineered to slip
past Hannah, because a candidate who did would fail the CSV take-home harder.

What Renée and Grace **jointly** demand of the resume:

1. **Domain signal in the first 8 seconds.** Both decide off the tagline and the most
   recent role. Special education, complex/PDA autism, and a genuinely current
   early-childhood role must lead. No leadership-abstraction headline.
2. **A built, reusable artifact — not a PD deck.** Both reject on "all delivery,
   nothing shipped." The resume needs at least one named, scoped artifact described
   with product verbs (designed, authored, scaled) and reach numbers, not
   facilitate/present verbs.
3. **The closest honest analog to product iteration, in product-adjacent language.**
   Neither expects a real software sprint; both accept the classroom/PD feedback loop
   *if* it is framed as a feedback loop (revised on feedback in short cycles) and the
   gap is named honestly, not hidden.
4. **Credentials present but subordinate.** EdD, Dissertation of the Year, and the
   AERA/UCEA record belong at the foot of the page — they help both personas sell the
   hire internally, but leading with them trips Renée's title-chasing peeve and
   Grace's academic-register peeve.
5. **Honest scoping.** Renée wants an honest pivot bridge; Grace wants the age band
   scoped precisely (3-5 / preschool, not a flat 0-6). Candor is rewarded by both;
   overclaiming is punished by both.

Where they diverge: Renée wants the **compliance-audit craft loud and high**; Grace
wants compliance pushed *down* and the **developmental/play/family-facing** content
pulled up. A single unified resume can serve both — compliance stated as craft inside
a consulting entry (high enough for Renée, not in the headline for Grace), with
developmental and family-facing language leading the profile and the current role.
That is the resume the projected version below builds.
