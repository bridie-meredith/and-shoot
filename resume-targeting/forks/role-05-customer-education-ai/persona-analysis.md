# Persona Analysis — Role 5: Customer Education at an AI Company

Two gatekeepers screen Catherine for the Customer / Partner Education Manager (Education segment) role at a Series-C AI company selling AI-platform deployment into enterprise and K-12 district customers. Comp band $130-180k base + equity. Sofia owns the craft of the hire; Marcus holds a revenue-side veto. A candidate Sofia loves but Marcus reads as "a pure teacher" does not get an offer — so Catherine must clear both bars.

---

## Persona 1 — Sofia Mendel, Head of Customer Education (Education Segment)

**Who she is.** The hiring manager standing up a brand-new K-12 field-education team. She does the first-pass skim herself — 8-15 seconds per resume — because the role is too rare to delegate to a recruiter keyword filter. Her core fear: hiring a brilliant educator who turns out to be an "AI tourist" and learns the product on customers' time.

### What Sofia is looking for — the bar for a YES

A YES means: *this person can stand in front of a skeptical district leadership team, demo our AI product live without flinching, answer a hostile compliance question accurately, make the room trust them — and they already understand AI well enough that I won't spend a quarter teaching them what a system prompt is.* A YES is **dual-fluency proven, not claimed** — one foot credibly in education, one foot credibly in AI, both load-bearing. The single strongest positive signal is a real, watchable public-speaking track record with named venues; the rarest valuable credential is the ability to lead the K-12 compliance/IDEA/SpEd conversation that almost no AI-side candidate can survive.

### Instant-reject triggers

- **AI tourism** (blocker): gestures at AI — "passionate about emerging technology," "AI-curious" — with zero artifact, zero product fluency, zero evidence.
- No AI signal anywhere on the page; resume reads as a teaching CV with a tech cover letter stapled on.
- Speaking experience claimed but never substantiated (no named venue, no audience numbers).
- Pure K-12 district-org-chart language with no translation to customer/tech-velocity context.
- A wall of duties with no scale, no audience, no outcome; "thought leadership" with nothing published.

### Top questions Sofia will be asking while screening Catherine

1. **Does this person credibly touch BOTH AI and education, or just one?** — Her first-8-seconds test; if the top third reads "education only," she needs a strong reason within five seconds not to move on.
2. **Has she actually touched the technology with her hands — used, taught, or deployed an AI tool?** — The blocker the whole screen exists to catch. She wants concrete power-user evidence in the top third (Claude, ChatGPT, MagicSchool, Brisk, NotebookLM; an AI-literacy PD session; a district AI-policy engagement).
3. **Is there a public AI artifact I can click and watch her think about AI?** — She *will* click. A Substack/LinkedIn article series on AI + special education, or an AI-themed conference session. "Right now there is nothing to click" caps her at MAYBE-to-YES instead of fight-for-her YES.
4. **Can she hold a webinar audience and survive a hostile live Q&A?** — Closed-room teaching ≠ field education. She needs the *stage* proven, with named venues (AERA, UCEA, panels) and audience headcounts.
5. **Can she lead the district compliance/accessibility conversation that every K-12 AI deployment hinges on?** — IEP/504/IDEA, FERPA/COPPA, accessibility. She wants this framed as a *customer-facing asset* ("can lead the district compliance conversation"), not a buried classroom duty.
6. **Does the resume open with a bridging positioning sentence that does the dual-fluency work for me?** — If she has to assemble the bridge herself from scattered bullets, she usually won't.
7. **Has she sold and delivered to districts already — walked into rooms cold, managed multi-site stakeholders?** — The daily texture of the job; she looks for consulting/client-facing history.
8. **Are the speaking claims substantiated with named venues and numbers?** — Vague "presented at conferences" reads as inexperience or inflation.
9. **Is the AI claim honest, or inflated?** — She trusts a rough, modest public artifact over a polished overclaim; "AI strategist" would read worse than honest silence.
10. **If I ask "walk me through an AI PD session you ran," is there a real one behind the bullet?** — The resume must not write a check the interview cannot cash.

## Persona 2 — Marcus Hale, VP of Go-To-Market / Customer Success (Education Segment)

**Who he is.** The revenue-side co-signer. He runs GTM and CS for the education vertical; the role reports dotted-line to him. He gets 6-8 resumes the night before a calibration call and spends ~8 minutes each, fast. He is not grading instructional-design polish — he is pattern-matching for **commercial instinct**: has this person ever been measured on whether a customer kept paying, expanded, or renewed?

### What Marcus is looking for — the bar for a YES

A YES means: *I believe this person understands the customer relationship is a revenue relationship, can sit across from an enterprise or district buyer without flinching, and treats education as a lever on retention and expansion — not as a classroom they happen to run inside a software company.* He'll trade teaching polish for commercial instinct every time. He wants a retention or expansion number the candidate owns, evidence of holding a paying institutional customer, buyer-level stakeholder management, scaled/repeatable program design, plain AI product fluency, and self-direction with a P&L flavor (ran a practice, set rates, sold the work).

### Instant-reject triggers

- **No commercial framing anywhere** (blocker): the whole resume is teaching, curriculum, and credentials with not one mention of adoption, retention, renewal, revenue, account health, or customer outcomes.
- Zero numbers anywhere; a pure teaching CV with course lists and standards.
- No evidence the person has ever faced an external paying customer.
- Reads as a teacher, not an account-protector — "taught/instructed/facilitated" with no customer relationship on the hook.
- Mission language standing in for results; credential stacking with no measured outcome.

### Top questions Marcus will be asking while screening Catherine

1. **Does the positioning line speak to customers and outcomes, or only to students and pedagogy?** — His first scan-order test; he wants buyer language ("adoption," "customers"), not classroom language.
2. **Has this person ever been measured on whether a customer kept paying, expanded, or renewed?** — The core commercial-instinct test.
3. **Where are the digits — adoption, retention, account count, revenue, engagement lift?** — He sweeps for any percentage or dollar figure tied to an owned outcome. "30+ engagements" is volume, not outcome; he wants a current outcome metric.
4. **Has she held a relationship with a paying, demanding institutional customer across multiple stakeholders?** — Managing districts as *clients*, not just teaching in one. He reads multi-site compliance-audit work as exactly this muscle.
5. **Is the consulting practice a client business, with renewals visible?** — He needs the word "retention/renewal" and the pattern: engagement counts, repeat clients, renewed contract cycles stated as integers ("renewed across 3 OSPI contract cycles").
6. **Does she work at buyer altitude — superintendents, SpEd directors, procurement, cabinet?** — Tells him she can survive an institutional sales cycle and account politics.
7. **Is her program design scaled and repeatable (train-the-trainer, certification, cohort) rather than bespoke 1:1?** — Scale signals she thinks about cost-to-serve.
8. **Can she demo the AI product to a buyer and answer "how do we deploy this" without freezing?** — He doesn't need an engineer; he needs named tools and use cases. A customer-education hire who can't demo is a liability in front of a buyer.
9. **Is the most recent role going to mislead me — does she read as a teacher or an operator?** — The current "Special Education Teacher" line title is a yellow flag; he reads section order as priority.
10. **Does the word "proven" earn its place, or is it credential-stacking?** — "Proven institutional-client track record" implies measured results; he will test it and wants a number to back it or softer language.

## Synthesis — What the two personas jointly demand

Sofia and Marcus screen the **same candidate from two angles**, and their demands overlap on three non-negotiables and diverge on one each.

**Joint non-negotiables (both must see):**
- **Demonstrated AI product fluency, made visible in the top third.** This was the round-1 central blocker for both. It must be concrete, named, and honest — named tools used in real work, a named course, a real delivered AI PD session — not an inflated "AI strategist" claim, which both would penalize harder than silence.
- **The compliance/SpEd credential reframed as a customer-facing AI asset.** Both want "can lead the district compliance conversation that stalls AI rollouts," not a buried classroom audit duty. It is the credential almost no AI-side candidate has, and the reason either picks up the phone.
- **A bridging positioning line at the very top** that does the dual-fluency work in one sentence, in adoption/outcome language.

**Where they diverge — and what closes each:**
- **Sofia needs one clickable, public AI artifact.** She *will* click; without it she caps at MAYBE-to-YES. A short LinkedIn article or 3-post Substack series — e.g. "Five Compliance Questions Districts Must Ask Before Buying AI Tools" — converts her to a calendar invite.
- **Marcus needs current outcome digits tied to owned results.** He has retention *language* but no retention *numbers*; he wants integers — educators trained at scale, sites served, repeat-client ratio, renewed contract cycles. Digits move him from BORDERLINE to YES.

**The combined bar:** Catherine's education-side profile (speaking, compliance, client-facing consulting) is already a YES for both. The hire is won or lost on proving — visibly, in eight seconds, with something clickable and something countable — that the AI half of the bridge is real and that her consulting is a measured client business, not a teaching practice. Close both gaps and she is one of the few candidates both screeners would fight to interview; leave either open and the strongest SpEd resume in the stack still gets capped a tier below the top pile.
