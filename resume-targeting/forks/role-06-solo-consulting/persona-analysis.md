# Role 6 — Solo Consulting to Districts: Buyer Analysis

These three personas are district **buyers**, not hiring managers. They screen Catherine's
consultant one-pager / capabilities sheet / proposal under time pressure, looking for a
reason to discard. A "yes" is a purchase decision with political and professional skin in
the game. The analysis below grounds every buyer question in the persona card content.

---

## Buyer 1 — Renata Okafor, Director of Special Education (mid-size suburban district)

Renata buys in three bands: workshops ($3-8k, the audition tier she can self-approve),
projects ($15-75k, requiring a PO and a paper trail), and retainers ($3-8k/month, converted
into after a project lands). She is personally on the hook for an Ohio-style AI-policy
mandate and a federal-monitoring corrective-action plan, with one line item and one quarter
to spend. She skims a stack of one-pagers in under a minute and shortlists 3, calls 2,
hires 1.

### The questions she is asking

1. **Does the headline name *my* exact problem?** She is hunting for "special education,"
   "compliance," and "AI" in the same line, near each other, in the first 8 seconds — not
   "AI in education."
2. **Has this consultant actually sat where I sit?** Real IEP/IDEA compliance-audit work,
   multi-site, state-agency-adjacent (OSPI/ESC/state monitoring). This is the single
   strongest signal she will protect the district rather than produce a deck.
3. **Can I picture the deliverable?** Does the page name concrete procurement nouns — "a
   compliance audit report, a prioritized findings list, a board-ready summary" — or does it
   hide behind transformation verbs (empower, reimagine, transform)?
4. **Will this person embarrass me in front of staff or auditors?** Does she use IEP, 504,
   IDEA Part B, LRE, "least restrictive environment," "corrective action," "evidence of
   fidelity" correctly — or as costume?
5. **Does the most recent line title match the seniority claim?** A profile arguing
   senior-consultant expertise while the latest role still reads "classroom teacher" forces
   *her* to do the explaining to a skeptical board. Consultants who make her explain lose.
6. **Is there AI *risk* literacy, not just AI hype?** Any mention of student-data privacy,
   FERPA, bias against disabled students, IDEA exposure — or is it all-upside? All-hype reads
   as a liability she'd be importing into her department.
7. **Does she understand neurodivergent learners specifically — and what AI gets *wrong* for
   them?** Autism, PDA, complex profiles. A point of view on AI failure modes for her
   highest-need kids proves she isn't selling the same slide deck to everyone.
8. **Are the rates in my bands, and is there a warm path?** Workshop/project/retainer
   ranges so the first call is scoping, not negotiation; plus a referral, shared conference,
   or ESC connection that de-risks her.

### The bar for a YES (booking her)

A clear niche line that is *her exact problem* + at least one piece of real compliance/audit
proof + at least one concrete deliverable named in plain English. Three things. A referral
line on top of those three moves her from "call" to "call first."

### Instant-reject triggers

- Generic "AI in education" framing with zero SpEd-specific content.
- Compliance vocabulary used wrong (IEP/504 conflated, IDEA cited with no Part).
- All-hype, no-risk AI framing.
- No nameable deliverable anywhere on the page.
- A profile so generalist it could be selling math PD next quarter.

## Buyer 2 — Dr. Eleanor Voss, Deputy Superintendent / Chief of Staff

Eleanor is commissioning a single, specific thing: a formal, board-adoptable **whole-district
AI policy** — acceptable use for all staff and students, vendor/data-privacy standards,
academic-integrity provisions, accessibility/SpEd obligations, and a teacher-training rollout
plan. A 3-6 month, $25-75k engagement. She is a forced buyer on a state-mandate clock. She
rejects two-thirds of inbound in 8 seconds and works a shortlist of 3-5. A "yes" puts her
personal political capital on the consultant for two quarters.

### The questions she is asking

1. **Has this consultant authored a board-adopted policy before?** A named district + named
   governing body + an adoption outcome ("AI policy adopted by X Unified's board, 6-1"). This
   is her single hardest screen.
2. **Does the page say *policy and governance*, or *training and workshops*?** Training
   language gets parked for the PD line; only policy/governance language survives for *this*
   engagement.
3. **Can this person author the *whole-district* document, not just the SpEd chapter?** A
   specialist who frames the engagement entirely through one program lens reads as the wrong
   author for a policy that has to cover everyone.
4. **Does she speak governance fluently?** Board policy vs. administrative regulation,
   first/second reading, public-comment period, union consultation, work session — the
   vocabulary that flips her read from *vendor* to *peer*.
5. **Is there a defensibility frame?** Is the policy built to survive a board member, a
   parent FOIA request, a journalist, and a state compliance reviewer — her actual risk
   surface — or is it merely pedagogically sound?
6. **Is there a concrete stakeholder process for a *divided* board?** Her board has factions
   (ban-AI vs. lead-on-innovation), a worried parent group, and a union. Pedagogy without
   politics tells her she'll absorb every political hit.
7. **Does the proposal name *my* state's actual mandate and deadline?** Citing WA's specific
   AI guidance/timeline signals "written for me," not blasted to fifty districts.
8. **Is there a fixed-fee, phased, dated two-page scope and three callable references?**
   Something she can drop straight into a board consent agenda; references at peer level
   (deputy superintendents, board members), named up front, not "available on request."
9. **Is the author visibly independent?** A consultant who also resells or is sponsored by
   an AI product cannot write a vendor-neutral policy.

### The bar for a YES (top-of-band engagement)

One named board-adopted policy + governance fluency + WA's mandate cited correctly + a clean
fixed-fee two-page scope + three dialable references. Hit four of those five and her
assistant emails to set up a call. Note: she picks the consultant who makes her look
*prepared*, not the most exciting one.

### Instant-reject triggers

- Hype vocabulary ("revolutionary," "AI transformation," "future-ready") in the first
  paragraph.
- No board-adopted policy artifact anywhere on the page.
- An obviously mass-blasted generic deck with no district named.
- A consultant who also sells an AI product (vendor entanglement).
- Pedagogy/research with no adoption-ready output; open-ended hourly billing.

## Buyer 3 — Joanne Pruitt, Building Principal (540-student Title I elementary, WA)

Joanne runs a building, not a program or a board. She commissions building-level PD in the
$3-8k band: a full-faculty staff PD day, a train-the-trainer for 6-10 coaches, or a half-day
/ after-school series. She treats every staff-development day as a non-renewable resource
she must defend to a tired, skeptical faculty. She also functions as a **referral node** —
a good workshop in her building travels up to the SpEd director and the deputy super. She
skims fast, often on her phone, and wants to make exactly one call before committing.

### The questions she is asking

1. **Does this person teach, or did they recently?** Her eye goes straight to experience.
   "17 years in the classroom" / "special education teacher" lands hard; "EdD,"
   "researcher," "strategist" alone makes her tense.
2. **Is the offer *my size*?** Is there a staff day / workshop / train-the-trainer in the
   $3-8k band — or is everything multi-month district transformation she has no door into?
3. **What do my teachers walk out *holding*?** A concrete deliverable — a protocol, a
   template, a toolkit, three things they use next week — not a topic or a "session on."
4. **Can I picture this in my cafeteria?** Does the language sound like a person who has
   stood in front of skeptical teachers, or like a brochure? Any signal about pacing,
   respecting teacher time, no death-by-slides.
5. **If it's an AI session — is it concrete enough for Monday?** Named teacher-facing tools,
   examples, tied to real pain (IEP paperwork, behavior data, differentiation for the SpEd
   caseload) — not "AI integration strategy" or future-of-education.
6. **Has she actually delivered *this* content to a teacher audience?** On the verification
   call she asks "tell me the hardest twenty minutes of this PD and how you handle it" and
   "which three tools, exactly, and have you run this session before?"
7. **Can I verify a referral locally?** A named WA district or principal she can call; a
   colleague's word outweighs every credential.
8. **Will this consultant respect — not "fix" — my staff?** Any whiff of "teacher buy-in
   problems" or condescension and she protects her faculty over the consultant.

### The bar for a YES (booking the staff day)

Practitioner credibility up top + a niche she actually needs + a right-sized offer with a
named, concrete deliverable. That combination gets her on the phone — where she tests one
thing: can the consultant describe the hardest twenty minutes of the PD and how they handle
it.

### Instant-reject triggers

- Pure academic framing with the classroom invisible.
- All keynote/inspiration, no Monday takeaway.
- Jargon she'd have to translate for her own faculty.
- Only big-ticket ($25-75k) engagements on offer — wrong door.
- Any line that treats teachers as the problem.

## Synthesis — what the three buyers jointly demand

All three are time-pressured, defensive skim-readers making a *purchase* with personal
political/professional exposure. Across the three, the joint demands are:

1. **A niche stated in the first 8 seconds, fused not scattered.** Generalist "AI in
   education" framing is an instant-reject for all three. SpEd + compliance + AI, named and
   adjacent, is the escape from the flood.
2. **Concrete deliverables in plain nouns.** Each buyer needs to picture what they are
   buying — a board-ready audit summary (Renata), a board-adoptable policy + administrative
   regulation (Eleanor), three-tools-Monday + protocols/templates (Joanne). Transformation
   verbs fail everywhere.
3. **Proof of finished work, not credentials alone.** Real engagements that shipped:
   compliance audits (Renata), a board-adopted policy (Eleanor), a delivered AI-tools
   session and a verifiable local reference (Joanne). The EdD/dissertation/AERA record is
   *cover* and *depth underneath* — never the headline.
4. **Right-sized scope and visible rates.** Each must see their own door and their own band
   without doing arithmetic or enduring an awkward negotiation call.
5. **Verifiable references and a warm path.** Named, callable references de-risk the spend
   for all three; a referral moves a consultant to the front of the line.

**The unavoidable divergence — ordering.** The same page cannot lead with two identities.
Leading with *special-education compliance* wins Renata and Joanne (their world) but keeps
Eleanor pattern-matching to "program specialist." The resolution the buyers' feedback
converges on: keep the compliance spine, but add an explicit *whole-district* clause so
Eleanor sees a whole-district author whose SpEd depth is the differentiator on the hardest
section — not a specialist limited to it. The deeper divergence is **artifact readiness**:
Renata buys on audit proof (Catherine has it), Joanne on practitioner credibility + a
delivered session (closeable), Eleanor on a *named board-adopted policy* (an operational
gap no copy can write around). Eleanor stays a scoping-call / co-author buyer until that
artifact exists.
