# Role 2 — Frontier-Lab Full-Time — Persona Analysis

Three gatekeepers screen Catherine for scarce education-flavored ICs at a frontier AI lab (evals, human data, education Trust & Safety). 10-30 such seats exist across all labs; each req draws hundreds of applicants. Below: the questions each persona asks, the bar for a YES, the instant-reject triggers.

---

## 1. Maya Trent — Technical Recruiter (first-pass cold skim)

Maya owns first-pass screening. She gives a cold application an 8-second skim of the top third of page one. She is not a domain expert; she screens on *proxies* — artifacts, named institutions, retargeting signal. A "yes" from her is permission to spend a hiring manager's 30 minutes, and she is judged on whether managers respect her forwards.

### Top questions Maya asks
1. **"What's the artifact?"** — Is there a link I can click that proves this person actually engaged with AI, not just claims interest?
2. Does the headline/positioning line tell me, in one read, which req family this is aimed at — or is it generic?
3. Does the top third name the req's nouns (evals, model behavior, human data, rubric, policy, trust & safety, accessibility)?
4. Was this resume *retargeted* to this job, or sprayed at the whole industry?
5. Is there a rare, legible domain I can hand a hiring manager in one phrase?
6. Is there a warm-intro tag / referral in the system? (Her single biggest multiplier.)
7. The doctorate sits over a classroom-teacher line title — is that mismatch bridged, or does it make me stall and guess?
8. Is the resume written in a vocabulary I recognize, or is it K-12/district private language I have to decode?
9. If I click the artifact, does it hold up — or is it thin SEO filler that reverses the gain?

### Bar for a YES
A clickable AI artifact above the fold + a one-read positioning line aimed at the req + a rare named domain. That earns a *second pass* (full read). A clean *forward* additionally needs a warm referral — without it, even a strong cold resume only gets "second pass, soft-no."

### Instant-reject triggers
- Pure hype headline ("passionate about AI") with no linkable evidence.
- Resume in another industry's private vocabulary with zero translation.
- Cold apply to a scarce req with no artifact AND no referral AND no retargeting (practical blocker).
- Unbridged doctorate-over-junior-title mismatch on a cold app → "park it."

## 2. Devang Rao — Evals Lead, Model Behavior / Education (technical shortlist read)

Devang owns the education-evals pod. He reads 8-12 shortlisted resumes himself, between meetings. He is the only non-CS person on his pod and is protective of the education hire: he needs someone who survives a room of researchers without him translating, and who makes the pod *more* rigorous. He is hiring exactly one. His culture is document-driven — "show me the spec."

### Top questions Devang asks
1. **"Give me the eval, not the philosophy"** — is there a clickable artifact proving she has *already thought in writing* about AI and her domain?
2. Does any bullet contain *framework / rubric / spec / audit / standard*? (Compliance auditing reads to him as eval design.)
3. Is the domain genuinely *scarce* — special education, IDEA, complex autism, accessibility — or undifferentiated "AI in education" generalism?
4. Has she built a repeatable, criteria-based *instrument*, or just performed duties?
5. What's the failure mode / edge case she audited for — can she operationalize an ambiguous behavioral question?
6. Are the bullets construction verbs (designed, authored, audited, built) or duty verbs ("responsible for")?
7. Has she survived cross-functional rooms with people who speak a different professional language (lawyers, researchers, district counsel)?
8. Is the AI claim *honest and verifiable* — has she actually used the tools — or is it "familiar with AI" with nothing to click? (His blocker.)
9. Is there an *eval-flavored* artifact specifically — a rubric or test sketch — not just a policy listicle?
10. Do scope numbers (multi-site, 60-person cohorts, 17 years) show she operated a system, not just a classroom?

### Bar for a YES
A clickable artifact + the phrase "compliance audit framework" (or equivalent instrument language) in the first third + a plainly stated narrow domain. If he can defend hiring a non-engineer to a room of engineers because she reads as *the rigorous kind* of education expert, it is a yes. An *eval-flavored* second artifact would make the yes unarguable for his specific req.

### Instant-reject triggers
- Summary that is all mission, no instrument.
- "AI" claimed with nothing to verify (blocker — reads as a tell she hasn't used the tools).
- Resume that is entirely K-12 classroom duties with no systems-level or assessment-design work.
- Adjective inflation ("transformational," "innovative," "passionate") — each lowers trust.
- Credential pile with duty verbs and no built things.

## 3. Naomi Feldman — T&S / Education Policy Lead (hiring manager, twice-read)

Naomi runs the youth-and-education slice of Trust & Safety. She hires exactly one person to own K-12 product posture, minor-data practices, accessibility commitments, and the company's face to education regulators. It is a writing job and a meeting job in equal measure. She reads every shortlist resume twice. The resume's only job: make her believe the writing exercise won't waste her panel's afternoon.

### Top questions Naomi asks
1. **"Where is the noun that is a document?"** — has she authored a binding policy, standard, protocol, or framework an organization had to follow?
2. Does a regulatory acronym (IDEA, FERPA, COPPA, 504) appear *in context* — showing she knows what the statute *demands*, not just that it exists?
3. Can she show a *stakeholder fight with its seams visible* — competing interests and the defensible outcome she produced between them?
4. Has she thought about *technology / AI / data as a policy surface* — or is this a domain expert applying into a policy role?
5. Can she hold a *competing constituency* in the same hand, or is she a single-constituency advocate (a liability in a stakeholder room)?
6. Does she have *specific* child-development depth — can she reason about how a 7-, 12-, and 16-year-old differ for age-appropriateness?
7. Is accessibility treated as a first-class concern, or buried as a footnote? (A real hole on her team.)
8. Is the writing itself tight, concrete, no padding — does the resume pass as a writing sample?
9. Does each line describe a *decision* she made, or a *duty* she held?
10. If I click the artifact, is it a real policy memo with judgment — a regulatory requirement set against a competing interest — or a thin listicle?

### Bar for a YES (advance to the writing exercise)
A named binding artifact in the first two roles + regulatory fluency stated as a working tool + at least one visible stakeholder seam + one AI/tech-policy bridge (an artifact showing she has crossed into policy thinking about technology) + disciplined writing. Clear that, and she advances Catherine to the one-page writing exercise and the Legal/Product/Comms panel.

### Instant-reject triggers
- No artifact — roles and responsibilities listed, no document ever named (blocker).
- Buzzword regulation ("ensured FERPA compliance") with nothing showing she knows what compliance required.
- Pure advocacy voice — only ever argues for one constituency.
- Zero tech-policy proximity — education credentials alone read as domain expert, not policy hire.
- Every bullet starts with "responsible for"; a one-pager of credentials and awards with no work shown.

## Synthesis — what the three jointly demand

The three personas converge on **one diagnosis and three demands**.

**Shared diagnosis:** Catherine has genuine, scarce domain substance (IEP/IDEA compliance auditing, complex-autism/PDA depth, EdD-level rigor). The risk has never been substance — it is *packaging built for a district hiring panel* and *the absence of AI evidence*.

**The three joint demands:**

1. **A clickable AI artifact, above the fold.** All three hunt for a link in the first 8 seconds. For Maya it converts "park it" to "second pass"; for Devang it is close to a blocker if absent; for Naomi it is the bridge that converts "adjacent" to "on-target." The current resume has one (the LinkedIn AI-vendor-compliance piece). The shared dependency is that **it must be genuinely substantive** — every screener clicks it, Naomi's bar is highest, and a thin listicle reverses the gain into a credibility hit.

2. **Audit work named as an authored instrument, not a duty.** "Authored a multi-site IEP/504/IDEA compliance audit protocol" satisfies Devang ("reads like designed an eval suite") and Naomi ("a document-noun with judgment in it") simultaneously. Construction verbs, traceable criteria, statutory translation.

3. **A positioning line legible to all three at once** — recruiter, engineer, lawyer. Not "leadership expert." A compliance/auditor/enforceable-practice framing with the req's nouns reachable in two lines.

**Where they diverge:** Maya alone cannot reach a clean forward on a cold app — she needs a **warm referral** (her single biggest multiplier). Devang wants a second, **eval-flavored** artifact (a rubric/test sketch) to make his yes unarguable for *his* req specifically. Naomi will accept a policy-flavored artifact but will **probe AI/tech-policy depth hard** in the writing exercise — a true-state limitation the resume cannot paper over.

**Net:** the resume substance clears the bar for two of three (Devang, Naomi ADVANCE; Maya "second pass"). The remaining work is not resume editing — it is (a) ensuring the artifact is real, (b) deepening AI/tech-policy evidence with a second eval-flavored piece, and (c) securing a warm intro.
