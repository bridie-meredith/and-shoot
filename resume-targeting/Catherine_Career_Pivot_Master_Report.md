### Market context — the three converging trends, and what works against her

Three trends are converging right now that make this an unusually good moment for Catherine specifically:

1. **State AI mandates are creating forced-buyer markets.** Thirty-five states plus Puerto Rico now have official AI guidance for education. Ohio mandated district AI policies by July 2026; California's AB 2876 requires AI literacy in K-12 curriculum. Districts must comply but most have no internal expertise — a 2-3 year window where consulting demand outstrips supply.
2. **Frontier labs are spending billions on human training data.** Mercor pays out roughly $1.5M/day to contractors and carries 30,000-plus experts. The bottleneck is no longer compute; it is high-quality expert-generated data. Education and child development are explicitly named domains.
3. **Special education is structurally underserved by ed-tech.** SpEd students are 14% of US public-school enrollment but capture less than 5% of edtech R&D dollars. AI changes this, but the people building those products mostly do not have SpEd backgrounds. There is a real talent shortage.

**Working against her:** public-education hiring is slow and conservative (the experience she already lived); many edtech companies are still recovering from the post-pandemic correction; and generalist "AI in education" consultants are flooding the market — she must differentiate fast.
