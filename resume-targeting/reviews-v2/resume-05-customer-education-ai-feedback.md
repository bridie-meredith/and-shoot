# Resume 05 — Customer Education at an AI Company — Critic Feedback (Round 2)

This is the second-round review of the revised resume. Round 1 produced a clear, unanimous verdict: the education-side profile was already strong (speaking, compliance, client-facing consulting), but the resume failed both screeners on a single hard blocker — no demonstrated AI product fluency — and failed Marcus additionally on commercial framing. The revision was instructed to assume Catherine has taken one modest, credible AI step: completing a "Generative AI for Educators" (Google/ISTE) PD course and routinely using Claude and ChatGPT in her consulting and PD-design workflow. No deeper AI experience. The question for this round is whether the revision closes the AI-fluency blocker *credibly* — enough to flip the screen — without overclaiming a fluency Catherine does not have.

## Screener: Sofia Mendel (ai-co-customer-education-lead)

**Verdict: ADVANCE — moves to the MAYBE-to-YES pile; she reaches for a second pass.**

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.**
My eyes go to the tagline. *"K-12 customer & partner education | Solving the compliance and SpEd objections that stall AI rollouts."* Good — that bridges, and "AI rollouts" is in the first line. It's in the top third. It's specific — a named course, named tools, a named use context. It is not "passionate about emerging technology." This is no longer the resume that confirms my fear. It's the resume of an educator who has at least touched the technology with her hands. I keep reading, and I'm reading to advance, not to reject.

**Strengths Sofia sees.**
The speaking track record is still the single strongest card in my stack and it survives intact — named venues, real conferences, Dissertation of the Year, guest lectures. The compliance/SpEd depth is now framed exactly as I need it: the PROFILE line *"One of a small number of candidates who can answer the hostile district question … live, in the room, without flagging a lawyer"* is the customer-facing reframe I asked for in round 1, and the first bullet under WHY I AM EFFECTIVE states it as a claim — *"the objections that kill product rollouts."* That is the rare credential surfaced as an asset, not buried as a duty. The consulting history reads as client-facing, walk-in-cold work. And the AI line — modest as it is — is honest. It says "PD course" and "uses tools routinely," not "AI strategist" or "led AI transformation." I trust an honest modest claim far more than an inflated one; an inflated AI line would have been a worse outcome than the round-1 silence.

**Round-1 risks — resolved / remaining / new.**
- *RESOLVED — "no AI product fluency anywhere on the page."* The blocker that sat in my NO pile last cycle is addressed. The PROFILE names a course and two tools; the Olver Consulting role says *"Design and deliver AI-literacy PD for educators using Claude and ChatGPT; advise on responsible AI use under FERPA and COPPA"* and *"Completed Generative AI for Educators (Google/ISTE, 2026); use AI tools daily to draft, summarize source documents, and stress-test rubrics."* That is hands-on, named, and placed where I look. It clears the blocker for me.
- *RESOLVED — compliance angle buried as a job duty.* Now stated in the PROFILE and as the lead WHY bullet, in customer-facing language.
- *RESOLVED — positioning line with no AI in it.* The tagline and PROFILE now both bridge.
- *REMAINS — no public, clickable AI artifact.* This is the one round-1 ask the revision does not satisfy. I said in round 1: "Sofia will click it; right now there is nothing to click." There is still nothing to click. Catherine *delivers* AI PD and has *taken* a course, but there is no published piece — no Substack, no LinkedIn article, no AI-themed conference session — that lets me watch her think about AI in public. For most candidates that would still be fatal. For Catherine specifically, the watchable education-side speaking record plus the now-credible hands-on AI line is enough to get her to a real second pass and likely an interview. But it caps her: she is a confident MAYBE-to-YES, not a fight-for-her YES. The artifact is what would close that last gap.

**New problems the revision introduced.**

Today is May 2026, so a 2026 completion is plausible — but a credential dated the same year as the application can read as just-in-time résumé-padding to a skeptical screener. It is honest and I would not reject on it, but it is visible.
- *"AI-literacy PD … using Claude and ChatGPT" is doing a lot of work.* It is credible given the assumed facts, but it sits one verb away from overclaim. It currently survives because the surrounding language stays modest. Catherine must be able to back this live in interview — if I ask "walk me through a session you ran," there has to be a real one. The resume now writes a check the interview has to cash.

**Specific further fixes (Sofia).**
1. *Add one clickable AI artifact and link it.* This is the single highest-value remaining move for my screen. One LinkedIn article or short Substack series — e.g. "Five Compliance Questions Districts Must Ask Before Buying AI Tools," which is directly in her wheelhouse — converts her from "MAYBE-to-YES" to "calendar." It does not need to be deep; it needs to exist and be watchable.
2. *Name the AI-literacy PD with a number.* "Design and deliver AI-literacy PD" — for how many educators, across how many sites? "Delivered AI-literacy PD to N educators across M sites" turns a claim into a proven session and pre-empts my interview question.
3. *If any AERA 2026 session touches AI, say so in the session title line.* That single edit upgrades the speaking record from education-only to dual-fluency. If none do, leave it — do not invent it.

## Synthesis

**Where Sofia and Marcus agree.**
Both screeners agree the revision is a genuine, material improvement and that the round-1 *central* blocker — no AI product fluency — is now cleared at the level each of them screens at. Neither would instant-reject this resume; both would read it through. Both also agree the revision handled the AI gap the right way: it is modest, named, and honest, and an inflated AI claim would have read worse than the round-1 silence. The compliance-as-customer-asset reframe and the bridging positioning line satisfy both. The resume has moved decisively off the NO pile.

**Where they diverge.**
They split on what still holds Catherine back, and the split is the same one round 1 predicted. Sofia's remaining gap is the *public AI artifact* — she will click, and there is still nothing to click; that caps her at a strong MAYBE-to-YES rather than a fight-for-her YES. Marcus does not care about an artifact at all — he is held back instead by the *absence of current outcome metrics*: the revision gave him retention language but withheld retention numbers, and he reads "30+ engagements" as volume, not outcome. So the two screeners now want two different things to get to a clean YES: Sofia wants one clickable AI piece; Marcus wants two or three digits tied to owned results. Neither blocker is fatal — both screeners advance her past reject — but each caps her at one tier below their top pile.

**Is it submission-ready?**
Close, but not quite. The resume is now *submittable* — it would survive both screens and likely earn interviews, which it would not have done in round 1. But two cheap, high-leverage edits would convert "survives the screen" into "wins the screen," and both are achievable without any new experience or any overclaim.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits still worth making.**

1. **Add one clickable, public AI artifact and link it.** This is the single edit that closes Sofia's last gap. One LinkedIn article or a short 3-post Substack series — "Five Compliance Questions Districts Must Ask Before Buying AI Tools" is directly in Catherine's expertise and requires no new skill — gives Sofia something to click and converts her from MAYBE-to-YES into a calendar invite. It is also the lowest-effort item on this list relative to its payoff.

2. **Inject two or three current outcome digits into the consulting and OSPI blocks.** This closes Marcus's last gap. Replace every vague "multiple" with an integer: "renewed across [N] OSPI contract cycles," "AI-literacy PD delivered to [N] educators across [M] sites," "[N] repeat district clients." The facts exist; they are simply not yet written as numbers. This is the difference between Marcus's BORDERLINE and Marcus's YES.

3. **Tag the AI-literacy PD with scale, and tag an AERA session with AI if one qualifies.** Naming a headcount on the AI-literacy PD bullet serves both screeners at once — it gives Marcus a digit and gives Sofia proof the PD is a real delivered session rather than a résumé line, pre-empting her interview question. If either AERA 2026 session genuinely touched AI, saying so upgrades the speaking record from education-only to dual-fluency. Do not invent an AI session that did not happen — the credibility of the whole modest-AI-step framing depends on it staying honest.

**On overclaim.**
The revision closes the AI-fluency gap *credibly* and does not overclaim — the language stays at "hands-on user," "completed a PD course," "uses tools routinely," which is exactly the modest, true claim the assumed facts support. The two watch-items are the same-year (2026) credential date, which is honest but visible, and the phrase "AI-literacy PD using Claude and ChatGPT," which is credible only if a real session exists behind it. As long as Catherine can speak to an actual delivered session in interview, the resume is honest. The instruction to take "one modest, credible step" was executed correctly: the resume reads as an educator who has genuinely touched the technology, not as an AI tourist and not as an inflated AI expert.
