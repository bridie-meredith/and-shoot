# Resume 03 — Edtech Learning Scientist — Critic Feedback (Round 2)

This is the second-round review of the revised resume. The revision did three substantive things: (1) retargeted the headline from "learning scientist" toward **Learning Experience Designer**, with SpEd and early-childhood edtech named in the tagline; (2) restructured the document around a "What I Bring to a Learning-Design Team" block that leads with domain depth and replicable artifacts rather than the EdD; (3) added a modest, honest AI-familiarity line — a completed "Generative AI for Educators" PD course plus routine ChatGPT/Claude use in consulting workflow. The same three screeners are re-applied below. Each verdict explicitly tracks which round-1 risks are now RESOLVED, which still REMAIN, and any NEW problems the revision introduced.

The headline question this round: **was the "learning scientist" → "learning experience designer" repositioning sound?** Short answer — yes, decisively, for two of the three screeners, and it correctly concedes the third. It is the single best decision in the revision.

**New problems introduced:** None for this screener. The revision is honest in exactly the way round 1 asked for. Line 13's parenthetical ("not causal-inference methods") is a small act of integrity that Hannah would, if anything, respect.

**Bottom line for this screen.** Round 1 said: do not target this variant until a genuine quantitative artifact exists. The revision did not manufacture one — it retargeted instead. That is the right move. Hannah remains a SCREEN-OUT, but the resume is no longer *trying* to beat her, so the screen-out is now a deliberate scoping decision rather than a failure.

## Synthesis

**Where the three screeners agree.** All three agree the revision is a substantial, honest improvement and that the structural round-1 complaint — identity-and-credential framing ahead of role-calibrated craft — is **resolved**. The "What I Bring to a Learning-Design Team" block now front-loads domain depth, replicable artifacts, and iteration instinct; the EdD and Dissertation of the Year have moved to a subordinate research-grounding line and the Education section. No screener's credential-shield / credential-as-pitch / academic-register peeve fires this round. All three also agree the AI-familiarity addition was handled correctly: modest, credible, non-overclaiming — it passes Hannah without inviting scrutiny, and it actively helps Grace.

**Where they diverge.** Hannah remains a SCREEN-OUT; Renée and Grace are both now ADVANCE. The divergence is structural, not fixable, and the revision correctly conceded it: the "learning scientist" → "learning experience designer" retarget moved the resume out of Hannah's req entirely and into the domain-and-design subset where Renée and Grace live. The repositioning was **sound** — it is the single best decision in the revision. Renée and Grace want subtly different things in the *residual* polish (Renée wants quantified audit scope and a one-line cadence-gap acknowledgment; Grace wants the AI line and the Areas block tilted toward developmental/early-childhood language and away from MTSS/ABA), which is the same point round 1 made: a single resume serves both well, but a fully optimized version would fork into a SpEd-edtech variant and an early-childhood variant. As shipped, this one resume is genuinely strong for both — the fork is an optimization, not a requirement.

**Is it submission-ready?** **Yes — for SpEd-focused and early-childhood edtech roles, it is submission-ready now.** Both intended screeners advance it. It should **not** be submitted to general-purpose efficacy-research "learning scientist" reqs (Duolingo/Khan shape) — that is Hannah's door and the resume correctly no longer targets it. The three edits below are upside, not blockers.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits still worth making:**

1. **Quantify the compliance-audit scope.** The lead consulting bullet still says "multiple districts and private schools." Replace "multiple" with a real count ("across 6+ districts..."). For Renée — Catherine's strongest screener — the compliance audit is the single most valuable line on the page, and a quantified scope turns a strong bullet into an unforgettable one. Lowest-effort, highest-return edit on the document.

2. **Add an early-childhood / child-development clause to the AI bullet.** Currently the AI line is framed only around consulting and PD-prep workflow. Extend it (honestly) to name early-learning content or parent-guidance tools — "...exploring how developmental expertise should shape AI-generated early-learning content." This converts a generic currency signal into one aimed straight at Grace's product roadmap, at zero cost to honesty.

3. **Add one honest cadence-gap line to the pivot bridge.** The bridge sells Catherine's strengths well but does not name the one thing she will be learning — product/sprint cadence. Both Renée and Grace explicitly trust candidates who name their own gaps before the screener finds them. One clause ("...and ready to learn the product cadence — roadmap, release cycles, cross-functional iteration — that turns curriculum design into shipped content") pre-empts the only residual interview risk and reads as exactly the honest-pivot maturity both screeners reward.

**On the repositioning, explicitly.** Retargeting the headline from "learning scientist" to "learning experience designer" was the correct and necessary call. "Learning scientist" at a general-purpose edtech implies causal-inference methods and large-dataset fluency Catherine does not have, and round 1 was clear that a resume engineered to clear that bar would fail the take-home. "Learning experience designer," scoped to SpEd and early-childhood edtech, is a title Catherine's actual record lands without overreach — and it is the title both viable screeners are hiring for. The revision turned a one-of-three near-miss into a two-of-three clean advance by being honest about which door it is standing in front of.
