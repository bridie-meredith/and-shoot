# Resume 03 — Edtech Learning Scientist — Critic Feedback (Round 2)

This is the second-round review of the revised resume. The revision made three substantive moves: (1) it retargeted the headline from "learning scientist" toward **Learning Experience Designer**, with SpEd and early-childhood edtech named explicitly in the tagline; (2) it restructured the document around a "What I Bring to a Learning-Design Team" block that leads with domain depth and replicable design artifacts rather than the EdD; (3) it added a modest, honest AI-familiarity line — a completed "Generative AI for Educators" PD course (2026) plus routine ChatGPT/Claude use in consulting and PD-prep work. The same three screeners from round 1 are re-applied below. Each verdict explicitly tracks which round-1 risks are now RESOLVED, which still REMAIN, and any NEW problems the revision introduced.

The headline question this round: **was the "learning scientist" → "learning experience designer" repositioning sound?** The short answer is yes — decisively for two of the three screeners, and it correctly concedes the third rather than pretending to clear a bar Catherine cannot clear. It is the strongest single decision in the revision. The detail is in each section.

## Screener: Hannah Brooks — Senior Manager, Efficacy Research (general-purpose edtech, quantitative bar)

**Verdict: SCREEN-OUT** (unchanged from round 1 — and correctly so)

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.** Hannah's eyes go to the tagline and the "What I Bring" block hunting for a statistical method and a language name. The tagline now says "Learning Experience Designer." She registers that instantly — *this is not an application for my req.* If this resume reaches her at all, it has been mis-routed by a recruiter. Scanning down: "practitioner-research," "qualitative and practitioner-research, not causal-inference methods" (line 13), "AI-in-education literacy" with ChatGPT/Claude usage. No R, no Python, no SQL, no regression, no named dataset, no row count. Eight seconds, done. She does not reach for the CSV take-home; she would not have screened this resume in the first place.

**Strengths she sees.** The EdD and the Dissertation of the Year still register as research stamina and genuine quality. The AERA/UCEA record tells her Catherine can survive peer review. Seventeen years of SpEd domain depth is real and rare. The 60% referral-reduction line (line 25) is now framed as "district outcome data showed a 60% reduction in SpEd referrals vs. prior-year baseline" — better than round 1, because it at least names a comparison ("vs. prior-year baseline"). She still cannot weight any of it: none of it is a quantitative research deliverable in her instrument.

**Round-1 risks — status:**
- **RESOLVED (by concession, not by edit):** The round-1 "credential shield" peeve is materially defused — not because the credentials moved, but because line 13 now states outright that Catherine's record is "qualitative and practitioner-research, not causal-inference methods." Hannah respects the honesty; a candidate who names her own gap before Hannah finds it does not trip the *inflated-tool-claim* blocker. The round-1 "data-informed practice" pet-peeve trigger is also RESOLVED — that phrase and the vague "outcome measurement" bullet are gone entirely.
- **REMAINS (and is unfixable):** The core gap. No R/Python, no large-dataset analysis, no experimental or causal-inference work. The public posting names programming and large-dataset analysis as a *requirement, not a preference*. This is the instant-reject condition and no resume edit touches it. The 60% line still has no methodological frame — no n, no design, no analyst named — so it remains a practitioner outcome, not a study.
- **NEW problem:** None of consequence. The revision did not manufacture any false quantitative claim, which is the most important thing it could have gotten right against this screener. The "Research grounding for data-leaning teams" bullet (line 13) is the one mild risk — the phrase "for data-leaning teams" flirts with implying quantitative fit — but the same bullet immediately disowns causal-inference methods, so it lands as honest scoping rather than overreach.

**Remaining screen-out risk.** Total, on this specific req — but this is by design. The revision's retarget away from "learning scientist" is the correct response to round 1's blunt verdict: the resume should not be engineered to beat Hannah, because a candidate who slipped past her would fail the take-home harder. The revision honors that.

**Specific further fixes.** None for Hannah's screen — and that is the right call. The only "fix" is the targeting decision the revision already made: do not send this resume to a general-purpose efficacy-research req. If Catherine ever wants this door, the fix is off-resume — one genuine quantitative artifact (a cleaned dataset, a regression, a pre-registered pilot with a comparison condition), at which point the resume gets rewritten, not patched. Hannah's section of this review now exists only to confirm that the retarget was sound: it was.

## Screener: Renée Acosta — Director of Learning Design, SpEd-Focused EdTech

**Verdict: ADVANCE** (up from BORDERLINE in round 1)

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.** Renée reads the tagline first: "Learning Experience Designer — SpEd & early-childhood edtech · complex/PDA autism · practitioner-researcher." Every word of that is a signal she screens *for*. Complex/PDA autism in the first line is the unfakeable noun — almost nobody in her 80-150-applicant stack can claim it. Her eye drops to the most recent two roles: early-childhood SpEd teacher, then K-5 SpEd teacher with a "documented, replicable" intervention. Then she hits "Educational Consultant — Compliance Auditor & Designer" and the lead bullet is the IEP/504/IDEA compliance-audit work. That is the line that makes her stop and re-read. By second eight she is not on the fence — she is reading for confirmation, not for a reason to cut.

**Strengths she sees.** The SpEd depth is loud, specific, and load-bearing from the first line. Multi-site IEP/IDEA compliance auditing is now a top-placed, stated-as-craft bullet (line 29) — "identified systemic gaps and authored remediation frameworks adopted by those programs" is exactly the product-critic framing she values. The "replicable" framing of the 6-8 week intervention (lines 9, 25) reads as the artifact signal she hunts for — something built once, documented, used by others, not one-off delivery. The "WHAT I BRING TO A LEARNING-DESIGN TEAM" block does real work: "Replicable design artifacts," "Iteration instinct," "built once, revised on feedback, used by others" (line 9) is product-adjacent language without overclaiming a software sprint. The pivot bridge is present and confident — the profile's closing sentence and line 9-10 acknowledge practice/PD origins while naming the transferable spine.

**Round-1 risks — status:**
- **RESOLVED:** Round-1 risk #2 (title-and-credential framing). The generalist "EdD with 15+ years of K-12 teaching" opener is gone. The tagline and profile now lead with special education, complex autism, and learning/content design. Credentials are subordinated to a clearly-labeled "EDUCATION & RECOGNITION" block at the foot of the page — present to help Renée sell the hire, never the lead pitch. This was the single fix she most needed and it landed cleanly.
- **PARTIALLY RESOLVED:** Round-1 risk #1 (product-iteration gap). The revision did meaningful honest work here — "Iteration instinct" as a named capability, "tight iteration cycles," the OSPI bullet's "translated findings into iterative program improvements" (line 32), and the explicit "content design that has to scale" framing (line 5). Renée reads these as a genuine feedback-loop instinct, which is enough to move her off BORDERLINE. But the gap is not fully closed: still no roadmap, no release, no engineers/PMs, no version cycle, no content revised against *product usage data*. The revision foregrounds the closest honest analog, which is the right move — but Renée will probe this hard on the call, and the resume should not pretend the analog is the same muscle.
- **REMAINS (minor):** "Pure consultant framing" is softened but not eliminated. Two of the five experience entries are Olver Consulting / OSPI consulting. Renée's soft peeve here is now well-managed — the consulting entries lead with audit *craft* and qualitative *inquiry*, not keynote speaking — but the resume still has no entry that is unambiguously embedded, accountable, team-based product work. That is a real-world gap, not a writing failure; the resume handles it about as well as it honestly can.
- **NEW problem:** Mild. The "Selected Experience" block lists five roles including the 2010-2012 Fairbanks role, and the two consulting entries overlap heavily in date range (2019-Present and 2023-Present) and in described activity (PD design, qualitative inquiry, autism work). Renée may briefly wonder whether these are two distinct engagements or one practice sliced into two lines. A one-clause differentiator on the OSPI entry — what makes it structurally different from the general Olver Consulting line — would remove the flicker.

**Remaining screen-out risk.** Low. The only thing that could still cost the call is the product-iteration gap surfacing as evasion rather than honest bridge. The resume currently handles it honestly; Renée pivoted herself and rewards exactly that. As written, she advances Catherine to the 30-minute call.

**Specific further fixes (all minor — this is an advance):**
1. Add one product-cadence noun to the "Iteration instinct" bullet (line 10) — even "revised on feedback in short cycles" or "shipped, then revised" — to make the feedback-loop instinct read one notch closer to sprint cadence without overclaiming.
2. Disambiguate the two consulting entries with a single distinguishing clause on the OSPI line (e.g., what the state contract structure required that the private-school audit work did not).
3. Optional: the Fairbanks 2010-2012 entry is the weakest line for Renée's screen and adds length without adding a SpEd-edtech signal she does not already have. Consider compressing it to a single line or cutting it; it currently competes for skim attention with stronger material.

## Synthesis

**Was the retarget sound?** Yes — it is the best decision in the revision. "Learning Experience Designer / curriculum-and-content design" is the honest descriptor for the two doors Catherine can actually walk through (Renée's SpEd-edtech and Grace's early-childhood edtech), and it correctly stops claiming the one she cannot (Hannah's efficacy-research req, which demands causal-inference methods and large-dataset tooling Catherine does not have). Round 1 said the same thing in three voices; the revision listened. Both viable screeners moved from BORDERLINE to ADVANCE, and the non-viable one is now a clean, honest non-application rather than a misrepresentation that would fail a take-home.

**Where the screeners agree.** All three agree the revision fixed the round-1 structural failure: the resume no longer leads with credential and identity (EdD, Dissertation of the Year, leadership) ahead of role-calibrated craft. Domain-specific signal is now in the first eight seconds for everyone. All three also agree the revision was honest — it added a real, modest AI step and a real iteration analog without manufacturing quantitative claims or product-sprint experience. That honesty is what converts Renée's and Grace's verdicts and what keeps Hannah from firing her inflated-claim blocker.

**Where they diverge.** The familiar three-way split is intact but now well-managed. Hannah is a permanent SCREEN-OUT — the gap is the work itself, not the writing, and the resume rightly no longer targets her. Renée wants the compliance-audit craft and SpEd specificity loud and embedded-team evidence; she got the first two and enough of the third to advance. Grace wants developmental milestone fluency, the 0-6 band, family-facing craft, and a forward-looking AI signal; she got all of it, with only a soft caveat that the band is really 3-5, not 0-6. The one remaining structural divergence: this single resume now serves Renée and Grace *well enough to advance both* — the round-1 recommendation to split into two variants is no longer strictly necessary, though a Grace-specific variant that foregrounds developmental/play language even harder and drops compliance further would squeeze out marginal additional fit. For submission purposes, the unified resume is good enough for both.

**Is it submission-ready?** For the SpEd-focused edtech and early-childhood edtech targets — **yes, it is submission-ready.** Both screeners advance it to the call as written. The fixes below are polish that raise the floor and pre-empt interview friction; none is a blocker. Do not send this resume to a general-purpose efficacy-research / learning-scientist req — the retarget already encodes that, and it is correct.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits still worth making (all minor):**

1. **Scope the age band honestly: "ages 3-5 / preschool," not a flat "early-childhood" implying 0-6.** Grace will notice the absence of any 0-3 signal. Precise scoping costs nothing, reads as candor, and pre-empts a credibility ding in the only place the current resume slightly oversells.

2. **Relocate the "Generative AI for Educators" course to a short Professional Development line near EDUCATION & RECOGNITION; keep the applied ChatGPT/Claude usage in the What-I-Bring block.** A one-day PD course listed beside an EdD and an AERA record can read as padding-by-adjacency. Splitting the credential from the practice keeps the AI signal (which Grace values) while removing the only line an unkind screener could mock.

3. **Add one product-cadence word to the "Iteration instinct" bullet and disambiguate the two consulting entries.** "Revised on feedback in short cycles" nudges the honest feedback-loop analog one notch closer to sprint cadence for Renée without overclaiming; a single distinguishing clause on the OSPI line removes the flicker of "is this one practice or two?" These are the last two things standing between ADVANCE-to-call and ADVANCE-with-no-questions.
