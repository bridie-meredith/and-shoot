# Resume 06 — Solo Consulting to Districts — Critic Feedback

## Context

This artifact is a buyer-facing consultant one-pager, not a job application. The three reviewers below are not hiring managers performing candidate screens — they are district buyers deciding whether to spend district money on Catherine Olver's services. Each is reading defensively, under time pressure, looking for a reason to discard rather than a reason to shortlist. Their verdicts are purchase decisions with political and professional skin in the game. Feedback below is organized by buyer, then synthesized across all three.

---

## Buyer: Renata Okafor — Director of Special Education

**Verdict: WOULD EXPLORE** (on the edge of shortlist — survives the skim, earns a cautious read, does not yet earn a call)

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.**
Her eye lands on: *"AI policy • SpEd compliance • Leadership development."* Okay — three words, two of them hers. Then she skims to "ENGAGEMENT AREAS" and sees "AI policy & implementation" in the lead slot, followed by "Special education compliance & audits." That order is the problem. She's been emailed by forty "AI in education" consultants this month. Leading with AI drops Catherine into that pile before the SpEd proof arrives to pull her out. Renata keeps reading only because "SpEd compliance" is visible in the second bullet and the OSPI line shows up under "Recent Engagements" — but it's a near miss. A buyer who is a faster discarder than Renata doesn't get there.

**What builds trust.**
- **"OSPI-funded contracts (2023-Present)"** is the single hardest-working line on the page. State-education-agency contract work tells Renata this person has operated at the altitude where compliance is decided, not just complained about. She circles it.
- **"Private school and EC compliance audits (2019-Present)"** — six years of multi-site audit work is exactly the proof her instant-reject filter looks for. This is not decorative.
- **"88% of students receiving services spent the majority of their day in general education"** — this is a named outcome from real practice. She slows down here.
- **The EdD plus Dissertation of the Year plus AERA/UCEA record** reads as board armor, not personal achievement. When her superintendent asks why this consultant, these credentials are the cover she uses to defend the spend.
- **PDA autism, trauma-informed practice, MTSS, complex autism** — this signals Catherine is not selling the same slide deck to everyone. Renata's highest-need kids are exactly the students most AI tools and most AI consultants get wrong. This differentiation survives the generalist flood.

**What raises doubt.**
- **The AI-first positioning with no risk-literacy signal.** The one-pager asserts "AI policy & implementation" as the lead engagement area, but there is no line — not one — about what AI gets wrong for SpEd kids: student-data privacy, FERPA, bias against disabled learners, IDEA exposure. To Renata, a consultant selling AI services without naming the risk surface is a liability she'd be importing into her own department. This is a blocker unless she reads deep enough to trust Catherine's SpEd depth will cover it — and she may not read that deep.

  *Exact line causing hesitation:* "AI policy & implementation for districts — author/refine district AI policies aligned with state mandates (35+ states have published guidance); vendor evaluation, teacher PD plans, ongoing governance."

  This bullet names only upside functions. Renata wants to see: *vendor evaluation includes IDEA-compliance and student-data-privacy review* or *AI tools assessed against LRE and IEP obligations*. Without it, this reads as a vendor's pitch, not a compliance auditor's.

- **The positioning is still tuned for a job seeker, not a consultant.** *"Inclusive education consulting | AI policy • SpEd compliance • Leadership development"* is closer than the job-application version, but "leadership development" as a third headline pillar dilutes the SpEd-compliance core that Renata is actually buying. She is not buying leadership coaching. She is buying protection from the auditors.

- **No deliverable is named in procurement nouns.** "Detailed findings with prioritized remediation" is close, but Renata's bar is: *you will receive [noun], [noun], and [noun] — a compliance audit report, a prioritized IDEA-exposure findings list, and a board-ready summary.* The current wording still hides behind verb phrases. "Detailed findings" is not a named deliverable; it's a promise about a deliverable.

- **Rate/scope signal is absent.** Nothing on this page tells her what band she's buying in. She cannot approve a PD line purchase she hasn't scoped. The call she'd otherwise make turns into an awkward negotiation, and consultants who make her do that work lose ground.

**Specific one-pager fixes.**

*Problem 1 — AI leads the engagement list with no risk framing.*
Current: "AI policy & implementation for districts — author/refine district AI policies aligned with state mandates (35+ states have published guidance); vendor evaluation, teacher PD plans, ongoing governance."
Rewrite: "Special education compliance & AI governance — IEP/IDEA/504 compliance audits, multi-site, with prioritized findings and board-ready summaries; district AI policy authoring that integrates FERPA, LRE, and IDEA obligations into vendor evaluation and governance — because AI policy for a district that serves disabled students has to be compliance-grade, not just pedagogically sound."

This single reorder and reframe fuses her three words (SpEd, compliance, AI) and provides the risk-literacy signal in the same breath.

*Problem 2 — Deliverables named in verb phrases, not nouns.*
Current: "detailed findings with prioritized remediation"
Rewrite: "Deliverable: a compliance audit report, a prioritized findings list with IDEA-exposure flags by site, and a board-ready summary — structured to survive a state monitor's review."

*Problem 3 — No rate/scope signal anywhere.*
Add a line under ENGAGEMENT FORMATS: "PD workshops: $3,500–7,500. Compliance audits and policy projects: scoped per engagement, typically $15–65k. Fractional SpEd director advisory: monthly retainer available."

*Problem 4 — Headline buries the niche.*
Current header tag: "Inclusive education consulting | AI policy • SpEd compliance • Leadership development"
Rewrite: "Special education compliance consulting | IEP/IDEA audits • AI governance for SpEd • Inclusive leadership"

## Buyer: Eleanor Voss — Deputy Superintendent / Chief of Staff

**Verdict: WOULD PASS** (reads as a program specialist applying for the wrong engagement — does not self-separate from the SpEd-director's-call bucket)

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.**
Eleanor's eye lands on the headline tag — *"Inclusive education consulting | AI policy • SpEd compliance • Leadership development"* — and is neutral-to-positive. Then she reads the WHAT I HELP section: *"I help K-12 districts and private schools make inclusive practice real, compliant, and sustainable."* "Inclusive practice" is program vocabulary. Eleanor is not commissioning a program review. She's commissioning a board-adoptable AI governance document. The moment "inclusive practice" leads instead of "district AI policy," she has started pattern-matching this profile to the SpEd director's desk, not hers. She continues reading because the AI line is there, but she's reading with a deficit now.

**What builds trust.**
- The **IEP/IDEA compliance-audit credential** is Eleanor's strongest signal. She reads it as: *this person audits programs against federal law and writes findings. That is the same discipline as authoring a board-adopted policy — a document built to survive scrutiny because scrutiny is their day job.* That's a real asset and it almost survives the framing problems.
- **"OSPI-funded contracts since 2023"** signals she can operate at the system level with public money. Eleanor reads that as institutional trustworthiness, not just personal expertise.
- The **EdD, Dissertation of the Year, AERA/UCEA presentations** read as intellectual horsepower. Eleanor won't worry Catherine can't structure a complex document.
- **"Active OSPI-funded contracts since 2023"** — state-funded active work means she's not a has-been or a once-a-year conference speaker. She's operating.

**What raises doubt.**
- **No board-adopted AI policy artifact.** This is Eleanor's hardest single screen, and Catherine's one-pager has nothing to offer here. Workshops, PD, compliance audits, speaking engagements, research presentations — but no line reading "I authored the AI policy adopted by District X's board." For the $25–75k AI-policy engagement Eleanor is commissioning, this gap is close to a blocker. She can rationalize around it (a compliance auditor who has never written *that exact document* but has written its functional equivalent), but Catherine has to do that work for her. The one-pager does not.

  *Missing line:* Something like "Model AI policy and administrative-regulation template, adoption-ready — available for review" would partially bridge this until a named board adoption exists.

- **The SpEd-specialist identity dominates and narrows.** The ENGAGEMENT AREAS section names five service lines. Three are clearly SpEd-program-level work: compliance audits, inclusive leadership, family/community engagement. One is broad PD. Only one — the AI-policy bullet — addresses Eleanor's commission. She reads this as a specialist whose primary market is program directors, with a secondary offering she might be available for. That reads as the wrong author for the whole-district governing document she needs.

  *Exact lines causing the narrowing read:*
  "Inclusive leadership development — principal preparation and instructional leadership coaching grounded in award-winning dissertation research."
  "Family and community engagement design — family-facing programs on regulation, child development, navigating systems."

  Neither of these signals whole-district governance competence. They signal program depth, which Eleanor respects but does not need for this engagement.

- **Governance vocabulary is absent.** Nowhere on this page do the words *board policy, administrative regulation, first reading, public comment, union consultation, board work session* appear. Those are the words that flip Eleanor's read from *vendor* to *peer*. Their absence is louder than any credential on the page.

- **Academic register with no operational landing.** "AERA 2024-2026 and UCEA 2024 — accepted research presentations" — this line actively works against Catherine with Eleanor. It's appropriate for a CV; for a consultant one-pager it reads as someone who lives in the research world and lands her deliverables at conferences, not in board packets. Eleanor cannot use a research presentation as a reference for her board.

- **No named district for the AI work.** "Kent SD (Oct 2025) — guest presenter on District-Wide MTSS" is a speaking engagement, not a policy engagement. There is no named district where Catherine executed the AI policy authoring she is selling. Eleanor notices this gap.

- **No scope, no fee, no references.** The page names engagement formats but zero dollar figures and zero callable references. Eleanor's bar is three references she can dial — peer administrators, board members, a superintendent. "Available on request" is how she loses her.

**Specific one-pager fixes.**

*Problem 1 — Headline and lead read as program specialist, not policy author.*
Current: "I help K-12 districts and private schools make inclusive practice real, compliant, and sustainable."
Rewrite: "I author district AI governance policy — board-adoptable, compliance-grade, built to survive a divided board, a parent community, and a state compliance reviewer. My edge: 17 years inside the most compliance-intensive corner of K-12 means the hardest part of any AI policy — special education, accessibility, IDEA obligations — is where I'm strongest."

*Problem 2 — No governance vocabulary anywhere.*
Add to the AI policy bullet: "Engagement includes stakeholder input sessions (board work session, union touchpoint, parent advisory), first and second reading support, and delivery of both the board policy and companion administrative regulation."

This single addition converts the pitch from "AI workshop provider" to "policy drafter who has worked inside district governance."

*Problem 3 — Academic-register line undermining operational credibility.*
Current: "AERA 2024-2026 and UCEA 2024 — accepted research presentations."
Either cut this from the one-pager entirely (save it for a CV), or reframe it: "Research on inclusive leadership cited at AERA 2024–2026 and UCEA 2024 — the evidence base underlying my compliance and policy work."

*Problem 4 — No board-adopted artifact.*
Add a line in RECENT ENGAGEMENTS: "Model district AI policy and administrative-regulation template — adoption-ready draft, available for review upon request." Until a named board adoption exists, the form of the deliverable must be visible.

*Problem 5 — No references.*
Add a references line under CREDENTIALS or ENGAGEMENT FORMATS: "References available: peer administrators, OSPI contacts, SpEd directors at prior engagement sites."

Joanne's eye lands on "Inclusive education consulting | AI policy • SpEd compliance • Leadership development" and her first response is: *this looks like a district-office hire, not my person.* "AI policy" reads as above-building, above-budget. For a $3-8k staff-day buyer, this reads as: *her real work is somewhere else, and I'm the afterthought.* Joanne will wonder if Catherine will show up half-present for her building's PD day.

## Synthesis

**Where all three agree.**
All three buyers independently hit the same structural problem: the ordering of the page works against Catherine's strongest evidence. Her compliance-audit proof (Renata's primary qualification signal), her governance credibility (Eleanor's shortlist gate), and her practitioner depth (Joanne's trust unlock) are all present on the page — but each is outranked in visual priority by framing that signals something adjacent-but-wrong to each buyer. The one-pager is not failing on content; it's failing on sequence. Every credential these buyers need is here; none of it is where they look first.

**Where they diverge.**
Renata and Joanne are both reachable with targeted revisions to the existing structure. Eleanor is closer to a hard pass, because her single hardest requirement — a named, board-adopted policy artifact — cannot be fixed with copy changes; it's a gap in the engagement record that must be addressed operationally (produce and publish a model policy document or pilot a small-district adoption) before the pitch to that buyer is live.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits.**

1. **Reorder the ENGAGEMENT AREAS list so SpEd compliance audits lead, with AI governance framed as an extension of that compliance practice.** The current order (AI policy first, compliance second) costs Renata the 8-second skim and Eleanor the governance-specialist read. Compliance-auditor-who-extends-into-AI is a stronger, rarer, more defensible identity for this buyer set than AI-consultant-who-also-does-SpEd.

2. **Add a practitioner-first credential line at the TOP of the document** — before the EdD, before the consulting description. Something like: "17 years special education practitioner — complex autism, PDA, trauma-informed practice, MTSS" as the first line under the header name. This single reorder collapses Joanne's ivory-tower risk, reassures Renata of real-world SpEd depth, and gives Eleanor a credibility frame for the compliance-auditor identity.

3. **Name concrete deliverables in procurement nouns, not verb phrases, for each engagement area.** "Detailed findings with prioritized remediation" becomes "compliance audit report + prioritized IDEA-exposure findings list + board-ready summary." "Teacher PD plans" in the AI bullet becomes "a practical AI-literacy workshop with a SpEd-specific tool guide teachers open Monday." Each buyer is reading for a noun they could put in a purchase order or board packet; verb phrases read as consulting-speak.

**On "AI policy" leading the headline: it dilutes more than it elevates.**
"AI policy" in the headline position signals a different market — the generic AI-in-education consultant wave that all three buyers are actively filtering out. Catherine's actual competitive advantage is that she is an SpEd compliance auditor with the depth to make AI governance legally sound for the hardest-to-serve population. That is rare and differentiated. "AI policy" as a standalone lead discards that advantage and places her in a crowded bucket. The SpEd-compliance core is what all three buyers actually trust her for — it should be the identity, with AI positioned as the extension: *"special education compliance consulting — and what AI changes about it."* That ordering is both more honest to Catherine's real expertise and more persuasive to each of the three buyer types above.
