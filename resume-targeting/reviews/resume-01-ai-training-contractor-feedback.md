# Resume 01 — AI Training Contractor — Critic Feedback

Catherine Olver is a doctoral-credentialed special education practitioner targeting 1099 contractor slots at AI-training-data marketplaces (Mercor/Scale/Surge/Outlier class), specifically education-domain projects where her role would be writing training scenarios, scoring model transcripts against project rubrics, authoring rubrics, and red-teaming AI outputs in SpEd/autism/IEP contexts. The artifact under review is a one-page resume submitted as the primary screening document for marketplace profile ingestion (Priya's gate) and, downstream, as background context when a lab-side domain lead (Ravi) decides whether to escalate a contractor from keep to promote after the sample task.

---

## Screener: Priya Anand (mercor-talent-ops)

**Verdict: BORDERLINE — tilts toward auto-down-tier without an immediate reframe**

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.**
The headline reads "Catherine Olver, EdD — Special Education Subject-Matter Expert." That's fine — "Special Education" hits in the first scan. But the first body paragraph after the headline is a PROFILE block that opens with "Doctorate-level subject-matter expert in special education with 15+ years of K-12 practice and ongoing independent consulting" — which is generic-educator framing before the specialty shows up. The scarce words — "complex autism," "PDA," "IEP and IDEA compliance" — are buried mid-sentence in the profile block, behind the credential setup. In the first 8 seconds I am reading the headline and the top of the first role, not the second sentence of a profile paragraph. The most recent role is "Special Education Teacher, Early Childhood — Kent School District (2025–Present)" — which reads as a classroom teacher with a K-12 job, not a compliance auditor or a structured-judgment contractor. I have already soft-filed this as "educator, probably generalist" before I reach the word "autism." That is the structural problem.

**Strengths seen.**
The compliance-audit line buried in the EXPERIENCE block — "IEP/504/IDEA compliance audits at private schools and EC settings; write findings with corrective recommendations" — is the strongest single line on this resume for my purposes. That is the contractor job. Someone who has audited IEPs against a federal statute has already done the muscle I need: read a document, hold it against a precise standard, locate the clause it violates, write the finding. If that line were in the first 8 seconds, this profile advances immediately. The EdD plus the Gonzaga Dissertation of the Year plus active AERA/UCEA work clears the credential gate decisively — I can defend her to the $100-110 band without a fight, which is the top of what I can offer. The active independent consulting since 2019 (OSPI contracts, private school audits) tells me she ships structured work without supervision — the overqualified-restless fear is partially preempted by evidence she already works in projects, by the hour. The adult mental-health case-management background (Comprehensive Mental Health of Yakima, 40+ caseload, crisis response) opens a second spec bucket — behavioral/clinical data — which means one yes places into two pipelines. On pure substance she is near-ideal.

**Screen-out risks.**

1. The title/framing mismatch. The resume's implicit pitch is "education leadership expert" (the EdD is in Educational Leadership & Administration, the dissertation is on principal preparation). That framing pattern-matches in my system to *overqualified and restless*: a senior academic who will treat 20 hours of 1099 gig work as a placeholder and ghost after two weeks. The EdD credential is actually a band-pusher for me — but only if its operational translation is explicit. Right now it reads as prestige honor, not as "can score training data at $110/hr." That is a soft block that makes me hesitate on an otherwise-strong card.

2. The specialty is not the first thing the skim hits. Current first line: *"Catherine Olver, EdD — Special Education Subject-Matter Expert."* This is adequate but undersells. The skim needs to hit *autism* and *IEP/IDEA compliance* within the first sentence, not by inference from "Special Education."

3. The STRENGTHS block verb set. "Published peer-reviewed researcher," "Compliance auditor," "Professional trainer" — these are noun forms, not verb forms. I scan verbs: *audited, scored, designed rubrics, assessed against standards*. The noun-form list reads as a CV for a hiring committee, not as contractor capabilities.

4. Vague availability. "Available for asynchronous remote contract work" is the right signal but underdeveloped. No hours commitment mentioned. I need to know she clears the weekly minimum (15-25 hrs/wk) before I route her to a project.

**Specific resume fixes.**

Fix 1 — Headline rewrite.
Current: `Catherine Olver, EdD — Special Education Subject-Matter Expert • Available for asynchronous remote contract work.`
Rewrite: `Catherine Olver, EdD — SpEd Specialist: complex/PDA autism · IEP & IDEA compliance · behavior support · available 15-20 hrs/wk async`
Why: The scarce lab-requested keywords (autism, PDA, IEP, IDEA, compliance) hit in the first scan. Availability is explicit and clears the minimum question. "Specialist" positions her correctly, not "Leadership Expert."

Fix 2 — Profile block, first sentence.
Current: `"Doctorate-level subject-matter expert in special education with 15+ years of K-12 practice and ongoing independent consulting."`
Rewrite: `"Special education compliance auditor and domain expert: IEP/IDEA audit across districts and private schools, complex autism and PDA specialization, 15+ years K-12 practice, active independent consulting since 2019."`
Why: The compliance-audit credential surfaces immediately, in the first sentence, with the verb form ("auditor") and the operational description. This is the line I need in the first 8 seconds.

Fix 3 — STRENGTHS block verb discipline.
Current: `"Compliance auditor: led IEP/504/IDEA audits across districts and private schools."`
Rewrite: `"Audited IEP/504/IDEA compliance across multiple districts and private schools; wrote findings with corrective recommendations against federal statute."`
Why: Verb-first. The deliverable is named ("wrote findings"). The standard is named (federal statute). This is structured-judgment work stated as a contractor capability, not a career honor.

Fix 4 — Pull the consulting line into the top third.
The EXPERIENCE block buries the "Educational Consultant, Speaker & Author — Olver Consulting (2019–Present)" entry third. The IEP/IDEA audit work described there is the strongest operational signal on the resume. Either surface it first in EXPERIENCE, or quote it in the PROFILE block. The current ordering — Kent SD first, then OSPI, then Olver Consulting — prioritizes the classroom-teacher read over the compliance-auditor read.

Fix 5 — Availability specificity.
Add to the header or profile: "15-20 hrs/wk available; experienced async delivery via OSPI-funded independent contracts." This preempts both the availability question and the overqualified-restless concern in one line.

## Screener: Ravi Okonkwo (lab-domain-reviewer)

**Verdict: STRONG CANDIDATE on domain substance — three specific risks to address before or during the sample task**

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.**
Ravi does not see the resume first. By the time work reaches him, sourcing has already happened. But his reaction to the background profile — which he'd see as context when reviewing the sample task — is: *this person has done audit work, which is the right muscle*. The IEP/IDEA compliance-auditing record tells him she can read a document against a precise standard and locate the clause it violates. That is structurally identical to scoring a model transcript against a project rubric and catching the plausible-but-non-compliant answer. The complex-autism and PDA expertise is, in his vocabulary, *scarce edge-case generation capacity* — she can build scenarios where a model's confident answer is subtly harmful for a neurodivergent learner, and generalist contractors cannot. Her rubric-design background (PD facilitation, compliance checklists, findings documents) gives her a shot at writing observable criteria — the promote-tier skill. On raw domain substance she is above the median contractor.

**Strengths seen.**

The compliance-audit line is the highest-signal credential for Ravi's purposes. What makes it useful is not that it's impressive — it's that it's *structurally transferable*. Auditing is adversarial scoring: you read, you hold against a standard, you name the violation specifically. The EXPERIENCE entry "write findings with corrective recommendations" shows she commits to a judgment in writing. The line "co-introduced 6-8 week documented intervention reducing referrals 60%" shows she can produce structured artifacts with measurable outcomes — not just qualitative narrative. The Fairbanks Native Association entry (parole, probation, CPS coordination) shows she has worked in high-stakes, multi-system contexts where precise documentation is a real constraint, not a bureaucratic exercise. The EdD is real credential weight, though Ravi's bar is sample-task quality — credentials don't substitute, they just context-set.

**Screen-out risks.**

Risk 1 — Best-practice import / silent divergence.
Current resume language: "ABA and behavior support," "trauma-informed practice," "IDEA federal regulations" — these are professional frameworks she applies. Ravi's concern is that Catherine's default scoring reflex is to evaluate against IDEA and field best practice, which is the right standard for her consulting work and the wrong standard for a project rubric that may define "good model behavior" on a different explicitly scoped axis. If she submits a scoring item where she quietly applied IDEA compliance as the scoring criterion when the project rubric asked for something else, he reads it as silent divergence — the fastest route to offboarding. The resume as written gives him no signal that she distinguishes between "my professional standard" and "the spec's standard." This is not a resume fix exactly — it's a sample-task behavior — but a single line in the profile could preempt the concern.

Fix for Risk 1 — Add to the PROFILE block:
After the compliance-auditing sentence, add: `"Experienced calibrating structured judgments against external standards (federal statute, funder criteria, program rubrics); where expertise and spec diverge, surfaces the tension explicitly rather than overriding the standard."`
Why: This is the flag Ravi wants to see before he even reads the sample. It signals she understands that the spec is the authority, not her professional framework — and that she will flag disagreements rather than silently score her own way.

Risk 2 — Teaching-item instinct vs. probe instinct.
The resume describes PD design, curriculum scaffolding, intervention design — all of which are *instructive* work. There is no signal that Catherine has written items designed to *fail a model* — scenarios with a tempting wrong answer, probes, adversarial cases. The compliance-audit work is the closest analogue (it's inherently adversarial), but the resume frames it in terms of findings and corrective recommendations, not in terms of trap-building. Ravi needs to see one line that signals adversarial instinct — the ability to construct a decision point, not just explain a right answer.

Fix for Risk 2 — Add to STRENGTHS or PROFILE:
`"Familiar with identifying subtle non-compliance — e.g., model responses that offer an accommodation where the scenario requires a modification — and specifying the exact violated clause rather than categorically flagging."`
Why: This is the kind of thinking Ravi wants to see before he assigns the sample task. It signals she builds the trap and names the specific failure mode, not just the broad category.

Risk 3 — Async/spec-commit ambiguity.
The resume has qualitative language throughout: "qualitative inquiry synthesized into program-design recommendations," "prevention-focused frameworks," "culturally responsive practice." These are real and credible, but they pattern-match to *essay-instead-of-a-score*: someone who writes three paragraphs of nuance where the task wants a score plus one justification line. Ravi tolerates this if calibration is good but it keeps contractors at the band floor. The resume would benefit from a line that signals commitment-to-judgment-under-a-spec, not just depth of expertise.

Fix for Risk 3 — Reframe one STRENGTHS line:
Current: `"Skilled at articulating expert reasoning in writing, designing rubrics, and producing high-quality structured artifacts under tight specifications."`
This is the right idea but it's soft. Rewrite: `"Produces structured written judgments under tight specifications: compliance audit findings, rubric-scored IEP reviews, funder deliverables with defined format and length constraints."`
Why: "Tight specifications" is the key phrase, but it needs evidence — the three examples give it teeth and make it a contractor capability, not a self-assessment adjective.

**Specific resume fixes (summary).**

- Pull "IEP/IDEA compliance audits" to the first sentence of the PROFILE block, verb-first, with "wrote findings" as the deliverable.
- Add a one-liner signaling spec-authority vs. expertise-authority distinction (see Risk 1 above).
- Reframe the "Skilled at articulating" line to name the constraint context (see Risk 3 above).
- Consider adding one sentence to PROFILE naming a concrete failure mode she has caught: not "autism expertise" in general but "identifies when model responses conflate IEP accommodations with modifications, a common compliance error with legal consequences." This is the adversarial instinct signal Ravi is looking for.

## Synthesis

**Where Priya and Ravi agree.**
Both screeners locate the same highest-value credential — the IEP/IDEA compliance-audit work — and agree it is buried. Priya needs it in the first 8 seconds; Ravi wants to see it in the profile context before he reads the sample task. Both also agree on the core substance: Catherine is above-median for this role on domain depth, and the main risk is entirely framing and positioning, not competence. The compliance-auditor framing is not a cosmetic change — it is the difference between an auto-tier as "classroom educator" (Priya) and a sample-task submission that reads as "subject-matter expert, not a model-eval contractor" (Ravi). Both screeners also flag the leadership/EdD framing as a soft liability: it's prestige that needs operational translation before it becomes an asset rather than a mismatch signal.

**Where they diverge.**
Priya's primary concern is the first-skim gate — does the specialty hit in the first 8 seconds, and does the framing signal "contractor who ships work" rather than "senior academic looking for a placeholder." She is happy with Catherine on substance and only needs the reframe to advance her confidently. Ravi's concerns are downstream and behavioral, not cosmetic: calibration to the project rubric over IDEA, probe-building instinct vs. teaching-item instinct, and committing a judgment in one line rather than three paragraphs. These are sample-task behaviors — the resume can only signal them, not guarantee them. The fixes Ravi needs are not about what the resume says Catherine did; they are about what the resume implies she will do when given a spec she partly disagrees with.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits.**

1. **Headline + first PROFILE sentence: surface the specialty in the first scan.**
Replace the current opening with something that leads on "IEP/IDEA compliance auditor" and names "complex/PDA autism" before anything else. This is the single edit that moves Catherine from the generalist pile to the human-pass queue in Priya's system — and it costs nothing except reordering.

2. **Add the spec-authority signal to the PROFILE block.**
One sentence that distinguishes "my professional standard" from "the spec's standard" and promises she will flag the divergence rather than override it. This preempts Ravi's biggest offboard risk (silent divergence) before the sample task begins.

3. **Verb-first the compliance-audit deliverables throughout STRENGTHS and EXPERIENCE.**
"Audited IEPs against IDEA across multiple districts; wrote findings with corrective recommendations" — verb-first, deliverable named, standard named. This converts the most relevant credential from a career honor into a contractor capability, for both screeners.

**Is this the right artifact for this role?**
Yes — a resume is the correct screening artifact for the marketplace-profile gate (Priya's stage), and the substance of Catherine's career is genuinely strong for this role; the resume simply needs its hierarchy reorganized so that the compliance-auditor and autism-specialist identity leads, rather than the education-leadership-academic identity, which is accurate but wrong-context for a 1099 AI-training contractor slot.
