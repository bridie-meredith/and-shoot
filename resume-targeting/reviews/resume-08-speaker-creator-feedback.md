# Resume 08 — Speaker / Creator — Critic Feedback

This document is a speaker/creator one-sheet, not a job application. Its job is not to land an interview — it is to earn a platform slot or a purchase decision from two very different strangers who will spend eight seconds on it before moving on. Camille Duarte is deciding whether to bet her platform's launch machinery on Catherine as a revenue-share instructor. Denise Carver is deciding whether to block Thursday afternoons and defend a line item to her assistant superintendent. The one-sheet serves both only if it speaks the language of audience proof and deliverable outcomes, not the language of credentials and competencies. As written, it does not yet do that.

---

## Gatekeeper: Camille Duarte (productized-pd-gatekeeper)

**Verdict: PASS — with a clear path to SHORTLIST in 12-18 months**

PASS, not ONBOARD and not SHORTLIST. The niche is strong enough that Camille mentally files this person rather than bins her, but there is no call today because the distribution evidence is absent. SHORTLIST requires an audience already in motion. What is here is a conference presenter who has not yet built one.

### First-8-seconds skim reaction

The headline — "Speaker • Writer • Researcher | Inclusive leadership and complex autism" — tells me the subject matter but not the point of view, and gives me zero audience signal. I have read seventeen of these this month. I immediately scroll for a number: list size, follower count, sold-out workshop, a waitlist. There is no number anywhere on this sheet. Not a small number. No number at all. That is the moment my interest drops by half. The ABOUT paragraph leads with the EdD, the dissertation award, and the AERA/UCEA conference record — that is a CV lead, not a creator lead. By the time I finish the first paragraph, I know she is credentialed and I still do not know who is already following her or why they would pay to do so.

### What signals a marketable instructor

The thesis is genuinely sharp. "Complex autism (PDA)" in the headline is a wedge I can put on a landing page in one sentence — that corner of the SpEd/AI compliance space is structurally underserved, and she has real receipts behind it: OSPI-funded consulting, paid speaking, independent practice since 2019. That is more commercial history than most academics who pitch me. The credential stack (EdD, Dissertation of the Year, Emerging Scholar) functions as credibility insurance — it lowers buyer conversion friction once an audience is already in motion. The talk topics list also contains the seed of a thesis with an enemy: "Special Education Is Not a Place" is a claim, not a competency. "Living with PDA: a practical, neurodiversity-affirming framework" is specific enough to name a buyer. And the line "currently teaches early childhood special education in the Kent School District while consulting on OSPI-funded family programs" tells me this is someone who has receipts, not assertions — most academics pitching me have never sold anything. Catherine has.

### What's missing — and it is the whole game

There is no audience and no platform. This is the single fact that controls the verdict. The one-sheet lists formats (keynote, workshop, cohort, family series, written work) but not evidence that anyone is currently following the ideas in between engagements. A course slot on my platform is a distribution bet. I am not looking for credentials; I am looking for 800 subscribers who lean in, a consistent content cadence, and proof of paid demand at some repeatable scale. The one-sheet gives me none of those.

Specific gaps:

- No newsletter, Substack, or LinkedIn subscriber count anywhere on the sheet.
- No audience size on any listed engagement — what was attendance at AERA? Did the Kent SD session generate requests for follow-up? Nothing.
- No waitlist, no pilot cohort enrollment, no sold-out workshop claim.
- No content cadence evidence — nothing showing she publishes consistently between engagements. "Presents at AERA and UCEA annual meetings every year 2024-2026" is conference delivery, not creator cadence. Running a room is not the same muscle as shipping content every week for a year.
- No testimonial or social proof. Not one quote from a superintendent, SpEd director, or workshop participant. "Recent Engagements" is a list, not proof of impact.
- Five talk topics that read as a service menu, not a franchise spine. The formats section lists every possible thing: keynotes, half-day, full-day, cohort programs, family series, essays, white papers, research articles. That is a menu, not a backbone. I back IP backbones. What I read instead is: "I will do anything."
- Nine audiences in the "AUDIENCES I SERVE BEST" list. A course for everyone sells to no one. Nine audiences signals the primary buyer has not yet been decided.

### Specific one-sheet fixes

**Current line:** "Catherine Olver, EdD — Speaker • Writer • Researcher | Inclusive leadership and complex autism."

Problem: There is no thesis and no point of view. This is a title, not a claim. I cannot put this on a sales page.

Rewrite: "Catherine Olver, EdD — Most AI in schools is built for the median student. I help districts make it work for the 14% it currently fails — and stay IDEA-compliant doing it." The EdD follows in line two as support, not the lead.

---

**Current line:** "Catherine Olver, EdD, is a researcher, speaker, and 15-year veteran of K-12 special education."

Problem: Opens with credentials, not thesis. Credential-as-platform is a blocker. The EdD is trust, not reach, and leading with it tells me I am reading a CV, not a creator pitch.

Rewrite: Pull the credential to a two-line footnote after the point of view and the audience proof. Lead the About section instead with: "Independent consulting since 2019. OSPI-funded contracts since 2023. Paid speaking engagements across K-12 and university contexts. [Newsletter: X subscribers.] The EdD and Gonzaga Dissertation of the Year are the credibility layer behind a practice that already has paying clients."

---

**Current line (FORMATS OFFERED):** "Cohort programs for school leaders, instructional coaches, SpEd directors."

Problem: This is a format listing, not a franchise pitch. I need to see the IP spine — the single thesis that runs through all three cohort audiences and generates repeatable product. A syllabus is what I build after I say yes. What I need to see now is the backbone.

Rewrite: Replace this bullet with: "Cohort spine: AI Compliance for SpEd Directors (primary cohort, 4-6 weeks) → Inclusive AI Leadership for Principals (cohort 2) → the book as the same argument, durably. One thesis, three audiences, repeatable revenue." Show me the backbone, not the menu.

---

**Current line:** "AUDIENCES I SERVE BEST: Superintendents & senior district leaders · Principals & assistant principals · Special education directors · Instructional coaches & PD designers · Early childhood and K-12 educators · University education programs · Parents and family advocates · Edtech and AI product teams."

Problem: Eight audiences. This is niche-stated-as-breadth. Every additional audience I read after the third, my interest in the platform bet drops. A course for everyone sells to no one.

Rewrite: Cut to one named buyer with a deadline: "Primary buyer: Special education directors and district leaders in the 35+ states now under AI guidance or mandate." That is a buyer with a deadline, which is my favorite kind of buyer. Everyone else is a stretch goal named in a conversation, not on a one-sheet.

---

**Bottom line from Camille:** "You have the rarest thing — a real point of view and paying clients behind it. The thesis is there. The niche is there. The receipts are there, partially. What you don't have yet is an audience and a consistent content cadence, and those are the only things I'm actually betting on. Go publish 12 consecutive weeks of specific, thesis-driven writing on SpEd and AI compliance. Get 800 of the right subscribers. Come back with those numbers in the first screen of your pitch. I will have a slot with your name on it. Today the answer is no, and you deserve a better no than that."

## Buyer: Denise Carver (cohort-pd-buyer)

**Verdict: MIGHT ENROLL — interested in the niche, blocked by missing practicality signals**

Not a WOULD PASS. The topic is acutely Denise's problem, not adjacent to it. But she is not a yes today because every piece of information she reaches for in the first 30 seconds is absent: no deliverable list, no time math, no price, no peer proof, no sample, and no clear signal that Catherine has sat at the IEP table in practitioner (not researcher) mode. She forwards this to her inbox and probably never comes back to it.

### First-8-seconds skim reaction

The headline names "inclusive leadership and complex autism" — that is partially her world. But the label "Speaker • Writer • Researcher" is not her world. She is not buying a speaker; she is buying something she can use on Monday. The ABOUT paragraph opens with "researcher, speaker, and 15-year veteran" — she scrolls to find out if this person has run IEP meetings and she does not see it stated directly. "Currently teaches early childhood special education in the Kent School District" is the closest signal, and it is buried in the third sentence. "Consulting on OSPI-funded family programs" is vague — consulting on what exactly? Did she audit IEPs? Write prior written notice? Denise asks "is that in here, or not?" and the answer is: not directly.

Then she looks at FORMATS OFFERED. "Cohort programs for school leaders, instructional coaches, SpEd directors" — good, SpEd directors is named. But there is no time commitment, no price, no deliverable list, no sample lesson. She is a 55-hour-a-week director who gets two hours a week if nothing blows up. She needs to know the actual load before she can say yes to blocking her calendar. She does not have that information. She closes the tab.

### What works as a demand signal

The PDA / complex autism expertise is specific in a way most PD pitches are not. The line "Living with PDA: a practical, neurodiversity-affirming framework for educators and families" tells her this is not a generic-AI-with-a-SpEd-slide deck. The OSPI-funded program work signals a practitioner — but only if Denise knows what OSPI is (she might; she might not, being in Ohio). The AERA / UCEA conference record reads as credibility, but not as "this person has sat at my IEP table," which is the bar she is actually trying to clear.

### What's missing — and these hit the blocker list

1. **No deliverable list anywhere on the sheet.** Denise wants to see a noun list — policy template, IDEA-compliance checklist, vendor-evaluation rubric, IEP-team script. "Cohort programs for school leaders, instructional coaches, SpEd directors" is a category, not an outcome. She cannot picture the artifact she will walk out holding, and that means she cannot say yes. This is the single most decisive gap for her.

2. **No time math.** "Half-day and full-day workshops" is in the formats section but the cohort has no stated time commitment per week. Her ceiling is approximately two hours a week, and that ceiling is fragile. If the sheet does not say it, she assumes the worst. Silence here reads as "5-7 hours" and that is dead on arrival.

3. **No price.** No price signal anywhere. For the cohort/course positioning this sheet is also doing, an absent price makes her suspicious and means she has to send an email before she can decide whether this is even in her budget. She does not have time for that email.

4. **No peer proof.** Not one quote. Not one "SpEd coordinator, Westerville City Schools." The Recent Engagements list is all presenter credentials, zero buyer testimonials. A superintendent quote about an MTSS presentation does nothing for her. A SpEd director saying "I rewrote our AI vendor checklist the week after this cohort" does a great deal.

5. **No sample.** Nothing she can taste before paying. One free checklist, one sample lesson, a "five questions to ask before buying an AI tool for your IEP team" PDF — something. An unknown creator has to let Denise try the practicality before she trusts it with her budget.

6. **The practitioner bio is buried under the researcher bio.** The opening line — "Catherine Olver, EdD, is a researcher, speaker, and 15-year veteran of K-12 special education" — reads as academic with a research focus. The actual practitioner biography — IEP teams, compliance audits, currently in Kent SD classrooms — is scattered across the sheet in secondary clauses. The one signal that clears Denise's "has this person been on my side of the table" check is underemphasized while the conference record is front.

## Synthesis

### Where Camille and Denise agree

Both personas find the niche genuinely strong. Both want more from the same place: proof that the expertise is practical and that someone has already paid for a version of it. Camille is missing the audience signal and content cadence evidence. Denise is missing the deliverable list, time math, and practitioner-first bio ordering. These are different expressions of the same gap: the one-sheet is a conference-presenter credential document dressed up as a creator/instructor pitch, and it does not yet speak the productized-PD language either reader requires.

### Where they diverge

Camille's blocking issue is distribution — she literally cannot place a bet today because there is no audience to distribute to, and no amount of rewriting the one-sheet fixes that. It is a real-world action gap, not a copy gap. Denise's blocking issues are mostly copy and product-design gaps — missing deliverables, time math, price, practitioner-first bio order — and several of them could be fixed in a revision pass this week. Denise is closer to a yes than Camille, because the thing Denise needs (a concrete, well-designed offer with honest time math and a deliverable list) can be built now. The thing Camille needs (twelve months of consistent publishing and an audience in motion) takes twelve months.

### Top 3 highest-leverage edits

**1. Flip the bio order: auditor first, academic second.**

The phrase "researcher, speaker, and 15-year veteran" in the opening buries the single credential both Camille and Denise are actively scanning for — someone who has done the actual job. Lead with "17 years in special education, IEP teams, IDEA compliance audits across multiple districts" and put the EdD and UCEA recognition in line two. This is a ten-minute rewrite that materially changes how both personas read the opening screen. It does not require building an audience or designing a new product; it is pure ordering.

**2. Add a deliverable section to every cohort or product mention.**

Not a formats list. A noun list: what does the buyer walk away holding? A policy template. A compliance checklist. A vendor-evaluation rubric. A script. For Denise this is a near-blocker; for Camille it signals a real product exists rather than a syllabus. Neither persona can picture paying for "cohort programs for school leaders" with no artifact named. Adding the noun list converts a format into an offer.

**3. Collapse the audience list to one named buyer per offering.**

The current eight-audience list actively signals "for everyone" to both readers. Camille reads it as niche-stated-as-breadth. Denise reads it as generic-AI-with-a-SpEd-slide risk. Pick one buyer per product format and name them with specificity: "SpEd directors at districts with a state AI mandate" for the cohort; principals for the leadership workshop; families and advocates for the family series. Three separate pitches, three single-buyer lines — better than one eight-audience list trying to do all three simultaneously.

### The core gap

Conference-presenter credibility is earned by showing up and being cited; productized-PD/creator credibility is earned by showing what the buyer walks away holding and proving someone has already paid for a version of it — and this one-sheet is fluent in the first language and nearly silent in the second.

### Is the one-sheet trying to serve too many audiences at once?

Yes, and it is a structural problem, not a polish problem. The document simultaneously pitches conference organizers (keynote formats, AERA/UCEA record), university audiences (panelist, guest lecturer, education programs), cohort-platform gatekeepers (cohort programs, IP spine), end buyers (SpEd directors, families), and publishers (written work: essays, white papers). These five targets require different first sentences, different evidence hierarchies, and different calls to action. A platform gatekeeper and a SpEd director buyer should not be reading the same document — they are asking different questions, in a different order, with different blockers.

The one-sheet as written is a portfolio of everything Catherine does, which is useful as a career document and actively unhelpful as a sales document for either reader reviewed here. The fix is not better writing within the current format; it is splitting into at least two documents: a speaker-and-conference one-sheet (which this mostly already is) and a separate cohort/course landing page or pitch deck that leads with the buyer, the deliverables, the time math, and the practitioner bio. These are different documents with different jobs, and trying to make one document do both jobs is why neither reader gets what they need in the first eight seconds.
