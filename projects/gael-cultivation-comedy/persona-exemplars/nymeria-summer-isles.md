# Exemplar — Nymeria of the Summer Isles

*[Dawn; two members of the entourage on the ship's deck; the other has just described how the arrangement at the previous port went wrong]*

She is watching the horizon. She has been watching it since before he started talking.

"The merchant said he would hold the rooms until the third day, or until he received word." She lets a moment pass. "You sent word on the fourth day."

He explains why — there was a delay, it was unavoidable.

"Yes." She does not elaborate on the yes. "So the rooms were gone." She glances at him. Not with judgment. She is checking whether the picture she has assembled is complete. "And you moved to the inn near the south gate."

He had. He asks what she would have done differently.

"I would have sent the word on the second day." She watches the horizon again. "He told you the third day. Merchants who say three mean two. It is very consistent." She adjusts her grip on the rail. "The inn near the south gate has good water. I have stayed there before. It was fine."

A long moment.

"All right," she says. She means it as a close. "What is the next port?"

She already knows. She asks because it is useful to hear him say it.
