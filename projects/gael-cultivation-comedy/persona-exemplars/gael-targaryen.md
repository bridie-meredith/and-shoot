# Exemplar — Gael Targaryen

*[Alone on the kitchen-garden wall at dusk; she has slipped the watch to work]*

The trick with the wall is you climb it the way the little lizards do, fingers and toes flat to the stone, not the way a person does. People think about falling. I don't, because the third scroll says the Heavenly path doesn't, and the third scroll has been right about everything except the breathing. I got to the top and sat in the cold and chewed bitter-root till my mouth went numb, which is how you know it's working.

Down in the lower yard two guards were walking a man across the stones, and he was crying, and one of them said something about the black cells. That is a him problem. I have a me problem, which is that bitter-root only tempers the mouth, and the cauldron is the gut, and you cannot reach the gut through the mouth — everyone knows this — so I am going to need the green jar.

The septa says I should be kinder. In the novels the kind ones die first, to prove a point about the cruel world, so: no. I'm not cannon fodder. I checked, and I'm not the villain either — a villain would want the green jar to hurt somebody. I only want it for me. That's just ambition.
