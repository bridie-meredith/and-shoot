# Prop — The Wildfire Shard

A fragment of the Alchemists' Guild's manufactured magical substance, swallowed during the Book I Movement 3 incident. The prop has no present physical form — it was consumed. This card documents what it was, what it did, and what it seeded.

The wildfire-shard ingestion is the broken clock's first genuine ring. Not because qi is real. Because wildfire is genuinely magical, and genuinely magical Westerosi materials produce real effects when ingested. The fever was not a cultivation advancement. It was an uncontrolled toxic-and-magical reaction to a substance whose properties no pharmacological framework — not the maester's, not the cultivation-register's — correctly predicted. The clock was right this once, and she has no idea which time it is.

**Cross-reference:** `cond-alchemists-guild-122ac` — the institution she encountered and whose product this was. `cond-westeros-reagent-tier-map` — the Tier 3 classification of wildfire as a genuinely magical Westeros material. `prop-dose-log` — entry 31, the one-line documentation of the incident. `PLANT[BLACK-STONE-PRECEDENT]` — the template this event seeds for Book III.

## Physical Description (Pre-Ingestion)

A fragment of solidified or semi-crystallized wildfire — the Guild's manufactured magical fire-compound in a cooled, non-liquid state. The normal form of wildfire is a viscous bright-green liquid; this fragment came from a preparation the Guild had apparently allowed to partially cure in a container, producing a translucent green-tinged solid with the consistency of hardened wax or crystallized resin. In daylight: slightly luminescent, the color shifting between bright green and deeper jade depending on the viewing angle.

Size: approximately the size of a thumbnail, possibly smaller — the account of the incident in the dose-log describes it only as "a fragment obtained from the Guild's laboratory during the inspection incident." She kept it for approximately three days before swallowing it, which is documented in the dose-log's preamble to entry 31.

Smell (pre-ingestion): faintly sulfurous, sweet in a chemical way that the dose-log describes as "inconsistent with the reagent's external presentation — the sulfur principle is volatile and present; beneath it something less categorizable." This is accurate. Wildfire's smell is one of the few things about it that doesn't resolve into standard pharmacological vocabulary.

## Affordances / Uses

**As a reagent in her framework:** She classified it pre-ingestion as a Tier 3 material — "pure fire-attribute qi crystallized in liquid form by the Furnace Sect's refinement process." The tier assignment is essentially correct; wildfire is a genuine Tier 3 magical material. The mechanism is completely wrong. She believed ingesting it would produce a controlled realm-advancement event. She was wrong about the control.

**What it actually did:** Wildfire is a manufactured magical substance — genuinely alchemical, genuinely not explainable by mundane medieval chemistry. Ingesting a fragment produced: a three-day "heavenly tribulation" fever with genuine physiological crisis; episodes of uncanny awareness that the adults found alarming and she found instructive; the first time in her life that the adults were scared rather than charmed. The specific mechanism of the effect — whether the fragment's magical properties acted directly on her body, or whether the wildfire's physical chemistry at a small solid dose is itself acutely toxic, or both — cannot be determined from the dose-log's documentation. Entry 31 reads: "tribulation sustained; duration three days; uncanny surfacing noted and documented; the cauldron's Foundation Establishment advancement is confirmed." The uncanny surfacing is not described further in the log. The adults' reactions are not in the log.

**What the adults experienced:** The septa kept vigil. Viserys sent for the maester three times. Helaena sat outside the door for the second night. The fever broke without intervention — there was nothing the maester could administer, because he could not identify what had been ingested. He suspected some exotic poison. He was not wrong. He did not know about the wildfire. No one did except her.

## Sensory Hooks (The Incident)

- The fever: documented in the dose-log as a sustained high temperature with "periodic clarification intervals" — she was lucid between the fever peaks, which she used to continue the dose-log entry; the handwriting of entry 31 changes mid-entry, going from neat to large and slightly irregular, then recovering; she noted this in the margin: "the tribulation's intensity registered at the instrument"
- The uncanny surfacing: not described in the dose-log, but referenced in two subsequent entries (32 and 33) as "the aperture event during the tribulation — a widening of the spiritual sense that has not fully closed." In the project's real-state reading: an unusual perceptual state produced by wildfire's magical properties on a body that was, at the same time, running a high fever and processing a toxic magical compound. What she perceived is not documented. That she perceived something is
- The smell carried through: for approximately two weeks after the incident, the still-room had a faint chemical-sweet overtone she documented as "residual tribulation-period qi-saturation" and the maester described (to himself, not to her) as "some kind of fire-compound contamination from an unidentified source"
- The color: she documented the shard's color as "correct" — meaning it registered for her as the right tier visually, which she filed as preliminary reagent-quality confirmation. The bright green of wildfire is distinctive; she would have known what the Guild made

## Functional State

**Consumed (Bk I M3, dose-log entry 31):** the shard no longer exists as a physical object. Its functional state is entirely the downstream consequences:

1. **The dose-log entry 31 (permanent):** one line, no drama. "Wildfire fragment; estimated weight 0.3 grain-weight by the first measure. Tribulation: three days. Foundation Establishment advancement confirmed. Aperture event noted. The cauldron holds." The brevity of the entry is deliberate — she did not document the details she found alarming, only the conclusion she needed.

2. **The "Foundation Establishment" misread (permanent narrative artifact):** her cultivation-register filed the incident as a realm breakthrough — Foundation Establishment, the second step on the cultivation ladder. This is the cultivation-register's most significant misclassification. A genuine uncontrolled reaction to a genuinely magical substance was filed as controlled advancement. The clock chimed, and she read the chime wrong.

3. **The PLANT[BLACK-STONE-PRECEDENT] (Bk III activation):** the wildfire-shard incident establishes the template. A genuinely magical Westerosi material, ingested, produced a real uncontrolled effect — an effect that was scary to the adults around her, that pushed past the parameters she expected, that produced an "aperture event" she didn't fully understand. The oily black stone, when it becomes narratively relevant in Book III, is seeded by this template: she has a prior experience of a genuinely magical ingested material producing real effects. The black stone's effect — whatever it is, ruled or pending — lands against the memory of the wildfire incident. The cauldron has done this before. What the cauldron could not do before was consciously choose to go further. In Book III she is reaching toward the stone with an appetite the wildfire incident does not explain but does rhyme with.

4. **The Lock I function:** the wildfire incident is the precipitating event for Lock I — the septa moves to have her cloistered because of it. The shard is therefore indirectly responsible for the King's-hand note being deployed and the paper-heist's master instrument coming into existence. The consumed prop is the keystone that knocked every other lock into place.

## Her Cultivation-Register Gloss (Belief Column)

She narrates: *"The wildfire fragment — a Tier-3 fire-attribute reagent of the Furnace Sect's manufacture, correctly assessed pre-ingestion. The tribulation confirmed the tier rating. Foundation Establishment was achieved through the controlled processing of a fire-attribute reagent beyond the cauldron's prior tolerance ceiling. The three-day duration is consistent with a breakthrough-tier tribulation event. The aperture — the widening of spiritual sense during the tribulation's second interval — is consistent with the realm-advancement phenomenology of the traditional Foundation Establishment event. The Furnace Sect produced this reagent. They clearly possess fire-cultivation knowledge they have not disclosed to petitioners. My application to the sect was not accepted. I will note: the sect's product produced the advancement their instruction did not. The cauldron has processed what the sect would not teach."*

**Real-state column:** Wildfire is a manufactured magical substance that the Guild cannot fully explain even to themselves. The fragment was approximately 0.3 grain-weight of a solid cured form. Ingested at that small quantity, it was simultaneously chemically toxic (wildfire is not food; its chemical composition produces acute toxicity at any non-trivial dose) and magically reactive (wildfire is a genuinely magical material; at Tier 3 in the reagent classification, it interacts with the body in ways that exceed mundane toxicology). The three-day fever was an acute physiological crisis. The "aperture event" was a neurological and perceptual side-effect of the combined chemical-and-magical reaction — real in the sense that something unusual happened to her sensory experience; not real in the sense of qi-aperture-opening. Foundation Establishment did not occur. Something else did, and its full nature is the broken clock's most carefully guarded ambiguity.
