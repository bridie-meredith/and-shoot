## 1. Canon ground-truth (the world she's caged in)

**Household / handlers.** The central handler is the **septa** — godsworn woman who is
simultaneously tutor, governess, and chaperone (canonical template: Septa Mordane).
Also: **ladies/companion girls** (institution canon; specific girls = INVENT), the
**maester** (under Viserys, Grand Maester Mellos), a **wet-nurse** (canon: wet-nursing
used as a *political* tool — Viserys had Daeron & Jacaerys nursed as "milk brothers" to
prevent enmity). **Kingsguard** guard the family inside Maegor's; **Dragonkeepers** (order
of 77) guard the dragons, not the children. → **"Escaping handlers" = giving the septa
the slip.** Soft walls (propriety + the septa's watch), not bars. Comedy-friendly.

**Education (septa-taught).** Reading, writing, sums; **needlework** (heavily emphasized);
piety & the Faith; deportment/courtesies; explicitly "future duties as wives and mothers";
**High Valyrian** (a *family* tongue for Targaryens, not just academic); music, dancing,
history. Daily shape = lesson-block + chaperoned leisure, supervised throughout. (Exact
timetable = INVENT.) → This is where she "meditates" instead of attending.

**Dragons & dragonlessness (the load-bearing canon).**
- **Cradle-egg custom** (made customary under Viserys): a dragon egg placed in a newborn
  Targaryen's cradle to bond as they grow. **It often fails** — "some eggs never hatched,
  and some Targaryen children never became dragonriders."
- **The dragonless precedent is real and sad:** **Rhaena** (dau. of Daemon, b.116) — her
  cradle hatchling was **sickly and died within hours**; given a replacement egg, she
  **"slept with it every night and prayed for a dragon to match her sister's,"** dragonless
  for years while twin **Baela** rode Moondancer. **Aemond** was mocked as the dragonless
  one (his brothers gave him a **pig** as his "dragon") until he claimed Vhagar at 10.
- **Social meaning:** a Targaryen's mystique is dragon-blood-made-visible; no dragon = "the
  magic didn't take in *you*." Pity or mockery. **Political meaning:** a dragonless princess
  is a weaker bride and a weaker piece — value collapses to pure bloodline. → **She has all
  the constraints of a Targaryen princess and none of the payoff.** That's the engine.

**Movement.** Home base = **Maegor's Holdfast**: 12-ft walls, spiked **dry moat**, single
**drawbridge**, royal nursery inside — she literally lives in a fortified box. The wider
**Red Keep** (Great Hall, Tower of the Hand, godswood, yards, gardens) is accessible but
improper to wander unescorted. The **Dragonpit** (atop Rhaenys's Hill, 40 undervaults,
Dragonkeeper-guarded) — **a dragonless princess can visit the building full of everyone
else's dragons.** King's Landing proper = escorted, exceptional. Dragonstone = a boat-trip
destination.

**Marriage.** Targaryen **brother-sister** norm (Faith *tolerates* per Jaehaerys's
compromise — needs acknowledgment, not blessing); fallback = uncle/cousin/nephew. **Betrothed
in childhood** (Baela & Rhaena betrothed at age ~2), **married ~13** (Helaena wed brother
Aegon at 13 in 122 AC). → A dragonless princess gets **betrothed young, at a discount, to
someone worse** — a live comedy/stakes lever.

**The cohort (foils & kin):**
- **Helaena** (half-sister, b.109, rides Dreamfyre) — gentle, **odd, obsessed with insects/
  spiders, speaks in riddles/prophecy, dislikes being touched, smallfolk love her.** The
  canon "sheltered odd princess." → **Pitch: Saerys's ally-sister; a kinship of weirdos.**
- **Aemond** (brother, b.110) — the dragonless-striver who *seized* a dragon by daring. →
  Foil: he solved dragonlessness by conquest; she "solves" it by deciding dragons are food.
- Baela (bold dragonrider, b.116), Rhaena (the dragonless egg-prayer, b.116), Jaehaera
  (b.123, cradle-egg success), Maelor (b.127).
- **Thematic wink:** the *next* generation (Aegon III's daughters) is routinely dragonless;
  one Rhaena **becomes a septa**. "The future is dragonless" is canon.

## 2. The cage — confinement reframed (harvester 3)

**Core reframe:** the cage is the **attendant, not the door.** A highborn girl is never
alone; **freedom = subtracting a watcher from the room, not unlocking one.** Every scheme is
a logistics problem: *who is watching, and how do I account for the next two hours.* → She
beats the cage the **same way she beats the realm and steals the ship: with logistics.** The
whole story rhymes. Character thesis in one line: **she has a steward's training and a
prisoner's permissions** — given every skill, denied the field.

**Cost of defiance is calibrated, not catastrophic:** not the dungeon, but married off
*faster, cheaper, worse*, financially de-fanged — or (Westeros ceiling) shipped to a **sept**
as a disposal mechanism for inconvenient royal women.

**The six loopholes (each a real historical latitude; each dramatizable):**
1. **Father-vs-household gap** — the King indulges; the septa disciplines. **A note in the
   King's hand is a master key that overrules every chaperone below him.** (= the good-king's
   love is simultaneously her cage *and* the skeleton key. Load-bearing.)
2. **Charity / piety cover `[KEYSTONE]`** — alms and tending the sick are *expected* noble
   duties, unsupervised by definition, morally unimpeachable (objecting makes you look
   wicked), and require exactly the supply lines (herbs, imported drugs, money to "the poor")
   a logistics empire needs. **A charitable sick-house / apothecary is how her trade-and-
   medicine network grows in plain sight.**
3. **Performed illness / seclusion** — a "sickly" princess "not to be disturbed" buys
   unmonitored hours, enforced by her own staff. (Really she's in the cellar reconciling
   ledgers.)
4. **Eccentric-royal latitude** — royals are *allowed* to be odd; "it's the dragon blood."
   A deviance budget the merely-noble don't get. (Helaena already spends it on spiders.)
5. **A buyable below-stairs faction** — servants have their own economy; she pays in
   *patronage and protection*, not coin — becomes the person below-stairs people owe.
   **She builds a faction, not a bribe list.**
6. **Stewardship-as-foothold** — noblewomen demonstrably ran estates and accounts; once she's
   the only one who understands the household books, no one can take them back without chaos.
   **Competence files its own cage-bars.**

## 3. The ship-heist toolkit (harvester 3)

**How she touches money:** personal income/dower revenues (lightly audited), **household
stewardship** (the account-book is the battlefield), **factors/agents** who transact in her
name in distant cities (entirely normal for rank — keeps her hands clean and body accounted
for), and **patronage** outflows no one scrutinizes.

**The instruments (system trusts tedious paper over people):**
- **Bill of exchange** — informal letter ordering an agent-banker to pay abroad; authority is
  *handwriting + relationship*, not a seal. Funds the trade network invisibly.
- **Charter-party** — heavily standardized ship-hire contract; forge by being *more correct
  than the clerks expect.*
- **Bills of lading / manifests** — the paper *is* legal title to the cargo; control the
  manifest, control what the ship "officially" carries.
- **Victualling / provisioning orders** — routine, low-status, unread; how you quietly ready
  a ship for sea.
- **Ship's articles** — crew obeys *the paper*, not their memory of who owns the hull.
- **Writs & seals** — authentication rests on the *seal*; a slightly-broken seal is itself the
  standard alibi for irregularities. She needs one genuine impression on the wrong document.
- **Letter of marque** — Crown license to seize a foreign vessel as redress for a debt;
  a *bureaucratic remedy*, not a war act. Manufacture the debt → "repossess" the ship lawfully.

**Four combinable "paper ship" mechanisms for the climax:**
- **(A) Accounting-error laundering** — run a real apothecary-import operation through the
  books for a year until one *extra* hull can be entered as "already chartered, already paid."
  Defeats the audit by drowning the fake in real prior transactions.
- **(B) Over-correct forged charter** — flawless boilerplate, "for importing medicines
  against the coming plague." Defeats the harbor master, who hunts a *malformed* document.
- **(C) Provisioning coup** — no fake ownership; forged victualling orders + articles
  re-task a ship already in port and authorize its sailing. Visible only in aggregate, after
  she's three days out.
- **(D) Repossession-by-marque** — manufacture a debt the owner "owes" her import-house, get
  a letter of marque, **seize the ship as redress.** "She doesn't steal it; she repossesses
  it," while the owner shouts at a shrugging harbor master holding a sealed writ.

**Climax throughline:** *she never lays a hand on the ship.* She steals an oceangoing vessel
the way a steward balances a book — quill, borrowed seal, a stack of correct forms — and what
defeats every man hunting a thief is **a document more perfect than anything they've ever
filed.**

## 5. Story-mechanic locks (how the facts ground our beats)

- **Inciting inversion:** like Rhaena, Saerys is given a dead cradle-egg + a replacement to
  **pray over nightly** (canon behavior). She doesn't pray. **She grinds it.** Her first
  reagent is the sacred egg she was supposed to worship.
- **Cold open works as canon:** the Dragonpit is genuinely a place a dragonless princess may
  visit — so the toddler heist needs no canon-bending.
- **The cage = the comedy engine:** soft walls + the septa-handler + the six loopholes mean
  every antic is a *who's-watching* caper, and her escapes scale from "ditch the septa" to
  "launder a ship."
- **The keystone is charity:** the sick-house alibi is the through-line that turns toddler
  rock-eating into a realm-spanning trade-and-medicine empire without a single implausible step.
- **Helaena is the warm spot:** an odd elder sister who already spends the "eccentric-royal"
  budget — the one person who doesn't find Saerys baffling. A relationship the war can break.
- **The good-king's-love irony is mechanical:** Dad's indulgent note is the master key; his
  love literally springs the cage he keeps her in — and he never understands either fact.
