# Broad outlines — run-01

Target: ~80k words, structured as **a few linked novellas** (~4 × ~20k).
Working name for the protagonist: **Saerys** (provisional — swap freely).
All outlines assume the locked spine: dragonless setting-blind Targaryen girl,
locked-inside POV, father = Viserys I, idiot-savant, gay, alchemy/reagent path,
trade-and-medicine power base, mortal entourage, grief-curdle at the Dance.

The real fork between these is **scope**: how much of the saga 80k tries to hold.

---

## Outline A — "The Whole Wheel" (compressed full saga · picaresque)

Cradle to Dance in 80k. Four self-contained reagent-quest novellas that time-skip,
each ratcheting the meta-arc and darkening the tone one notch. Delivers the *entire*
promised arc — including the curdle — at the cost of breadth over depth. Most
genre-authentic (cultivation novels skip years between arcs).

- **N1 — "Spirit-Beast Essence" (Tier 0 · age 3–7 · King's Landing).** The childhood
  antics ladder at full farce: the eggshell heist, the Kingsguard weirwood, "meditating"
  through lessons, the confoundment engine (each faction building her myth), the father's
  *delighted* misread-love. Turn: she eats a shard of *real* magic, runs a fever she calls
  "tribulation," and survives strangely — the first time the adults are *scared* instead of
  charmed. The myth grows teeth.
- **N2 — "The Furnace Sect" (Tier 1 · age 8–12 · KL guilds).** Schemes a maester
  apprenticeship for lab access; covets the Alchemists' Guild; the **wildfire caper** that
  "gives someone fits" and nearly burns down something that matters. The **dragonrider girl**
  enters. Marriage-talk begins (real threat). Turn: the stunt forces her father to punish or
  protect — the love-cage tightens — and she resolves she needs her *own* means, not his
  permission.
- **N3 — "The Long Roads" (Tier 2–3 · age 12–15 · Essos → Yi Ti / Sothoryos).** The
  trade-and-medicine revolution + the **entourage** assembles + the first real expedition for a
  far reagent (a Yi Tish herb; Sothoryi jungle materials). Turn: **first bodyguard death** — the
  cost made flesh; the comedy takes its first real wound. The love deepens; she brushes the
  deep-magic frontier (a whisper of the oily black stone). Returns home powerful, changed — and
  home is on the brink.
- **N4 — "The Dance" (Tier 4 + curdle · age 15–17 · the war).** Viserys dies; the war she never
  studied erupts around her. Dragons die → her windfall arrives amid apocalypse. Father gone,
  lover riding to war, an entourage member lost. She finally reaches for the forbidden deep magic
  — and the comedy is *gone*. The question lands: ascend alone over a field of corpses, or come
  down and be mortal with the people she loves while they're still alive. Ends on her choice.

**Trade-off:** complete and satisfying; the war is necessarily brisk.

## Outline B — "Volume One: The Reagent Years" (childhood-deep · series opener)

80k covers cradle → the *ignition* of the war. Goes deep and funny on the comedy years;
the Dance is the cliff the final page steps off. Tiers 0–2 fully, Tier 3 teased. The
global tiers + the Dance proper are Volume Two.

- **N1 — "Cradle" (Tier 0 · age 2–5).** The antics ladder, deep. Pure farce; the myth begins;
  the father's love established as the warm cage.
- **N2 — "The Apprentice" (Tier 1a · age 6–9).** Maester lab access; apothecary mastery; the
  first elixirs that *seem* to work (broken clock); the dragonrider girl enters.
- **N3 — "The Furnace & the Cage" (Tier 1b–2 · age 10–13).** Wildfire caper; trade networks
  begin; marriage pressure becomes a vise; the gay refusal goes from bit to genuine crisis.
- **N4 — "The Gathering Storm" (Tier 2–3 tease · age 13–15).** She builds real power (trade/
  medicine, first sworn swords); the realm fractures around her; Viserys ailing. Final pages: he
  dies, the war ignites, and she's standing in the middle of it holding a mortar and pestle —
  about to learn what the lessons she skipped were *for*.

**Trade-off:** richest comedy and character; defers the payoff — this is explicitly a Book 1.

## Outline C — "The People She Doesn't Save" (drama-forward · structured by loss)

Same events, but the novellas are anchored to **bonds she'll lose**, so the grief-curdle is
structural from page one rather than a third-act turn. More literary; comedy still rides on top,
but the spine is the cost ledger. Use this if you want the *drama* to drive.

- **N1 — The Father.** Childhood under Viserys's loving, uncomprehending shelter (Tier 0). The
  warmth and the cage. She takes him utterly for granted — he's just the mortal who feeds her
  reagent-hunt by accident.
- **N2 — The Lover.** The dragonrider girl (Tier 1–2). Love as the one thing she can't refine,
  meme, or transcend. The marriage plot as the blade over it.
- **N3 — The Sworn Sword.** The entourage and the far roads (Tier 3). A bodyguard who believes in
  her dies for an ingredient. First true grief — and she *refines it into resolve* instead of
  feeling it, which is the tragedy.
- **N4 — The Self.** The Dance (Tier 4). Everything she refused to mourn comes due at once. The
  final reckoning isn't "did she ascend" but "did she ever come down." Her own buried mortality —
  the toddler she actually is under the immortal act — is the last person she has to save.

**Trade-off:** most thematically potent and unified; the comedy is a garnish, not the meal —
furthest from the pure-farce brief.

## Ending `[STICKING]` — the bookkeeping escape

The finish isn't the grief-reckoning — it's an **escape**, and it's pure character. When the
Dance takes everything (father, maybe the lover, a sworn sword), Saerys does NOT break down and
mourn. She does what she always does: she **cooks the books.** Her real power was never
cultivation — it was the trade-and-medicine logistics empire she built, all manifests, writs, and
accounts — so she **steals a ship through bookkeeping shenanigans** (forged provisioning orders, a
falsified charter, a vessel that exists only as a clerical error) and sails off — **without
parental or royal consent** — on a **grand exploratory voyage** toward the deep-magic horizon she's
been reaching for all along (Sothoryos, Ulthos, the sunset sea, the lands the oily black stone came
from). The unsanctioned escape IS her refusal of mortality, dramatized: she will not stay and
grieve; she absconds toward transcendence, entourage in tow, leaving the war and the wheel of
dynasty literally over the horizon. Funny, cold, and the perfect payoff for a logistics magnate —
her climax is an accounting heist, not a battle. Also the natural launch-point for the voyage she
always wanted (sequel horizon).

Applies across outlines: it's A's N4 finish and C's N4 finish; for B (Volume One) it's the
opening of Volume Two.
