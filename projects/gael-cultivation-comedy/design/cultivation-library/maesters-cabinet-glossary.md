## In-World Access Structure: What a Princess Can Actually Raid

Understanding who controls these materials shapes every still-room / sick-house / poisoning scene. Three gatekeepers and one loophole:

**The Maester (primary gatekeeper).** The Citadel trains maesters in natural philosophy, medicine, and the properties of herbs, minerals, and preparations. A keep's maester is the de facto apothecary, physician, and pharmacist. He controls the still-room, the supply cabinet, and the knowledge of what each substance does. Grand Maester Mellos holds this role at the Red Keep under Viserys I. `[canon]`

**The Alchemists' Guild (secondary gatekeeper for fire-chemistry).** The Guild maintains a jealous monopoly on pyrotechnic and fire-based compounds — wildfire above all, but also saltpeter preparations, certain phosphorescent materials. `[canon]`

**The chain-link hierarchy (knowledge index).** A maester earns each link of his chain by demonstrating mastery of a field. Silver = medicine. Copper = history and records. Black iron = ravenry. Gold = money and accounting. Pale steel = smithing and building. Pewter = astronomy. Iron = warcraft. Valyrian steel = great distinction (rarely awarded). Red gold = the art of making fire. The chain's composition signals which disciplines a given maester has studied. `[canon — chain confirmed in ASOIAF; individual metal assignments mostly canon; some assignments canon-uncertain]`

**The charity / piety loophole (Saerys's exploit).** Alms-giving, sick-house tending, and the preparation of medicines for the poor are expected noble piety — unsupervised by definition and morally unimpeachable. This is the keystone of her supply-chain architecture. She sources through the charitable network what she cannot simply take from the supply cabinet. The maester's dependency on her ledgers (competence-as-foothold) gives her access to the rest. See `active-project/design/run-01/constraints.md` § 2 for the full loophole taxonomy.

**The gradient of access:**
- *Freely accessible (social license):* common culinary herbs (rosemary, thyme, ginger, garlic), freely traded spices, table salt, common vinegar, honey, beeswax, tallow candles. A princess simply has these.
- *Maester's still-room access:* willow bark, feverfew, chamomile, mint, common tisanes, boiled cloth, leeches, honey as wound dressing, mild purgatives. She has de facto access through the maester; he does not always know the frequency of her borrowing.
- *Locked cabinet access:* concentrated tinctures, prepared poisons, milk of the poppy above therapeutic doses, sweetsleep, strong emetics, mineral acids, arsenic preparations, quicksilver. Key obtained early. See `loc-still-room.card.md`.
- *External sourcing required:* Essosi exotics (tears of Lys, manticore venom, basilisk venom), rare Eastern herbs, the Strangler, substances traded through the Braavosi banking network or the below-stairs faction's import chain.
- *Effectively inaccessible (Book I baseline):* the Alchemists' Guild's locked preparations, wildfire (she encountered it by accident, not design), the oily black stone, weirwood paste (geographically remote).

## Category I — Analgesics, Sedatives, and Sleep Agents

### Milk of the Poppy
`[canon]` — named repeatedly in ASOIAF; the maester's standard surgical anesthetic and pain reliever.
**What it is:** An opium preparation — expressed latex of the opium poppy (*Papaver somniferum*), prepared as a thick white liquid or syrup. The active principles are morphine, codeine, and related alkaloids.
**Real properties:** Binds opioid receptors; produces analgesia, sedation, and respiratory depression. Therapeutic dose: deep pain relief + sleep. Elevated dose: unconsciousness. Toxic dose: respiratory arrest. Dependency develops with regular use.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** The maesters administer it before cutting or setting bones, and after serious wounds. It is the most-used pharmacological tool in the maester's kit. See `cond-westerosi-poison-pharmacology.card.md` for full dose-window.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** She calls it a "Tier 0–1 Water/Yin attribute qi-calmer." Accurately notes its calming the vital fire at low doses, extinguishing it at high. Points at Tier 0–4 table in `cultivation-genre-reference.md` § Part IV.B.

### Sweetsleep
`[canon]` — named in ASOIAF and HOTD; a sleep-inducing drug the maesters use.
**What it is:** A powerful sedative, mechanism analogous to barbiturate or strong benzodiazepine. The exact source-herb is `[canon-uncertain]`, but its profile suggests an extract of a concentrated hypnotic plant.
**Real properties:** Dose-dependent sedation → deep unconsciousness → respiratory depression → death, with a steep dose-response curve. The victim looks peaceful in death. Very difficult to detect.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Small dose = gentle sleep. Larger dose = unstoppable sleep (no waking). Death is quiet. Used by maesters pre-surgery; misused as a poison.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Yin-attribute qi-suppressant" Tier 1. Points at full classification in `cultivation-genre-reference.md`.

### Dreamwine
`[canon-uncertain]` — referenced in ASOIAF as a sleep/dream-inducing wine preparation; precise canon status unclear.
**What it is:** A wine-based preparation incorporating soporific herbs — likely a compound of valerian, hops, and possibly traces of sweetsleep or poppy in very small doses.
**Real properties:** Mild sedative; primarily calming effect; may produce vivid or memorable dreams in susceptible individuals. Much weaker than milk of the poppy or sweetsleep; appropriate for children and routine insomnia.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** A cup at bedtime induces easier sleep; not a surgical sedative. Not a significant poison risk at normal quantities.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 transitional — too weak for cauldron-tempering purposes; suitable for recovery-phase nights."

### Valerian
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — genuine medieval herb widely used as a sleep aid; plausible staple of any Westerosi still-room.
**What it is:** Root of *Valeriana officinalis*. Active compounds (valerenic acid, isovaleric acid) modulate GABA activity in the brain.
**Real properties:** Mild sedative and anxiolytic. Reduces sleep latency; decreases nighttime wakings. Non-addictive at standard doses. The root has a strong, unpleasant odor when dried.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Effective for mild insomnia and anxiety. Does not produce unconsciousness at any normally administered dose. A component of dreamwine-type preparations.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 Yin-herb; adequate for blending but insufficient as a primary reagent."

### Henbane (Black Henbane)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Hyoscyamus niger*; central medieval anesthetic and pain-relief herb; plausible in Westerosi still-rooms.
**What it is:** Contains hyoscine (scopolamine) and hyoscyamine — anticholinergic alkaloids.
**Real properties:** Strong sedative, analgesic, and antispasmodic. Produces drowsiness, confusion, and at higher doses hallucination and delirium; can cause death. Used in medieval surgery as part of the "soporific sponge" preparation alongside opium and mandragora. Narrow therapeutic window.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Low dose: calming, sedating, antispasmodic. Moderate dose: confusion, delirium, hallucination. High dose: death. The same compound as in nightshade (see § Category II) but in a different plant and concentration profile.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 early — overlaps with the nightshade series in attribute profile; useful as a supplementary tempering agent when the primary nightshade series is under tolerance consolidation."

### Mandrake (Mandragora)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Mandragora officinarum*; one of the most important medieval analgesic-soporific herbs; extremely plausible in a Westerosi maester's still-room given the Mediterranean-analogue climate of southern Westeros.
**What it is:** Contains scopolamine, hyoscyamine, and atropine — the same anticholinergic compound family as henbane and nightshade.
**Real properties:** Strong sedative, analgesic, emetic. Component of medieval surgical anesthesia (soporific sponge). The root has a strongly human-shaped form, which generated extensive folklore. At large doses: coma and death.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** The "soporific sponge" (henbane + mandrake + opium soaked into a cloth, held to the patient's face) was the closest thing to general anesthesia available in the medieval period. Effective but imprecise.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 — classified in the nightshade-henbane attribute cluster; body-form of the root classified as a Heavenly Dao cosmological coincidence with no practical significance."

### Opium (Raw / Crude)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — distinct from milk of the poppy (the prepared Westerosi extract); raw opium refers to dried latex from scored poppy capsules.
**What it is:** The dried, unprocessed latex from *Papaver somniferum*. Contains morphine, codeine, thebaine, papaverine in variable concentrations depending on crop and processing.
**Real properties:** The same opioid-receptor activity as milk of the poppy but less standardized and less predictable. Maesters would generally prefer the prepared extract (milk of the poppy) for known concentration. Raw opium is what a trader might stock; the still-room version is the processed extract.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Variable potency — higher risk of accidental overdose than the standardized preparation. The same dependency risk.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 raw-grade equivalent — less reliable than processed preparations; acceptable for initial tolerance-establishment work when only the raw form is accessible."

## Category II — Poisons and Toxins

*Primary cross-reference: `cond-westerosi-poison-pharmacology.card.md` for dose→lethality windows, tolerance ceilings, and the mithridatism efficacy assessment for each substance Saerys has directly worked with. This section covers the full cabinet — including substances she knows by report and has not yet sampled.*

### The Strangler
`[canon]` — ASOIAF: the poison used to kill Joffrey Baratheon; crystals hidden in Sansa's hairnet.
**What it is:** Small blue-violet crystals dissolved in wine. Causes violent muscular constriction of the throat and neck; death by asphyxiation. Characteristic presentation: purple-red discoloration of the face. The exact source-material is `[canon-uncertain]` — described as derived from rare plants from the Jade Sea region, Asshai, and the Westerlands.
**Real properties:** Pharmacological analog: possibly a concentrated strychnine-class compound or a novel convulsant targeting the laryngeal muscles specifically. Rapid-acting; narrow window between onset and death.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Fast-acting (minutes); very high lethality at effective dose. The face-color presentation makes it relatively identifiable post-mortem. See `cond-westerosi-poison-pharmacology.card.md` for Saerys's tolerance assessment (not yet sampled; classified as protocol gap).
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 high — Metal attribute constriction technique; the rapid mechanism makes tolerance protocol design difficult; flagged but not yet initiated."

### Tears of Lys
`[canon]` — ASOIAF: used repeatedly; one of the most refined fictional poisons in the world; mentioned in multiple ASOIAF books.
**What it is:** Odorless, colorless, tasteless poison that causes death through slow dissolution of vital organ tissue — mimics natural wasting disease. Produced in Lys, which has a cultural tradition of assassination-by-poison.
**Real properties:** Mechanism analogous to a concentrated digestive enzyme or corrosive compound targeting tissue specifically; slow-acting (hours to days); the wasting-disease presentation makes death appear natural.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Moderate dose: onset 6–12 hours; death 72–120 hours; appears to be natural illness throughout. See `cond-westerosi-poison-pharmacology.card.md` for full dose-window and Saerys's documented tolerance series (entries 100–115 in dose-log).
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 peak — primary operational reagent. Water-attribute qi-disruptor. The most-documented substance in the cauldron's tempering series."

### Sweetsleep (as poison, double entry)
See § Category I. When sweetsleep appears as a poisoning instrument rather than a sedative, the substance is the same; the intent and dose differ. Noted here for completeness as it crosses both categories in the texts. `[canon]`

### Nightshade / Belladonna
`[canon]` — referenced in ASOIAF as a poison/herb; in use throughout the texts.
**What it is:** *Atropa belladonna*. Active compounds: atropine, scopolamine, hyoscyamine — anticholinergic alkaloids.
**Real properties:** Low dose: anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, tachycardia, pupil dilation). Moderate dose: hallucination, delirium, fever. High dose: cardiovascular collapse, death. The hallucination range is narrow and the experience is typically terrifying rather than pleasurable.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Death possible at approximately 10–20 berries for an adult; root is more concentrated. Saerys has an established tolerance series (dose-log entries 3–15 per `cond-westerosi-poison-pharmacology.card.md`); notes that hallucination response persists at lower doses even with tolerance.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 early-to-spirit — Earth attribute with Void-element side effect (the hallucinations). Training reagent series."

### Wolfsbane / Monkshood / Aconite
`[canon]` — named in ASOIAF as a known poison/plant.
**What it is:** *Aconitum* species. Active compound: aconitine. One of the most acutely toxic plant-based substances.
**Real properties:** Opens sodium channels in cardiac and nerve tissue. Produces: tingling and numbness starting at the mouth, cardiac arrhythmia, extreme pain before numbness sets in, convulsions, respiratory and cardiac arrest. Lethal at very small absolute quantities.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** The tingling-mouth response is her calibration marker. A very narrow gap between noticeable and lethal in untreated individuals — her tolerance work has widened this gap. See `cond-westerosi-poison-pharmacology.card.md` for her documented tolerance ceiling (approximately 8-grain-weight; further escalation contra-indicated at current regime).
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 early-to-spirit — Fire/cold attribute duality (fire in early onset, cold in later numbness). Foundation training reagent."

### Hemlock
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Conium maculatum*; classical-world poison (Socrates); plausible in any pseudo-Mediterranean setting.
**What it is:** Contains coniine and gamma-coniceine — nicotinic acetylcholine receptor antagonists.
**Real properties:** Ascending paralysis from extremities toward the center; mind remains clear as the body fails. Death by respiratory paralysis when the diaphragm is affected. The subjective experience — fully conscious but unable to move — is documented in historical accounts.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Dose-dependent paralysis onset; sub-lethal doses produce peripheral weakness and tingling; lethal doses produce complete immobilization followed by respiratory arrest. See `cond-westerosi-poison-pharmacology.card.md` for her tolerance assessment: "the cauldron experienced a complete mobility tribulation; the consciousness-field remained active."
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 early — Metal-attribute restriction technique; the paralysis mechanism is the most direct bodily-immobilization reagent in the series."

### Widow's Blood
`[canon]` — named in ASOIAF (used to kill Jon Arryn in the original timeline). `[canon-uncertain]` — exact canonical composition unspecified.
**What it is:** Stops the workings of the bowels — produces death over days from internal impaction and systemic toxicity. One of the slowest and most agonizing deaths in the Westerosi poison canon.
**Real properties:** Likely a concentrated anticholinergic or opiate at high dose affecting gastrointestinal motility, or a botanical paralytic targeting smooth muscle. Extended lethality timeline (days).
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Progressive gastrointestinal shutdown over 3–7 days. The slow mechanism makes it useful for producing a "natural illness" appearance with a longer death window than tears of Lys.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Earth attribute — most directly attacks the cauldron's operational pathway (the elimination cycle); the cauldron has particular professional interest in resistance to this compound."

### Basilisk Venom / Blood
`[canon-uncertain]` — the basilisk appears in ASOIAF as an Essos creature; properties extrapolated; canon details thin.
**What it is:** Venom of the basilisk — described in Essosi context as dangerous; possibly also the creature's blood has toxic properties. Probable neurotoxin producing paralysis and death.
**Real properties:** Likely a protein-based neurotoxin (as with most reptilian venoms) that could produce paralysis, tissue destruction, or coagulation cascade disruption. The "stone attribute" Saerys assigns suggests she has observed a rigid or fixed presentation in victims.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Unknown precisely; she aborted one preliminary exposure (dose-log entry 89); rated as currently beyond her established tolerance. External sourcing required (Essos).
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 peak — Stone attribute; Rank-4 spirit-beast venom; flagged for Tier-2 testing window when the cauldron is prepared."

### Manticore Venom
`[canon-uncertain]` — the manticore appears in ASOIAF as an Essos creature; described as causing excruciating death; details `[canon-uncertain]`.
**What it is:** Venom of the manticore — described as causing extreme pain followed by death; likely a dual-mechanism toxin.
**Real properties:** The pain-first, death-later mechanism suggests a compound with a nociceptive (pain-signaling) component and a separate lethality mechanism (possibly cardiac or respiratory). Dual-mechanism toxins are harder to build tolerance to.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Rapid; she aborted initial preliminary (dose-log entry 133) at early-onset symptoms with acute pain response; protocol requires revision before re-entry. External sourcing required (Essos).
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 peak border / Tier 2 entry — dual Fire-and-Metal attribute venom from a Rank-5 spirit-beast. The cauldron is not yet prepared."

### Greycap / Death Cap
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Amanita phalloides*; the most lethal mushroom known; plausible in a temperate-climate pseudo-medieval setting.
**What it is:** Contains amatoxins (alpha-amanitin et al.), which inhibit RNA polymerase II and cause progressive destruction of liver and kidney tissue.
**Real properties:** Characterized by a symptom-free lag period of 6–24 hours after ingestion, followed by sudden violent gastrointestinal illness, apparent recovery (the "walking ghost" phase — 24–72 hours), then fulminant liver failure and death. Death within 5–10 days. No antidote in any pre-modern setting. Almost impossible to detect post-ingestion in the lag period.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** A single cap can kill an adult. The "walking ghost" phase makes it a perfect poisoning tool — the victim appears to be recovering when they are already past the point of no return.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 — Earth-attribute slow dissolution; the lag mechanism is noted as methodologically interesting for cases requiring temporal deniability; the cauldron has documented a tolerance series with caution (the mechanism does not yield clean tolerance thresholds via standard escalation)."

### Foxglove / Digitalis
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Digitalis purpurea*; a genuine medieval cardiac drug/poison; plausible in Westerosi still-rooms, possibly known by a different name.
**What it is:** Contains cardiac glycosides (digoxin, digitoxin). In therapeutic doses: slows and strengthens the heartbeat. In toxic doses: arrhythmia and cardiac arrest.
**Real properties:** Extremely narrow therapeutic window. Toxic dose causes nausea, vision disturbances (classic: seeing yellow halos around lights), heart block, and cardiac arrest. Death is cardiovascular collapse. The drug and the poison are the same compound at different doses.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Therapeutic: strengthens failing heart, slows racing pulse (atrial fibrillation). Toxic: arrhythmia and death. Historical use in treating dropsy (congestive heart failure). A maester would know it; most would have it.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0–1 Fire/Water balance reagent — acts on the heart's rhythm, which the cauldron reads as a qi-circulation modulator; interesting dual-use profile; tolerance work has been careful given the narrow window."

### Hemlock Water Dropwort
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Oenanthe crocata*; among the most acutely toxic plants of the temperate zone.
**What it is:** Contains oenanthotoxin, a compound that induces severe convulsions and respiratory failure.
**Real properties:** Produces a characteristic "sardonic grin" (risus sardonicus) as the facial muscles convulse — the historical origin of the phrase. Rapid-acting; can cause death within 3 hours of significant ingestion.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** A small amount of root causes convulsions rapidly. The death-presentation (convulsions, grin, collapse) is distinctive. Not ideal for a poisoning intended to appear natural.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 early — Wind/Thunder attribute; the convulsive mechanism is documented but not targeted for deep tolerance work given the speed of action and the signature presentation."

### Arsenic (White Arsenic / Arsenic Trioxide)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — arsenic preparations were widely used in medieval medicine and were the "poison of kings" of European history; would be known and present in Westerosi maesters' still-rooms.
**What it is:** Arsenic trioxide (As₂O₃) — a white odorless powder. Both a medicine (in tiny doses: tonics, skin treatments) and a classic slow poison.
**Real properties:** At low doses over time: peripheral neuropathy, fatigue, gastrointestinal disturbance. At larger doses: acute arsenic toxicity — vomiting, diarrhea, peripheral vascular collapse, death. Chronic low-dose exposure: skin changes (hyperkeratosis, Mees' lines on nails), neuropathy, liver damage. The classic slow-poison presentation (chronic illness over weeks/months) is distinctive.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** The maester's cabinet use: tonic-level doses in preparations for "strengthening the blood." The poison use: progressive doses mimicking illness. Mithridatism to arsenic is real and documented historically — the Mithra dates legend involves arsenic tolerance.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0–1 Earth-attribute mineral reagent; the historical tolerance-building precedent (the actual Mithridates precedent she has read about) is cited in her dose-log as foundational justification for her entire program."

### Lead (Lead Acetate / Sugar of Lead)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — lead compounds used in medieval and ancient medicine (lead acetate = sweet-tasting); a significant slow poison.
**What it is:** Lead acetate was used as a sweetener in wine and food; also appeared in cosmetics and medicines. The compound has a deceptively sweet taste.
**Real properties:** Lead is a neurotoxin accumulating in bone and tissue; chronic exposure causes progressive neurological damage, anemia, abdominal pain, cognitive decline, and eventually death. Acute large-dose poisoning causes encephalopathy and death. No route of elimination is fast — it accumulates.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Chronic low-dose: slow neurological decline. Acute: encephalopathy. No safe therapeutic dose. A maester familiar with the historical record would know that lead-sweetened wine caused widespread chronic poisoning in Roman contexts.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 mineral — classified under the Saturn planetary correspondence; noted as a foundational-grade impurity-accumulating compound whose mechanism (metal accumulation in bone) resonates with the body-tempering model, but the accumulation-without-elimination is a protocol concern."

### Ergot (St. Anthony's Fire)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Claviceps purpurea*; a fungus infecting rye; one of the most powerful naturally-occurring psychoactive and vasoconstrictive substances; known in medieval Europe.
**What it is:** Ergot alkaloids (ergotamine, lysergic acid amides) — powerful vasoconstrictors and partial LSD-like psychedelics. Found in contaminated grain.
**Real properties:** Ergotism: two presentations. Convulsive: seizures, hallucinations, distorted perception. Gangrenous: severe vasoconstriction causing burning sensations and dry gangrene of the extremities (historically: "St. Anthony's Fire" — the limbs appear to be on fire, then die). At extreme doses: cardiovascular collapse.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Contaminated bread has caused mass poisoning events historically (Salem witch trials, multiple medieval European outbreaks). The ergot-derived alkaloids include the chemical precursor to LSD.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 — Fire/Thunder attribute; the gangrenous mechanism is documented; the hallucination component is noted; the cauldron prefers cleaner isolated preparations over the contaminated grain form."

### Monkshood (second entry: topical application)
See above for primary entry. Noted here additionally: aconite preparations were used topically in medieval medicine as a numbing agent for nerve pain and arthritis — the skin absorbs aconitine and produces local numbness. The same compound, a different route of administration and vastly lower systemic dose. `[real-world / plausible in setting]`

## Category III — Antiseptics, Wound Care, and Surgical Materials

### Boiling Wine / Strong Wine
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — the standard medieval wound antiseptic; boiling wine poured directly into wounds to prevent infection; plausible throughout Westeros.
**What it is:** High-alcohol wine (or spirits of wine) applied to wounds. The alcohol — ethanol at sufficient concentration — denatures bacterial proteins and disrupts cell membranes.
**Real properties:** Genuinely effective antiseptic when alcohol content is sufficiently high (above ~20% vol as a minimum; higher is more effective). The "boiling" in the name refers to the sensation, not an actual temperature requirement, though warm application may improve effectiveness by increasing skin permeability.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Standard first-line wound treatment in medieval settings. Not as effective as modern antiseptics but substantially reduces infection compared to untreated wounds.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the most reliable and widely available wound-purification reagent in the Westerosi system; effective not because of qi but because of the alcohol principle (she correctly identifies the active component as the volatile distilled fraction)."

### Myrish Fire (Ethanol / Distilled Spirits)
`[canon-uncertain]` — referenced in ASOIAF in wound-care contexts; possibly a trade name for strong distilled spirits from Myr.
**What it is:** Distilled grain spirits or wine spirits — essentially concentrated ethanol, likely 50–70%+ alcohol content. The name "Myrish fire" suggests production or trade-route association with Myr.
**Real properties:** Higher-proof distilled spirits than ordinary wine; more effective antiseptic. The distillation process concentrates alcohol above what fermentation achieves alone.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Superior wound antiseptic to simple wine. Also used as a solvent and preparation medium (spirits of wine) for tinctures. See also § Category VIII (Bases, Solvents, and Preparation Media).
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — classified alongside boiling wine but at higher effectiveness-grade; she notes the distillation process concentrates the active mercury principle, consistent with the spagyric framework she has absorbed alongside her cultivation gloss."

### Honey
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — ancient wound dressing; plausible throughout pseudo-medieval settings including Westeros.
**What it is:** Concentrated sugar solution produced by bees; contains hydrogen peroxide (produced enzymatically from glucose), bee-derived antimicrobials, and a low-moisture environment hostile to bacterial growth.
**Real properties:** Effective wound dressing — the high osmolarity draws fluid from the wound (reduces bacterial activity), the hydrogen peroxide provides mild antisepsis, and the physical barrier prevents external contamination. Raw honey is more effective than processed honey (more enzyme activity).
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Applied directly to wounds and covered. Particularly effective for burns. Remains in active medical use.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — Earth-attribute stabilization reagent; noted as effective beyond what the basic classification implies; she uses raw honey sourced through the below-stairs faction (the kitchen supply) rather than the maester's jar, which she considers over-processed."

### Vinegar
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — acetic acid solution; classic medieval antiseptic and wound-wash.
**What it is:** Dilute acetic acid (typically 4–8% in table vinegar; stronger preparations possible). The acidity disrupts bacterial cell function.
**Real properties:** Antimicrobial against many common wound pathogens. Used diluted as a wound wash or wound pack. Also useful as a preparation medium for extractions (acetic acid as a mild solvent).
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Less effective than strong wine but widely available and suitable for wound packing and cleaning. The smell (acetic acid) is sharp and distinctive.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the acid-attribute base reagent; excellent for initial processing of plant materials and mineral preparations."

### Maggots (Wound Debridement)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — maggot therapy for infected wounds is ancient and medieval practice; documented across many cultures.
**What it is:** Sterile fly larvae (*Lucilia sericata* in modern use; any available blowfly larvae historically) applied to infected or gangrenous wounds. The maggots consume only necrotic tissue, not living tissue; they also produce allantoin (a cell-proliferation promoter) and secrete antimicrobial compounds.
**Real properties:** Effective debridement — removes dead tissue that would otherwise harbor infection. The limitation is revulsion on the part of both patient and practitioner, which is typically the main obstacle to use.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Applied to the wound, covered with a mesh to prevent escape, removed after 24–48 hours. Non-toxic. The most effective soft-tissue debridement available pre-modern.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "A biological-grade impurity-purging mechanism; the cauldron respects it precisely because it works and the mechanism is legible: the agent consumes only the waste."

### Leeches
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — bloodletting by leech was universal in medieval medicine; plausible staple of a Westerosi maester's kit.
**What it is:** *Hirudo medicinalis* or similar species. The maester's bloodletting tool. Leeches produce hirudin (an anticoagulant) and various anesthetic compounds in their saliva; a medicinal leech bite is relatively painless and bleeds freely.
**Real properties:** Bloodletting in the context of Galenic humoral medicine was the standard treatment for excess blood (the hot, wet humour). Modernity regards most Galenic bloodletting as net harmful. However, medicinal leeches genuinely remain in use for one application: post-surgical vascular congestion relief, where their anticoagulant saliva prevents venous stagnation at reattachment sites.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** A leech removes approximately 5–15 mL of blood before dropping off; a maester would apply multiple. The main risk is secondary infection if the leech site is not kept clean.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — Yin-attribute excess-extraction mechanism; she uses them pragmatically and has noted they are more useful than humoral theory predicts for the specific vascular congestion application."

### Fire / Cauterization
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — red-hot iron applied to wounds to stop bleeding and prevent infection; universal ancient and medieval practice.
**What it is:** Thermal cauterization — burning the wound surface to coagulate blood, seal vessels, and destroy surface tissue (including pathogens).
**Real properties:** Effective at stopping bleeding (hemostasis) and preventing some surface infections. Extremely painful. Destroys tissue which can impair healing. The maester would have a cauterization iron; Saerys's portable still-room kit's brazier can serve this function.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Used on wounds that will not stop bleeding, amputations, and infected tissue. The burn itself is a wound that must then be managed.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Fire-attribute cauterization — the body-tempering register classifies this as an external fire-tempering event rather than an internal one. She prefers internal tempering and notes that external fire application is, technically, inferior technique."

### Needle and Catgut (Suturing)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — suturing with catgut (processed animal intestine) and a metal needle is ancient surgical technique; standard in any competent maester's kit.
**What it is:** Catgut suture (processed sheep or goat intestine; the enzyme activity in the intestinal tissue provides some antimicrobial effect as the suture is absorbed) or silk/linen thread; a fine metal needle (bronze or iron in this setting).
**Real properties:** Wound closure promotes healing by holding edges together. Catgut sutures absorbed by the body over time, eliminating the need for removal. Silk/linen sutures require removal. The skill is in proper tension — too tight and you cut off blood supply; too loose and the wound reopens.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Standard wound care for clean cuts. In the maester's kit alongside the cauterization iron and the poppy.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "External body-repair technique; the body-tempering path prefers internal regenerative capacity over external structural correction; she nonetheless learned suturing at age seven and uses it when indicated."

### Boiled Cloth (Wound Dressing)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — boiling cloth to reduce contamination before applying to wounds; medieval-plausible.
**What it is:** Cloth (linen preferred) boiled in water before being used as a wound dressing or surgical cloth. The boiling kills most surface pathogens and loosens physical contaminants.
**Real properties:** Not true sterilization (that requires sustained high-temperature processing) but meaningfully reduces the microbial load introduced to a wound by a dressing.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Better than unboiled cloth; much better than nothing. The maester's standard pre-dressing practice for serious wounds.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 preparatory process — she insists on boiled cloth in the sick-house and notes (correctly) that wounds managed with clean cloth have significantly better outcomes, which she attributes to the cloth's qi-purification-by-heat rather than reduced pathogen load."

### Poultice (Plantain, Comfrey, Elder)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — poultices of specific wound-healing plants are ancient practice; plausible throughout Westeros.
**What it is:** A mashed plant preparation applied directly to the skin, usually held in place with a cloth. Common wound-poultice herbs:
- *Plantago major* (plantain): has documented antimicrobial and wound-healing properties; anti-inflammatory.
- *Symphytum officinale* (comfrey/knitbone): contains allantoin (promotes cell proliferation); traditional bone-healing and wound-healing herb.
- *Sambucus* species (elder): leaves traditionally used in wound poultices; mild anti-inflammatory.
**Real properties:** Plantain and comfrey poultices have genuine supportive wound-care properties — not dramatic, but real. Better than nothing; the practical wound-management kit.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Applied to bruises, minor wounds, sprains. Not a substitute for antisepsis for infected wounds.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the base layer of the apothecary's wound-care kit; she carries plantain and comfrey in the portable kit as a matter of course."

## Category IV — Fever, Inflammation, and Infection

### Willow Bark
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Salix* species; salicin-containing bark; the direct natural precursor to aspirin; plausible in any temperate-climate setting.
**What it is:** The inner bark of willow species, dried and prepared as a decoction or infusion. Contains salicin, which metabolizes to salicylic acid — the active anti-inflammatory and fever-reducing compound.
**Real properties:** Genuine analgesic, antipyretic (fever-reducing), and anti-inflammatory. Less concentrated than synthesized aspirin; requires larger quantity; slower onset. Does not cause the stomach irritation of aspirin in most preparations. A reliable, widely available fever treatment.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** A cup of willow bark decoction reduces mild-to-moderate fever and relieves headache and joint pain. Standard fever management in the sick-house.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the most reliable fever-management reagent in the Westerosi catalogue; she notes with professional satisfaction that the bark of a tree widely available at the roadside surpasses the maester's more expensive compound preparations for the basic fever application."

### Feverfew
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Tanacetum parthenium*; a traditional fever and headache herb with genuine anti-inflammatory properties.
**What it is:** A daisy-family plant. Contains parthenolide, which inhibits platelet aggregation and prostaglandin synthesis — genuine anti-inflammatory mechanism.
**Real properties:** Modest analgesic and antipyretic. Particularly traditional for headache/migraine relief, where clinical evidence is stronger than for general fever. Bitter taste; typically infused in tea.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Supportive fever care. Useful for headache-dominant presentations. A standard sick-house herb.

### Chamomile
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Matricaria chamomilla*; among the most widely-used medicinal herbs across Eurasian history; plausible throughout Westeros.
**What it is:** Dried flower heads of chamomile. Active compounds include apigenin (binds GABA receptors — mild sedative), alpha-bisabolol (anti-inflammatory), and various flavonoids.
**Real properties:** Mild sedative, anti-inflammatory, and antispasmodic. Genuine calming effect; good for anxiety and mild gastrointestinal spasm. Anti-inflammatory effect useful topically for skin irritation.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** A cup of chamomile tea has documentable mild anxiolytic and antispasmodic effect. Not a strong pharmacological intervention; valuable for symptomatic care and patient comfort.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the most socially acceptable vector for delivering a mild Yin-calming preparation to the sick-house patients who would otherwise refuse 'the apothecary's strange teas.' Useful for cover and for genuine calming effect."

### Elderflower and Elderberry
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Sambucus nigra*; traditional fever and cold remedy; plausible throughout temperate Westeros.
**What it is:** Elderflowers (anti-inflammatory, diaphoretic — induces sweating) and elderberries (antioxidant, antiviral-in-vitro, flavonoid-rich). Prepared as decoctions and syrups.
**Real properties:** Elderflowers genuinely promote sweating, which reduces fever through evaporative cooling. Elderberry syrup has some documented immunomodulatory effect (reduces severity and duration of cold-like illness in some studies). Not dramatic interventions; useful for symptomatic fever management.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** The "sweat the fever out" approach of traditional medicine has a genuine rationale: fever resolution often involves a sweating phase, and diaphoretic herbs support this. Elder preparations are a standard sick-house tool.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — valuable for patient-management applications; the diaphoretic mechanism is read as a qi-expulsion event (sweating = purging fire-attribute toxins)."

### Yarrow (Achillea)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Achillea millefolium*; one of the oldest documented medicinal plants; used as a wound herb, fever herb, and hemostatic; plausible throughout Westeros.
**What it is:** A feathery-leaved plant. Contains achilleine (hemostatic — stops bleeding) and various anti-inflammatory compounds.
**Real properties:** Genuine hemostatic (staunches bleeding — a field emergency application). Anti-inflammatory. Diaphoretic (fever-reducing through sweating induction). Bitter tonic for digestive issues.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** The field-dressing application: crushed or powdered yarrow applied directly to bleeding wounds. The tonic application: infusion for fever or digestive complaints. Standard across many traditional medicine systems.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — one of the most multi-purpose low-tier reagents; the hemostatic property is noted as particularly practical for field medicine (the sick-house operates outside the maester's surgical capacity)."

### Turmeric (and Analogues)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Curcuma longa*; known in the medieval Mediterranean/Asian trade routes; plausible as a trade spice reaching Westerosi ports, particularly in Oldtown and the coastal cities.
**What it is:** Dried rhizome of the turmeric plant. Active compound: curcumin — a potent anti-inflammatory compound.

## Category V — Digestive, Purgative, and Emetic Agents

### Mustard Seed (Emetic)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — mustard-seed preparations as emetics are ancient and medieval practice; universally plausible.
**What it is:** Warm water with large quantities of mustard powder acts as a rapid-onset emetic — the gastrointestinal irritant effect of allyl isothiocyanate induces vomiting within minutes.
**Real properties:** Effective emergency emetic for recent ingestion of poisons. Not always reliable; does not affect toxins already absorbed. Used as first-line treatment for poisoning by someone with no other options.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Several tablespoons of mustard powder in warm water induces vomiting within 5–15 minutes. The window for emesis-based poison management is narrow (within ~30 minutes of ingestion for most poisons).
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the most accessible emergency-reversal technique in the cauldron's protocol set. She notes with particular clarity that this is the first response for any suspected overdose, which is correct."

### Activated Charcoal (Charcoal as Absorbent)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — crude charcoal was used in medieval medicine as an absorbent; modern activated charcoal is a refinement of the same principle.
**What it is:** Finely powdered charcoal (not the same as the brazier charcoal — specifically prepared from organic material and processed at high temperature to maximize surface area). Binds to many toxins in the gastrointestinal tract, preventing absorption.
**Real properties:** One of the most effective poison-management interventions available pre-modern when used within 1–2 hours of ingestion. Binds alkaloids, heavy metals, and many organic toxins.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Large dose (50–100g in modern use; analogously a substantial quantity in the medieval context) swallowed immediately after known toxic ingestion. Turns the patient's evacuations black (useful as confirmation that it was administered and is passing through). Does not work for already-absorbed toxins.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — classified as an Earth-attribute impurity-binding reagent; the cauldron notes that this is one of the few substances whose mechanism she comprehends with more accuracy than most: it binds toxins because it has maximum surface area. She has noted this in the dose-log as 'the mechanism resonates with the impurity-removal step of pill refinement.'"

### Senna and Cascara (Cathartics)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — senna pods (*Cassia senna*) and cascara are classic cathartic (purgative) herbs; senna was traded along the Silk Road and reached medieval European apothecaries; plausible in Westerosi trade-port cities.
**What it is:** Contains anthraquinone glycosides which stimulate colonic motility (muscle contractions of the large intestine) — producing bowel evacuation.
**Real properties:** Reliable, predictable cathartic. Used by maesters for constipation and as part of general "humoral rebalancing" purging protocols. Also useful for eliminating toxins in the lower GI tract.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Takes 6–12 hours to act. Produces cramping and then bowel evacuation. Too large a dose causes excessive cramping and watery evacuation. Not dangerous at therapeutic doses.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — impurity-expulsion reagent; part of standard cauldron-maintenance protocol during the intervals between active tempering events."

### Rhubarb Root
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Rheum* species; the medicinal rhubarb root (not the cooking stalk) was widely traded; known in medieval European medicine as a purgative.
**What it is:** The dried root of *Rheum palmatum* or related species. Contains anthraquinones (similar to senna) as well as tannins (astringent — at lower doses, actually anti-diarrheal). Dose-dependent effect: small dose → constipation treatment; large dose → purgation.
**Real properties:** A classic bidirectional dose-dependent bowel regulator. Used for centuries in Chinese and European medicine.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Low dose: tonic, astringent effect. High dose: cathartic purgation. The dose-dependency makes it a useful flexible tool for the maester managing GI complaints.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — noted for its dose-dependent reversibility; she classifies this as evidence that the reagent holds two distinct qi-expressions depending on the cauldron's processing intensity."

### Ginger
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Zingiber officinale*; among the most widely traded spices; universally plausible in Westeros via the Essosi spice trade.
**What it is:** Rhizome of the ginger plant. Active compounds include gingerols and shogaols — anti-emetic, anti-inflammatory, and digestive-stimulating.
**Real properties:** Genuine anti-emetic (reduces nausea) — documented evidence for seasickness, morning sickness, and post-operative nausea. Also warms the digestive tract and promotes gastric motility (useful for sluggish digestion).
**Dose/effect at a glance:** A cup of ginger tea reduces nausea in most presentations. Useful in the sick-house for managing nausea as a symptom, including nausea from her own tempering events.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the primary anti-nausea reagent in the tempering-event management protocol; the Fire attribute is consistent with the warming sensation; she notes clinically that this is one of the few Tier 0 compounds she takes regularly for its genuine effect rather than its training value."

### Mint (Peppermint / Spearmint)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Mentha* species; universal medicinal herb across Eurasian history; plausible throughout Westeros.
**What it is:** Contains menthol (peppermint) or carvone (spearmint). Antispasmodic for smooth muscle; cooling sensation from menthol activating cold receptors without temperature change.
**Real properties:** Genuine antispasmodic for digestive cramping (relaxes the lower esophageal sphincter and intestinal smooth muscle — also causes reflux in susceptible individuals, but beneficial for spasmodic cramping). Anti-nausea effect. The cooling sensation is pharmacological, not actual temperature reduction.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Standard digestive herb; widely available; mint is one of the most common herbs in a Westerosi garden or still-room.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — Wind/Water attribute; the cooling-without-cold mechanism is noted as an interesting example of attribute expression (Wood) versus actual element (not cold); she uses this as a teaching example in her private notes on attribute classification."

### Wormwood (Artemisia absinthium)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Artemisia absinthium*; the classic bitter digestive tonic of medieval European medicine; also an anthelmintic (expels intestinal worms).
**What it is:** Contains absinthin and other bitter sesquiterpene lactones; also thujone (a mild convulsant at very high doses — the basis for absinthe's dubious mystique).
**Real properties:** Genuine bitter digestive tonic (stimulates bile production and gastric secretion). Effective anthelmintic — parasitic worms are expelled by wormwood preparations. At therapeutic doses: safe. At large doses: thujone toxicity (convulsions, but requires very large quantities of the plant material).
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Standard anti-parasitic and digestive tonic. Also a component of moon tea preparations (see § Category VI).
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the bitterness-as-qi-stimulation principle; she notes that the extremely bitter plants (wormwood, gentian) produce a sharper digestive response than the sweet ones, which she reads as Fire-attribute stimulation rather than bile secretion."

### Garlic and Onion
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Allium* species; universal cooking and medicinal herbs; universally plausible throughout Westeros.
**What it is:** Garlic contains allicin and related organosulfur compounds, released when the clove is crushed. Genuine antimicrobial properties — allicin inhibits bacterial growth through enzyme inhibition.
**Real properties:** Genuine antimicrobial, antifungal, and antiviral effects (documented in vitro and in some clinical studies). Used historically for wound antisepsis, respiratory infections, and general "strengthening" — the antimicrobial effects are real, if less dramatic than the folk reputation.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Raw crushed garlic has more active allicin than cooked garlic. Applied to wounds or consumed for respiratory infections. A cheap and widely available antimicrobial.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the most mundane but also the most accessible tier-0 compound; she includes it in the sick-house diet for patients with respiratory infections with a note that the mechanism is Fire-attribute qi-expelling (which is wrong about why, correct about the respiratory benefit)."

## Category VI — Women's Medicine and Reproductive

### Moon Tea
`[canon]` — named explicitly in ASOIAF as a Westerosi contraceptive/abortifacient preparation; widely used.
**What it is:** An herbal contraceptive/early abortifacient preparation. The exact recipe varies by source and region; the canonical components are tansy and pennyroyal as the primary actives, with mint and other herbs filling out the formulation.
**Real properties:** Moon tea is a real pharmacological preparation — the herbs named have genuine abortifacient or contraceptive effects (see individual entries below). It is not infallible; effectiveness depends on dose, timing, and preparation. Also carries risks: the effective dose for abortifacient effect is dangerously close to the toxic dose for most of the active components.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Used for contraception (regular low-dose) or early pregnancy termination (higher dose, early timing). Known and used widely in Westeros at all social strata — maesters prepare it for highborn ladies; midwives and herbwomen prepare rougher versions for common women.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — Yin-cycle regulatory preparation; she has documented the component analysis in the dose-log and notes that the preparation is pharmacologically coherent but poorly standardized in most Westerosi practice; she has developed a standardized formulation for the sick-house."

### Tansy
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Tanacetum vulgare*; a classic European abortifacient; the primary active in moon tea.
**What it is:** Contains thujone (concentrated in the essential oil) and other terpenoids with uterotonic properties. Traditionally used to bring on menstruation and induce abortion.
**Real properties:** Genuinely uterotonic — the thujone and other compounds stimulate uterine contractions. The problem: the effective uterotonic dose overlaps significantly with the toxic dose. Thujone at high doses causes convulsions; tansy overdose has historically caused death. The margin is narrow.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** At correct dose: menstrual induction / early abortifacient. At overdose: convulsions, organ failure, death. A substance that requires careful dosing.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0–1 — primarily a Yin-cycle regulator but with a dangerous Fire-attribute threshold above which it becomes a convulsant; she has documented the tansy toxicity threshold with particular care and notes that the 'moonblood regulation' applications require the most precise dosing of any Tier 0 compound in her still-room."

### Pennyroyal
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Mentha pulegium*; the other primary European abortifacient; widely known in medieval medicine.
**What it is:** A mint-family plant containing pulegone (metabolizes to the toxic compound menthofuran in the liver). Abortifacient through multiple mechanisms including uterine stimulation and possible direct toxicity.
**Real properties:** Genuine abortifacient but extremely dangerous. The toxic dose is very close to the effective abortifacient dose; pennyroyal oil overdose causes liver and kidney failure and death. The history of pennyroyal-related deaths in women attempting self-induced abortion is extensive.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Effective at inducing abortion but the therapeutic window is extremely narrow. A maester who prepares moon tea would use a carefully measured low concentration. The component is also used in lower doses as an insect repellent and mint-flavoring.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0–1 — the most dangerous compound in the moon tea formulation; she has documented a specific dosing protocol for the sick-house that errs toward lower concentrations of pennyroyal relative to tansy, based on observed risk profiles in the literature she has accessed (she has read every text in the maester's library on women's medicine)."

### Mugwort (Artemisia vulgaris)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — traditional uterotonic herb used alongside tansy and pennyroyal in European abortifacient preparations.
**What it is:** Contains artemisinin (the compound now used in malaria drugs), camphor, and various terpenoids. Traditionally used to "promote menstruation" — uterotonic effects.
**Real properties:** Documented uterotonic activity weaker than tansy or pennyroyal; often used in combination. Also has antiparasitic properties from the artemisinin fraction.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** A gentler uterotonic; used in combination preparations rather than alone. The antiparasitic component is an unexpected secondary benefit in the sick-house context.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the supporting Yin-regulator in the moon tea series; she notes the artemisinin fraction makes mugwort doubly useful (menstrual regulation + parasite management) and has cross-referenced it with the anthelmintic protocols."

### Blue Cohosh
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Caulophyllum thalictroides*; North American uterotonic used by indigenous populations; may have reached European apothecaries via trade routes; plausible as a Essosi import.
**What it is:** Contains alkaloids (methylcytisine) and saponins with uterine-stimulating activity.
**Real properties:** Uterotonic; can induce premature labor. Toxic at higher doses; can cause cardiovascular complications. Historically used in birthing preparations in some traditions.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Strong uterotonic; historically used to accelerate slow labor or stimulate a late delivery, not just as an abortifacient. High risk of cardiovascular complications at elevated doses.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 early — more potent than mugwort or tansy in the uterotonic register; classified as a Yin-activation compound rather than a simple regulator."

### Raspberry Leaf
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Rubus idaeus*; traditional preparation for uterine toning, particularly in late pregnancy.
**What it is:** Dried leaves of the raspberry plant; contain fragarine, which has mild uterine tonic properties, and various tannins, vitamins, and minerals.
**Real properties:** Weak uterotonic; used throughout pregnancy to "tone" the uterus and prepare for labor. Also a nutritive tonic. The evidence for significant pharmacological effect is modest; its use in late pregnancy is primarily traditional and nutritive.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** A gentle pregnancy tonic; extremely unlikely to cause harm at normal doses. The maester's still-room version of prenatal care.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the minimum-intervention Yin-maintenance compound; useful for the sick-house's maternal care cases."

### Black Cohosh
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Actaea racemosa*; North American plant also used as a uterotonic; plausible as Essosi import; distinct from blue cohosh.
**What it is:** Contains triterpene glycosides with weak hormonal (estrogenic) activity. Used traditionally to induce labor and regulate menstruation.
**Real properties:** Documented estrogenic-type effects. Used in traditional medicine for menstrual irregularity, menopause symptoms, and to stimulate labor. Potential liver toxicity at high doses (documented rare cases).
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Mild estrogenic compound; useful for menstrual regulation; labor induction at higher doses. Safer profile than tansy or pennyroyal.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — Yin-hormone-adjacent reagent; she uses the estrogenic-effect pattern as a calibration reference for distinguishing Yin-balance compounds from straightforward uterotonic compounds."

**What it is:** Wine fortified with honey and spiced with ginger, cinnamon, cardamom, and other warming spices. Used as a restorative and digestive tonic.
**Real properties:** The wine provides ethanol as an antiseptic and mild sedative; the spices provide genuine anti-inflammatory, digestive, and warming effects; the honey provides energy. The combination is calorie-dense and palatable.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** The medieval equivalent of a hot toddy. Standard sick-house restorative for patients well enough to drink warm liquids.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the acceptable public face of the cauldron's tonic protocol; she administers fortified wine to sick-house patients because no one objects to offering a well-prepared warming drink, while the ginger and honey serve legitimate purposes she does not need to explain."

## Category VIII — Minerals and Chemical Reagents

### Sulfur / Brimstone
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — elemental sulfur; known throughout antiquity; central to alchemical tradition; plausible in any Westerosi still-room and in the Alchemists' Guild.
**What it is:** Yellow crystalline solid; the sulfur principle in the tria prima. Burns with a distinctive blue flame and acrid SO₂ fumes. Used in fumigation, as an antiseptic, and in early chemical preparations.
**Real properties:** Topical antiseptic — sulfur ointment was used for skin infections including scabies and ringworm. The SO₂ gas from burning sulfur was used to fumigate spaces against insect infestation and (incorrectly) against disease. An ingredient in gunpowder (saltpeter + sulfur + charcoal) and in wildfire preparations.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Topical: fungicidal and parasiticide. Fumigation: useful against insects; the gas is irritating to mucous membranes. A maester would have it; the Alchemists' Guild would have it in quantity.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — sulfur principle; the yellow color confirms its solar correspondence and Fire-attribute classification; she uses it in fumigation as a matter of course but notes that its qi-value is limited to its cleansing attribute rather than a body-tempering one."

### Quicksilver / Mercury
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — liquid mercury; central to alchemical tradition; used in medieval medicine; plausible in Westerosi alchemists' and maesters' cabinets.
**What it is:** Elemental mercury — a liquid metal at room temperature. Silvery, heavy, flowing. The mercury principle in the tria prima.
**Real properties:** Used in medieval medicine as a treatment for syphilis (highly toxic; the dose that killed syphilis often killed the patient — "a night with Venus, a lifetime with Mercury" was the proverb). A potent neurotoxin, nephrotoxin, and contact poison. Volatilizes at room temperature; mercury vapor is highly toxic.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Heavy metal poisoning: neurological damage, kidney failure, death. Used in some skin preparations (mercuric chloride as an antiseptic — effective but extremely dangerous). Also used in gilding, mirror-making, and alchemical preparations.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 1 early — the mercury principle in material form; the volatility (it moves like qi without being qi) is classified as a Wind-attribute expression; she works with it very carefully and notes that the tria prima identification of mercury as the 'fluid principle' is surprisingly accurate for a non-cultivationist framework."

### Antimony (Stibnite)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — *Stibium*; used in ancient and medieval medicine and cosmetics (kohl eyeliner); plausible in Westerosi contexts, particularly Essosi trade goods.
**What it is:** Antimony compounds (primarily antimony sulfide — stibnite). Used as a cosmetic (kohl), in some medicines as an emetic, and in alchemical preparations.
**Real properties:** Antimony compounds are toxic — they produce nausea, vomiting, and at high doses organ failure. The emetic use exploits this toxicity. As kohl eyeliner, the absorption through the conjunctiva is low enough to be primarily decorative.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Powerful emetic at medicinal dose. Chronic low-dose exposure from cosmetic use is toxic at accumulated levels. Antimony sulfide was sometimes used as a laxative.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0–1 — the classic 'medicine at one dose, poison at another' compound; she has a particular interest in these compounds for what they reveal about the dose-dependency principle."

### Arsenic (cross-reference; see § Category II)
Extended note here: arsenic's role in the maester's cabinet goes beyond its poison application. Arsenic compounds were used in medieval medicine as tonics, skin treatments, and for treatment of certain fevers. The maester's cabinet would typically have arsenic trioxide in both capacities. See § Category II for the primary entry.

### Alum (Aluminum Potassium Sulfate)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — alum was universally traded in medieval Europe and the Mediterranean; its applications are numerous; plausible throughout Westeros.
**What it is:** Aluminum potassium sulfate, a double salt. Strongly astringent; used as a hemostatic, in tanning, in dyeing, and as a food preservative.
**Real properties:** Strong astringent — causes tissue contraction and protein precipitation on contact. Used to stop minor bleeding (hemostatic), treat mouth sores and canker sores, and as a styptic pencil equivalent. Also used in preparing fabrics for dyeing (mordanting) and in preserving animal hides.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Applied topically to bleeding wounds — coagulates surface tissue and stops bleeding. Also useful for mouth ulcers and as a general wound-styptic.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the most reliable minor-hemostatic in the still-room kit; she carries it in the portable kit as a standard-supply item."

### Saltpeter (Potassium Nitrate)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — potassium nitrate occurs naturally and was produced from animal waste decomposition; an alchemical reagent; a component of gunpowder; plausible in Westeros particularly in the Alchemists' Guild's work.
**What it is:** KNO₃ — potassium nitrate. White crystalline solid; strong oxidizer.
**Real properties:** Used as a food preservative (curing salt for meats). A component of black powder/gunpowder (with sulfur and charcoal). In medicine, used as a diuretic and for treating chest pain from certain cardiac conditions (similar effect to nitroglycerin — dilates blood vessels).
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Medicinal use: mild diuretic, vasodilator. The Alchemists' Guild would have large quantities for pyrotechnic preparations.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the Salt principle in its most visible form; she notes that saltpeter's role in the Guild's fire-preparations is consistent with the Salt-as-body principle: it is the stable, fixed fraction that gives the fire-preparation its structure."

### Lime (Calcium Oxide / Calcium Hydroxide)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — quicklime (CaO) and slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂); ancient chemical materials used in construction and in disinfection.
**What it is:** Quicklime (burned limestone) and its product on contact with water, slaked lime. Strongly alkaline; caustic to tissue.
**Real properties:** Caustic disinfectant — kills pathogens through high pH. Used historically to disinfect latrines, burial sites, and disease-contaminated areas. In medicine: used topically as a caustic agent to treat skin infections, warts, and other surface lesions. Internally dangerous — causes severe tissue damage.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** External: cauterizing and disinfecting agent. The sick-house use is primarily sanitation — the lime wash of floors and surfaces.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the simplest Earth-attribute purification compound; she uses lime wash throughout the sick-house and notes that the death rate in lime-washed spaces is lower than in untreated spaces, which she attributes to qi-purification of the air but which is actually pathogen suppression."

### Vitriol (Iron Sulfate / Copper Sulfate / Sulfuric Acid)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — "vitriol" in medieval chemistry referred to metal sulfates; "oil of vitriol" (sulfuric acid) was produced by distillation; important alchemical reagents.
**What it is:** Blue vitriol = copper sulfate. Green vitriol = iron sulfate. White vitriol = zinc sulfate. Oil of vitriol = concentrated sulfuric acid (produced by distillation of metal sulfates — a genuine early chemical process).
**Real properties:** Copper sulfate: emetic, antiseptic, antifungal; toxic in significant quantities. Iron sulfate: used to treat iron-deficiency anemia. Zinc sulfate: used as an antiseptic eyewash. Oil of vitriol (sulfuric acid): corrosive acid; used for dissolving metals and minerals; extremely dangerous.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Each vitriol has specific uses. The maester's still-room would have copper sulfate (antiseptic/emetic) and possibly iron sulfate. Oil of vitriol is an advanced alchemical reagent more likely in the Guild's laboratory.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0–1 (the sulfate salts); Tier 1 (the oil) — she notes the vitriol series as an example of how the same fundamental qi-pattern (sulfur-acid) manifests differently depending on the Salt-complement."

### Copper Salts (Verdigris)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — verdigris (copper acetate) produced from copper exposed to acetic acid; used in medieval medicine and as a pigment.
**What it is:** Copper acetate/carbonate compounds — the green patina on copper. Both a pigment and a medicinal compound.
**Real properties:** Antiseptic; used topically on wounds. Also causes copper toxicity if absorbed in significant quantities — nausea, vomiting, liver damage.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Topical antiseptic. The toxicity risk means it is used externally only and with care.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — copper-element expression; she notes the color (green, associated with Wood-attribute in the five-element system) creates an interesting cross-classification problem: copper produces a Wood-color result but through a Metal mechanism."

### Salt (Sodium Chloride)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — sea salt and rock salt; universal; plausible everywhere in Westeros.
**What it is:** Sodium chloride — table salt. The most fundamental and widely traded mineral.
**Real properties:** Essential electrolyte for human physiology. Preservative (kills spoilage bacteria through osmotic water removal). Saline solution (salt water) is used to irrigate wounds; isotonic saline is approximately the same concentration as body fluids.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Wound irrigation: dilute saline removes debris and provides a gentle antiseptic environment. Preservation: enough salt prevents bacterial growth entirely. Electrolyte replacement in dehydrated or ill patients.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the Salt principle in its purest form; she uses salt solution extensively in the sick-house for wound care and considers it the most accessible and underestimated reagent in the cabinet."

## Category IX — Bases, Solvents, and Preparation Media

### Spirits of Wine (Distilled Ethanol)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — distilled wine spirits; the standard solvent of medieval pharmacy; produced by distillation of wine; plausible in the Westerosi context, particularly in Myr and in the Citadel's alchemical laboratories.
**What it is:** High-proof ethanol — wine distilled to concentrate the alcohol fraction. In medieval pharmacy, "spirits of wine" is the universal solvent for tinctures, dissolving active compounds from plant materials.
**Real properties:** An excellent solvent for organic alkaloids (the active compounds in most medicinal herbs). Tinctures prepared with spirits of wine are more concentrated and longer-lasting than water infusions. Also an antiseptic above ~20% ethanol concentration; more effective as the percentage rises.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Primary preparation medium for concentrated tinctures. Also used directly as wound antiseptic (see Myrish fire, § Category III). The small alembic in her portable kit can distill wine to produce spirits-quality concentration.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the mercury-principle solvent; she notes that spirits of wine extracts more active principle than any other solvent she has tested, which she attributes to its mercury-principle resonance with the volatile fraction of each herb; this is a functional description of what solvation actually does, in the wrong vocabulary."

### Olive Oil and Infused Oils
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — olive oil is a staple trade good in pseudo-Mediterranean settings; widely available throughout Westerosi coastal cities.
**What it is:** Expressed oil of *Olea europaea* olives. Used as a cooking oil, skin emollient, and as a base for oil-extraction of fat-soluble medicinal compounds (infused oils).
**Real properties:** An excellent solvent for fat-soluble medicinal compounds — some herbal compounds are fat-soluble and are poorly extracted by water but well-extracted by oil. Olive oil as an emollient has genuine skin-soothing and barrier-protective properties. Also a caloric food for convalescent patients.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Infused oils (calendula-infused, lavender-infused, comfrey-infused) are applied topically for skin conditions, burns, and wound care. Consumed as a laxative in large quantities.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the Earth-attribute extraction base; she maintains a small collection of infused oils in the still-room for topical preparations."

### Lard and Tallow
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — rendered animal fat; the standard unguent base of medieval pharmacy.
**What it is:** Rendered pig fat (lard) or beef/mutton fat (tallow). Used as the base for ointments, salves, and skin preparations.
**Real properties:** An effective emollient base — fat-based preparations penetrate skin and carry fat-soluble compounds into tissue. Tallow has a higher melting point than lard and is more suitable for firm ointments; lard is softer and better for topical wound preparations.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** Applied topically as a vehicle for other compounds. Also useful as a plain skin barrier in cold or dry conditions.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the Salt-principle base; she uses lard for topical preparations and notes that the still-room's chronic fat-base preparations are among the most stable preparations she makes."

### Beeswax
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — beeswax was universally available in pre-modern settings.
**What it is:** Wax secreted by honeybees. Melting point ~60–65°C. Used as a vehicle/thickener in ointments and creams; also for candles and waterproofing.
**Real properties:** Inert — has no direct pharmacological effect but provides physical structure to topical preparations. An ointment made with beeswax is more stable and less likely to go rancid than a pure lard preparation. Also a physical skin barrier.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** The stiffener/structuring agent in unguents and creams. Pharmaceutical ointments (beeswax + lard or oil + active compound) have been the standard topical preparation form for centuries.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the least reactive component in the preparation matrix; she classifies beeswax as a 'formation-stabilizer' that holds the preparation's qi-pattern in place without contributing its own."

### Vinegar (double entry as solvent)
See § Category III for the antiseptic use. As a solvent: dilute acetic acid (vinegar) is useful for extracting alkaloid compounds from plant materials through acid-base chemistry — alkaloids (which are bases) dissolve well in acidic solutions. Used for preparing tinctures of some herbs, particularly those with poorly water-soluble alkaloids.

### Water
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — the most fundamental preparation medium.
**What it is:** Water used for infusions, decoctions, and dilutions. Quality matters: clean spring water or boiled water versus contaminated water.
**Real properties:** An effective solvent for water-soluble compounds (polar molecules, sugars, some alkaloids). Clean water is essential for all preparations — contaminated water introduces infection risk. Boiling water is used both to dissolve compounds (hot infusion/decoction) and to reduce contamination.
**Dose/effect at a glance:** The baseline preparation medium. Infusions (herbs steeped in hot water — tea method) and decoctions (herbs simmered in water) are the most basic pharmaceutical forms.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "Tier 0 — the Water attribute in its most basic form; she insists on clean sourced water in all still-room preparations, which is correct and for the wrong reasons (she attributes the improved outcomes to Water-qi purity)."

## Category X — Preparation Forms

*Not individual substances but the forms and preparations that the maester's still-room produces. Understanding these is essential for staging any still-room / apothecary scene with accuracy.*

### Tincture
`[real-world / plausible in setting]`
**What it is:** A preparation made by macerating plant material in spirits of wine (or sometimes vinegar or water-alcohol mixture) for an extended period, then straining off the liquid. The liquid retains the dissolved active compounds.
**Properties:** Concentrated; stable (the alcohol prevents spoilage); consistent dose across batches; longer shelf life than fresh preparations. Applied in drops or small measured doses.
**Still-room relevance:** Her most common preparation form for concentrated medicinal compounds. The labeled vials in the maester's still-room and her parallel labeling system behind the jars are primarily tinctures.

### Decoction
`[real-world / plausible in setting]`
**What it is:** Plant material simmered in water for 15–30 minutes (longer for roots and bark than for leaves and flowers), then strained. Less concentrated than a tincture; less stable; must be used fresh or within a day or two.
**Properties:** Good for releasing compounds from hard plant materials (roots, bark, seeds). The heat can destroy some volatile compounds while extracting others.
**Still-room relevance:** Used for bark preparations (willow bark, cinchona), root preparations (valerian, ginger), and any material where a hot-water extraction is the most appropriate solvent.

### Infusion
`[real-world / plausible in setting]`
**What it is:** Plant material steeped in hot water without extended simmering — the tea method. Used for delicate plant materials (leaves and flowers) where heat-sensitive compounds would be destroyed by boiling.
**Properties:** Less concentrated than decoction; more concentrated than cold-water maceration. Fresh only.
**Still-room relevance:** Chamomile, mint, elderflower, and similar preparations are made as infusions. The sick-house's daily tonic teas are primarily infusions.

### Salve / Unguent
`[real-world / plausible in setting]`
**What it is:** A solid or semi-solid preparation of fat (lard, tallow) plus beeswax plus active compounds, applied topically. The fat vehicle penetrates skin, carrying the active compounds into tissue.
**Properties:** Physical barrier function plus active-compound delivery. Shelf-stable when fat quality is good.
**Still-room relevance:** Wound salves (honey + comfrey + plantain), antiseptic preparations (sulfur ointment, copper-based preparations), and skin preparations are made as salves.

### Poultice
See § Category III (Wound Care).

### Powder
`[real-world / plausible in setting]`
**What it is:** Dried, ground plant material or mineral compound in powdered form. Applied topically, sprinkled in a wound, mixed into food, or dissolved in liquid.
**Properties:** Long shelf life when properly dried and stored; versatile application. Concentration can be precisely measured by weight.
**Still-room relevance:** Her precision scale and powder preparations are how she conducts her dose-escalation work — exact weights matter. The dose-log records in grain-weights.

### Pill / Bolus
`[real-world / plausible in setting]`
**What it is:** Active compounds bound with honey, beeswax, bread crumb, or a similar binder, formed into a pill shape for swallowing. The standard medieval oral dosage form.
**Properties:** More convenient than loose powder; easier to dose precisely than a decoction; can mask bitter or unpleasant taste.
**Still-room relevance:** The maester's standard pill preparation. She uses this form for stable, precisely-dosed preparations intended for the sick-house patient population.

### Syrup
`[real-world / plausible in setting]`
**What it is:** An aqueous preparation heavily concentrated with honey or sugar, incorporating active compounds. The sugar content acts as a preservative.
**Properties:** More stable than plain decoctions; palatable; good for children and those who cannot swallow pills.
**Still-room relevance:** Elderberry syrup, cough syrups, tonic syrups for convalescent patients.

### Plaster
`[real-world / plausible in setting]`
**What it is:** A medicated preparation spread on cloth or leather and applied to the skin — a patch system, essentially. Used for sustained local delivery of compounds (drawing poultices, counterirritants, warming preparations).
**Properties:** Extended local delivery; can be left in place for hours. The "mustard plaster" (drawing out heat) is a classic form.
**Still-room relevance:** Used for joint pain, chest congestion, and external treatment of localized infections.

### Fumigation
`[real-world / plausible in setting]`
**What it is:** Burning herbs, sulfur, or other materials to produce medicinal smoke. Inhaled or used to treat a space.
**Properties:** Reaches the respiratory tract directly; room-fumigation kills insects and was used (with variable justification) to treat disease.
**Still-room relevance:** Sulfur fumigation for insect control; aromatic herb fumigation (rosemary, lavender, thyme) for the sick-house air quality; she uses fumigation to manage the still-room's olfactory profile.

## Category XI — Tools and Equipment

*The instruments of the maester's still-room and Saerys's portable kit. Understanding these makes any still-room scene concrete.*

### Mortar and Pestle
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — universal pharmacological tool; granite, marble, iron, or ceramic; universally plausible.
**What it is:** A bowl (mortar) and a club-shaped instrument (pestle) used to grind plant materials, minerals, and compounds into powder. The grinding surface is roughened to increase friction.
**Real properties:** The most basic pharmaceutical implement. Essential for producing powders from dried plant material; for breaking down mineral compounds; for mixing semi-solid preparations.
**Still-room relevance:** The fixed mortar in the still-room is her primary reduction tool. A smaller mortar travels in the portable kit. The still-room's ambient sounds include the grinding of the mortar during active preparation work.

### Scales and Balance
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — precision scales are ancient and plausible throughout Westeros.
**What it is:** A beam balance with counterweights, or a precision scale. Measures weight in apothecary units (grains, scruples, drams, ounces in the Roman/medieval system).
**Real properties:** Essential for precise dosing — the difference between a therapeutic dose and a lethal dose is often grams or fractions thereof. The maester's kit would include scales; her portable kit has her own scale that she calibrated at age eight and trusts in preference to any other.
**Still-room relevance:** The dose-log records everything in grain-weights (her personal unit established from her own scale). Any scene involving her preparation work includes the scale.

### Alembic (Still)
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — the alembic was the standard distillation apparatus of medieval and early-modern chemistry; plausible in the Westerosi still-room context, particularly given the Alchemists' Guild's distillation work.
**What it is:** A distillation apparatus: a flask (the body, or cucurbit) heated over a flame; a condensing cap (the alembic head, or alembic proper) that condenses the vapor; a receiver flask that collects the distillate. Used to separate compounds by boiling point — producing concentrated distillates (spirits of wine from wine; essential oils from plant material; concentrated medicinal extracts).
**Real properties:** The key tool for producing concentrated preparations. The small travel alembic in her portable kit produces spirits-of-wine concentration from wine; the fixed alembic in the still-room is larger and used for extended distillation runs.
**Still-room relevance:** Her alembic is mentioned in the `loc-still-room.card.md` portable kit section. It is the most sophisticated piece of equipment she travels with.

### Crucible
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — a heat-resistant vessel for high-temperature work; standard in any alchemical or assay laboratory.
**What it is:** A small, high-temperature-resistant vessel (clay, graphite, or metal) used for calcination, melting, and high-heat chemical operations. Holds material at temperatures that would crack ordinary ceramic.
**Real properties:** Used for calcining mineral preparations (reducing to ash/oxide), for assaying metals, for high-temperature extractions.
**Still-room relevance:** Less commonly used in her work than the alembic and mortar; reserved for mineral-preparation work.

### Scalpel and Surgical Blades
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — iron or bronze surgical blades; the maester's surgical kit.
**What it is:** Sharp metal blades in various sizes for cutting tissue. The maester's scalpel for surgical procedures; the lancet for bleeding and lancing abscesses.
**Real properties:** Essential for: wound debridement (removing dead tissue), abscess drainage, minor surgery, bloodletting.
**Still-room relevance:** Present in the still-room as part of the maester's surgical kit she has access to. She does not perform surgery but has observed the maester and understands the implements.

### Jars, Vials, and the Apothecary Cabinet
`[real-world / plausible in setting]` — ceramic jars, glass vials, the wooden cabinet.
**What it is:** Ceramic storage jars (sealed with wax or fitted lids) for bulk dry and semi-solid preparations; glass vials (expensive; the Myrish and Essosi glass trade) for liquid tinctures; the wooden apothecary cabinet with drawers and compartments.
**Real properties:** Proper storage is essential for maintaining preparation potency. Ceramic jars protect against light degradation; glass vials allow visibility. The apothecary cabinet's organization determines how quickly and reliably the practitioner can access specific preparations.
**Still-room relevance:** The dual labeling system (maester's labels on front, her parallel cards behind) is the key narrative detail. The cabinet is operationally hers; the maester's label system is the public-facing layer.

### The Maester's Chain as Knowledge Index
`[canon]` — the chain is confirmed canon; see note on metal-to-discipline mapping above.
**What it is:** A maester's chain, worn around the neck, with links of different metals representing mastery of different Citadel disciplines. The presence or absence of specific links indicates what a given maester knows.
**Relevance:** The chain is the knowledge-access index. When Saerys encounters a maester, she reads his chain to assess what he knows. A maester without the silver link has not studied medicine at the Citadel level. A maester with the red-gold link has studied fire-making — potentially alchemical knowledge she wants.
**Saerys's cultivation-tier note:** "She reads the chain as a sect-rank indicator — each link represents the completion of a specific cultivation path's knowledge tier. A maester with many links has advanced in multiple lineages simultaneously, which she finds inefficient by cultivation standards ('no sect would permit a core-disciple to split their training across eight different paths') but admirable as a breadth achievement."
