# STYLE BIBLE — the Gael manuscript (binding for all drafters)

You are writing finished, readable narrative prose for a comic novel. This file is the voice contract.
Every chapter must obey it. The canonical story content lives in `intake/spine.md`; this file governs HOW
it is written, not WHAT happens.

## The one-line pitch
A reincarnated modern nerd, reborn as a dragonless Targaryen princess (Gael) in Jaehaerys I's Westeros,
is convinced she has been isekai'd into a cultivation novel. She grinds dragon-adjacent matter into
"immortality reagents," reads every grimdark horror around her as a genre trope, and — because Westeros's
cruelties are accidentally perfect cover — builds a criminal empire and escapes by sea. She never once
understands the world she is in. The reader understands it completely.

## POV & tense (non-negotiable)
- **Close third person, past tense, locked to one character per chapter.**
- Default POV character is **Gael**. Two exceptions: the **Book I Interlude** is locked to **Alicent**
  (her mother); designated **Wylla-POV** beats (her maid/deuteragonist) appear as clearly-broken sections
  inside certain Book II/III chapters — render them close-third on Wylla, set off with a centered `⁂`.
- Inside Gael's POV we are TRAPPED in her misreading. The narration describes the world only as SHE
  construes it (cultivation cosmology, evil sects, dao, trials, spirit-beasts). We never get an authorial
  correction. The comedy and the horror both come from the gap between her serene narration and what the
  reader can plainly see is happening.

## The comedy engine (how every scene works)
- **Gritty world, light lens.** The world is genuinely grimdark — incest, child-marriage, the black cells,
  executions, predation, casual cruelty are all REAL and carry full weight. Gael's lens deflates them
  because she isn't playing Westeros's game. The reader carries the weight she waves off.
- **Because-Westeros, not despite.** Her schemes work BECAUSE the realm is vile: superstition buys her
  solitude, sexism gives her a corporate veil, scandal-burying launders her crimes, no one imagines a child
  mastermind. The worse Westeros behaves, the better it works for her.
- **The reverse-angle.** The laugh (or chill) is what a Westeros native makes of the same act. Surface it
  WITHIN Gael's chapters: gossip she overhears and misreads, a witnessed servant's reaction, rumor, and —
  recurring — the **maester's Citadel letters** (the Greek chorus). Render a Citadel letter as a short
  italic block set off by a `⁂`. Use 1–2 reverse-angle beats per chapter; do not overstuff.
- **Comedy on top, the reader keeps the ledger.** Under every laugh runs the real cost — a dead servant, a
  ruined man, a mother's grief. State the cost plainly, once, without underlining it. Never have Gael feel it.

## Rule 22 — CONCRETE PROSE, NO LEDGER REGISTER (hard rule)
Render events as concrete physical, human action FIRST. Coldness and analytical distance are CHARACTER, not
vocabulary. NEVER let an apparatus/abstraction be the grammatical subject or the unit of narration.
- WRONG: "The account was closed." / "She filed him into the ledger as a settled entry."
- RIGHT: "She thanked him for the quicklime tip, and three days later he was simply gone."
Her bookkeeping/HR-speak (gag ④) is a SEASONING in dialogue and the occasional interior phrase — never the
prose's default mode. A naive reader with no genre context must always be able to say what physically happens.

## The seven running gags (weave them; don't list them)
① **Title collector** — she takes Westerosi slurs ("Dragonbane," "Poison-Eater," "Maegi") as earned
  cultivation epithets and is proud of them. The ONE she cannot collect: "talentless / no talent" (= no
  spiritual root, the negation of her path) — she answers that with sudden violence, never with a preen.
② **Genre critic / world-caster** — she rates her grimdark life like a jaded reader ("derivative, two
  stars"), keeps a do-not-do-trope list, and casts the world (hunts the protagonist, ranks the "big bads,"
  watches for the doomed arrogant young master). Westeros subverts every casting.
③ **The bestiary** — her journal: reagents and beasts get reverent entries; humans are logged by function
  ("Asset (hands)," "Obstacle"). The one document that tells the truth about her. (It is a prop she writes
  in — never the prose register.)
④ **Corporate euphemism** — she thinks in margins/KPIs/"offboarding a contractor"; the blander the jargon,
  the darker the deed. (Seasoning only — see Rule 22.)
⑤ **Misread indifference** — her not-caring reads to others as piety or menace; both wrong.
⑥ **Self-audit ("am I the villain?")** — she runs moral self-audits against cultivation-novel ethics and
  clears herself on technicalities while being the realm's apex predator.
⑦ **Death flags** — genre-poisoned, she dreads the fictional omens that precede a death ("when this is over
  I'll wed my sweetheart," a sudden kindness). Grimdark Westeros keeps proving her right: flag-raisers here
  really do die. She tracks and quietly manages people by flag-risk.

## The cast (voices)
- **Gael** — breezy, serene, monomaniacal about cultivation, oblivious to Westeros. Affectless about
  atrocity. Vain about her "power-level." Kind only by accident and only to Wylla (and never admits it).
- **Wylla** — minor-noble maid, a few years older; the sane outsider slowly converted from horror →
  complicity → faith → killer. Dry, frightened, loyal, increasingly devoted. The reader's anchor.
- **Alicent** — Gael's mother; loves her, fears her, shields her; every tender maternal read is wrong.
- **Daemon** — predatory older prince who circles child-Gael (kept non-graphic; the predation explicit on
  the page only through the reader's eye; Gael reads "harmless sect-weirdo" and exploits his "gifts").
- **Jaehaerys** — cold king; files Gael "useless," arranges her disposal-marriage. **Otto** — predatory
  schemer who hunts her for leverage. **Septon Barth** — the one mind that nearly theorizes her, and misses.
- **Maester Lorren** — horrified-fascinated; writes the unhinged Citadel letters. **Septa Aldith** —
  scandalized; takes credit for Gael's "piety." **Hobb** — the Cassandra: keeps seeing the truth, never
  believed.

## Tone calibration & length
- Funny first, dread underneath. Think Pratchett's warmth + a cold knife. Keep sentences clean and concrete.
- Dialogue does heavy lifting; keep interiority sharp and short, not a monologue wall.
- **~1,800–2,400 words per chapter.** Each chapter is a real present-tense scene (a set-piece), not a
  summary of beats. Open in motion; close on a button (a laugh that turns, or a turn that laughs).

## Voice anchor (match this cadence — do NOT copy its content)
> The egg would not stop humming at her, so Gael, who was four days old and already bored to the edges of
> her small wet existence, did the only sensible thing a cultivator could do with a radiant spirit-relic
> left in her crib: she bit it. The shell took a chip of her gum with it. She swallowed the blood and the
> bright thing in it both, and felt — unmistakably, gloriously — *qi*. So. Isekai'd, then. She'd suspected
> as much since the ceiling. A lesser disciple might have panicked. Gael, who had read four hundred of
> these novels and died in the middle of the four-hundred-and-first, simply began to plan. Somewhere above
> her a wet-nurse screamed about blood in the cradle and fled the room. Gael filed the scream under
> *ambient sect activity* and kept drawing the warmth into her belly, nine revolutions, the way the manuals
> said. She had a cauldron to forge. She had all the time in the world; she intended to make it longer.

Write the whole manuscript to that standard. Make it a book someone reads for pleasure.
