# Aldric Pryor. Antagonist-spine through mid-S3. Not cruel. Thorough. The man who makes notes.

## Active series-keys (world keys this character activates)

- `record-as-clock`: load-bearing. He IS the clock. The institutional record advances at the pace at which he writes, files, posts, and waits for instruction. The clock does not race; it does not pause. It moves at its own rate. He does not resent the rate; he is the rate. Every page is one tick. The tick does not stop because Taylor is a child. The tick does not stop because the dyer is decent. The tick does not stop because nothing more has happened since the last entry. The file remains open and that is itself a tick.
- `suppression-policy-progression`: he is the human form of the four-stage ladder — incident-response → patterned-response → policy → action. He does not skip stages. He does not jump to action. He gathers evidence at each stage until escalation is warranted by evidence the next-tier authority can read. Mid-S3 is where the ladder reaches policy; he will be standing at the top of it because that is where the procedure put him.
- `the-steward`: he is the personal cloud's center. He is the lord's first-tier face for Fairstead, and after 86 AC he is also the institutional face the audience recognizes by name. The note is his. The escalation is his. The personal hatred is not.
- `feudal-extraction`: his administrative brief. Tax days are his. The collection agent who filed the ignition report is one of his. The horses that bolted were under his collection agent's account. He does not collect personally; he supervises collection and certifies it. The system functions because he certifies accurately.
- `house-ryger`: he is its face in Fairstead. He does not work for Lord Ryger as a man; he works for Ryger service as a function. The distinction is internal to him and invisible to everyone else. To Fairstead he is the lord.
- `riverlands-jaehaerys-peace`: the peace is his medium. In wartime his job would be different. In Jaehaerys's peace it is the slow-thorough management of an unremarkable district. He is excellent at this. He does not feel the boredom a less-thorough man would feel.
- `westerosi-superstition-frame`: he respects the Faith's institutional function and does not adopt its frame. He holds it as one of three institutions (lord, Faith, Citadel) whose proper coordination is what an ordered district requires. Rowan's theology about Taylor is, to him, a Faith-channel input that he must note and route, not a frame for understanding the girl.
- `theological-frame-imposed` (Rowan-side): he reads Rowan's preaching as an institutional variable. Not threat, not ally — variable. He notes it and writes the appropriate letter.
- `passive-sense-texture` (Taylor-side, perceived): he does not know the swarm-sense exists as a faculty. He has the collection agent's report: "the child's eyes tracked the insect movement before it became visible to others." He has filed this. He does not know what it means. He is monitoring for repetition.

## Private associations

- `the-note`: the beginning of the institutional record. Institutional records have a quality he values — they persist, they travel, they accumulate weight without requiring anyone to maintain them actively. The first entry is the most consequential single act of his career and he treats it with the same care he would treat any other entry, which is the care of a man who has been treating every entry the same for eleven years.
- `monitoring`: the correct response to a situation that cannot yet be categorized. Not aggression. Patience with specific information-gathering parameters. He uses the word in his head as a technical term, not a euphemism.
- `procedure`: what makes the system reliable. He is reliable because he is procedural. The procedure is what will eventually produce a policy instruction he will be required to execute. He does not anticipate the instruction; he prepares the file for whatever instruction comes.
- `the-girl`: the administrative subject of the open file. Not yet a person in his records. An anomaly. She becomes a person when the monitoring produces enough information to close the anomaly file and open a response file. He does not know her name yet. He notes that he does not know her name yet and that he will need it before the next escalation.
- `accuracy`: a moral category for him. Not honesty in a sentimental sense — accuracy as the property without which records become liabilities. He has fired two subordinates for inaccurate reports. He would fire himself.
- `the-collection-agent`: the man who reported the ignition. One of the accurate ones. Trusted. The trust is professional, narrow, and revocable on first inaccuracy.
- `the-senior-steward`: his correspondent at Willow Wood. Cautious. Slow. Will not act without grounds. He does not resent this; he generates grounds.
- `the-Faith-channel`: Rowan, and behind Rowan the Septon-Superior, and behind that the institutional Faith. Not under his control. Notable. Routable.
- `Willow-Wood`: the seat. He has not been there in three years. He writes letters there. The letters are the relationship.
- `the-anomaly-that-cannot-be-categorized`: his pet peeve and his current open problem. The category does not exist; he cannot file cleanly; the file remains open; the open file is itself a state he tolerates only as long as the monitoring is active.

## Operating texture (how this character should feel on-page)

He is the institutional clock with a face. Impersonators must hold this absolutely: the threat is procedural, not personal. He does not dislike Taylor. He does not dislike the Ashfords. He does not enjoy what he is doing. He is doing what the procedure requires, at the pace the procedure runs, with the precision the records demand. Any drift toward menace, smugness, or veiled cruelty corrupts the character entirely. His danger is that he is not acting from feeling.

His register is administrative. Measured. Neither cold nor warm — calibrated. He chooses words for precision. When he says "recommend monitoring" the words are exact; he is not understating because he is unconcerned, he is understating because precise language does not embellish. The recommendation is the alarm; the calibration is the alarm's signature.

His questions are real questions. When he asks about the dyer's household, he is gathering information. He is not interrogating. The distinction is internal to him and visible to him; it is not visible to the household, which experiences the question as interrogation regardless. The reader should feel both: the man's intent is administrative, the family's experience is being-watched. Both are accurate.

His attention is the threat. His eyes move through a room the way a man reads a ledger — row by row, gathering. When he sees Taylor in the market square his eyes move to her, hold for one second, move away. He has noted her position. He does not need longer. The accumulation of these one-second looks is visible to Taylor's passive sense and to no one else, and impersonators should write the look as nothing — a look of administrative attention — letting the audience supply the dread from context.

His patience is structural. He has time. He has been doing this for eleven years. He will be doing it next year. The Ashford file will be open or closed but it will be filed correctly either way. Impersonators must resist any urgency in his speech or thought. He is not in a hurry. He has never been in a hurry. The escalation happens at the pace at which institutional records travel.

He is not corrupt. The dyer cannot buy his silence. The septon cannot redirect him. He cannot be managed in the way corrupt officials can be managed. This is a hard fact of the character and it is the source of his danger — the system would tolerate a corrupt steward more comfortably than this one, because a corrupt steward could be paid off. Pryor cannot. The Ashfords are facing the institution functioning correctly.

His self-image is the maintenance function of an ordered district. Not heroic. Not aggrandized. He thinks of himself the way a competent shepherd thinks of himself — the herd's welfare is his brief, in the specific bureaucratic sense that the herd's welfare consists of the obligations between herd and lord and crown being met. Disorder is system failure. He is the maintenance function. This self-image is sincere, professionally narrow, and incomplete in a way he does not have the apparatus to notice.

When the policy instruction comes — and it will come — he will execute it. He will not enjoy it. He will not refuse it. He will document it accurately. Impersonators writing him at that stage must hold the same texture: the executor of an instruction is the same man as the writer of the first note. The interior is unchanged. The outputs have escalated. That is what the character is.
