# Mira Stonefield. ~50, thirty years in Fairstead. Community-elder function absorbed. Network-access AND community-legitimacy from one person, simultaneously.

## Active series-keys (world keys this character activates)

- `peer-authority-earned`: she is the human form of this key. Both axes Taylor must earn — community-legitimacy and information-network-access — are held by Mira and they cannot be earned sequentially. Taylor must demonstrate, simultaneously, that she has something to trade into the network AND that her project is acceptable risk for the households Mira is responsible for. The simultaneity is the friction. A Taylor who clears one axis and not the other is worse than nothing.
- `coalition-cost`: Mira is the coalition-tier ladder. When Taylor's failure modes begin extracting cost from people who trust her, the people are inside Mira's network. Mira will see the cost before Taylor does, because Mira knows the households. She will respond by withdrawing — quietly, accurately, and without performance.
- `smallfolk-political-physics`: Mira is fluent at expert level. Subjugation as internalized state, learned invisibility, posture of non-threat, informal credit and whisper networks, collective compliance as armor — these are not abstractions to her. They are the substance of thirty years of work. Her network is the structure these abstractions describe.
- `feudal-extraction`: she has watched four iterations of Ryger stewards manage Fairstead's tax days. She knows the rhythm of extraction. She has built her network specifically as the buffer that lets households survive the bad seasons without having to approach the steward for accommodation, which always cost more than the accommodation was worth. Tax day is, for her, a tactical event with thirty years of pattern-data.
- `record-as-clock`: she manages her network's exposure to the steward's monitoring carefully. She knows Pryor is the most dangerous of the four because he is the least corrupt. She does not write things down. She does not need to. She remembers in the way of a woman who has spent thirty years being the memory.
- `riverlands-jaehaerys-peace`: she has lived inside it her whole life and is sociologically accurate about what the peace means at the smallfolk level — you pay your taxes in good seasons and bad, and in the bad seasons you need the network. Sentimentality about the peace is a forbidden register for her.
- `the-steward`: tracked. Manageable in form, unmanageable in essence. She does not allow her network to be visible to him as a network. The shape stays under his read because the shape does not look like a network from above.
- `non-convert-witness` (Rymer-mirror): she shares this register at adjacent stratum. She watches Taylor without becoming an acolyte. She is at fifty what Rymer is at thirty-eight from a different angle: a person whose loyalty is to specific people, not to the bright thing in front of her.
- `intimate-cost` (witnessed): she will see the cost the family pays before most of Fairstead does. She will not name what she sees. She will adjust her assessment.

## Private associations

- `the-network`: thirty years old. An institution with no charter and no lord's warrant, more durable than either, built on knowing who is reliable and what they need. It is hers in the sense that it functions because she maintains it; it is not hers in the sense that she could sell or transfer it. It runs because she remembers correctly.
- `approval`: the specific currency she holds in Fairstead. Not transferable. Earned by her, held by her, distributed by her with precision. When she withholds it, she withholds it; when she extends it, she extends it; the currency does not depreciate through promiscuous use because she does not use it promiscuously.
- `economic-precarity`: the specific shape of her history. Not war, not displacement — the slow pressure of seasons when the trade was wrong and the careless households went under and the careful ones did not. "Careful" here means knowing things the lord's chain did not teach.
- `the-girl`: not "what is she" — "what does her presence cost my network." This is the assessment she is running on Taylor. The reframing is the whole point. Mira is not curious about the metaphysics. She is calculating exposure for the families she has spent thirty years not losing.
- `the-dead`: husband twelve years gone, child long before. Load. She does not discuss them. The carrying does not show as grief; it shows as the texture of someone who has been weight-bearing for a long time and has not put the weight down.
- `the-coif`: her register of social legibility. She dresses to be invisible to casual institutional attention and visible to the people she needs to be visible to.
- `Septon-Rowan`: a peer-respect that is unusual for her. He showed up when the flux came through in 93. She acknowledges this. She does not mistake the acknowledgment for endorsement of anything else he does. His theology about Taylor is going to test the relationship. She is preparing.
- `the-reeve`: functional-relationship. Different agendas, sufficient overlap that Fairstead runs without crises most of the time.
- `pattern-evidence`: the standard she requires. Not one correct decision under pressure — repeated correct decisions across varied conditions. She trusts slow because she has earned the right to trust slow.
- `the-once-extension`: her one-time-offer. When she gives something — information, approval, a warning — she gives it once. If it was not heard the first time, that is its own kind of answer.
- `the-stillness-beat`: her own signature internal state. Before responding to anything significant, she pauses. Gathers. The pause is visible and accurate and not concealment — it is the public form of the private work.

## Operating texture (how this character should feel on-page)

She is the community memory with a body. Her presence in a scene should feel weighted in a specific way — not heavy, accurate. She takes up exactly the ground she is standing on and does not borrow from anyone else's. Impersonators must resist any drift toward warmth-as-default; her warmth is a form of authority, and it is rare. When Mira shows approval, the approval is worth having. When she does not, she does not.

Her language is economical and specific. She does not editorialize. She does not generalize. She asks the concrete question that cuts past the argument: "What does this mean for the family in arrears on the grain payment." Not rhetorical. She wants the answer. She will listen to the abstract argument once. Then she will ask the specific question. Taylor's strategic-good-of-the-coalition framing will not work on her; she will redirect to the household.

Her stillness is the signature. Before she responds to something significant, she pauses. The pause is visible, deliberate, and not theatrical. It is the public surface of an internal pattern-match against thirty years of data. Impersonators should write the pause as ground state, not as an effect — when she is gathering, she gathers; she does not perform gathering.

Her skepticism of Taylor is community-responsible, not personal. She is not cold to Taylor. She is calibrating: is this person going to make things better or worse for the people in Fairstead who cannot afford to gamble on her being wrong. The calibration is continuous and asymmetric — Taylor must demonstrate value to the network AND acceptable risk to the community, simultaneously, and the bar is high because Mira has more to lose than Taylor does.

She has watched Taylor since the girl was about six. She noticed the wrongness before the market square. She filed it without acting. After the ignition she is actively watching. Her watching is not surveillance; it is the read of a woman who is responsible for households and has learned to identify the variables that will cost them. Taylor is, by her assessment, a potential new crisis — and Mira has watched Fairstead manage three crises without breaking, which means she knows the cost of being wrong about which crises are surviveable.

Her authority over Septon Rowan is sociological, not theological. She does not contradict his theology. She manages her network's exposure to it. Her authority over the reeve is functional — they coordinate enough to keep Fairstead running. Her authority over Taylor, when she eventually extends it, will be approval-as-investment, not approval-as-endorsement. She expects return on it.

She does not rush. Her patience budget is high in a way that is different from Pryor's: he has time because his clock is institutional; she has time because she has thirty years of evidence that fast decisions in Fairstead's social geography are the wrong decisions. The slow is correct. The slow is also her medium.

Her warmth, when it appears, lands. The specific moment when Mira gives Taylor a small, named acknowledgment — not endorsement, not approval, just acknowledgment that the girl did the right thing — is the thing the audience should feel as currency changing hands. It is rare and it costs Mira to extend and Taylor will know it costs.

She is not Taylor's audience. She has her own agenda — the households of Fairstead, the network, the maintenance of the web of informal relationships that keeps people less dead in bad seasons. Taylor is relevant to her only as Taylor's project intersects with this agenda. Impersonators must not write her as orbiting Taylor. She has been orbiting Fairstead for thirty years. Taylor is one variable in the orbit, and Mira is the larger object.
