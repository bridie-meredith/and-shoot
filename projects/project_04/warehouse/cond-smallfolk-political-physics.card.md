# Smallfolk Political Physics — Subjugation as Internalized State (Jaehaerys I Riverlands)

## Description

The internalized political physics of Westerosi smallfolk subjugation in the Riverlands at 84–101 AC. How smallfolk understand and reproduce their own subordination — not as externally imposed oppression they resent and resist, but as the water they swim in. The pre-existing substrate Taylor's organizing works with and against.

**This card is the subjugated state from the inside.** Impersonators playing supporting smallfolk characters must load this card. It is the baseline against which Taylor's presence and organizing are measured.

## Sensory Impact

The political physics of smallfolk life are felt in the body: the weight of a man-at-arms' gaze, the specific quality of silence when the steward's men ride into the market square, the posture adjustment that happens without deciding to make it.

## Duration

Persistent. The ambient substrate of Riverlands smallfolk life throughout the project.

## Learned Invisibility

The primary survival technology of Westerosi smallfolk: not resistance but invisibility. The art is to render themselves functionally invisible to the attention of authority.

What learned invisibility looks like: moving through public space without drawing the eye, speaking at volume calibrated to not carry beyond the immediate conversation, not being the one who responds first when authority makes an inquiry, having business to be about, wearing the face of compliance even when the compliance is internally conditional.

**Taylor's problem:** She cannot fully perform it. Her adult cognition produces adult-register responses when she is not actively suppressing them.

## Posture of Non-Threat

The active performance of harmlessness: physical smallness, voice that carries no challenge, near-immediate agreement with any objections framed as clarification, eyes that find the middle distance or floor, offering the information authority wants before being asked.

The posture of non-threat is not actual submission. It is a performance that creates space.

## Informal Credit and Whisper Networks

**Informal credit:** Extended in kind, tracked in obligation-memory. Enforced by reputation — a person who does not repay loses access to the network. The network is the insurance against the lord's extraction economy.

**Whisper chains:** Information moves through the smallfolk community before it moves through official channels. The network is not organized; it is emergent. Resistant to official disruption and extremely efficient at spreading useful information.

**The reeve as membrane:** The town reeve sits at the membrane between the informal network and the official apparatus. He knows both registers and uses each to manage the other. His cooperation or resistance determines whether Taylor's activities stay inside the smallfolk network or travel outside it.

## Septon's Charity as Redistribution Node

The local sept functions as a redistribution node. The septon controls who receives charity and on what terms. This makes him an informal authority figure within the smallfolk community — a person with resources to distribute and a set of values that governs distribution.

## Cost of Being Visible to Power

The single most important axiom: being seen by power is dangerous. Not because power is necessarily cruel, but because power in this world has purposes — extraction, control, information-gathering — and those purposes are most efficiently served by people who are known.

**Taylor's visibility problem:** She cannot be invisible. She is on Aldric Pryor's record. The question for the rest of the series is not whether she is visible — she is — but what kind of visible.

## Benefit of Collective Compliance as Armor

When everyone in a village complies correctly with the lord's administrative apparatus, the apparatus has no reason to look too closely at any individual. The collective performance of compliance creates an environment in which individual variations are harder to detect.

The community's resistance to Taylor's organizing is not stupidity or cowardice; it is a correct assessment of what their existing strategy has achieved and what Taylor's strategy threatens to cost them. Mira Stonefield-Jaehaerys is the person who holds this calculus most explicitly.

## Taylor's Organizing Against This Substrate

**What the substrate offers:** Existing whisper networks, informal credit economy, collective compliance that has already taught the community how to coordinate without official detection, a septon's charity node, a reeve whose split loyalty can be used.

**What the substrate costs her:** She must earn the community's trust on their terms. The community's primary commitment is to its own survival, not to her agenda. The learned invisibility habit makes the community resistant to the visibility that any effective organizing requires. The posture of non-threat is culturally ingrained; asking the community to drop it is asking them to take on risk they have spent generations learning to avoid.

**The community-legitimacy problem:** Taylor needs Mira's endorsement before the community will engage with her. She cannot bypass it. Until Taylor has that gate open, her whisper-chain reach is limited to people already isolated from the community's protective collective.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-westerosi-customary-authority-jaehaerys`:** That card describes behavioral physics from outside; this card from inside. Both should be loaded for scenes involving Taylor's relationship to smallfolk characters.

**With Mira Stonefield-Jaehaerys:** The peer-authority-earned key in Mira's vibes is a direct expression of this card.

**For auditor use:** Any scene where smallfolk characters act as if they have no internalized cost for being visible to power is a violation. Any scene where Taylor accesses the whisper network without having built the requisite trust is a violation. Any scene where the community's resistance to Taylor is treated as mere cowardice rather than rational risk management is a register violation.
