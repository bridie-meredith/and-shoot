# Westerosi Superstition Frame — How Uncanny Phenomena Are Categorized (Riverlands, 84–101 AC)

## Description

How Westerosi culture categorizes uncanny events in the Riverlands at 84–101 AC. Covers: the general folk framework, the specific Riverlands superstition layer (mid-region, between old-gods north and Faith-dominant south), and the specific categories through which Taylor's swarm events will be narrated by witnesses.

This card tells impersonators of supporting cast how to narrate Taylor's power to themselves and others.

## Sensory Impact

The superstition frame is a cognitive condition. Its sensory expression is the pause before a witness speaks — the moment of choosing which word to reach for. "The bees moved wrong." "The flies — they went where she looked." "Something was with her, in the air."

## Duration

Persistent. The background cultural epistemology of Westerosi witnesses throughout the project.

## The General Westerosi Folk Framework

**Omens:** Events in the natural world that carry meaning beyond themselves. Not caused by a person; happen in relation to a situation. Predict or signal without identifying mechanism.

**Blessings:** Intercession by a positive force (the Seven, the old gods) on behalf of a person or cause. Operates through natural elements in service of whoever is being blessed.

**Curses:** Malefic working through natural elements. The adversarial read.

**Signs from the Seven:** Direct manifestation of a specific divine aspect. More specific than a general blessing.

**The Old Gods' work:** In the Riverlands specifically, old-gods attribution is available even for people who nominally follow the Seven.

**Old magic, from before the Conquest:** The broadest and least specific category. Things that feel older or stranger than Seven-theology fits.

**Witchcraft or diabolism:** Active working by a person who has made a compact with malign forces. The accusatory extreme. Requires specific evidence of agency and intent.

## The Riverlands Superstition Layer

**Dual-tradition residue:** Older Riverlands families have traces of both traditions. They attend the sept on holy days and they don't cut down a weirwood tree if they find one. Pragmatic folk superstition that covers its bases.

**Specific Riverlands mid-region palette:**
- Rivers have spirits — the Blue Fork and Gods Eye have local spirit-names in folk tradition
- Bees associated with the Maiden; ravens with the Crone; flies with the Stranger
- The concept of the "touched" — a person who has been brushed by the divine or uncanny, carrying some residue in their daily behavior. Not a witch, not a saint. A person who is "touched" is avoided in some contexts and sought in others.
- Seasonal uncanny: the period around the autumn equinox amplifies reports of uncanny events.

## How Taylor's Swarm Events Are Categorized

**The ignition event:**
- The collector's retinue leader: "The flies — they moved like a fist." Does not reach for supernatural category. Writes "the animals went strange" and files as unexplained anomaly.
- The smallfolk woman being coerced: categorizes it as the Mother's protection. Her whisper-chain narrative: "The Mother's hand was there."
- Market square smallfolk bystanders: "touched" is their most likely category for Taylor.
- Septon Rowan: builds a layered interpretation (Stranger's presence, expressed as protection, enacted through a child in special relationship with the natural world). His record: "she carries something."
- The traveling maester: "anomalous arthropod aggregation, coordinated, apparently directed by or associated with the Ashford child. No naturalistic explanation available."

## The Narrative Vocabulary for Taylor's Power

**Words characters reach for:**
- "the bees moved with her"
- "the Maiden's favor"
- "something touched the air"
- "old magic, from before the Faith"
- "the creatures answered her"
- "the Stranger was walking in that square today"
- "she's got something in her, that girl"
- "the beasts know her for something"
- "it was a blessing" / "it was a curse"
- "touched"

**Words characters do NOT reach for:**
- "parahuman," "power," "ability" (in the modern sense), "mutation," "superpowers"
- "she controls insects" (too direct and mechanistic)
- "magic" as a self-contained noun

## The "Touched" Category as Taylor's Best Outcome

Taylor's goal in early arcs: stay in the "touched" category and out of the "witch" category. The ignition event's aftermath narrative (the Mother's hand, not Taylor's power) is the folk frame that accomplishes this — and the smallfolk woman's testimony (or silence) is what anchors that interpretation in the whisper chain.
