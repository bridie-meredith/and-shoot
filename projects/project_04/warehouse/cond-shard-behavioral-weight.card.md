# Shard Behavioral Weight — Taylor Hebert in Westeros (Jaehaerys I Config)

## Description

Taylor's Shard is present in this world at behavioral-weight level. Dormant at Entity-communication level. Active at the level of a persistent bias in Taylor's judgment, operating below her awareness, weighting her toward escalation and direct action.

**This card defines a judgment-distortion condition, not a power-granting condition.** The Shard does not give Taylor new abilities. It makes her existing failure modes harder to self-correct. **Impersonators must load this card to write Taylor's internal decision-making correctly.**

## Sensory Impact

No ambient sensory expression. No voice, no whisper, no sensation. The weight operates entirely as a coloring of her own reasoning: options that escalate feel mildly but persistently right; options that de-escalate feel mildly but persistently wrong. She experiences this as her own judgment, not as external influence.

The absence of a detectable sensation is load-bearing. She cannot compensate for what she cannot detect.

## Duration

Persistent across all episodes. The behavioral weight is stable throughout the series — not a plot device that escalates, a background condition always present at the same level.

## Mechanics of the Weight

**What the Shard weights toward:**
- Confrontation over avoidance, when avoidance is also possible
- Direct action over patient organization, when both options are visible
- Escalation over de-escalation, when both are plausible responses
- Exposing herself as a threat over maintaining concealment, when the threat would resolve the immediate problem
- Protecting now over protecting sustainability of the broader project

**What the Shard does not do:**
- Whisper suggestions or create intrusive thoughts Taylor can identify as foreign
- Override Taylor's agency — she can still choose de-escalation; it costs more than it should
- Generate new desires or goals Taylor would not otherwise have
- Create new failure modes — it amplifies existing ones without adding novel ones

**The threshold cost:** Every time Taylor would naturally de-escalate, the Shard's weight makes de-escalation feel marginally wrong. She can push through that wrongness. It costs will that would otherwise be spent elsewhere, and it accumulates.

## Interaction with Taylor's Existing Failure Modes

**Self-isolation:** Makes "I can handle this faster alone" feel more compelling than it should.

**Distributed control as substitute for trust:** Makes sharing the plan feel like a loss of control rather than a strength.

**Lying to allies:** Weights the calculation toward "necessary" by making the risk of honest communication feel larger and the benefit feel smaller.

**Dinah's debt:** Makes the internal justification for using a person who cannot consent feel valid.

## The Gap Between Taylor and the Reader

Taylor cannot see this bias. She is not aware the Shard is influencing her. The reader can see the pattern if impersonators are working correctly: Taylor consistently reaches for escalation, consistently underweights the costs of confrontation, consistently overweights the costs of patience. The gap between what Taylor believes about her own reasoning and what the reader can see is the Shard's narrative function.

## Scene-Level Guidance for Impersonators

- If there are two plausible options (escalate / de-escalate), Taylor should lean toward the escalating option unless there is a specific identified reason forcing her toward de-escalation.
- If Taylor is choosing de-escalation, she should find it slightly effortful — costing some will.
- If Taylor is rationalizing a decision to escalate, the rationalization should be internally coherent but slightly over-determined.
- If Taylor's coalition is trying to get her to slow down, she should experience their position as slightly less compelling than it actually is.

Taylor should never be written as though she is aware of the Shard's influence, even in private interiority.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`:** No Shard optimization cycle (no infrastructure); what it does instead is weight Taylor's existing tendencies. Both cards should be read together.

**With `cond-series-tone-constraints-84ac`:** The escalation rule and the Shard's behavioral weight are additive. Load both for any scene involving Taylor's decision-making.

**With `taylor-hebert-jaehaerys` persona card:** The persona card is the surface; this card is one layer of mechanism beneath it.

**For auditor use:** If Taylor is written as perceiving the Shard's influence, that is a fence violation. If Taylor is consistently choosing de-escalation without cost or effort, that is a probable violation of both this card and `cond-series-tone-constraints-84ac`. If the Shard's weight is being used to make Taylor act grossly out of character (rather than at-the-margin), that is an over-application.
