# Series Tone Constraints — taylor-hebert-westeros

## Description

Project-level tonal law. This card defines the operational registers this series runs in, the registers it does not run in, and the specific escalation-as-structure rule that governs how scenes must be constructed. These constraints apply to all agents across all scenes — they are not Taylor-specific persona rules, they are production rules for the entire series. The auditor loads this card when reviewing scene-level register drift.

## Sensory Impact

Tonal constraints have no ambient sensory expression. Their expression is felt in scene velocity, in the ratio of action beats to reflection beats, in whether a scene's center of gravity is forward-moving or interior. A scene that violates these constraints will feel slow, over-explained, or intellectually remote from its physical stakes.

## Duration

Persistent across all episodes. These are series-defining rules, not episode-specific constraints.

## The Primary Register

**Fast. Pulpy. Dramatic.**

Scene pace is driven by physical stakes and immediate consequence. The sentence-level and beat-level writing style prioritizes forward velocity. A scene that lingers must be lingering on something that escalates. If a scene is lingering on a feeling or a thought that does not create a new problem or reveal a new danger, it is drifting.

Pulpy register means the world's drama is felt in the body: cold, hunger, fear, pain, proximity, speed. Abstract analysis of a political situation is not the primary register; the physical experience of being in the middle of the political situation is. Taylor's tactical cognition produces rapid assessments, not extended disquisitions. She thinks fast and moves; she does not think slow and consider.

Dramatic register means the series acknowledges that what is happening matters and treats it accordingly. Understatement is allowed when the understatement amplifies the drama. Flat affect in the face of horror is allowed when the flat affect is itself the dramatic signal. What is not allowed is ironic detachment that deflates stakes or meta-awareness that distances the reader from consequences.

## The Prohibited Registers

**No chess-match scheming as the primary mode.** Taylor has tactical intelligence. She reads situations fast and identifies leverage. She uses that intelligence in service of action. What she does not do, in this series, is sit in a room for a scene and plan twelve moves ahead in elaborate detail. Planning happens in motion or in brief, and then the situation changes and the plan is worthless anyway. The series is not interested in a protagonist who wins by out-thinking everyone; it is interested in a protagonist who escalates faster than everyone and then has to live with the consequences.

**No extended introspection as the primary mode.** Taylor has an interior life. Gold Morning is present in her at all times. She has grief, she has moral complexity, she has the weight of things she has done. None of this is suppressed from the fiction. What it cannot do is stop the scene. Interior moments happen in the gaps between action beats, not as extended set-pieces. A paragraph of Taylor's Gold Morning weight is allowed when it earns its place. A full scene of Taylor processing her grief with no external action is a register violation.

**No teaching-the-reader-the-world as a scene function.** Lore dumps are a register violation. Taylor has extensive internal knowledge of Worm-universe mechanics and some increasingly active knowledge of Westerosi mechanics. This knowledge surfaces as relevant action, not as a character's internal encyclopedia entries. She does not remind herself of what she knows; she uses what she knows. The reader learns the world through what happens, not through Taylor's internal wiki.

## The Escalation Rule

**Taylor's escalation-reflex is structural.** When she is threatened — physically, procedurally, or in terms of someone she is protecting — she escalates. She does not de-escalate by default. She does not take the cautious path by default when an aggressive path is available. This is not a flaw she is working on; it is a load-bearing feature of who she is and what makes her interesting in this world.

Every escalation has consequences. The Westeros world does not absorb escalation neutrally. A child who responds to an armed man with unusual sharpness creates a record. A child who appears to control animals creates a reputation. A child who successfully resists impressment becomes a problem someone will eventually solve violently. Each escalation closes some options and opens others, and the series runs forward on that dynamic.

**The escalation ratchet is one-directional within a scene.** A scene that has escalated does not de-escalate. The new equilibrium is the floor of the next scene's starting tension. This does not mean every scene ends in catastrophe; it means every scene ends at a higher tension level than it started, and the series arc is constructed from the accumulation of those steps.

## The Violence-as-Fluency Rule

Taylor speaks the language of organized violence more fluently than almost anyone in this world. This is both an asset and an isolating condition. The asset: she can read a combat situation faster than the people in it, she can identify leverage points in a threatening scenario before they become visible to others, she can organize defense and offense with a cognitive efficiency that her body cannot yet fully express. The isolating condition: the fluency is visible. People in this world who are themselves violence-fluent recognize it. People who are not are made uneasy by it. There is no social register in Westeros for a child who speaks that language.

This rule does not mean Taylor is always right about violence. It means she reaches for violence as a framework faster than the situation sometimes warrants. The escalation-reflex is related: she reads threats in terms of what violence would resolve them, even when the correct move is something else. This is allowed to cost her. It is not allowed to be corrected into a softer default — that would be a different character.

## Interaction Notes

**With `taylor-hebert-westeros` persona card:** The persona card carries escalation-reflex and violence-as-fluency as Taylor-specific traits. This card is the series-level authorization for those traits — they are not just Taylor's personal defaults, they are structural features the series is built around. The two cards are complementary; the persona card is Taylor-specific, this card is production-level.

**For auditor use:** Flag any scene where the primary action is uninterrupted planning, strategy session, or introspective monologue with no forward physical movement. Flag any scene where Taylor de-escalates by default without external constraint forcing her to. Flag any scene where the lore density exceeds what the action can carry. Flag any scene that uses ironic register to undercut immediate stakes.

**For impersonator use:** The fast/pulpy/dramatic constraint means Taylor's output in any scene should be short, targeted, and forward-moving. Her internal voice does not over-explain. Her dialogue is functional. If you find yourself writing extended internal monologue, you have drifted; trim and accelerate.
