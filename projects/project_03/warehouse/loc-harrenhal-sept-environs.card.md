# The Small Sept Near Harrenhal

## Geography

A small sept and its outbuildings positioned approximately half a league south of Harrenhal's outer walls — close enough that the castle's black towers are the horizon in every northern view, far enough that the castle's garrison has not considered it worth maintaining directly. The sept was built perhaps eighty years ago to serve the scatter of smallfolk farming households in the castle's immediate shadow: workers, cottars, the seasonal labor that Harrenhal has always required in numbers it never quite employs.

The sept itself is built of the same local stone as the surrounding farmland walls — grey limestone, mortared, nothing dressed or worked. The building is roughly the size of a modest hall: single nave, five-pointed star carved above the door (worn, the points blurred), a small chancel at the east end with a table that serves as an altar. The interior floor is cracked stone; the roof is good — recent repairs in the last decade, before the previous castellan's tenure ended. The sept holds perhaps forty standing souls.

The outbuildings are more recent: a timber-and-daub cottage for the septon's residence, a small wood store, and a low shed that has been used at various points for animals, storage, and as overflow sleeping space. These are the only buildings between the sept and the road to Harrenhal.

The surrounding area is a mix of managed pasture and tilled smallfolk plots. Approximately a dozen households work land within sight of the sept. Not a village — there is no common and no common hall — but a loose clustering of farms that have organized around the sept as their social anchor for three generations.

## Layout

- **The sept nave:** Entry from the west through the worn door. The interior is dim; the narrow windows admit little light. Five alcoves along the walls, each with a low shelf for candle-offerings to one of the Seven. The cracked floor is uneven; the cracks run east-west and have been filled with sand.
- **The chancel:** Raised two steps at the east end. The altar table holds two permanent candles and a painted wooden panel of the Mother (the paint worn to suggestion). This is where Septon Aldric conducts services and, currently, where he is dying.
- **The septon's cottage:** Timber-framed, one room with a sleeping loft. The ground floor holds a fireplace, a table, the septon's writing materials and what passes for a library (seven books, three borrowed from the previous castellan's collection and never returned). The loft is where Taylor sleeps.
- **The yard between buildings:** Hard-packed earth, a kitchen garden against the south wall of the cottage (herbs mostly, some root vegetables), a rain-barrel. The yard is enclosed on three sides by building walls and on the fourth by a low stone wall.
- **The road approach:** A dirt track runs from the sept north toward Harrenhal and south toward the nearest hamlet. The track is visible from the sept's front door; arrivals can be seen three minutes out.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Tallow from the sept's candles — a constant, the smell of the sept itself. Old wood and cold stone inside. Fever-sweat from the septon's cottage: wool and illness and the specific medicinal smell of whatever the village women have brought to manage his dying. Outside: damp earth, livestock from the nearby farms, the indefinite smell of autumn Riverlands. When the wind is right, wood smoke from the castle's towers — faint, institutional.

**Sound:** Ravens in the bell tower. The sept has no bell — the tower was built for one, the bell was never hung; the ravens have been there longer than anyone can remember. Their calls carry a specific quality: flat, civic, unconcerned with the human activity below. Inside the cottage: the septon's breathing, audible. The fire. Small sounds of the night. Outside: the farm sounds of the surrounding households, muted by distance. On the road: nothing, and then the sound of approaching horses, which carries far on still days.

**Light:** The sept's interior is always dim; the candles provide devotional light, not working light. The cottage is warmer-lit — a fire, a rush-dip. The yard in daylight is open sky; at night, the Harrenhal towers are visible to the north as shapes against the stars, and sometimes the castle shows firelight in the upper windows.

**Touch and texture:** The sept floor's cold through boots. The cottage's loft boards, which creak in a specific pattern Taylor has memorized. The garden wall's rough stone, good for sitting. The well-worn groove on the sept door's iron latch.

## Fixed Props

- The altar table with two permanent candles and the painted Mother panel
- The septon's writing materials and seven books (in the cottage)
- The rain-barrel in the yard
- The kitchen garden (herbs and root vegetables, late-season)
- The raven-occupied bell tower (accessible by internal ladder; the ravens treat this as their property)

## Exits

- **North, the Harrenhal road:** direct track toward the castle, visible for half a league. Thirty minutes on foot to Harrenhal's outer walls. This is the direction the castellan's men will come from.
- **South, the hamlet road:** track continues to the nearest cluster of smallfolk households, about ten minutes on foot. The hamlet has no lord's authority but is the social community of the sept's regular congregation.
- **East, farmland paths:** informal trails through the managed fields to the east; not a road; known to people who work these fields, not to travelers. Connects eventually to the Red Fork tributary path.
- **West, scrubland edge:** the land west of the sept grades into rougher ground — uncultivated, briar and hawthorn scrub, the remnants of a field that was plowed but not replanted after a bad year. Not a route but not impassable.

## Hazards

- **No institutional protection.** The previous castellan maintained a nominal relationship with the sept that provided a small degree of protection for the septon's household. The new castellan has no such relationship and does not know or care that it existed.
- **The road from Harrenhal is visible and direct.** Arrivals from the castle cannot be avoided, only anticipated. The three-minute window from first visible approach to arrival at the door is the only warning available.
- **The septon's death is anticipated.** The surrounding smallfolk households know he is dying. When he dies, the formal ward-protection he provides over Taylor terminates. The local community is aware of this in a loose, unspoken way. Some of them are calculating.
- **The ravens.** The bell tower's ravens have been there for decades and are known to the castle. Ravens congregating abnormally — more than usual, behaving unusually — will be noticed by anyone who watches the castle's animals.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- The ravens in the bell tower settling or flushing — arrival of something unusual on the road, or a cue from Taylor's fauna-control that hasn't been fully managed
- A village woman arriving at the cottage with broth for the septon, who will see Taylor's face and draw whatever conclusion she draws
- The sound of horses on the Harrenhal road — the three-minute window — followed by the appearance of the castellan's men on census business
- The septon's breathing changing register in the night (labored → quiet → absent) — the sequence of dying is audible to anyone in the cottage
- Rain starting: the yard empties, the sept's cracked floor begins to seep, the road to Harrenhal becomes visible through a curtain of water

## Interaction Notes

**Primary opening location:** The sept environs are Taylor's starting location at episode 1 open. She has lived here long enough to know the rhythms of the place — which boards in the loft creak, where the ravens are at what hour, which of the village women visits when. This is the space she has made as close to safe as this world has offered her.

**Institutional exposure:** The sept's protection is borderline at episode 1 open and terminates at Septon Aldric's death. The censusing operation will reach this location. The ward-protection claim will be challenged. The clock is running.

**Fauna-control specifics:** The ravens in the bell tower are the most immediately useful fauna-control resource in the location. They can be dispatched for reconnaissance, can be used to disrupt approaches, or can inadvertently signal Taylor's nature if they behave in obviously coordinated ways. The surrounding farmland has rats (in the storage buildings) and insects (normal Riverlands summer/autumn density). Nothing about this location provides cover for extended fauna-control use.
