# Riverlands Political and Social State — 120 AC

## Description

The political and social texture of the Riverlands at 120 AC. This card defines what is true about the Riverlands as an ambient backdrop — the faction pressure, the smallfolk experience, the specific quality of the pre-Dance tension — during the opening episodes of the taylor-hebert-westeros project.

## Sensory Impact

The Riverlands in 120 AC feel like a place that is already deciding things without having decided them. No war is happening; the war feels like it is being prepared. Foraging parties from lords' garrisons appear with more frequency than usual. The road system that should carry merchants and pilgrims is increasingly carrying armed men with ambiguous missions. Villages that were visited once a year by a lord's tax collector are now visited twice — by different lords' men, claiming different authorities.

The smallfolk experience of this: a slow tightening. Grain stores are asked about more often. Men are asked their ages and occupations more often. The normal rhythm of the agricultural year — sow, tend, harvest, pay your dues — is interrupted by requests, demands, and sometimes direct confiscation that comes from no single clear authority but from many unclear ones.

## Duration

Persistent through the project's duration. This is the ambient political backdrop against which all events occur, not a temporary escalation. The Dance of Dragons is approximately nine years away from 120 AC. The condition worsens over time; it does not improve.

## The Factions and Their Current State

**House Tully / Riverrun:** The nominal lords paramount of the Riverlands. Tully has not publicly declared for either the Targaryen succession factions. They are positioning. Their silence is read differently by different subordinate lords. Tully's inaction means the Riverlands lacks a unified authority, which is the specific condition that allows lords like Lord Hatch to operate with broad administrative freedom.

**Bracken and Blackwood:** In one of their recurring cold-conflict phases. Border incidents are ongoing. Neither house is escalating to open war; both houses are using the broader political instability to advance territorial claims through harassment and counter-harassment. Ser Aemon Bracken's holding is in the Bracken-adjacent territory; his independence from the Bracken main line means he is affected but not obligated.

**The Hightower-aligned lords:** The faction that is explicitly backing Alicent's sons over Rhaenyra's claim is already active in the Riverlands, placing adherents in administrative positions (like Lord Hatch at Harrenhal). Their influence is strongest near Harrenhal and in the western Riverlands.

**Rhaenyra's eventual allies:** The Black faction is in formation but not yet organized. Individual lords who will eventually back Rhaenyra are currently watching and waiting. No coordinated opposition to Hightower faction moves is happening yet.

**The Brotherhood Without Banners analog:** No specific Brotherhood equivalent exists at 120 AC — that formation is specific to the War of the Five Kings, centuries away. However, the Riverlands has a tradition of outlaw and resistance groups that form during periods of weak central authority. At 120 AC, small bands of displaced soldiers, hedge knights, and organized peasant resisters are beginning to form in the areas where faction pressure is highest. They are not yet a significant force; they are a pressure valve and a complication.

## Smallfolk Experience

**Foraging requisitions:** Smallfolk throughout the Riverlands are subject to foraging parties from multiple competing garrison lords. The foraging is technically lawful under a lord's right to provision his forces; in practice, it is extraction that frequently exceeds what the law authorizes. There is no effective appeal mechanism when multiple lords claim authority over the same land.

**Road extortion:** Checkpoints on the major roads are maintained by competing patrol groups. A merchant traveling the River Road may pay tolls to three different authorities in a day's ride, each of which claims sole legitimate authority over its stretch. Smallfolk traveling without merchant goods face a different version of the same pressure: they are assumed to be potential census subjects for whichever lord is nearby.

**Impressment sweeps:** New garrison lords — like Lord Hatch — conduct impressment census operations as part of establishing control. This is happening throughout the Riverlands at 120 AC, not just near Harrenhal. The pattern is familiar enough that smallfolk in newly-garrisoned areas know what it means when men-at-arms arrive from the nearest castle.

**The king is old and ill:** This is common smallfolk knowledge. Viserys I Targaryen is alive but increasingly incapacitated. The smallfolk don't know the details of the succession dispute, but they know that a sick king means arguing lords, and arguing lords mean they pay the cost of the argument. This knowledge is held as a generalized dread rather than a specific anticipation.

## What Smallfolk Know vs. What They Don't

**Know:** The king is ill. Lords are arguing. The arguing is costing them. Which lords are currently aligned with which faction changes faster than they can track. Harrenhal has changed hands (again). The census is coming (or already here).

**Don't know:** The specific terms of the Targaryen succession dispute. Which lords are "blacks" and which are "greens" (this terminology is court-level, not smallfolk-level). That the Dance of Dragons is coming and what it will cost. Any intelligence about the larger political maneuvers happening at King's Landing.

**Can infer:** A lord who arrives with administrative efficiency and starts taking a census is attached to a faction that has recently gained some kind of formal authority here. The faction identity matters less than the practical question: will they be worse or better than what came before? The answer is almost always "about the same, possibly worse."

## Interaction Notes

**For Taylor's foreknowledge:** Taylor knows the Dance is coming. She knows Rhaenyra will win and lose and that the Riverlands will be devastated by it. She is nine years ahead of the event. This gives her tactical foreknowledge that no Westerosi character has; it also means she is watching a slow-motion catastrophe she cannot stop without establishing the kind of credibility that takes years to build.

**For Mira Stonefield:** Her survivor network is responding to this ambient condition in real time. The network's value — and the information it carries — is a direct product of the faction pressure and displacement the Riverlands is experiencing at 120 AC.

**For Ser Aemon Bracken:** His calculation — how to position his minor holding to benefit from the coming conflict without being consumed by it — is running against this ambient political backdrop. The Dance's timing (nine years away) gives him approximately the same window Taylor has to build something useful.
