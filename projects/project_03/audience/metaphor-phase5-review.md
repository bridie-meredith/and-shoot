### worm-canon-pedant (primary)

**Verdict: VOICE-OK**

The central question: does this sound like Taylor's POV register processing mira's act? Working through the figure clause by clause.

"A peer who will not look is not gone" — "peer" is base-card social-category vocabulary: she classifies by role and function, and peer is the correct category-noun for mira in the ally/not-ally ledger. "Will not look" versus "cannot look" is a precision Taylor's base register enforces — she distinguishes choice from incapacity because her cost-language tracks agency (§Vocabulary: *leverage, budget, threshold, exposure* — all agency-bearing). "Is not gone" is her negation-before-naming cadence: she establishes what the thing is not before naming what it is. This is her inference-without-announcement form; she does not announce the insight, she delivers the negative frame and lets the positive follow.

"She is choosing the side the silence names" — "choosing" carries the agency-read forward from "will not." "The side" is lateral-alignment vocabulary consistent with §Vocabulary (side-of-ledger thinking; sides as ledger positions, not metaphysical poles). "The silence names" renders silence as designation-agent. This is Taylor operating in her cape-trained social-mechanics register: she sees the social act underneath the surface behavior. The terminal verb "names" is her precision-word for acts of social classification — she names the naming, which is exactly her doubled-register awareness in action. The insight is what a cape-trained reader of social rooms produces when she watches a peer's body-position under adult attention.

Emma monument connectivity: base card §Memory monuments rules that the emma friendship-and-betrayal are structural and she does not unpack them in dialogue or interior except in trigger-adjacent scenes. The metaphor is licensed by monument-emma-betrayal but does not name emma, does not leak Earth-Bet vocabulary, does not say "betrayal." The figure operates at the structural level: a peer's silence as active allegiance-choosing mirrors the monument's shape without naming its Earth-Bet origin. This is doubled-register authoring held correctly: legible at the Westerosi surface (a child reading her peer's social position) and at the Earth-Bet monument register simultaneously. AP5 label-fence honored.

No hot-button triggers. No voice-register contradiction. No lore leak. No power-mechanics or cape-context vocabulary surface.

**Citation:** base card §Vocabulary (peer, side-of-ledger, cost-language, precision-words for social classification), §Cadence (negation-before-naming, terminal load-bearing verb "names"), §Voice tells (inference-without-announcement, pre-calculation cadence); Westerosi overlay §Memory monuments (hard fence held, doubled-register honored), §Register markers (two-register discipline maintained — this is interior/close-third, base-card register).

### dark-fantasy-reader (secondary)

**Verdict: VOICE-OK**

Is "the side the silence names" Westerosi-plausible in a sept-ward context where sides are about to be chosen publicly in nine years?

The figure draws on sides-being-chosen as a structural concept. A sept ward in Harrenhal's shadow, 120 AC, lives in a world where allegiance and silence as complicity are not abstractions — they are institutional facts. The Faith Militant's suppression (living memory: §Memory monuments, Westerosi overlay — Maegor's reign and the Faith Militant Uprising, one century prior) established that institutional silence was itself an act of allegiance-choosing. What a sept ward does not say in the presence of power is a known social act in this culture's frame. A child raised in a sept reading peer silence as an act of sides-choosing is operating in the register her world has prepared for her.

"The silence names" — silence as designating-agent. Westerosi culture's institutional language of omission (small council, the Faith, sept chapter procedure) makes silence-as-act legible to a literate ward. The figure is not reaching past the world's own understanding of what silence does.

The temporal layering is this reader's preferred register: the metaphor resonates at the present scene (mira's act) and carries the weight of the coming Dance (nine years; sides will be named very loudly) without naming it. The figure works as consequence-that-propagates in both directions: backward to Taylor's prior-life monument and forward to the world's approaching rupture. §taste: consequence that propagates, fear of things that are actually dangerous, the smell of a world before someone puts a name to it. The silence has named a side; the world has not yet put its names to those sides publicly. This is the register.

No hot-button triggers. This is not hope-that-costs-nothing — it is the opposite: a figure that registers cost (the loss of a peer's witness, named precisely).

**Citation:** §taste (consequence that propagates; world before the naming; Westerosi-specific political weight); §voice (single slow exhale register — the figure earns its weight by paying it in full). No hot-button triggers activated.

### pulp-enthusiast (secondary)

**Verdict: VOICE-OK**

Does the two-clause structure (`X is not Y; she is Z`) do structural work or is it padding?

The two clauses are not two observations — they are a reversal. "A peer who will not look is not gone" establishes the reading the figure refuses (she has withdrawn; she is absent; she is not available to be a problem). "She is choosing the side the silence names" replaces that reading with its opposite (she is present; she is active; she has already acted). The structure is [wrong frame] → [correct frame]. This is a clean reversal, and the pulp-enthusiast §taste reads for exactly this: "Clean reversal. Tactical moves that work in unexpected ways." The two-clause form is the structural work; it is not padding.

The board-worsening test: does the metaphor make the situation worse or more interesting? Yes. Before the metaphor: mira is an ally who has dropped to neutral. After the metaphor: mira is an agent who has chosen against. The board is worse. The board-worsening is the payload of the figure — it reframes what has happened from a passive loss (ally-count drops) to an active one (peer has voted). §taste: complications that change the board, not just the mood. This changes the board.

"The side the silence names" — does it risk vague-consequences? No. The figure specifies the consequence precisely: mira's silence is an act of allegiance-designation. This is the opposite of "things got complicated." It names what the silence did. Hot-button cleared.

Length: two short clauses, a semicolon, terminal verb "names." No agonized decision-making. No padding. The pulp-enthusiast would lean forward, not fidget.

**Citation:** §taste (clean reversal; complication that changes the board; tactical move that works in unexpected way), §hot_buttons (vague consequences cleared — the figure names the consequence precisely).
