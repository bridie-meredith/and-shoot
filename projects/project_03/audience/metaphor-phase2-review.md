### pulp-enthusiast (secondary)

**Verdict: VOICE-OK**

The load-bearing question for this persona: does the figurative reach earn its keep, or does it feel grafted onto the beat?

The figure earns its keep. "Her breath goes out like something set down at a threshold it has crossed before" converts a somatic event (breath empties, feeling:4) into a cost-recognition signal that no upstream facet already carries. The proto-line and feeling-flag together give the physical fact of the breath-out; the simile adds the dimension the physical fact alone does not carry — that the body has already priced this crossing, that the threshold is pre-recognized, that the expenditure is deliberate. This is board-deepening: the next action (the crossing) reads as pre-calculated, which escalates the sense that what is about to happen has been long-coming. For a plot-hungry reader at the closing beat of an episode, this is the payoff of prior scene accumulation arriving in one figure.

The "something" question: is the placeholder evading specificity or doing functional work? The figure does not name its cost-object because the point is the pre-recognition, not the currency. The relative clause "it has crossed before" does the specificity work the noun withholds — the threshold is named-as-recognized even if the deposit-object is not. For this persona, this reads as controlled indeterminacy (the figure holds, the momentum holds) rather than vague consequences (§hot_buttons: "vague consequences — 'things got complicated'"). The distinction is that vague consequences obscure what happened; this figure specifies exactly what the breath is doing (being set down as cost at a known threshold) without obscuring the action. The figure is not filler; it is the closing beat's payload.

The relative clause length: "it has crossed before" extends the simile past the persona's preferred quick-figure register. Adjudicated as VOICE-OK because the clause is the closing-twist payoff structure (base card §Syntax: "a long sentence often ends with a clause that retroactively reframes the rest") and lands in one read-beat without structural re-entry. One figure, single clause, at the episode's final beat. Not three paragraphs of agonized decision-making.

**Citation:** §taste (payoffs landing several scenes after setup — this is that payoff; brief setup accepted when the threat is present). No hot-button triggers.
