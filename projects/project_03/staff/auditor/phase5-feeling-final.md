### Entry 3 — @57 edric-cray: his weight takes the back foot before the step

**Verdict: CORRECT**

#### Form check

#### AP16 — "takes" verb register check (primary seam)

The Phase 3 seam identified that "his weight finds the back foot" attributes agency to a non-agent (weight as searcher). The Phase 4 revision replaces "finds" with "takes."

"Takes" applied to weight-distribution: the question is whether "takes the back foot" is working-language or an original-figure metaphor attributing agency to weight.

Hostile reading: "takes" is a verb of volition (one takes what one chooses to take; one cannot take a thing involuntarily in the transitive-volitional sense). "His weight takes the back foot" could be read as weight-choosing-to-take-possession-of the back foot — still an agency attribution, lower amplitude than "finds" but same family.

Working-language reading: military and fencing register uses "take the back foot" as an established phrase meaning "move into a defensive/retreating stance." "Take the back foot" = assume the rearward position. In this register "takes" is not volitional-of-the-weight but is a fixed phrase for a physical positioning act. The subject "his weight" is the agent-of-distribution (the mass going rearward), and the phrase "takes the back foot" is the stance-idiom applied to that mass.

Resolution: the Phase 4 defense correctly identifies "settles onto / shifts to" as cleaner alternatives if the seam cannot be resolved, but the defense argues "takes" is preferred for register reasons (low, plain, no softening). On balance: "takes" in "his weight takes the back foot" reads as the established weight-distribution idiom at the body-mechanics register level, not as an original-figure metaphor attributing volition to weight. The critical distinction from "finds" is: "finds" semantically codes search-and-discovery (one finds what was hidden or unknown); "takes" at the body-weight register codes weight-going-to a foot (weight-bearing, rearward transfer). The construction "take the back foot" has the idiomatic defense the Phase 4 defense claims. The AP16 risk is lower than "finds." The residual risk — that a careful reader hears "weight as agent choosing to possess the back foot" — is present but below the threshold of a form refusal given the working-language defense. This is a soft residual, not a fault. **AP16: PASS with soft caveat** (see caveats section).
