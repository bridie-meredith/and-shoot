## Notes

The naive baseline exhibits one dominant contamination pattern: **2-field saturation**. The writer treated the 2 rung as the default "scene is tense, character is present" signal rather than a per-beat charge signal, resulting in 53% of beats rated 2 against a 20–30% target. The 2 rung has been stripped of its discriminative value; downstream facets conditioning on tensometer would receive near-uniform charge signal across the episode's lower-stakes half.

The second pattern is **sustained climax bleed**: the writer extended the 3 rung backward into lead-in beats (@23, @25, @26, @27, @29) and forward into follow-through beats (@40) around each genuine peak. This produces a smeared 3-cluster rather than a sharp rupture, and it means the @37–41 climax cluster loses distinctiveness — when @23–29 are also rated 3, the climax is one of two apparent peaks rather than the episode's unique high point.

The third pattern is a **coda failure**: the exit sequence (@70–77) was kept at sustained 2s when the officer had already departed and all stakes were resolved. This reflects a failure to execute the release phase of the curve — the writer held tension at 2 through the exit rather than allowing the fall to 1 that the resolved arc calls for. The single egregious outlier is @77 (rated 3, should be 1), which shows the writer attaching narrative-closure weight to a transitional exit beat.

Speech-beat default (8 entries) is the fourth pattern and is systemic across the full episode — the writer consistently over-rates proto-line speaking acts rather than separating the dialogue file's charge from the SVO's on-face charge.
