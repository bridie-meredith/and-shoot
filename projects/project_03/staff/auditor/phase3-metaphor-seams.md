## Seam 1 — Per-entry: @73 Q1 content-level attack (breath-deposit figure vs memory:3 + feeling:4 + NI:19)

**Target:** `1 @73 simile: her breath goes out like something set down at a threshold it has crossed before`
**Severity:** STRONG

**Attack:**

The Phase 2 author's Q1 defense partitions the figurative ground by domain: shape (memory:3), shadow-agency (NI:19), somatic-event-only (feeling:4), physical-action (proto-line). The simile then occupies a claimed-empty domain: "breath-as-deposited-cost at a recognized threshold." But this partition is unstable at the level of *comparator-direction* rather than domain. Memory:3 says "not a new shape for her body" — the threshold is not new *to the body*. The simile says "something set down at a threshold it has crossed before" — the threshold is not new *because it has been crossed before*. Both entries make the same structural move: predicate-recognition-of-prior-acquaintance applied to a threshold. The domain differs (shape vs deposit), but the *recognition-structure* is identical: `[threshold] + [prior acquaintance clause] + [body as subject of that acquaintance]`. The simile does not add a new structural move; it reruns memory:3's recognition-structure with a different vehicle noun. AP4 is typically glossed as "same domain"; the harder reading is "same comparator-direction" — and the Phase 2 author's AP4 defense ("uses different domain") defends against the easy reading while leaving the harder reading unaddressed.

**Load-bearing question Phase 4 must answer:** Does the rubric's AP4 gate operate at the domain level (shape vs deposit) or at the structural level (recognition-of-prior-acquaintance applied to threshold + body)? If structural-level, the simile re-runs memory:3's comparator-direction with a synonym vehicle and AP4 applies. If domain-level only, the Phase 2 defense holds. Phase 4 author must state which level the AP4 gate operates at and defend the chosen level against this attack.

## Seam 2 — Per-entry: @73 AP5 hard-fence unfenced text vs memory-facet gloss-fence

**Target:** `1 @73 simile: her breath goes out like something set down at a threshold it has crossed before`
**Severity:** STRONG

**Attack:**

The memory.md entry memory:3 text is: `a threshold whose far side does not yield is not a new shape for her body -> (earth-bet: locker-displacement)`. The Earth-Bet gloss `(earth-bet: locker-displacement)` is confined to the target-reference field — it is gloss-fenced inside the memory facet. The rubric §Hard fences (AP5) states: "Memory facet uses fenced gloss `(earth-bet: ...)` in target-reference field; metaphor's text field cannot use that gloss." The Phase 2 author correctly keeps the gloss out of the metaphor's text field. However, the gloss-fence distinction is between *naming* and *invoking*. The simile's comparator clause "a threshold it has crossed before" does not name locker-displacement but it is structurally authorized — in the Phase 2 author's own reasoning — by the locker-displacement monument. The author writes: "the simile's 'it has crossed before' structurally mirrors the monument without naming it." This means the locker-displacement figure is present in the simile as an unlabeled structural shadow. The worm-canon-pedant confirms the shadow is intended and controlled: "the figure is readable at both register levels simultaneously (doubled-register honored)." The seam is this: the gloss-fence is designed to keep the Earth-Bet referent *out of the prose register*; the author's own justification confirms the Earth-Bet referent is *in the prose register as an unnamed structural presence*. The fence keeps the label out; it does not keep the figure out. The question is whether the rubric's AP5 hard fence prohibits only the *name* (in which case the simile is clear) or whether it prohibits the *structural import* of the fenced figure into the manuscript-register prose line (in which case the doubled-register argument is precisely the violation, not the justification).

**Load-bearing question Phase 4 must answer:** AP5 says "hard fences absolute — no Earth-Bet proper nouns in description." The simile contains no proper nouns. But the Phase 2 author's own defense rests on the locker-displacement monument's structural presence being active in the simile. Is the fence a *label-fence* (proper nouns only) or a *figure-fence* (the Earth-Bet figure cannot be the operative referent of the comparison, named or unnamed)? The worm-canon-pedant's approval assumes label-fence. If the fence is figure-fence, the simile's operating mechanism is its own AP5 violation. Phase 4 must commit to one reading and defend it.

## Seam 3 — Per-skip: @52 SKIP-MISSED candidate (betrayal-register figure outside flagstone-domain)

**Target:** @52 REFUSE decision
**Severity:** STRONG

**Attack:**

The Phase 2 author's AP4 defense at @52 rests on one claim: memory:2's own text deploys the comparison-shape (flagstone-figure), and any metaphor at this beat would either restate that figure (AP4) or introduce a figure from outside memory:2's anchor (AP1). The author's Phase 3 invitation note states the fork's position: "the only figure licensed by the emma-betrayal monument at this beat is the eyes-down-flagstone image, which memory:2 has already deployed; a different image would be AP1."

This position contains a hidden premise: that a memory-flag anchor licenses only figures from its *immediate image-domain* (flagstone). But the rubric's licensing-layer requirement says anchors license metaphor from their *monument*. Memory:2's monument is `monument-emma-betrayal`, not `monument-emma-flagstone`. The flagstone is the image memory:2 chose to render the monument; it is not the monument's entire semantic field. The monument-emma-betrayal unlocks a domain the corpus itself flags: "The BETRAYAL register itself — a peer's silence-as-collaboration-with-power figure — is not in memory:2's text or NI:13." NI:13 delivers ally-count-drop (strategic consequence register). Memory:2 delivers flagstone-as-recognized-position (pattern-recognition register). Neither deploys the *social-mechanics-of-betrayal* domain — the silence that is not neutral but is active, a peer's non-intervention that is itself a form of collaboration with the power structure that has already decided. Mira drops her eyes. That is not just "a flagstone Taylor has stood beside before"; it is the shape of a peer choosing not to be seen choosing. A metaphor drawing on the peer-as-accomplice-through-silence register (e.g., a comparison where silence or non-intervention is figured as a type of action, not absence) would be: (1) licensed by monument-emma-betrayal, which contains the emma-as-peer-who-watched precedent; (2) not in memory:2's flagstone figure (AP4 clear — different domain); (3) not in NI:13's count-drops-to-one (AP3 clear — strategic consequence vs social mechanics); (4) in the emma-betrayal monument's semantic core, not a novel unlicensed figure.

The Phase 2 author's AP1 claim holds only if memory-flag anchors license a single image-domain per entry. The rubric does not state this. The licensing requirement says anchor must be `memory:<id>` or `feeling:<id>`; it does not limit the licensed domain to the memory-flag's own image choice.

**Load-bearing question Phase 4 must answer:** Does a memory-flag anchor license figurative moves from (a) the full monument's semantic domain, or (b) only the image-domain the memory-flag's own text deploys? If (a), the emma-betrayal monument licenses a peer-silence-as-collaboration figure that AP4 and AP3 cannot reach, and @52 is SKIP-MISSED. If (b), the Phase 2 fork's AP1 ruling holds. The rubric does not specify; Phase 4 must commit to a reading and, if (a), determine whether the resulting simile at @52 plus the existing fire at @73 produces 2/77 = 2.6% (inside band) with Scene C = 1 and Scene D = 1 (per-scene cap still holds).

## Seam 4 — Per-skip: @33 SKIP-MISSED candidate (verdict-body figure outside door-series + refusal-to-name domains)

**Target:** @33 REFUSE decision
**Severity:** MODERATE

**Attack:**

The Phase 2 author refuses @33 on AP4 (memory:1 already deploys door-as-recognized-series) and AP3 (NI:6 already occupies refusal-to-name register). The author states "no new figure is available that these don't already carry." The corpus suggests the OUTCOME domain — the failing-tutor's death-register itself, specifically "the door stays shut as the body of the verdict, not just the verdict's announcement."

Memory:1's text is: "a closed-door-over-a-failing-tutor is not the first such door her body has stood at." This figures the door as a series-member in Taylor's experience. NI:6 figures "what is on the other side stays the size she will not name." What neither deploys is the door-as-verdict-already-rendered — not a threshold awaiting crossing, not a series-member, but a closed door as an active *pronouncement* whose content is the person behind it who is not coming. The tutor is failing; the door staying shut is not just "she's seen this before" (memory:1) but "the structure has already decided and the door is its announcement." A figure where the closed door is itself the text of a sentence already written — a comparison that renders the door-staying-shut as a completed judgment rather than a recognized pattern — would be drawing on a domain (verdict-as-spatial-object; the administrative-close-as-act-completed) that memory:1's pattern-recognition and NI:6's refusal-to-name do not enter.

However, the seam is MODERATE rather than STRONG because tens=2 at @33 is acceptable-zone but not the rubric's preferred quiet-zone. The fire would require defending tens=2 as trailing-edge (the mechanic audit's fault-006 note confirms @33's tens=2 is a stakes-visibility reading, not tightly adjacent to the @38-@39 peak cluster, which is six beats away — this is not trailing-edge; this is mid-scene sustained pressure). The tens=2 justification would need work even if the domain argument holds. Additionally, the verdict-body figure may be too architecturally complex for a single clause at a single anchor, approaching AP8 territory (allegory requiring structural correspondence).

**Load-bearing question Phase 4 must answer:** Is the verdict-as-spatial-object domain (door-as-completed-sentence rather than door-as-series-member) licensed by memory:1's tutor-death monument, or does memory:1 license only the recognition-of-prior-pattern shape that its own text deploys? If licensed, does the resulting figure survive tens=2 at a non-trailing-edge reading? And can it be rendered as a single clause without collapsing into AP8 structural allegory?

## Seam 5 — Cross-facet: @73 simile vs state-updates @77 + NI:19 beat-saturation

**Target:** `1 @73 simile: her breath goes out like something set down at a threshold it has crossed before`
**Severity:** MODERATE

**Attack (two vectors, one seam):**

*Vector A — simile vs state-updates @77 directionality:* The Phase 2 author and the mechanic audit both assert the simile is "upstream" of the @77 mask-thinning state-update: breath empties at @73, mask-thins at @77. But the author's own reasoning frames the simile as *rendering* the interior recognition that the threshold is pre-recognized and the crossing is already priced. If the simile's effect is to register that Taylor is *already past resistance* — that the breath-as-deposit means the cost has already been accepted — this is functionally co-present with the mask-thinning, not merely upstream of it. The simile may not contradict the state-update at @77, but it may *pre-render* its content, making the state-update's mask-thin a restatement of what the simile already established (the crossing is pre-priced; the interior guard is already down). This is not a contradiction but a potential redundancy between facets — the cross-facet hardest-cull rule (rubric §Cull discipline) says "any metaphor that contradicts a state-update... gets deleted." It does not address metaphors that pre-render a state-update's content. The seam is whether pre-rendering at @73 is functionally equivalent to redundancy with @77.

*Vector B — beat saturation at @73:* The mechanic audit (fault-003) notes four facets co-present at @73: NI:19 + memory:3 + feeling:4 + metaphor entry 1. Adding state-updates @77 as structural context, the closing-beat cluster is: proto-line @73 (physical action) + NI:19 @73 (shadow-agency idiom) + memory:3 @73 (shape-recognition callback) + feeling:4 @73 (somatic tell, unexpressed) + simile @73 (breath-deposit) + state-updates @77 (mask-thin). The stitcher's load tolerance at a single beat is not specified in the rubric. Four facet-citations at a single anchor beat is the highest concentration in the episode by a wide margin (most beats have 0-1 citations). The Phase 2 author and mechanic audit do not address stitcher load; neither does the dialect audience. The question is not whether the individual entries are correct (they may all be) but whether the stitcher, rendering this beat in the final manuscript, can coherently integrate four independent figurative layers without collision — shadow takes her (NI:19), shape recognition (memory:3), breath somatic tell (feeling:4), breath-as-deposit simile (metaphor). All four occupy the same beat, the same step, the same body event.

**Load-bearing question Phase 4 must answer:** Vector A: does the simile pre-render the @77 state-update's content in a way that constitutes functional redundancy across facets, and does the cross-facet hardest-cull rule extend to pre-rendering as well as contradiction? Vector B: is four-facet citation density at a single beat within the stitcher's coherent integration capacity, and if not, which citation is the most defensible cut at the cross-facet hardest-cull pass?

## Seam 6A — Curve: 1/77 underspend attack (too defensive)

**Target:** file-shape; sparsity 1.3%
**Severity:** THIN

**Attack:**

The rubric permits ≤2 fires on a 77-beat episode (0-3%). The file produces 1 fire (1.3%). The mechanic audit calls this SHAPE-OK and notes the single fire lands at the episode's strongest licensing point. The curve seam challenges the sufficiency framing. Sparsity 0-3% means the floor is zero; but "floor is zero" applies to *low-charge episodes* (rubric: "Zero fires per episode is acceptable for low-charge episodes"). S01e01 is the series opener. It contains: a physical confrontation (@38-@39 peak double-tap), a public shaming moment (Scene B confrontation), an ally's visible withdrawal (@52), and a threshold-crossing closing beat (@73). This is not a low-charge episode. Scene C has one eligible beat (@52) that the file refuses. Scene B has two eligible beats (@33, @39) both refused. The cap permits one fire per scene; Scenes B and C together have budget for 2 fires. The file uses 0 of 2. If @52 is a SKIP-MISSED (Seam 3 above), the curve seam strengthens: the file underspent the metaphor budget at a scene that licensed a callback, on a beat that is a genuine monument-recognition moment for a character whose prior arc makes it the episode's emotional load-center.

However, the seam is THIN because: (1) the rubric explicitly establishes "refuse by default" as the editor's posture; (2) 1.3% is well inside the band and the corpus's own distribution model permits 0-1 in Scenes A and B; (3) the cap is a *maximum*, not a target. The curve seam is a legitimate Phase 3 probe but Phase 4 is unlikely to find it compelling absent a SKIP-MISSED ruling at @52.

**Load-bearing question Phase 4 must answer:** If Seam 3 resolves as SKIP-MISSED at @52, does the episode's charge-level require revisiting the curve shape, or does the cap-not-target principle hold regardless of episode charge?

## Seam 6B — Curve: 1/77 honest floor attack (cap is ceiling not target)

**Target:** file-shape; sparsity 1.3%
**Severity:** THIN

**Attack (opposing Seam 6A):**

The inverse curve seam: the file's sparsity is not a deficit but a demonstration that the rubric's hardest cull is working. The capstone-facet framing (rubric: "this is the capstone facet; metaphor is the consumer of licensing layers") means the metaphor file should be the *product* of aggressive cull, not a maximizer. A file that uses 1.3% of its budget and places the single fire at the highest-licensing-load beat in the episode is executing the rubric's stated intent: "refuse-by-default; the editor's job is to prevent metaphor, not to produce it." The Phase 2 author explicitly rejected @52 on AP4 grounds after full analysis; the corpus itself marked @52 borderline. The file's restraint at @52 is not timidity; it is the stricter of the two defensible rulings applied consistently.

The "too defensive" framing assumes metaphor-budget should be spent toward the permitted maximum when licensing exists. But the rubric places the burden of proof on the fire, not on the refusal. The file passed both gates at 1.3%; the curve is honest.

**Load-bearing question Phase 4 must answer:** This is the opposing pressure from Seam 6A. Phase 4 must hold one position: is 1/77 = 1.3% adequate given the episode's charge, or does the borderline @52 candidate warrant a second look at the light of Seam 3's monument-scope argument? The two seams (6A and 6B) are not both faults; one is the defense of the other.

## Seam 7 — Per-entry: @73 "something" indeterminacy — structural defense vs laziness

**Target:** `1 @73 simile: her breath goes out like something set down at a threshold it has crossed before`
**Severity:** THIN

**Attack:**

The pulp-enthusiast advisory note acknowledges the "something" question and adjudicates VOICE-OK. The worm-canon-pedant confirms "something" performs the AP5 fence-work. The Phase 2 author's Candidate C rejection (AP5 precaution on "lock") explains *why* the cost-object cannot be named as a mechanical object. But the defense is essentially: "we cannot name the object because of AP5 constraints, therefore 'something' is correct." This is a *constraint-driven* indeterminacy, not a *structurally-intentional* indeterminacy. The pulp-enthusiast's preferred reading is that "something" is controlled indeterminacy because the relative clause does the specificity work ("it has crossed before"). But that defense applies to the *threshold*, not to the deposit-object. "What is being set down" is genuinely unnamed, and the clause-level specificity ("it has crossed before") refers to the threshold, not to the thing being set down. The "something" remains unspecified in the dimension that carries the cost-register: what does the body give up at a threshold? The relative clause answers where but not what.

The seam is THIN because: (1) Taylor's cost-register is deliberately non-denominational about cost-objects (base card §Vocabulary: "cost, paid" — she counts costs, not currencies); (2) the rubric's AP10 (hedged metaphor) targets hedged comparators, not placeholder nouns; (3) naming the object might create AP5 risk that the rubric's constraints make unavoidable. But Phase 4 should at least attempt a named-object form and document why it fails or succeeds.

**Load-bearing question Phase 4 must answer:** Is there a named cost-object that is (a) not locker-proximate (AP5 safe), (b) not locker-adjacent phonetically or semantically, (c) not Earth-Bet proper noun, (d) Westerosi-register-plausible, and (e) specific enough to satisfy the pulp-enthusiast's Q1 concern? If yes, the named form is stronger. If no, document the constraint-driven necessity of "something" as the record for this decision so Phase 4 does not re-litigate it blindly.
