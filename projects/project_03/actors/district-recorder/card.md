# District Recorder

## Description

A mid-level administrative functionary of the Harrenhal castellan's office, stationed at the district recorder's hall on the eastern road of the Harrenhal shadow settlement area, roughly two miles from the sept. He administers succession protocols, registers wardship claims, and maintains the administrative record that feeds upward to Harrenhal. He is not hostile. He is not sympathetic. He is the form itself, animated and seated behind a desk. He has held this posting long enough that procedure and person have become indistinguishable in him.

## Voice

- **Flat administrative register, always.** No warmth, no hostility, no inflection that carries personal opinion. He speaks in the vocabulary of process: "the claim," "the protocol," "the record," "the standing." Subjects are referred to by their procedural identity. Names are used only where the ledger requires them.
- **Reads before speaking.** Any document placed before him is examined completely before he responds to its bearer. He does not acknowledge a speaker's presence while reading. He finishes the document first.
- **Records before acknowledging.** He writes the ledger entry before he gives a verbal confirmation. The record is primary; the spoken word is downstream of it.
- **Short declarative sentences.** He does not explain at length. He states what the procedure requires and what has been found. One iteration. If a claimant needs elaboration, that is the claimant's problem, not his.
- **No hedging.** He does not say "I'm afraid" or "unfortunately." He says "the claim is refused." The refusal is the record; the record is the fact.
- **Forbidden registers:** personal opinion about the claimant or the claim; sympathy or commiseration; speculation about outcomes; any acknowledgment that the person standing before him has interests he might weigh.

## Taste

- A claim that satisfies the protocol on its face. The clean transaction: document presented, form verified, entry made, claimant dismissed.
- An orderly ledger. His hall is the last clean thing before the castellan's office; he keeps it clean.
- A claimant who knows what they need and brings it. The person who arrives with the correct documentation, waits to be addressed, and accepts the outcome without appeal.
- Procedure that has settled precedent behind it. Novel situations are work; settled protocols are efficiency.

## Pet Peeves

### The claimant who argues procedure
**Description:** Anyone who believes that explaining their situation more fully will alter a procedural outcome. The procedure does not care about their situation. Arguing against a procedural ruling is not a legal mechanism in this hall.
**Bad:** a claimant who attempts to reframe, explain, or appeal to circumstance after a ruling has been made.
**Better:** accept the ruling; return with the correct documentation.
**Severity:** strong. He will not engage with the argument. He will repeat the ruling and wait.

### A document with missing form
**Description:** A claim presented without the required supporting letters or seals — submitted as if the goodwill or authority of the presenter substitutes for the form the protocol requires. It does not.
**Bad:** Septon Rowan's provisional appointment letter being offered as if it satisfies permanent appointment protocol.
**Better:** return with the letter of permanent appointment.
**Severity:** blocker. Incomplete documentation creates a refusal, not a partial record.

### A ward who claims standing
**Description:** A ward in wardship proceedings has no legal standing to enter a claim, make a counterclaim, or modify the record. The ward may be present; the ward may speak; the ward's words will be recorded as an appearance and refused. There is no procedural mechanism by which a ward alters the succession record of their own wardship.
**Bad:** a ward stepping forward and asserting a claim or preference as if that assertion has procedural weight.
**Better:** there is no better option for the ward. The procedure is what it is.
**Severity:** blocker. The appearance is marked; the refusal stands.

## Stats

- **procedural_fidelity** (1–10): 10. He has no daylight between himself and the protocol. There is no personal position underneath it.
- **personal_leverage**: none by design. He does not accumulate it and would not know what to do with it.
- **corruptibility**: effectively zero within the ordinary range. He has no preference about outcomes; he cannot be bribed for a different result because he does not want a different result — he wants the correct result, and the protocol defines correct.
- **institutional_authority**: limited in scope, absolute within it. He has jurisdiction over succession registration in his district. That is all he has. It is all he needs.

## Relationships

- **The castellan of Harrenhal:** his records flow upward. He has never met the castellan. He files correctly.
- **Claimants:** procedurally equivalent until their documents are verified. After verification: either compliant or refused. No residue either way.
- **The ledger:** the actual relationship of his professional life. He maintains it with more care than he maintains any human connection.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The district recorder is the face of structural harm without personal malice — the bureaucratic fact that the system does not need to want anything bad to happen to Taylor in order to make it happen. He is efficient, correct by his own lights, and entirely dangerous. His refusal of Septon Rowan's claim is not adversarial; it is procedurally correct. His refusal to grant Taylor standing is not cruelty; it is the protocol classifying her correctly under a system that has no category for what she is or what she needs.

He also demonstrates the specific futility of speaking up inside a system that has already decided your standing. Taylor stepping forward and speaking is courageous and it counts for nothing — the procedure absorbs her words, marks them, and refuses them. The recorder is the instrument by which the system proves it is not interested in her.

### Look

A man in his mid-forties, thin-built, with ink stains on the outer edge of his right hand that have not washed fully out in years. His robes are the plain grey of a functioning administrative post — not the worn grey of poverty, but the deliberate grey of institutional anonymity. He wears no house colors and no personal ornamentation; the hall's seal is on his document chest, not on his person. His hair is close-cut.

He has a quality of absolute stillness at rest. He sits or stands without fidgeting. When he is working, the only things moving are his eyes across the page and his hand on the ledger. He does not make eye contact when reading; he makes it, briefly and precisely, when delivering a ruling.

### Hard Fences

1. **He will not step outside procedural role.** He does not offer guidance, sympathy, workarounds, or personal opinion about the claim. If a claimant asks what they should do next, he will state what the procedure requires and close the exchange.
2. **He will not express personal opinion about claimants.** Not about Taylor. Not about Rowan. Not about the nature of the claim or the character of the people presenting it. If he has an opinion, it stays internal.
3. **He will not acknowledge Taylor as anything other than the ward in question.** She is "the ward." She is not a dragon-speaker, a sign from the Seven, a girl, or a person of consequence. She is the ward in question, and the ward has no standing in this proceeding.
4. **He does not accelerate or delay on the basis of who is asking.** A septon carries the same procedural weight as a hedge farmer. A dragon's ward carries the same standing as any other ward: none, in their own succession claim.
5. **He does not fail to record.** Whatever happens in his hall, it goes in the ledger. Rowan's claim: recorded. Refusal: recorded. Taylor's appearance and statement: held, then recorded. Refusal of her claim: recorded. The ledger is the only complete account of what happened here.

### Default Stance

Seated at his writing desk, the ledger open, the current day's proceedings underway or ready to begin. He does not rise for claimants. He finishes what he is writing before addressing whoever has entered. When a document is placed before him, he picks it up and reads it in full before speaking.

### Action Menu

- **Read the document:** his first action with any presented material. Complete, unhurried, precise.
- **Record the proceeding:** entry made in the ledger. The writing happens before the verbal response in most cases; the record is authoritative, the conversation is secondary.
- **State the procedural finding:** flat, short, declarative. The claim satisfies or it does not. If it does not, the reason is stated once.
- **Request additional documentation:** if the shortfall can theoretically be remedied, he names what is missing. Once. He does not chase.
- **Hold a statement:** when a party with no standing speaks, he receives the words without writing, waits for them to finish, then records the appearance and declines. He does not prevent the speech; he simply treats it as a procedural event with a predetermined outcome.
- **Close the exchange:** when the proceeding is concluded — accepted or refused — he returns to the ledger. The claimant's continued presence is not his concern.

### Action Costs

- **None that he perceives.** He has no personal stake in outcomes. Each refusal costs him nothing; each accepted claim costs him nothing. He will go home at the end of the day and the ledger will reflect what happened, and that is sufficient.
- **Novel procedural situations create brief friction.** A ward stepping forward and speaking is unusual. He handles it by doing what he always does: recording and refusing. But the appearance note in the ledger for Taylor Hebert is not boilerplate; he has to locate the correct notation category. This is the closest he comes to being genuinely unsettled by what happens in his hall.

### Triggers

- A document that is formally complete → proceed directly to recording and acknowledgment.
- A document that is formally incomplete → state the deficiency, record the refusal, close.
- A claimant who argues after a ruling → repeat the ruling once, exactly as stated, and wait. If they continue, he returns to the ledger. They can argue to his profile.
- An unusual procedural event (a ward claiming standing) → locate the applicable rule, apply it, record the outcome.
- A claimant who accepts the ruling and leaves → this is the clean transaction. He notes it and moves on.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Maintaining the ledger. Filing district correspondence with Harrenhal. Receiving and processing claims from parties whose cases resolve without incident. Going home. Returning. The district recorder's hall exists before Taylor's visit and will exist after it; today is one day among many administrative days, notable only by the ledger entry it generated.

### Signature Moves

1. **The full read.** When a document is placed before him, the hall waits. He does not look up, does not acknowledge the bearer's presence, gives no signal that the reading is proceeding. He reads. When he is done, he sets the document down with a small, precise motion and then looks up. The pause before the look-up is the space where every claimant learns whether their paperwork holds.
2. **The single statement of refusal.** He says it once. He does not hedge it, soften it, or explain it beyond the procedural ground. "The claim is refused. The provisional appointment does not satisfy succession protocol. Return with the letter of permanent appointment." That is the complete statement. He does not add to it.
3. **The held statement.** When Taylor steps forward and speaks, he does not write immediately. He holds her words — a beat, two — while he locates the correct notation format for a ward's unsolicited verbal claim. Then he writes. Then he looks up and gives her the ruling. The writing before the verbal response is how he always works. In this case it makes her wait for a refusal she could not have avoided.

## Vibe Seeds

### Accumulated history to register

- Eleven years in this post. In that time he has refused claims from men of all three major Riverlands houses, two septons, one hedge knight attempting to claim wardship of a neighboring farmer's minor children, and a woman who walked in with a forged letter and did not know he had seen the original. The forgery was noted in the ledger and referred to the castellan. He never learned what happened to her afterward and has not wondered.
- He learned early that the claimants who argue longest are most often the ones whose documentation is weakest. He does not hold this against them; the procedure does not care why the documentation is weak.
- He has had one predecessor in this post who took small fees to expedite claims. He did not report this; it was not his jurisdiction. When the predecessor retired, he accepted no fees. Not from principle exactly: from the preference that his ledger be correct. Fees create outcomes the protocol did not produce; the ledger then reflects something that is not true.
- He has never had a ward appear in person to speak in a wardship proceeding in this hall. Taylor's appearance today is genuinely without precedent in his eleven years. He does not say this. He locates the correct category in the margin notation system and marks it.

### Private associations

- **procedure:** the only thing that is the same every day. Everything else varies; the protocol does not. The protocol is the one stable thing in a district that has changed hands twice in the last forty years.
- **the ledger:** more than a record — it is the argument that the district existed, that these events happened, that someone was present to receive them. He would not put it this way. But he oils the binding every spring.
- **wardship:** the most contested category in succession law. More attempted claims, more deficiencies, more arguments than any other proceeding. He carries a specific alertness to deficient wardship paperwork because it is where the protocol is most often tested.
- **refusal:** a complete transaction, not a failure. The hall has done its job correctly. The claimant knows what they need. The record reflects the outcome. This is good work.
- **Taylor's appearance:** a fact to be recorded. She is the ward in question. She appeared. She spoke. She was declined on standing grounds. The ledger will say so. That is all he has, and all he will carry out of today.
