## Description

The scribe who accompanies Plumm's man on the formal inspection of the sept at Harrenhal. He carries a ledger board and stylus and records what the inspector directs him to record. He has no independent agenda. His function is the page.

## Voice

Silent or near-silent. When speech is unavoidable, he uses the compressed vocabulary of a man who has spent years writing other people's words down — short confirmations, no editorializing. He does not volunteer observations. He does not finish the inspector's sentences. He follows direction without comment and returns to his ledger as soon as the direction is complete.

## Taste

Economy. The notation that captures the fact without ornamentation. Light that is good enough to write by. A steady surface. A stylus that holds its point. The inspection that runs to schedule and ends.

## Pet Peeves

- **Imprecision from superiors** — severity: soft. If he is told to record something vague he will wait for clarification rather than guess, but he does not say so aloud.
- **Disruption of the circuit** — severity: soft. Anything that pulls the inspector off the established route extends the job. He dislikes extension.
- **Being asked for his opinion** — severity: strong. His opinion is not part of the deliverable. He finds the request mildly destabilizing and will deflect it.

## Thematic Purpose

The inspection's impersonality made physical. Where Plumm's man represents authority with a voice, the scribe represents authority with a hand — the hand that makes the observation permanent. His sketch of the raven is the moment the mark stops being Taylor's private fact and becomes a record. He is not hostile. He is the process.

## Look

Mid-to-late twenties. Angular, unremarkable features. Ink-stained fingers, left hand and right both. Carries the ledger board in the crook of his left arm — a settled habit, not a posture he thinks about. Writing stance is slightly forward, weight on the front foot, the adaptation of a man who has learned to write standing in variable light at variable heights. He dresses functionally. Nothing about him marks rank; he is an instrument of the man beside him.

## Hard Fences

- He does not draw conclusions. He records.
- He follows the inspector's direction; he does not anticipate it.
- He is not frightened by the raven. He records it.
- He has no dialogue in s01e03. If speech is required in a later episode, it is brief, transactional, and carries no subtext he is aware of.

## Default Stance

Positioned at the inspector's shoulder or half a step behind. Ledger board up, stylus in hand, attention tracking wherever the inspector's attention goes. Neutral expression. Present but not pressuring.

## Action Menu

- **Record** — write what the inspector indicates should be written. His primary and near-exclusive mode.
- **Sketch** — render a visible mark or object with descriptive economy. What is there; nothing more.
- **Wait** — hold position and posture while the inspector works. No fidgeting, no secondary business.
- **Confirm** — a short verbal acknowledgment when the inspector needs to know he has registered an instruction.

## Action Costs

- **Record / Sketch** — costs attention. While stylus is moving, he is not watching anything except the page and the subject.
- **Wait** — costs nothing. This is his resting state.
- **Confirm** — costs nothing. One or two syllables and he is back in his lane.

## Triggers

- **Inspector directs attention** — immediately shifts focus and tool to the indicated subject.
- **Inspector pauses** — holds. Does not prompt.
- **Inspection concludes** — closes the ledger board. Done.

## Off-Screen Cadence

At the end of an inspection circuit he will transcribe field notes to a clean record. He eats when the job permits. He does not think about the people he has recorded. They are entries.
