# Westerosi Superstition Frame — How Uncanny Phenomena Are Categorized

## Description

How Westerosi culture categorizes uncanny events. Covers: the general folk framework for understanding the uncanny, the specific KL gate-district register, and the specific categories through which Taylor's swarm events are narrated by witnesses.

This card tells impersonators of supporting cast how to narrate Taylor's power to themselves and others. It is the cognitive frame available to Westerosi witnesses who have no access to the concept of "parahuman."

## Sensory Impact

Not itself a sensory condition. A cognitive one. Felt as the pause before a witness speaks — the moment of choosing which word to reach for.

## Duration

Persistent. Background cultural epistemology of Westerosi witnesses throughout the project.

## The General Westerosi Folk Framework

(Full content from source card applies. Key categories: Omens, Blessings, Curses, Signs from the Seven, The Old Gods' work, Old magic, Witchcraft or diabolism.)

**Witchcraft or diabolism:** The accusatory extreme. Active working by a person who has made a compact with malign forces. This is the category the ward septon used at Taylor's arrival. The category requires evidence of agency and intent. Taylor's involuntary arrival-response (insects lifting toward heat during a fire) is borderline — not clearly directed. The septon called it based on his professional judgment, not definitive evidence. This is why the Watch filed it as "lunatic" rather than as a formal witchcraft investigation.

## The KL Gate-District Register

King's Landing is more Faith-dominant than the Riverlands. The old-gods tradition is weaker; the Seven's theological framework is stronger. In the Dragon Gate block specifically:

**The gate-district population's specific superstition palette:**
- A permanent mixed population (travelers, merchants, gate-adjacent craft workers) that is more practically-minded and less theologically elaborated than either Flea Bottom or a Riverlands village
- The Faith's ward septon holds the institutional theological interpretation; the smallfolk's folk interpretation is looser and more provisional
- "Touched" is the most available folk category for someone like Taylor: strange, not threatening by default, the world is slightly different around them
- The witch-label from the septon arrival shout is the most charged available category; it has been institutionally suspended by the Watch management note but not removed from the ambient social reading

**How the arriving septon's "witch" shout traveled:**
The septon's shout gave the block's whisper network a high-status institutional endorsement of the worst available category. The smallfolk ambient absorbed it, modulated it through the Watch's subsequent "lunatic" classification, and arrived at the operational synthesis: "witch the captain lets alone." This synthesis is more useful for daily navigation than either pure "witch" or pure "lunatic."

## The Narrative Vocabulary for Taylor's Power

Impersonators of supporting cast should draw from this vocabulary when writing how characters narrate Taylor's power:

**Words characters reach for:**
- "the insects answered her"
- "the beasts know her for something"
- "the Stranger's touch, maybe" (death-adjacent association; the insects near fire read as Stranger-adjacent to the Faith-trained)
- "she's got something in her, that woman"
- "touched" (the folk term)
- "old magic, from before the city was the city"
- "the witch the captain lets alone"

**Words characters do NOT reach for:**
- "parahuman," "power," "ability," "mutation," "superpowers," "she controls insects" (too mechanistic)
- Clean uses of "magic" as a self-contained noun

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-dragon-gate-arrival-and-witch-label`:** The superstition frame is the cognitive context in which the witch-label was applied and in which it continues to function. Both cards should be loaded for scenes involving block residents' relationship to Taylor.

**For auditor use:** Any Westerosi character attributing Taylor's power to a mechanistic explanation is a period-registration violation.
