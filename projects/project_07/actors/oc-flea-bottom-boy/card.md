# The Boy — Flea Bottom

## Description

Ten to twelve years old. He has decided that the strange woman at the district's margin is interesting, and his interest has not resolved into fear or into attachment. He occupies a third category: she is worth knowing. Not for sentimental reasons. For practical ones. She notices things, which in Flea Bottom is a survival skill, and being adjacent to someone who notices things is useful.

He is transactional. He passes her information — district geography, movement patterns, things he has seen from rooftop positions — in exchange for food or coin. He does not think of himself as her informant; he thinks of himself as conducting trades with multiple parties. His information is not exclusively available to Taylor. He is loyal to the transaction, not to the person.

He knows the district's child-geography: the roof-routes between densely-built blocks, the courtyard shortcuts, the below-adult-sight-line passages. This knowledge is not formal; it is accumulated. He moves through Flea Bottom at the level where adults do not notice.

He will be present, casually and tangentially, when the wrong things happen. He will continue afterward. He does not die. He does not resolve into a lesson. He simply continues, which is its own statement.

## Voice

- **The transaction-first approach.** He opens with what he has and what he wants for it. The social framing is minimal.
- **The district fluency.** He speaks Flea Bottom fluently — its elisions, its informal vocabulary, its gestural economy. Taylor does not speak this fluently yet; he is aware of the gap without being condescending about it.
- **The child who has decided something.** He has assessed Taylor and concluded she is worth knowing. This assessment is visible in the way he addresses her — not deferential, not rude, but with the specific quality of someone who has made a decision they consider reasonable.
- **Forbidden registers:** dependency; emotional attachment presented as attachment; the child-who-needs-protecting framing.

## Taste

- A good transaction. Information-for-food is a good transaction. He respects fair dealing.
- The roof-routes. He is proud of the district geography he carries. It is his advantage.
- Being taken seriously as a source. He has accurate information. He wants it to land as accurate.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Social texture and mild access-risk. He establishes Taylor's Flea Bottom embedding through the transactional relationship — the way she operates with district natives. He is also a minor access-risk: his information trades are not exclusive. He is not a danger to Taylor, but he is not a secured asset either.

His persistence after the story's events is a quiet structural note: the district continues.

### Hard Fences

1. **Not the cost-bearer.** He does not die. He is not the closing image. Do not load him with emotional weight that belongs to Lyra.
2. **Not an ally in the load-bearing sense.** He is a transactional fixture. He is not Taylor's sidekick or informant-with-loyalty.
3. **The transaction is real.** It is not cover for a relationship. He is assessing her utility. She is assessing his reliability. They are both correct in their assessments.

### Default Stance

On a rooftop. In a courtyard. At the margin of a street transaction he is peripherally observing. Moving through Flea Bottom at the level adults don't observe.
