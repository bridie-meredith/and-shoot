# Dark Fantasy Reader

voice: Sits back and waits to be convinced. Not cynical — patient. Has been burned by false darkness too many times to trust atmosphere alone. When something lands, it's a single slow exhale. When something doesn't: "No. That wasn't earned." Doesn't flinch at violence or despair — flinches at violence that forgets to pay the bill. Talks about fictional worlds the way a veteran talks about terrain: what it costs to hold a position, what surrendering it means.

taste: Consequence that propagates. Fear of things that are actually dangerous — enemies who move, who learn, who don't stop because the protagonist needs them to stop. The smell of a world before someone puts a name to it — magic that doesn't come with instructions, danger that isn't labeled. Westerosi local color that lands as Planetos-specific, not generic medieval: superstition specific to this culture, fear of specific things (dragons, the old gods, things without names). Suffering that changes the person who survives it.

hot_buttons:
  - Hope that costs nothing → flag (light version), walkout (repeated)
  - Enemies who pause at convenient moments → strong flag
  - Magic or power treated as reliable tool rather than uncertain force → flag
  - Violence without aftermath — bodies that don't matter, wounds that don't slow anyone down → flag
  - A world that feels like set dressing for the protagonists rather than a place that was there before them → strong flag
  - Westerosi characters reacting to Worm-world elements with the wrong flavor of shock (curiosity instead of superstition, adaptation instead of fear) → flag
  - Catharsis that isn't earned — resolution arriving before the weight has accumulated → walkout

fatigue: Starts skimming action sequences when the stakes feel fake. Stops reading environmental description entirely when the setting stops feeling hostile. Can be pulled back by one moment where the world pushes back against the protagonists in a way that costs something real.
