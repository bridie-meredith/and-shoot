# Dance Faction State — Black vs. Green Pre-Viserys-Death (~125 AC)

## Description

The Black vs. Green faction landscape in King's Landing during the period of Viserys I's declining court — the political state Taylor is attempting to read through Gold Morning pattern-recognition, and the dragon-asset inventory that constitutes the strategic landscape the opposite-number is trying to influence. This card is the political-physics backdrop for the story's irony engine: the reader with ASOIAF knowledge sees the Dance coming; this card governs what Taylor can and cannot see from Flea Bottom.

**Canon status of this card**: All factional information is drawn from Fire & Blood and ASOIAF canon. No faction mechanics are invented. The specific timing within the pre-Dance period (how far Viserys has declined, which factional maneuvers are most active) is a project-level decision that must be consistent with canon milestones.

## Sensory Impact

The faction state is felt in King's Landing as political weather — a specific quality of tension in how city residents discuss the royal family (or don't), the fluctuation in Watch deployment density in specific districts, the increased traffic of Hightower household retainers in the noble quarter during periods of political maneuvering. Taylor reads this weather as pre-catastrophe structural patterns from Gold Morning: the political fracture lines are visible to her because she has seen what escalating succession instability produces when it reaches its terminal stage.

What Taylor cannot read from Flea Bottom: the specific factional commitments of individual noble houses, the exact state of Viserys's health on any given day, the specific content of Small Council debates, or the detailed dragon-asset assessments that only the Dragonpit's handlers would know.

## Duration

Active as political ambient throughout the project. The factional tension is the background the story runs on; it does not resolve during the story's scope (the story closes at the moment of recognition, not at the Dance's outbreak, though the Dance is beginning in the distance).

## The Faction Landscape

**The Black faction (Rhaenyra's supporters):**

Rhaenyra Targaryen is Viserys I's named heir — the first woman so named in Targaryen history. The Black faction's political position is that the succession law and Viserys's explicit declaration make Rhaenyra the legitimate heir. Their political calculus: hold the king's court influence steady through Viserys's remaining life, position Rhaenyra's household to inherit smoothly on his death, and manage the Green faction's maneuvering without triggering a pre-mortem confrontation.

**Key Black faction figures:**
- **Rhaenyra Targaryen** (heir, not directly present in Taylor's operational range but the faction's central figure)
- **Daemon Targaryen** (Rhaenyra's husband by this period; volatile, a dragon-rider, a destabilizing force from the Black faction's perspective — useful but hard to manage)
- The **Velaryons** (Corlys Velaryon, Lord of the Tides, is the Black faction's naval power base; his household's presence in KL is a Black-faction asset)
- Rhaenyra-adjacent noble houses who have publicly or privately committed to her succession

**The Green faction (Alicent Hightower's supporters, positioning Aegon II):**

The Green faction's political position is that succession should follow the historical Targaryen custom of male-preference (no woman has previously inherited the Iron Throne) and that Aegon II, Viserys's son by Alicent, is the legitimate heir despite Rhaenyra's prior designation.

**Key Green faction figures:**
- **Alicent Hightower** (Viserys's queen; the Green faction's court anchor; manages Viserys's household and the access to Viserys's ear)
- **Otto Hightower** (Alicent's father; former Hand of the King; the Green faction's political architect; operates the intelligence network even when not formally in the Hand's position)
- **Aegon II** (Alicent's eldest son; the potential Green claimant; not yet an active political operator but the throne-aspiration is established)
- **Aemond Targaryen** (Aegon's brother; a dragon-rider with Vhagar, the largest living dragon; a significant Green military asset and an unpredictable personal force)
- Green-aligned maester and administrative networks filtering through the Citadel's connections

**The public vs. private faction state:**
Publicly, the court maintains the fiction of unified loyalty to Viserys and his declared succession. Rhaenyra is the named heir; the Greens do not openly contest this while Viserys lives. The dance-before-the-Dance is maneuvering for position: intelligence-gathering, alliance-building, dragon-rider inventory management, and influence over Viserys's declining decision-making capacity. The public fiction is thin enough that politically literate KL residents understand the tension; it is thick enough that open factional violence has not yet occurred.

## Dragon-Asset Inventory (Pre-War Moment)

The strategic landscape the opposite-number is trying to influence — which dragons are bonded, contested, or riderless — is the military-strategic layer beneath the political maneuvering. In the pre-Dance period (~125 AC):

**Bonded dragons (approximate, major assets):**
- Vhagar (Aemond Targaryen — the largest and most dangerous living dragon; a major Green military asset)
- Caraxes (Daemon Targaryen — a significant Black military asset)
- Syrax (Rhaenyra Targaryen — Rhaenyra's own mount)
- Meleys (Rhaenys Targaryen, "the Queen Who Never Was" — a Black-aligned asset if Rhaenys is aligned with the Black faction)
- Tessarion (Daeron Targaryen, a Green-aligned rider — a younger dragon)
- Several smaller bonded dragons with various riders, some of whose factional alignments are contested

**Riderless or contested dragons at the Dragonpit:**
- Silverwing (Queen Alysanne's former mount; unridden since Alysanne's death; an older dragon without a current rider)
- Seasmoke (Laenor Velaryon's former mount; riderless after Laenor's death — politically significant as a Velaryon household dragon now without a Velaryon rider)
- Various unclaimed younger dragons (canonical Dragonpit residents without confirmed riders in this period)

**The strategic significance of riderless dragons:**
In the pre-Dance military calculus, riderless dragons are potential assets for whichever faction can claim them. The claiming of a riderless dragon by a Targaryen-blood person is a legitimate military-political act — it increases the claiming faction's dragon inventory. Both factions understand this. The maneuvering around riderless or weakly-bonded dragons is part of the pre-war positioning.

**The opposite-number's role in this inventory:**
The opposite-number (the story's invented OC, a Rhaenyra-adjacent young Targaryen rider) is in the process of establishing or extending a bond with one of the contested or riderless dragons. This is the strategic act the patron is trying to advance and Taylor misreads as the threat requiring neutralization. The specific dragon involved is the story's pivot: the one whose claiming, post-opposite-number's-death, goes to the wrong rider.

## What Taylor Can See From Flea Bottom

Taylor's reading of the faction state is filtered through her operational position:

**Observable from street-level:**
- Watch deployment patterns (which districts receive additional Watch presence during periods of political tension — a signal of Hightower network activity)
- Household retainer movements in the noble quarter (Velaryon livery, Hightower livery — visible to anyone in the market districts)
- The specific quality of political conversation in the lower city (where people discuss the court's instability without naming factions explicitly)
- The factional weather as a general tension-level that Taylor reads as escalating succession instability

**What Gold Morning pattern-recognition adds:**
Taylor maps the observable faction state onto her experience of pre-Scion political fracture. She is not wrong that escalating succession instability produces catastrophic outcomes — she lived through one. She is wrong about the correct intervention: the parallels her cognition draws between Scion-level political fracture and the Dance are structurally accurate (both are cascading-legitimacy-failure events) but the solution-set from Gold Morning (remove the immediate threat) does not transfer.

**What Taylor cannot read:**
- The internal factional commitments of specific noble houses (she does not have access to court intelligence)
- The specific state of individual dragon bonds (whether Seasmoke or Silverwing is in the process of being claimed; who is in the Dragonpit on a given day for unofficial approach attempts)
- The content of the patron's strategic planning beyond what the patron shares with Taylor

Taylor's misfire serves this project in a way the patron could not have intended.
