# Flicker Discipline Mirror — Taylor's Involuntary Precognitive Flicker (Binding Rules)

## Description

The complete binding rule-set for Taylor's flicker in this project. Every rule in this card carries from `world-notes.md` and is marked as binding. Any scene where an agent writes Taylor's flicker in a way that violates these rules is an auditor flag.

The flicker is Taylor's only power residual post-transit. It is not a tactical asset. It is an involuntary, unreliable, fragment-delivering process that costs her something every time it fires and that she cannot distinguish from misfire. The patron's theory (that near-dragon proximity amplifies it) is wrong. The kill-misfire is the story's load-bearing plot mechanism.

**Source authority**: `active-project/staff/showrunner/world-notes.md` — all rules marked `[1b carry-forward]` or `[audience-triad]` are preserved verbatim here.

## Sensory Impact

The flicker has a specific physical register: an involuntary arrest of motion, the eyes going slightly unfocused, a micro-period of absence from present sensory input before Taylor snaps back. Observers who know her well enough to notice read it as a distinctive tell. Near a dragon, the register intensifies: the absence-period is longer, the return is less clean, and Taylor's body often shows the Gold Morning startle-reflex (she blinks hard, her breath catches). The patron reads this intensified register as amplification evidence; it is trauma response.

## Duration

Persistent and involuntary throughout the project. The flicker does not improve, worsen, or become controllable over the story's arc.

## The Binding Rules

**Rule 1 — Involuntary: she cannot turn it on.**

The flicker fires without Taylor's decision. She cannot invoke it, direct it toward a specific question, or produce it on demand. Any scene where Taylor attempts to use the flicker as a tool — directing it at a specific question or attempting to fire it on command — is a fence violation, regardless of whether the attempt succeeds.

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "Power state = altered by transit. Taylor has an involuntary precognitive flicker: brief, uncontrollable, fragmentary glimpses of near-future states."

**Rule 2 — Unreliable: the glimpses are fragmentary and unverifiable.**

The flicker delivers fragments. Not complete scenes. Not labeled information. Fragments of sensory impression — an image, a sound, a physical sensation — that Taylor must interpret. The fragment may be accurate; it may be a misfire. She has no mechanism to verify which. She cannot check the flicker against reality before acting on it; she can only act on it or ignore it and learn retrospectively whether it was accurate.

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "Unreliable. Unverifiable. She cannot distinguish a correct reading from a misfire."

**Rule 3 — Must cost: each glimpse must cost something interpretive, not just informational.**

The cost is not merely that Taylor receives inaccurate information. The cost is that the flicker's arrival forces an interpretive choice under uncertainty, and the choice has consequences whether it is right or wrong. A flicker that confirms Taylor's existing beliefs without forcing any reinterpretation or any action-commitment under uncertainty is cost-evasion.

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "Any scene where flicker confirms her good intentions without costing her is cost-evasion and an auditor flag."

**Positive formulation of the cost rule**: The interpretive cost is felt when: (a) Taylor must commit to an interpretation of the fragment before she knows if it is correct, and that commitment closes off alternative actions; (b) the fragment forces a reinterpretation of something Taylor believed she understood; (c) the fragment is correct but the action Taylor takes in response to it produces a consequence she did not anticipate.

**Rule 4 — Near-dragon: louder and more violent, NOT more reliable.**

Near a dragon, the flicker fires with greater intensity — more visual violence in the fragment, longer absence-period, more disruptive physical response. This increased intensity is produced by the Gold Morning trauma trigger (dragons-at-proximity are Scion-scale threat-pattern activators in Taylor's nervous system). The increased intensity is increased noise, not increased signal. The flicker near a dragon is not more accurate than the flicker anywhere else; it is more physically costly to receive.

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "Near-dragon flicker is louder and more violent — but NOT amplified in the direction the patron believes. Near-dragon flicker costs more, not more usefully."

**The patron's misread**: Taylor's increased distress near a dragon is visible and consistent. The patron interprets it as the flicker firing with greater intensity and accuracy. This interpretation is the patron's wrong theory. The near-dragon flicker's increased output is increased noise; the patron cannot distinguish increased noise from increased signal. This misread is the structural seam where the patron's wrong theory of Taylor becomes a load-bearing story element.

**Rule 5 — Gold Morning pattern-recognition interaction.**

Taylor interprets flicker fragments through the Gold Morning lens. She is looking for Scion-equivalent threats — escalating succession instability, accumulating military capability, the specific structural markers of a political situation approaching cascade failure. She reads the Dance's pre-war faction maneuvering as pre-Scion political fracture because it has structurally similar patterns (legitimacy fracture, weapons of mass violence at faction disposal, escalating individual decision stakes). She is not wrong that the Dance is a catastrophe; she is wrong that the correct intervention is the same kind of act she deployed against parahumans threatening a Scion-equivalent event.

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "The Gold Morning pattern-recognition interaction: Taylor's flicker glimpses are interpreted through the Gold Morning lens. She is looking for Scion-equivalent threats."

**Rule 6 — The kill-misfire: the specific event that leads to the bad act.**

The flicker delivers: a dragon above a burning city with a rider's silhouette. Taylor reads the rider's silhouette as the threat (the person whose rider-ship produces the conflagration). She identifies the opposite-number as that rider. She is wrong. The fragment shows the outcome (dragon + burning city) but not the correct cause-chain. The rider in the fragment is not the opposite-number; it is the wrong rider, whose claim on the dragon follows from the opposite-number's death. Taylor's interpretation is internally consistent with her Gold Morning framework; it is factually wrong.

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "A glimpse of the dragon above a burning city with a rider's silhouette — she identifies the rider as the threat; she is wrong about which rider. The flicker shows her the outcome (dragon + burning city) but not the correct cause-chain (wrong rider, not the opposite-number)."

**Rule 7 — HARD FENCES.**

*From world-notes.md (binding):*

"Hard fence: Any scene where flicker gives Taylor reliable information without interpretive cost is a fence violation. Any scene where Taylor can distinguish a correct flicker from a misfire is a fence violation."

No exceptions. No project-level override. These fences apply in every scene involving the flicker.

## What the Flicker Is Not

For auditor clarity:

- The flicker is not Contessa-level path-finding. Taylor cannot see reliable chains of optimal action.
- The flicker is not precognitive in the predictive-model sense. She cannot query it.
- The flicker is not a power she can suppress or conceal by effort of will — it fires involuntarily; she can suppress its behavioral expression (the tell that observers read) with effort, but not the firing itself.
- The flicker is not amplified by proximity to dragons in the sense the patron believes. It is disrupted by proximity to dragons.
- The flicker does not provide Taylor with information she then simply acts on. It provides fragments she interprets, and her interpretation is shaped by a framework that is applicable but wrong-mapped.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-patron-amplification-theory-mirror`:** The patron's wrong theory is built entirely on misreading Rule 4. This card is the correct description; that card describes the misreading and its consequences.

**With `cond-shard-deposit-mechanics-mirror`:** The flicker's origin is the Shard-transit residual. That card describes what the transit deposited; this card governs how the residual operates in practice.

**With Taylor's close-third interiority**: The close-third POV must render the flicker from the inside — Taylor's experience of receiving a fragment, the interpretive process she runs, the cost of committing to an interpretation. The body text must never give the reader more certainty about the flicker's accuracy than Taylor has. The reader cannot know, from Taylor's interiority, whether a given flicker is a misfire until the consequences land. This is structural to the irony engine.

**For auditor use:** Any scene where Taylor decides to use the flicker as a deliberate tool is a Rule 1 violation. Any scene where the flicker delivers complete, verifiable information is a Rule 2 violation. Any scene where a flicker delivers correct information without extracting an interpretive cost is a Rule 3 violation. Any scene where near-dragon flicker is presented as more accurate (as opposed to more violent and disruptive) is a Rule 4 violation. Any scene where Taylor reads flicker-fragments with certainty about which rider the silhouette represents before the bad act is a Rule 7 fence violation.
