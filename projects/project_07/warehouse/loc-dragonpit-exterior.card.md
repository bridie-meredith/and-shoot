# The Dragonpit — Exterior Approach (Hill, Outer Wall, Approach Road)

## Geography

The Dragonpit sits atop Rhaenys's Hill, the northeastern prominence within King's Landing's walls. The exterior encompasses the approach road that winds up from the city's middling streets, the wide earthwork terracing at the hill's crown, the outer circuit wall, and the open ground between the wall's base and the terrace edge. The hill is not a gentle climb: the approach road gains elevation steadily for twenty minutes of walking from the eastern quarter, the final section steeper and exposed to wind off the bay.

This is city-within-city geography. The Dragonpit complex occupies the hilltop with the authority of infrastructure: the road exists to serve it, the space around it is deliberately open (cleared for fire clearance and crowd management during dragon-movement events), and the structures of the surrounding streets step back from the approach road in a way that frames the destination before the visitor arrives. There is no casual foot traffic here; the Dragonpit approach is a purposive walk.

Distance references: from the Velaryon townhouse on the upper hill slope, the outer wall is visible above the next rooftop tier and reachable in ten minutes on foot. From Flea Bottom's market-side junction, the approach takes forty to fifty minutes, ascending steadily. From the Fish Gate margin, closer to an hour. The approach from below passes through the eastern quarter, then through streets that grow progressively less residential and more service-facing: the trades that support the Dragonpit operation have their premises along the approach road's lower section.

## Layout

**Approach road lower section:** Wide enough for a cart hauling feed or bedding material. The surface is maintained at the lower section (cobbled, repaired regularly) and rougher toward the crown where cart access becomes limited. Service buildings along this section: grooms, supply factors, the keepers' secondary quarters.

**The terraced open ground:** Before the outer wall, the hill levels into a wide cleared ring. This is where people wait who have business with the Dragonpit but have not been admitted. On ordinary days it is nearly empty. During periods of Targaryen court activity, it holds retinues, petitioners, grooms with supply carts, and the permanent small group of the curious who come to experience the proximity without seeking access. The open ground is the first point where the dragons' presence is genuinely felt: sound, smell, and heat combine here into something that operates below the level of identified threat and above the level of simple ambient noise.

**The outer wall:** Stone, thick enough to be a fortification, though the Dragonpit was not built primarily as a defensive structure — it was built to contain. The wall has no decorative ambition. Its surface is stained with the product of decades of dragon exhalation: char deposits, soot, a dark water-stain pattern where the condensation runs. The gates are iron-worked, heavy, and guarded at all hours. The gatehouse arch is tall enough for a large load to pass; the postern gate to one side is for foot traffic admitted by specific permission.

**The gatehouse approach:** From the open terrace to the gatehouse, a short path of flagstones. This is the threshold Taylor is brought to — not past it, on her first exposure. The patron's theory requires Taylor's proximity; the misread of the flicker's cost as evidence of amplification happens here, in this transitional space where the outer wall is close enough to touch but the interior is not yet entered.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Sound:** The dominant sensory register before sight. The dragons are technically contained inside the structure, but containment is a legal and physical fact, not a sonic one. What reaches the terraced approach is a compound sound that does not resolve into specific components: a low resonance that operates at the chest register rather than as identifiable noise, occasional structural percussion (movement inside the pit that the outer wall transmits as vibration rather than sound), and — infrequently but memorably — a vocalization that begins inside the structure and arrives on the approach road as something that was a roar when it started and is only its echo by the time it lands. On a still day the sound-environment on the terraced ground is the reason people stop talking and look at each other before they enter.

**Smell:** The approach road's lower section smells of dung, hay, and working animals — the infrastructure of the keepers' support work. Moving up the hill, this smell transitions: the clean-animal note narrows, the char note opens beneath it. By the time the terraced ground is reached, the dominant ambient is an animal-musk and cold-fire compound that has no clean name because it has no common analogue. Not smoke, not burning — something that was smoke and burning long enough ago that the result is different. The wall's stone surface, in rain, releases a heated-rock note that adds a mineral register to the compound.

**Visual:** The Dragonpit dome is visible from many points in the city, but from the approach road its scale becomes specific: it is genuinely large in the way that structures built for creatures larger than horses are large — not the height of a tower, but the breadth of a space that was never designed around human proportion. The dome's silhouette from below has a quality of deliberate compression: the structure is lower than its footprint suggests it should be, because it was built to contain lateral movement, not vertical. The outer wall from the terraced ground is tall enough to block sightline to the dome for the first twenty paces of approach.

**Physical pressure:** The thermal differential is specific and learnable. The approach road, away from the wall, sits at ambient city temperature. The terraced ground runs two to three degrees warmer than the surrounding street level in still air, because the wall radiates the contained heat at a measured rate. At the gatehouse threshold, the differential is sharp enough that Taylor — arriving from a forty-minute uphill walk — steps through it and stops.

**Taylor's flicker register (project-specific):** The first time Taylor's flicker fires near the Dragonpit, she is on the approach road's mid-section, still fifteen minutes from the outer wall. What the flicker produces is not a glimpse of the future — it is a recognition-spike that her Gold-Morning experience classifies as a WMD proximity read. The pattern is the pattern that preceded Scion's arrival at a location: concentrated destructive potential, bounded but not controlled, held by something other than good intent. The flicker's cost at this proximity is not informational — she does not see anything useful. The cost is physical: a refractory period of several seconds, involuntary physical stillness, the specific nausea of post-glimpse processing when the glimpse produced no actionable signal. The patron, walking beside her, reads her halt and stillness as absorption of incoming information. This misread is the structural origin of the amplification theory.

## Fixed Props

None card-level.

## Exits

- **Approach road (descent):** Back down the hill to the eastern quarter and the city's middling streets. The only route Taylor knows in and out of this complex. The descent takes less time than the ascent; the incline is sharper at the crown.
- **Gatehouse gate (admission, guarded):** Entry to the outer wall and the interior approach. Not freely available; requires acknowledgment from the Dragonpit staff or evidence of authorized business. Taylor passes through this gate on patron-arranged visits.
- **Postern gate (foot traffic, restricted):** Secondary entry for authorized individuals without a retinue. Taylor may be admitted this way on later visits when she is known to the gate staff.
- **Service access (lower wall):** A secondary road, barely wide enough for a supply cart, that runs around the hill's northern face to the supply entrance. Not Taylor's route.

## Hazards

**Flicker misread exposure:** The approach road and terraced ground are where the patron first observes Taylor's flicker response and misidentifies its cause. Any subsequent visit reproduces this dynamic: the patron is watching for confirmation of the amplification theory, and Taylor's response — however it presents — will be read through that interpretive frame. The terraced ground is where Taylor is most legible to the patron's wrong theory.

**Open exposure:** The terraced approach is observed space. Anyone on the approach road is visible from the gatehouse, from the street-level intersection below, and from the open ground. A woman from Flea Bottom being escorted to the Dragonpit by Velaryon retainers is a legible social event. The Green faction has intelligence presence in the streets around the hill approach.

**Crowd dynamics during events:** Dragon-movements, feeding events, or significant Targaryen visits bring retinues and crowds to the terraced ground. The social geography of these crowds is factional — who stands where, who departs when, whose retinue waits in the gate's shadow — and Taylor is not fluent in reading it without the network she no longer has.

**The approach as commitment point:** There is no neutral reason for a Flea Bottom woman to be on the Dragonpit approach road. Arrival here implies either authorization (which means faction association) or unauthorized presence (which means risk of removal). Taylor's every visit is a statement of association, visible to faction observers.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A Targaryen retinue arriving on the approach road as Taylor ascends — the social choreography of precedence (who moves aside, who does not) playing out in real time around her
- The flicker firing on the lower approach road, before Taylor has reached the terraced ground — stronger than the previous visit, a sign of some change in the interior she cannot name
- The gatehouse staff acknowledging Taylor by sight on a later visit — the specific transition from escorted visitor to recognized presence, and what that recognition costs her in the social register of the approach
- A Green-faction observer in the crowd at the terraced ground, visible to Taylor as a behavioral irregularity (watching exits rather than the gate) before she can identify their faction affiliation
- An unbonded dragon vocal event during approach — the specific acoustic quality of a vocalization from a dragon without active handling, which sounds different from the contained-dragon ambient
- Rain on the approach road, the outer wall releasing its mineral-heated smell — and Taylor's flicker spiking in the downpour for reasons the weather has nothing to do with
- A keeper's body being removed from the supply access road on the hill's north face — visible from the terrace edge, processed by Taylor as a casualty rate data point in her WMD proximity model
