# The Dragonpit — Interior (Consultation Space and Kill Site)

## Geography

The Dragonpit's interior is the space within the outer wall and below the dome: a volume built to hold multiple large dragons at rest, chained and tended, with the human infrastructure of their keeping organized around the perimeter. The floor is the original quarried stone of the hilltop, worn smooth in the keeper pathways and still rough in the dragon-adjacent zones. The dome overhead closes the space; its ocular opening lets in a column of sky-light that moves across the floor over the day like a sundial.

This is not a human-scale space. The Dragonpit was designed around the horizontal extent of dragons at rest, which means the interior's most immediately felt quality is width: the far wall is far, in the specific way that the far wall of a cathedral is far — not because the structure is ornate, but because the scale was set by something other than human proportion. The immediate perimeter is organized for human access: chain-anchor stations, keeper galleries, the feed preparation areas off the east and west secondary walls. The center is the dragons' ground.

**Dual function (project-specific):** This card supports two distinct scene types:
1. **Patron-mediated consultation visits:** Taylor is brought inside to be near a dragon, as a test of the patron's amplification theory. These scenes happen in the keeper gallery section near the primary chain-anchor — close enough to the chained dragon for the flicker to respond, far enough from the dragon itself for the encounter to be managed. Rhaenys is present for the early consultations.
2. **The kill site:** The misfire and the death of Vaegon happen in this space. The spatial logic of the kill requires: a moment of reduced witness presence, a close approach to the dragon that brought Taylor and Vaegon into the same segment of the interior, and sufficient darkness between the entry torches that Taylor's read of the figure in the interior was plausible as mistaken identification. All three conditions are available in this space.

## Layout

**Entry passage:** From the gatehouse, a short covered passage enters the outer ring of the interior. The passage has torches at intervals; the torches are maintained at a specific density — enough for keeper navigation, not enough for the whole interior to be lit. Stepping from the passage into the main floor is a sensory threshold: the air changes, the scale changes, the light changes.

**Keeper gallery (perimeter, north):** The northern section of the interior perimeter has a raised stone gallery — a tier, barely wide enough for two people abreast — that runs between the chain-anchor stations. This is where non-keeper visitors stand when they are brought inside. The gallery puts a person at approximately the height of a dragon's shoulder when the dragon is lying down. This is where Taylor stands during consultation visits. The stone rail at the gallery's edge is chest-height on a standing adult; it is not a substantial barrier, but it marks the boundary of the keeper zone.

**Chain-anchor stations:** At intervals around the perimeter, iron anchor-posts are set into the stone floor and wall together. The chains are heavy enough that their movement produces sound before the dragon attached to them moves — a preliminary warning that the keepers learn to read. The chains have slack, measured in the specific arc that allows the dragon to breathe and shift weight without reaching the gallery rail. Taylor maps this arc on her first visit without being aware she is doing it.

**The center floor:** The broad interior floor between the perimeter gallery and the far wall is keeper territory — they move through it on established paths, the pathways defined by where it is safe to walk rather than by any marking. There is no formal barrier between the gallery and the floor. A person who stepped off the gallery into the keeper zone would not be stopped by architecture; they would be stopped by keepers.

**The fire-mark section (east secondary wall):** The east wall shows the specific staining of a dragon-fire incident that was contained but not erased: a wide darkening of the stone, starting at about knee height and running up to the ocular base, with a heat-fracture pattern in the stone above. This section of the wall has no chain-anchor stations. The keepers give it a wider berth than the physical damage requires. Taylor notices this without knowing its history; it reads as a zone the keepers treat differently, which her hazard-mapping instinct flags.

**The shadow zone (west segment, between torches):** The western section of the interior runs between two torch stations with a larger gap than elsewhere — the result of a damaged mounting that has not been replaced. This is the lowest-light section of the interior during normal operations. The kill happens here. Taylor's misidentification of Vaegon is enabled by this shadow zone: the figure she sees is backlit by the entry torch and positioned in the darker section, identifiable by silhouette (young Targaryen, near the dragon's chain arc) rather than by face. Her flicker fires at maximum cost in this position. She acts on what she sees.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** The dominant sensory feature of the interior, overriding all other registers on first entry. Animal musk at large-mammal scale, modified by reptilian specificity — not the warm damp smell of horse stabling, but a drier compound with a cold-fire overlay. Char is present everywhere: not active fire but the residue of centuries of exhalation, absorbed into the stone and released at a background rate. The keeper pathways smell of human activity (lamp oil, the specific body-smell of men who work in physical proximity to large animals and do not attempt to mask it). The dragon-adjacent zones smell of the dragon alone; the animal note is loud enough to suppress everything else at twenty paces.

**Sound:** The dominant acoustic register is the dragon's ambient vocalization — not roaring, not distress, but the specific sound-register of a very large animal breathing and existing. This sound is felt as much as heard: low-frequency resonance transmitted through the stone floor into the soles of feet, accompanying the audible breath. Chain sound is present when the dragon moves: the drag of iron link on iron link, the anchor groan when weight shifts. The entry passage kills city sound completely; stepping into the interior from the passage, the transition is between two acoustic worlds, and the interior world is the dragons'.

**Visual:** The ocular light column shifts across the day. Morning visits have the light in the eastern section; afternoon visits have it in the center or west. In the shadow zone between the western torches, the light column does not reach. At night, or in overcast conditions, the interior is torch-lit entirely — a reddish, directional light that creates strong shadows at the gallery rail and at the chain-anchor posts. In these conditions, the shadow zone between the western torches is dark enough that Taylor cannot resolve faces at the distance between the gallery and the center floor.

**Heat:** The interior runs warmer than the exterior approach. Not the ambient city temperature, not the comfort temperature of a maintained hearth — a specific warmth produced by large bodies in an enclosed space, underlaid by the residual warmth of stone that has absorbed exhalation heat for decades. The warmth is not clean; it carries the smell with it. There is no window ventilation; the only air exchange is through the entry passage and the ocular opening.

**Taylor's flicker register in the interior (project-specific):** Inside the interior, with a dragon at thirty paces, the flicker is not louder in volume — it is more frequent and its refractory cost is higher. The Gold-Morning proximity read that begins on the approach road reaches maximum intensity here. Glimpses fire without Taylor initiating them: involuntary, brief, fragmentary. The patron interprets the frequency of involuntary glimpses as confirmation that the interior is the amplification zone. What the patron does not observe is the cost — after each uninvited glimpse, Taylor's functional window for reliable interpretation narrows. By the time the patron is asking her to interpret what she sees, she has already been through four uninvited glimpse-cycles, and her interpretive reliability is at its lowest point of the visit. The misfire that kills Vaegon happens in this state.

## Fixed Props

- Iron chain-anchor stations (architectural, perimeter) — chain-arc radius is approximately twelve to fourteen meters from anchor post
- Keeper gallery stone rail (architectural, northern perimeter)
- Torch stations (fixed, perimeter intervals with one gap at west segment)

## Exits

- **Entry passage (primary):** The sole authorized entry and exit for visitors. Leads back to the gatehouse. Keepers know of secondary passages; Taylor does not.
- **East secondary passage (keeper access):** Leads to the feed preparation area on the east side of the outer ring. Not a visitor exit; Taylor notes its existence but does not use it.
- **West secondary passage (keeper access):** Behind the fire-mark section of the east wall — actually a west-wall passage, the asymmetry is intentional, mapped from Taylor's observation of keeper movement patterns. Leads to the supply loading dock. Could be used as an exit in emergency; would require knowing the passage exists and pushing past a latched door.
- **Ocular opening (dome):** Not a practical exit. Visible from the floor as the opening of sky above the central space.

## Hazards

**The dragon itself:** The chained dragon is not a stable threat under normal conditions, but its instability is non-zero. Keeper management is procedural and well-practiced; the procedures exist because the dragon's responses to stimuli are large-scale. A dragon that reacts to something in the interior — a sound, a smell, a proximity — can move through its chain-arc faster than a human in the keeper gallery can clear the rail and reach the passage. The keepers manage this; the visitors are expected to hold their positions.

**The shadow zone:** The western segment between the torch stations is the operational hazard for the kill sequence. The darkness is not total — peripheral torch light reaches it — but it is specific enough that Taylor's read of the figure in that zone, at the distance of the gallery rail, is genuinely ambiguous. The shadow zone is not an unusual occurrence; it is the normal low-light condition of that section during every interior visit.

**Flicker saturation:** The interior produces involuntary glimpse-cycles in Taylor at a rate that degrades her interpretive function. The longer she is in the interior, the less reliable her glimpse-interpretation becomes. This is the opposite of what the patron believes; the patron believes extended interior visits improve Taylor's readings. The structural hazard is that Taylor knows she is degrading but cannot communicate this to the patron without undermining the entire amplification theory and losing the patron's confidence.

**Witness thinning:** The keeper staff maintain a respectful distance from Targaryen household visitors in the interior. On the visit where the kill happens, the keepers have been positioned at the eastern chain-anchor stations — the patron has arranged this, believing that fewer keepers present will allow Taylor greater concentration. The witness reduction is the patron's courtesy; it is also what makes the kill possible.

**Post-kill containment:** After the kill, the interior is a specific kind of trap: the only authorized exit is the entry passage, which the porter guards from the gatehouse. The body is in the shadow zone. The dragon is present. Taylor has to exit through the passage, past the gatehouse, and down the approach road with the patron's retinue aware of what happened. The social geography of this exit is the patron-collision's mechanical surface.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A dragon-movement event during consultation — the chain-arc activated, the anchor groan, and the specific silence that falls in the keeper gallery when a large weight shifts unexpectedly
- A keeper passing through the center floor on a routine path, crossing the area between Taylor and the dragon, forcing an interruption to whatever the patron is asking her to read
- The ocular light column landing on the shadow zone during a specific visit — the one time the western section is lit, making the shadow-zone's normal darkness visibly absent and therefore notable
- A second dragon audible from the far end of the interior (the Dragonpit holds multiple chains; Taylor has only been shown to the northern anchor) — the sound arriving from a different direction than expected
- Rhaenys stepping forward from her usual receiving position, closing the gallery gap to Taylor — the first time the physical distance between them changes, and what it means that the patron is now standing beside her rather than observing from two paces back
- The postern fire-mark section drawing a keeper into a whispered consultation with another keeper, visible to Taylor in the gallery, about something she cannot hear — the keepers' private register, which the patron ignores
- A torch in the entry passage guttering during the visit — a reduction of the exit-light that makes the passage slightly less visible from the gallery floor, narrowing Taylor's ambient sense of the exit geometry
