# Series Drama Statement

## The collision

A protagonist trained to prevent extinction-level catastrophe arrives in a world facing dynastic war and applies her threat-model to it. The competence that ended Gold Morning is the competence that produces the Dance. Prevention, executed by the wrong instrument, produces the catastrophe it was trying to forestall.

## What cannot survive the collision

Vaegon's quiet bond with Silverwing — the brake on the Dance that no one but he was building, that Taylor cannot read because she has never seen prevention in this register.

Lyra — the cousin Vaegon was buffering, whose protection ends when the buffer dies.

Taylor's belief that her Worm-trained instruments are reliable in a world that does not run on Worm-physics. (She does not die. The belief does.)

The reader's option to read this as a story where the protagonist's intentions can be separated from her actions.

## The architecture of the road

Each act on the road is locally defensible from inside Taylor's POV. The flicker confirms her threat-reading. The patron's amplification-theory authorizes her access. The kill executes a prevention-strategy that ran successfully at Gold Morning's scale. None of the stones, looked at alone, are the road. The road is only visible from the chronicler's elevation, after.

## The series question

What does a survivor of extinction do when her survival-training is the wrong tool for the world she has been deposited into — and the only available instrument for reading the difference is the same instrument that misled her?

## Closure mechanism

The chronicler-coda names what would have followed if Vaegon had completed his bond. The body has been Taylor's interpretation throughout; the coda is history's verdict. The register-break is the irony mechanism. The cousin dies cold, in the closing image, with the wrong dragon visible above. The reader exits on the cost. Taylor is alive and broken and silent.

## One-book scope

Single closed arc. The Dance begins in the closing pages but is not followed in. The story is the road, not the destination.
