# Dark Fantasy Reader — state-updates adversarial review

## Lens: earned vs. invented bookkeeping / field-extensions as world-presence

The question I'm asking is not "does this technically pass the rubric." It's whether the field-extensions feel like they emerged from what this world actually costs — or whether they feel like scaffolding erected to manage complexity the writers are nervous about losing track of.

A world that was there before the protagonists would generate state-changes that have texture. Bookkeeping that was invented to track story mechanics reads differently: it's smooth, administrative, consequence-free in itself. The dark fantasy reader notices when a field-extension is a world-artifact versus a production artifact.

---

## What earns its place

**vigil-state kept-for-tya -> extinguished (@173) on oc-tanner-mother.**
This is the one field-extension in the file that lands as a world-artifact. The vigil-state has a specific cultural weight: a mourning-vigil marker with a beginning (Tya's death) and an end (the mother's standing, the candle going out). The field-extension note is careful to honor the distinction — "the ritual is what ends, not the ambiguity." That's a dark fantasy writer's instinct. The ambiguity persists; the ritual closes. The canonical grief-shape changes without resolution. That's earned.

**prop:oc-vigil-candle.state lit -> extinguished (@173).**
The candle is a prop, not a field on a character file. It exists as a physical object in the world — it was burning since before episode one, without being named until now. That's the right flavor: the world had this thing in it already, and now it doesn't. The extension is flagged as a project-original prop with no prop card, which is an honest acknowledgment of its status. The fire timing (commit-beat @173, not disclosure-beat @171) is the right call for a dark fantasy read: the speech is the announcement, the standing is the fact.

**physical_condition intact -> migraine-onset (@125).**
The cost fires after the expansion. That's the dark fantasy contract: power that isn't free. The stylus-drop is the body telling the story the text hasn't said directly. The state-update ratifies it as real rather than atmospheric. The field is already on Taylor's state schema (`physical_condition: intact` at project setup) — no extension needed, just a change. Clean.

**studio.doors_and_shutters.two-room-dwelling-latch: intact -> latch-broken (@85).**
A stranger breaks a door in Flea Bottom and the broken door stays broken. The state-update locks that in. The world isn't set dressing here; the eviction has a physical residue. The entry notes the latch is not repaired through episode close. Good. The world-as-terrain is behaving correctly: things that get broken stay broken.

## What concerns me

**stance-on-tya-category: privately-concluded-not-tya -> bodily-committed-withdrawal (@22).**
This is the entry that feels most like production scaffolding rather than a world-state. "Privately-concluded-not-tya" and "bodily-committed-withdrawal" are labels that describe the author's intention about the character rather than an observable fact about the world. The rubric requires posture-as-state to persist multi-beat AND be load-bearing for the next move. The multi-beat persistence is defended. But the old-state — "privately-concluded-not-tya" — is a psychological reading, not an observable field-state. It's tracking an internal conclusion that hasn't been previously established as a tracked field on his schema.

The field-extension defense says "per card 'He is withdrawing from her. The withdrawal is protective.'" That's a behavioral card note, not a prior tracked field. The extension is taking a card description and converting it into a field at the moment it first manifests as a visible act. That's a gray area. For the dark fantasy read: it doesn't feel like a world-artifact. It feels like the authors wanted documentation that this is the right withdrawal and not confusion. The posture-as-state entry (proximity-to-taylor) covers the visible behavior; the stance-on-tya-category entry is tracking the interior interpretation. Interior interpretation is not state.

**This is the entry I'd flag for revision.** The stance-on-tya-category field-extension is doing perception/interpretation work under the cover of a tracked-state label. The withdrawal is real; the labeling of its underlying conclusion as a state-field is not.

**transactional-posture: informal-grief-claim -> formalized-wage-claim (@100).**
This is closer to a clean field-extension — it tracks the formal shape of the claim, which is observable (the father's speech at @100 is the formalization act). But "transactional-posture" as a field name has an administrative feel that sits uneasily in a Flea Bottom setting. The claim is an old custom, not a contract. The field name imports a modern bureaucratic register. Marginal concern; the underlying state-change is real.

**vigil-state at @173 vs. knowledge.vigil-candle at @171 on Taylor.**
Taylor's knowledge.vigil-candle fires two beats before the canonical vigil-end. The split is rationalized: she hears it in speech at @171, the commit-act is @173. For the dark fantasy read, this creates a small problem. The dark fantasy contract is that knowledge of a death has weight — you don't just "register" that a vigil has ended the way you register a trade transaction. Taylor's knowledge.vigil-candle field-extension ("tracked-state aspect — the candle's extinction is the episode's terminal beat for the grief-shape change") is treating grief-knowledge as an information-state rather than as something the world imposed on her. The field-extension note is technically correct about what it's tracking. But the dark fantasy reader's concern is whether the writer reached for a field-extension when the canonical answer should have been that the *prop fire* and the *mother's vigil-state fire* do the work, and Taylor's interior response belongs in narrator-interest, not state-updates.

This is a softer flag than the stance-on-tya-category concern. The knowledge.vigil-candle entry is not wrong in the rubric's terms. But it reads like over-reach — as if the writers were nervous that the vigil-end wouldn't "register" on Taylor without a state-update to ratify it.

---

## The field-extension count as a whole

Thirteen field-extensions across 34 entries. That's roughly 38% of entries are extending into new tracked territory. For a second episode where many of the key relationships are being formalized for the first time, a high extension rate is expected. The extensions I've accepted — vigil-state, fauna_control_radius_m, physical_condition (base schema field, no extension needed), wage-claim-state, knowledge fields for the maester arc — all have genuine tracked-state warrant. The extensions I've flagged — stance-on-tya-category, and conditionally knowledge.vigil-candle — feel like they're tracking the *interpretation* of events rather than the events' structural facts.

## Verdict

**REVISE (conditional).**

Specific request: entry 8 in the consolidated file (`actor:oc-tanner-father.stance-on-tya-category: privately-concluded-not-tya -> bodily-committed-withdrawal @22`) should be defended more rigorously or dropped. The old-state value is a psychological reading, not a prior tracked field value. The proximity-to-taylor posture-as-state entry on the same beat already covers the visible behavior. If the stance-on-tya-category field cannot show an established prior-tracked value (i.e., this is genuinely first-touch on a previously-established observable state), the entry should be deleted — the behavioral card note is not a state schema field.

The knowledge.vigil-candle entry is a softer flag: defensible under the rubric but potentially redundant given the prop and mother-vigil fires. If it stays, the stitcher should do the work of distinguishing Taylor's *hearing* from the canonical *extinguishment* — the state-update alone won't carry that weight.

All other entries are accepted.
