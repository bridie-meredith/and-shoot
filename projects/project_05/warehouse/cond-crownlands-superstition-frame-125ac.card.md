# Crownlands Superstition Frame — "Tya Who Came Back Wrong" (125 AC)

## Description

Addendum to `cond-westerosi-superstition-frame`. Covers the Crownlands-specific superstition vocabulary available to witnesses of Taylor's situation at 125 AC, particularly as it applies to a dead person returning to a body. The base card (Riverlands, 84–101 AC) governs fauna-control wrongness-perception. This card governs the separate and earlier interpretive crisis: how Taylor's community of origin frames a corpse that woke up.

**Load this card alongside `cond-westerosi-superstition-frame` for all scenes involving the tanner-family, the tanner-origin village, and any Crownlands peasant character who knows who Tya was.** For scenes involving fauna-control wrongness-perception only (no death-return context), the base card alone is sufficient.

## Sensory Impact

The superstition frame manifests in the register of absence: conversations that cut short when she enters, hands that make warding gestures after she passes, the way the tanner-mother cannot look directly at Taylor's face for more than a breath. The village does not scream or flee. The village simply arranges itself around a category of wrongness it has a name for. That arrangement is felt in how the space around Taylor empties slightly, how voices drop a register, how the children are called inside without explanation.

## Duration

Persistent. The "came back wrong" category is not a temporary misidentification that clears when Taylor demonstrates she is harmless. It is a categorical assignment. She is in the category. The category does not dissolve into simple weirdness over time — it is renewed every time she does something the Tya-body would not have done, which is most things. Crownlands characters who knew Tya will hold this frame for the series duration. Characters who meet her only in Flea Bottom do not carry the Tya-context and apply different categories (see base card: "touched," "old magic").

## The Stranger-Leavings Frame

The Crownlands folk understanding of the returned dead draws on a specific Stranger-theology that differs from Riverlands folk practice:

**Stranger-leavings:** In Crownlands peasant folk tradition, the Stranger sometimes does not take fully. A body that died — particularly a young body, a fever-death, a death that came fast — may be imperfectly released. The Stranger takes what the Stranger claims but leaves something behind in the shell: not the soul, not the person, but a *remainder*. This remainder can animate the body. It is not the dead person. It is what the Stranger left when it took her.

What comes back is not Tya. It is the remainder of Tya — a vacancy that learned to walk. The community does not believe Taylor is Tya pretending to be wrong. They believe Tya is gone and *something else* is in the body that looks like Tya's body. The distinction matters: they are not accusing her of deception. They are correctly identifying that she is not Tya.

**Named category:** The folk term across several Crownlands villages is "Stranger's leavings" or simply "what was left." It appears in conversations as: "that's not Tya, that's what the Stranger left" — said quietly, not as accusation but as explanation. It frames her as an object of the Stranger's passage rather than an agent in her own right. This is both protective (she is not being malicious; she is an artifact of divine process) and denuding (she has no claims that belong to Tya; she is not a person in the community's frame, she is a remainder).

**The Stranger's aspect relevance:** The Stranger governs transitions, thresholds, and the passage between life and death. A remainder — something the Stranger left behind at a threshold — carries the Stranger's aura by proximity. The remainder is neither fully dead nor fully living. It exists in the threshold. This is theologically consistent in Crownlands peasant practice and gives the community a vocabulary that feels coherent to them.

## The "Tya Who Came Back Wrong" Register

The shortened formulation — "Tya who came back wrong" — is the working-language version of the Stranger-leavings frame. It carries specific register notes:

**"Came back" is not return of the soul.** When villagers say "came back," they do not mean "Tya came back." They mean the body came back — the Stranger returned it uncollected, or imperfectly collected. The wrongness is not in the return; it is in what returned.

**"Wrong" covers multiple registers simultaneously:**
- Behavioral wrong: she does not remember things Tya knew; she holds herself differently; her eyes move in unfamiliar patterns; her voice has the same texture but the accent is shifted slightly toward something they cannot place
- Relational wrong: she does not respond to Tya's relationships correctly — she is polite to people Tya was close to, but with the politeness of a stranger, not recognition
- Theological wrong: she exists in a category that should not exist; she is what happens when the transition process is incomplete; her presence is a reminder of something the community would prefer the divine to handle cleanly

**How the family uses the phrase:** The tanner-mother and tanner-father do not say "Tya who came back wrong" in her presence. They use it in conversations she is not supposed to hear. When speaking to her directly, they use "Tya" sometimes, when habit overrides theology, and then flinch slightly. The flinch is the tell. They know she is not Tya. They have not fully stopped responding to the body as if it were.

## KL/Crownlands Vocabulary Divergence from Riverlands

The base card's Riverlands vocabulary (Blue Fork spirits, bees-as-Maiden-creatures, seasonal uncanny at autumn equinox) does not fully transfer to the Crownlands and KL-proximate peasant folk tradition:

**What translates cleanly:**
- The "touched" category — available in KL peasant frame as well, though slightly less theologically elaborated than the Riverlands version; someone touched by the divine or the strange, marked but not necessarily malicious
- Old magic attribution — the age-of-the-world frame is available anywhere in Westeros
- Omen vocabulary — reading events as signs with meaning beyond the event
- Witchcraft as the adversarial extreme — same threshold logic, same social consequence

**What diverges in the Crownlands / KL-adjacent context:**

*Wildfire association:* Crownlands peasants live in proximity to King's Landing and the Alchemists' Guild. They have a baseline category for "unnatural fire" and "things the learned men do that go wrong." This creates a slight priming toward "it is something to do with the guilds / the arcane craft" that is not present in the Riverlands frame. For Taylor, this is marginally useful: insects behaving wrong reads as "something arcane nearby, perhaps from the city" rather than defaulting immediately to personal agency.

*Targaryen dragon-weight:* Crownlands smallfolk live within the long shadow of dragonfire. Their uncanny vocabulary carries a layer of dragon-association: fires that spread wrong, animals that flee without cause, the sky that changes before a dragon passes. This means large-scale fauna behavior (mass movement of birds, sudden insect silence) can be read in the Crownlands as "dragon approaching" before it is read as "that woman did something." This provides brief cover for large-scale operations that the Riverlands frame would more quickly attribute to a specific person.

*Maester-proximity awareness:* The Citadel's presence in the city, and the Red Keep's maester, means Crownlands peasants are slightly more accustomed to "the maesters know things about nature that ordinary people do not." This creates a marginal category of "perhaps there is an explanation that is not supernatural" — available in the Crownlands in a way that is less available in the deeper Riverlands. It does not reduce the superstition frame substantially; it adds a small rationalist-deferral option ("I don't know what it was, perhaps the maesters would").

*Flea Bottom specific register:* Within Flea Bottom, the superstition frame runs at slightly lower intensity than in the village. Urban density, higher tolerance for strangeness, and the fact that the "touched" category is crowded with other candidates (lunatics, beggars who claim divine contact, winesellers who see things) means Taylor's uncanny presentation has to be more acute to hit the same threshold. Low-level fauna-control wrongness in Flea Bottom reads as "that woman is odd" before it reads as "old magic." This is useful for early operational cover; it closes as her reputation builds.

## Narrative Vocabulary Specific to This Card

**Words the tanner-family and village characters reach for (not in the base card):**
- "the Stranger left her" / "what the Stranger left"
- "Tya who came back wrong"
- "it's not her" (said to a third party, not to Taylor)
- "the fever took her; this is what came after"
- "the shell walked home"
- "she looks at you like she's reading a letter you haven't written yet"
- "Tya would have known" (when she fails to respond to something Tya should have known)

**Words KL/Crownlands urban witnesses reach for (diverging from base card):**
- "wild thing" (for insects or small animals behaving unnaturally — more common in city than "old magic")
- "guild work gone wrong" (misattribution toward alchemists)
- "dragon weather" (for ambient wrongness without obvious source)
- "the septry has her kind" (routing her into the "touched" category via Faith institution rather than folk category)

**Words neither the village nor KL witnesses reach for:**
- Any name for her other than "Tya" (if they knew Tya) or "the woman" / "the girl" (if they did not)
- "dead girl walking" — too explicit; the category is less direct
- "revenant" — this is not a Westerosi folk term; it is an anachronistic import

## The Tanner-Family Situation as Ongoing Cost

The tanner-family's relationship to the "Tya who came back wrong" frame is the site of the ongoing cost-surface this card describes:

The family cannot grieve correctly. They buried Tya; they watched the maester pronounce her dead; they sat the vigil. Then the body stood up. The theological frame tells them Tya is gone and this is a remainder. Their bodies do not accept the frame completely. The mother's hands still reach to smooth the hair. The father still uses the name. These are not signs of acceptance or warmth; they are signs of a grief that has been interrupted mid-process and cannot complete.

For Taylor, this produces a specific discomfort that the prose must register: she is in a body that was loved, being treated with the residue of that love by people who know intellectually that the love's object is no longer present. She is receiving something she did not earn and cannot return. The tanner-family's reach is not toward her; it is toward a memory that her presence makes almost available and then withholds.

This is not a dynamic that resolves into community or warmth. It is a dynamic that costs both sides continuously. The carry-forward constraint from `cond-smallfolk-political-physics` — drift toward warmth is a flagged failure mode — applies here with extra weight. Any scene that treats the tanner-family's relationship to Taylor as moving toward genuine affiliation is a register violation of this card.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-westerosi-superstition-frame`:** Load the base card for fauna-control wrongness-perception. Load this card for any scene involving the tanner-family, tanner-village, or a character who knew Tya. The base card's narrative vocabulary remains in scope; this card extends it.

**With `cond-reincarnation-mechanics-125ac`:** The reincarnation card governs what is mechanically true about Taylor's arrival. This card governs how the Crownlands community understands and narrates that arrival. They are not the same thing. Taylor knows the mechanism; the community has the frame. Neither is wrong about what they can see.

**With `cond-smallfolk-political-physics`:** The Stranger-leavings category is a form of social assignment that operates like the "touched" category but is more categorical. A touched person is odd; a Stranger's leaving is not-a-person-exactly. The political physics of how the community treats a remainder are somewhat different from how they treat a touched person — a remainder is not sought for blessing, not relied upon for community functions, not really part of the informal credit and whisper network in the same way. Taylor's integration into the Flea Bottom community requires accumulating a different kind of standing than the tanner-village will ever grant her.

**For auditor use:** Any scene where the tanner-family treats Taylor as though she is Tya (full recognition without flinch) is a register violation. Any scene where the village accepts her as an ordinary community member is a violation — the category assignment is permanent within that community. Any scene where characters in Flea Bottom or KL use the village-frame vocabulary without having heard it from someone who knew Tya is an information-network violation. The Stranger-leavings frame is community-specific; it does not travel instantly.
