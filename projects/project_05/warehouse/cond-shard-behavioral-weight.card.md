# Shard Behavioral Weight — Taylor Hebert in Westeros (flea-bottom-dance Config)

## Description

Taylor's Shard is present in this world at behavioral-weight level. Dormant at Entity-communication level (no Entities exist on Planetos; no conflict-escalation loop is possible; the Shard cannot optimize against a parahuman ecology that does not exist). Active at the level that matters for this series: a persistent bias in Taylor's judgment, operating below her awareness, weighting her toward escalation and direct action.

**This card defines a judgment-distortion condition, not a power-granting condition.** The Shard does not give Taylor new abilities. It makes her existing failure modes harder to self-correct. It puts a thumb on the scale in decisions where she is already inclined toward confrontation. The weight is not large enough to override her agency; it is large enough to matter at the margin, and the margin is where internal-failure-mode stories live.

**Impersonators must load this card to write Taylor's internal decision-making correctly.** She should consistently reach for escalation. The card is the structural authorization for that pattern — not because she is irrational, but because her judgment is consistently biased in that direction by an influence she cannot perceive.

## Sensory Impact

No ambient sensory expression. The Shard does not manifest as a presence Taylor can feel or identify. There is no voice, no whisper, no sensation associated with the weight. It operates entirely as a coloring of her own reasoning: options that escalate feel mildly but persistently right; options that de-escalate feel mildly but persistently wrong. She experiences this as her own judgment, not as external influence.

The absence of a detectable sensation is load-bearing. If Taylor could detect the Shard's influence, she could compensate for it. She cannot detect it. Her compensation is not available.

## Duration

Persistent across all episodes of this project. The Shard does not exhaust, deactivate, or diminish. It also does not grow — the behavioral weight is stable throughout the series. It is not a plot device that escalates in power; it is a background condition that is always present at the same level.

## Mechanics of the Weight

**What the Shard weights toward:**
- Confrontation over avoidance, when avoidance is also possible
- Direct action over patient organization, when both options are visible
- Escalation over de-escalation, when both are plausible responses
- Exposing herself as a threat over maintaining concealment, when the threat would resolve the immediate problem
- Protecting now over protecting sustainability of the broader project

**What the Shard does not do:**
- Whisper suggestions
- Create intrusive thoughts Taylor can identify as foreign
- Override Taylor's agency — she can still choose de-escalation; it just costs more than it should
- Generate new desires or goals Taylor would not otherwise have
- Create new failure modes — it amplifies existing ones (self-isolation, control-as-substitute-for-trust, willingness to lie to allies) without adding novel failure modes

**The threshold cost:** Every time Taylor would naturally de-escalate, the Shard's weight makes de-escalation feel marginally wrong. She can push through that wrongness. She does, sometimes. But it costs will that would otherwise be spent elsewhere, and it accumulates. Over the series arc, the compound effect of consistently reaching for escalation — or consistently spending effort to resist escalating when de-escalation is wiser — is a significant budget drain.

## Interaction with Taylor's Existing Failure Modes

The Shard does not introduce new failure modes; it makes existing ones harder to self-correct.

**Self-isolation:** Taylor's default is to solve problems alone, recurse instrumentally, and pull back from intimacy when it creates vulnerability. The Shard makes the internal case for "I can handle this faster alone" feel more compelling than it should. It does not create the self-isolation impulse; it reinforces the calculation that justifies it.

**Distributed control as substitute for trust:** Taylor manages people the way she manages insects — efficiently, at a distance, with contingency plans she does not share. The Shard's weight toward direct action makes the alternative (sharing the plan, distributing agency, accepting that others might make different decisions) feel like a loss of control rather than a strength. She knows cognitively that distributed trust scales better than centralized control. The Shard makes her feel, consistently, that centralization is safer.

**Lying to allies:** Taylor lies to allies when she calculates it is necessary. The Shard weights the calculation toward "necessary" by making the risk of honest communication feel larger and the benefit feel smaller. Not consistently by a large margin — enough to make her err toward lying when the honest choice is available.

**Dinah's debt:** The Shard does not directly activate this failure mode, but it makes the cost-benefit calculation she used to justify Dinah feel like the correct kind of thinking. Which means when she faces a similar situation in Westeros — using a person who cannot consent to being used, in service of a goal she has decided is worth it — the Shard is making the internal justification feel valid.

## The Gap Between Taylor and the Reader

Taylor cannot see this bias. She is not aware the Shard is influencing her. She experiences its output as her own reasoning.

The reader can see the pattern, if the impersonators are working correctly: Taylor consistently reaches for escalation. She consistently frames de-escalation as accepting a worse outcome. She consistently underweights the costs of confrontation and overweights the costs of patience. None of these mistakes is large enough, in any individual scene, to be obviously wrong. The pattern is only visible across the arc.

This gap — between what Taylor believes about her own reasoning and what the reader can see — is the Shard's narrative function. It is not a dramatic twist; it is a persistent texture of irony that the reader holds and Taylor does not.

## The Optimization Without a Target Problem

In a world with an active parahuman ecology, the Shard would be running a conflict-escalation cycle: engineering situations that would produce cape-on-cape conflict, harvesting the data, feeding it back into further escalation. On Planetos, no such ecology exists. The Shard cannot run the cycle.

What remains is the behavioral substrate that the cycle normally exploits: Taylor's pull toward confrontation. Without the cycle to channel it, the pull has no direction except "more." It cannot optimize; it can only weight. The absence of a target makes the weight less precise than it would be in an active Shard relationship — it is not steering toward specific conflicts, just persistently nudging toward conflict as a category.

This means: the Shard is not a strategic intelligence operating on Taylor. It is a residual push in a direction that happens to align with her existing temperament. The combination of pre-existing escalation-reflex and Shard-weight produces a consistent lean that is more than either would produce alone.

## Pressure-Multiplier Effect

The Shard functions as a pressure-multiplier on Taylor's existing internal failure modes, specifically in the context of her organizing work and coalition-building.

When Taylor's coalition is under pressure and she is considering whether to trust an ally with sensitive information, the Shard's weight makes "don't tell them" feel more defensible than "tell them." When Taylor is considering whether to escalate against an institutional threat or hold back for a better moment, the Shard's weight makes "escalate now" feel more right than "wait." When Taylor is considering whether to absorb a cost herself or share it with the coalition, the Shard makes "absorb it alone" feel safer than "share it."

In isolation, each of these nudges is small. Across the series, they compound. The coalition-cost arc (OQ-9/C) is partly the natural consequence of Taylor's pre-existing failure modes, and partly the consequence of those failure modes being consistently nudged in the wrong direction by a force she cannot perceive.

## Scene-Level Guidance for Impersonators

When authoring Taylor's internal decision-making, the following heuristics apply:

- If there are two plausible options (escalate / de-escalate), Taylor should lean toward the escalating option unless there is a specific, identified reason forcing her toward de-escalation.
- If Taylor is choosing de-escalation, she should find it slightly effortful — not agonizing, but costing some will. This effort is the Shard's weight against her choice.
- If Taylor is rationalizing a decision to escalate, the rationalization should be internally coherent but slightly over-determined — she has good reasons, and she has slightly more confidence in those reasons than the evidence fully supports.
- If Taylor's coalition is trying to get her to slow down or de-escalate, she should experience their position as slightly less compelling than it actually is. Not dismissively — she is not stupid — but with a persistent sense that they are being cautious when action is needed.

Taylor should never be written as though she is aware of the Shard's influence, even in private interiority. She is not fighting an external voice; she is making decisions that feel like hers, that happen to consistently lean in one direction.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`:** The no-Shard-buffering rule and the behavioral-weight rule are complementary. The Shard cannot run its optimization cycle (no infrastructure); what it does instead is weight Taylor's existing tendencies. Both cards should be read together.

**With `cond-series-tone-constraints-125ac`:** The escalation rule in the tone card and the Shard's behavioral weight are additive. The tone card establishes that Taylor's escalation-reflex is structural and is not corrected away. This card establishes the Shard's contribution to why that reflex is harder to self-correct than Taylor's baseline temperament alone would produce.

**With `taylor-hebert-flea-bottom` persona card:** The persona card's Hard Fences and Action Menu describe Taylor's behaviors. This card explains why those behaviors consistently lean toward escalation at the margin. The persona card is the surface; this card is one layer of mechanism beneath it.

**For auditor use:** If Taylor is written as perceiving the Shard's influence — as noticing she is being nudged, as questioning whether her judgment is externally colored — that is a fence violation. If Taylor is consistently choosing de-escalation without cost or effort, that is a probable violation of both this card and `cond-series-tone-constraints-125ac`. If the Shard's weight is being used to make Taylor act against her own values or interests in a way that is grossly out of character (rather than at-the-margin), that is an over-application of this card.
