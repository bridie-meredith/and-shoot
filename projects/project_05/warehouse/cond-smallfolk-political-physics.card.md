# Smallfolk Political Physics — Subjugation as Internalized State (Jaehaerys I Riverlands)

## Description

The internalized political physics of Westerosi smallfolk subjugation in the Riverlands at 84–101 AC. This card describes how smallfolk understand and reproduce their own subordination — not as externally imposed oppression they resent and resist, but as the water they swim in. It is the pre-existing substrate Taylor's organizing works with and against.

**This card is the subjugated state from the inside.** `cond-westerosi-customary-authority-jaehaerys` is the behavioral physics from the outside (what authority looks like). `cond-feudal-hierarchy-law` is the underlying legal structure. This card is what the law and the behavioral physics look like when they have been so thoroughly internalized that they feel like common sense.

Impersonators playing supporting smallfolk characters must load this card. It governs how community-elder figures, the local septon's parishioners, the people at the market square, and any smallfolk in Taylor's operational area understand their own situation. It is the baseline against which Taylor's presence and organizing are measured.

## Sensory Impact

The political physics of smallfolk life are felt in the body and in the rhythm of daily existence. The weight of a man-at-arms' gaze. The specific quality of silence when the steward's men ride into the market square. The posture adjustment that happens without deciding to make it. The way a group of people at the well disperses when something official begins nearby. These are not dramatic moments; they are the texture of ordinary life.

Taylor experiences this sensory texture as information — she can read the social dynamics of a smallfolk crowd faster than most in it because she is reading it from outside its internalized frame. What she cannot do is dismiss the frame. It operates on everyone around her, and they will reproduce it in their responses to her.

## Duration

Persistent. This is the ambient substrate of Riverlands smallfolk life throughout the project. It does not change on a scene-by-scene basis; it is the background against which all scenes set among smallfolk are staged.

## Learned Invisibility

The primary survival technology of Westerosi smallfolk is not resistance but invisibility. A smallfolk who is not noticed by power is a smallfolk who will not be extracted from by power. The art is not to disappear — they cannot disappear; they have trades, homes, market days, the lord's tax obligation — but to render themselves functionally invisible to the attention of authority.

**What learned invisibility looks like in practice:**
- Moving through public space without drawing the eye — not hiding, but occupying the edge of view, not the center
- Speaking at volume calibrated to not carry beyond the immediate conversation
- Not being the one who responds first when authority makes an inquiry (let someone else answer; see how the authority receives the answer before committing to a response)
- Having business to be about — a person with visible, comprehensible business is less interesting to authority than a person who is standing around
- Wearing the face of compliance even when the compliance is internally conditional
- Knowing when to be sick, when to be slow, when to be confused — all of which are low-cost delay tactics that do not create a record of refusal

**Taylor's problem with learned invisibility:** She cannot fully perform it. Her adult cognition produces adult-register responses when she is not actively suppressing them. The learned invisibility of a Westerosi child requires not seeing what a thirty-year-old tactical cognition notices automatically. She manages the performance but it costs her, and the slips are recognizable to anyone paying close attention.

## Posture of Non-Threat

Closely related to learned invisibility: the active performance of harmlessness. Smallfolk who have survived authority-contact have learned that the fastest way to end the interaction is to be demonstrably not worth the trouble. This means:

- Physical smallness when possible (posture that takes up less space)
- Voice that carries no challenge — not exactly deferential, but stripped of any affect that could read as pushback
- Agreement that is near-immediate and near-total, with any objections framed as clarification rather than contradiction ("I may have misunderstood, ser, but I thought—")
- Eyes that find the middle distance or the floor rather than making sustained contact with the authority figure's gaze
- Offering the information the authority wants before being asked for it (removes the dynamic of being interrogated; replaces it with the dynamic of being helpful)

The posture of non-threat is not the same as actual submission. It is a performance that creates space. Most smallfolk who have survived generations of institutional authority have refined this performance to a near-automatic level.

## Informal Credit and Whisper Networks

Smallfolk political physics include a shadow economy of information and obligation that runs entirely through informal channels. These networks are the substrate Taylor will organize through, and understanding them is prerequisite to working with them.

**Informal credit:** Extended in kind (food, labor, materials), tracked in obligation-memory rather than ledgers. A woman who watched your children when you were sick has a claim on your labor when she is sick. A man who lent you grain before harvest has a claim on your grain after harvest. These obligations are enforced entirely by reputation — a person who does not repay informal credit loses access to the network. The network is the insurance against the lord's extraction economy, and access to it is a survival resource.

**Whisper chains:** Information moves through the smallfolk community before it moves through official channels. The woman at the well knows what the steward's men said at the ferry crossing before the reeve knows it. The carter who moves between the market town and the outlying farms carries information both ways as a natural function of his presence. The septon hears confession and gossips indirectly. The network is not organized; it is emergent. It does not have a center. This makes it resistant to official disruption and extremely efficient at spreading useful information.

**The reeve as membrane:** The local reeve sits at the membrane between the informal network and the official apparatus. The reeve knows both registers. He uses each to manage the other. Information that reaches the reeve might or might not travel upward to the lord's steward, depending on the reeve's calculation of what serves him. This makes the reeve a key figure in Taylor's organizing — he is the person whose cooperation or resistance determines whether Taylor's activities stay inside the smallfolk network or travel outside it.

## Septon's Charity as Redistribution Node

The local sept functions as a redistribution node for the smallfolk community. The sept receives donations and alms from higher-status visitors to the market town (merchants, occasional minor lords, travelers of quality) and redistributes them through the parish population as charity. This is the Faith's visible local function.

**Political implications:** The septon controls who receives charity and on what terms. This makes him an informal authority figure within the smallfolk community — not a power-holder in the lord's sense, but a person with resources to distribute and a set of values that governs distribution. Taylor's relationship with the local septon is complicated by this: the septon is a genuine pastoral figure who holds community resources, who may develop a theological framework around Taylor's presence, and whose charity access Taylor will eventually be in a position to affect.

**The charity network as organizing substrate:** Charity flows to people in need. People who have organized around mutual aid — who have built the informal credit networks described above — are both better positioned to receive charity (they have relationships with the local septon) and better positioned to redirect it (they can route it to people who need it without going through the septon's mediation). Taylor's organizing will intersect with this network.

## Cost of Being Visible to Power

The single most important axiom of smallfolk political physics: being seen by power is dangerous. Not because power is necessarily cruel, but because power in this world has purposes — extraction, control, information-gathering — and those purposes are most efficiently served by people who are known. Unknown smallfolk pay the same taxes as known ones but face fewer specialized demands.

**Being seen means:**
- Being remembered when the census arrives
- Being on the list when the lord needs labor corvée
- Being available as an example when the lord needs to demonstrate authority
- Being a target for the first round of extraction when resources are short
- Having your unusual characteristics on record

**Taylor's visibility problem:** She cannot be invisible. She is the tanner's daughter who may have done something impossible with insects at the market square. She is on record. The question for the rest of the series is not whether she is visible — she is — but what kind of visible, and whether the visibility can be shaped into something she chooses rather than something others assign her.

## Benefit of Collective Compliance as Armor

Individual compliance is a survival technology; collective compliance is a shield. When everyone in a village complies correctly with the lord's administrative apparatus, the apparatus has no reason to look too closely at any individual. The collective performance of compliance creates a social environment in which individual variations are harder to detect and interpret.

This is why Taylor's organizing is both assisted by and in tension with the smallfolk community's existing habits. The community's collective compliance is their armor. Taylor's organizing proposes to dent that armor — to make some people visible, to draw attention to the market town, to create a record that the collective compliance was insufficient to prevent. The community's resistance to Taylor's organizing is not stupidity or cowardice; it is a correct assessment of what their existing strategy has achieved and what Taylor's strategy threatens to cost them.

The community-elder figure (oc-tanner-elder) is the person who holds this calculus most explicitly. Their skepticism of Taylor is not personal antipathy; it is structural: Taylor's arrival disturbs an equilibrium that the community has worked to maintain. The question of whether Taylor's disruption offers enough in return to justify the cost of losing the armor is the question the elder is evaluating throughout S1.

## Taylor's Organizing Against This Substrate

Taylor's organizing operates on this substrate. Understanding what it offers and what it costs is essential for any agent modeling her tactics.

**What the substrate offers:**
- Existing whisper networks she can route information through
- Informal credit economy she can plug into (she can create obligations and redeem them through the same mechanisms everyone else uses)
- Collective compliance that has already taught the community how to coordinate without official detection
- A septon's charity node she can work with (or around)
- A reeve whose split loyalty can be used

**What the substrate costs her:**
- She must earn the community's trust on their terms before she can use their networks — she cannot simply insert herself
- The community's primary commitment is to its own survival, not to her agenda; they will drop her if her organizing appears to cost more than it returns
- The learned invisibility habit makes the community resistant to the visibility that any effective organizing requires
- The posture of non-threat is culturally ingrained; asking the community to drop it even partially is asking them to take on risk they have spent generations learning to avoid

**The community-legitimacy problem:** Taylor needs the elder's endorsement before the community will engage with her. She cannot bypass it. This is not a personal obstacle; it is a structural feature of how this substrate operates. The community's informal authority (the elder, the person with standing) is the gate through which any outside organizing must pass. Until Taylor has that gate open, her whisper-chain reach is limited to people who are already isolated from the community's protective collective.

## Interaction Notes

**With the project-active customary authority card:** That card describes the behavioral physics from the outside; this card describes them from the inside. Both should be loaded for scenes involving Taylor's relationship to smallfolk characters and her organizing work.

**With `cond-feudal-hierarchy-law`:** That card is the legal structure that produces these internalized physics. This card is what the legal structure looks like once it has been lived for generations. They are complementary, not redundant.

**With oc-tanner-elder:** The peer-authority-earned dynamic in the elder's vibes is a direct expression of this card. The elder embodies the community's survival calculus. Earning their trust means demonstrating, to someone who has survived decades of this substrate, that Taylor's offer is worth the armor it dents.

**For auditor use:** Any scene where smallfolk characters act as if they have no internalized cost for being visible to power is a violation of this card. Any scene where Taylor accesses the whisper network or informal credit economy without having built the requisite trust is a violation. Any scene where the community collectively resists Taylor and the scene treats this as mere cowardice rather than rational risk management is a register violation.
