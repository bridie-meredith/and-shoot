# Clinical Self-Erasure — The s1–s3 Dread Texture (flea-bottom-dance)

## Description

The ambient behavioral condition governing how Taylor's research register deteriorates across seasons 1 through 3. Not a sudden change or a decision. Calibration drift. What drops out of her research log over time without a decision to remove it. The omissions as load-bearing. The architectural absence as horror register.

**This card has two functions:** (1) an ambient condition card describing the dread texture that must pervade s1–s3, and (2) executable form notes for prose writers and auditors specifying what the prose must show, what it must not show, and what counts as a violation.

The s4 foreclosure event (density-saturation failure, log burned, one subject dead, one alive in four-hour memory increments) is the terminus of the arc this card governs. That event is load-bearing in direct proportion to how well s1–s3 execute the self-erasure correctly. If the clinical register is softened, qualified, or broken before s4, the foreclosure loses its weight.

## Sensory Impact

The self-erasure is not experienced as a sensation by Taylor. She does not notice it happening. That is the condition. The horror register is not felt by the person inside the condition; it is felt by the reader watching the drift from outside.

The sensory texture available for prose: the specific register of Taylor's research notes — clipped, precise, third-person distancing that increases over time; the things that vanish from her notational vocabulary; the narrowing of what counts as "the subject"; the moment when a person stops being named and becomes an instance.

## Duration

Progressive across s1–s3. Not a switch that flips. A gradient that arrives so slowly Taylor cannot identify a before-and-after. By s3, the register that was present at s1 open is no longer present — but there is no scene where it was removed. It eroded.

## The s1 Register: Full Clinical, Full Notation

At story open and through s1, Taylor's research records are exact. This exactness is her primary virtue in this arc — she registers that virtue in herself. The records note:
- Subject behavioral changes, day by day, with named individuals and specific descriptions
- Taylor's own physiological responses to extended operation (headache timing, nosebleed onset, recovery duration) — registered because accurate self-data is operationally necessary
- Questions she cannot yet answer about mechanism, recorded as open hypotheses
- Observations about the broken maester that include personal details: when he ate, whether he seemed unwell, what family members he mentioned and how often

**The specific s1 notation that will disappear later:** references to the subjects as named people. The maester's family members appear in s1 records because they appear in the maester's behavior (he mentions his sister more often when he is anxious; Taylor notices this because the data is there). By s3, the family members have dropped out of the notation. Not because Taylor stopped observing. Because she stopped recording what did not bear on the hypothesis.

## The s2 Register: First Narrowing

In s2, the register tightens. This is not dramatic; it is the natural tightening of a researcher whose hypothesis is clarifying. The research is advancing. The notation becomes more efficient. What reads as increased precision is also increased scope-narrowing.

**What drops out in s2, without a decision to drop it:**
- Personal details about subjects that do not bear directly on the question of insect-density relay capacity
- Taylor's emotional responses to the research (s1 records had brief functional notes: "aversion to extended close observation of the [subject]; noted, suppressed, not operationally limiting"; s2 records no longer contain the aversion notes — the aversion is still there, but it no longer rises to the level of notation-worthy data)
- Qualitative descriptions of subject distress (s1: "subject appeared agitated, vocalized, duration approximately 3 minutes, returned to baseline"; s2: "agitation event, 3 min, resolved" — the appearance detail vanishes, the vocalizing detail vanishes, what remains is the duration and the resolution category)

**The s2 narrowing is defensible.** A careful reader of the s2 records would not flag a violation. The records are accurate. They record what they record. The narrowing is within scientific norms for tightening notation as a hypothesis matures. That is the mechanism: the self-erasure is not a moral failure in any moment where it occurs. It is a sequence of individually defensible decisions that produce a collectively indefensible record.

## The s3 Register: Absence as Architecture

By s3, the self-erasure is structural. Taylor has been doing this for approximately two years of research time. Her notation is clean, efficient, and systematically incomplete.

**What is absent in s3:**
- Subject names. Subjects are instances, numbered or coded
- Physical descriptions of subjects other than what is operationally relevant (proximity to the insect-density nexus, mobility, response timing)
- Any reference to subjects' lives outside the observation window (family, home, work, relationships) — these simply do not appear in the record; they have been below notation-threshold for long enough that Taylor no longer registers them as missing
- Taylor's own discomfort at extended observation. This was already fading in s2. In s3, the category does not exist in the notation. It is not suppressed; it has dropped below the threshold of data that the record-keeping apparatus considers relevant

**What remains and should not be softened:**
- Exact operational data. The s3 records are precise about what they cover. Times, durations, range parameters, insect-density measurements, subject behavioral categories
- Taylor's hypothesis refinement — the in-world reasoning that connects insect-density to the candle relay apparatus. This must appear in the s3 records before s4 begins. It is not exposition; it is Taylor's actual intellectual work, visible in the record
- Internal notes flagging anomalies that do not match the hypothesis. Taylor is still a rigorous scientist at this stage. The s3 records show a scientist whose scope has narrowed past the point of ethical adequacy, not a scientist who has stopped being rigorous within that scope

**The prose must not show:** Any meta-awareness from Taylor that the register has changed. She does not notice. She would say, if asked, that she records everything relevant. She would be accurately describing her own perception. The horror is that she is right about what she believes about herself — and the records themselves prove she is wrong about what "relevant" now means.

## The Surviving Subject in the Fish Gate Margin

One of the s4 experimental subjects survives the density-saturation failure in four-hour memory increments — permanent memory-reset, reset is every four hours. This subject lives in the Fish Gate margin of Flea Bottom.

**The prose constraint (pre-s4):** This subject must appear in Taylor's surveillance — Taylor observes them through the insect-network, because they are within her range and her network covers Flea Bottom comprehensively. They must NOT appear in Taylor's log. The omission is not a decision Taylor makes in s4; it is the terminal expression of the self-erasure pattern that has been building since s1. By the time the s4 experiment begins, a person living in Taylor's operational range who has no formal connection to the research is simply not record-worthy. The network sees them. The record does not contain them. This is the s3 register applied to a subject who will later be destroyed.

**The pre-s4 surveillance / log gap is load-bearing.** If the surviving subject appears in Taylor's log before the s4 experiment, the s4 foreclosure loses a layer of its horror. The reader must be able to look back at s3 records and see that this person was being observed and was not being recorded — before they know what that means.

## Executable Form Notes

### What the prose MUST show

1. **Decreasing named-subject density across the arc.** Count the names. In s1, subjects in the research arc should be named people. By s3, they should not be. The gradient is gradual; there should be no scene where the naming stops.

2. **Taylor's in-world hypothesis chain visible in the records.** The theory connecting insect-network density to glass-candle signal propagation must appear in the prose before s4. Not as exposition. As Taylor's actual intellectual work, progressing hypothesis by hypothesis. The audience must be able to trace the working. The s4 falsification must be falsification of a visible theory, not a theory that appeared only when it was needed for the plot.

3. **At least one s1-or-early-s2 scene where Taylor hears the broken maester through the network (fragment overheard, behavioral read-through, or direct adjacency) and adjusts her behavior in response.** This is a dramatist carry-forward. The s2 scene where the maester names the Khepri threshold and is not heard must read as deterioration from the s1 responsiveness, not as first contact. The s1 responsiveness must be on the page.

4. **The Fish Gate margin surviving subject in surveillance and not in log, in at least one scene before s4.** The omission must be visible to the reader. Taylor's insect-network includes this person in its coverage. The prose of Taylor's research register does not.

5. **Taylor's own physiological responses in early records, absent in later records.** The headache notation, the nosebleed notation — present in s1 as operational self-data. Absent by s3 without a scene where Taylor decided to stop recording them.

### What the prose MUST NOT show

1. **Taylor noticing the drift.** She does not have a moment of "I should record more about who they are as people." She does not suppress an impulse to include the personal details. The impulse is not there. The register has closed around the hypothesis without Taylor being aware that the closing happened.

2. **A scene where the erasure is a decision.** There is no scene in which Taylor chooses efficiency over humanity. Each individual notation choice is locally defensible. The sequence of locally defensible choices is the horror. Any scene that implies Taylor made a single choice to stop caring is a violation — that is the cheap version of the arc.

3. **Sentimentality as counterweight.** No scenes in which Taylor's relationships with Flea Bottom community members "remind her of her humanity" in a way that is positioned as a brake on the clinical drift. The tanner-elder relationship is conditional, not warm. The dock-runner is a trust relationship, not a sentimental one. If these relationships are softening the clinical drift, the drift is not being executed correctly.

4. **The clinical register as Taylor's personality rather than Taylor's current state.** At s1 open, Taylor is a rigorous self-recorder because she has always been precise and because she knows the research requires it. The clinical register is not native to her personality in the affectless sense — she has feelings, she notices things, she carries the full weight of Gold Morning. The drift is a state she enters, not a trait she has always had. Any prose that treats the clinical register as Taylor's baseline voice from ep 1 is missing the arc.

5. **Redemptive notation.** No late-s3 scene where Taylor's records include a personal detail she has been suppressing — a brief humanizing note that signals she has not lost touch entirely. The self-erasure is not performing self-erasure while maintaining secret humanity. It is actual erasure, below the threshold of Taylor's self-perception. A humanizing notation in s3 is a violation unless it is operationally motivated (a personal detail that directly bears on the research question in that specific instance, not a general expansion of the record's humanity).

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-series-tone-constraints-125ac`:** The tone card's prohibition on cheap horror and the requirement for demonstration-not-triumph at the s3 climax both depend on this card executing correctly. If the clinical register is softened before s4, the contemplative-procedural-horror register is not being maintained. The self-erasure IS the procedural horror. Load both cards for any scene-level review of register.

**With `cond-reincarnation-mechanics-125ac`:** The reincarnation card specifies that Taylor's memory is intact and does not degrade. This card specifies that Taylor's notational practice degrades. These are not in conflict: Taylor remembers everything she observed; she records decreasing amounts of what she observed. The gap between remembered-and-unrecorded widens across the arc. The records are not her memory; they are her research apparatus. The research apparatus is what self-erases.

**For auditor use:** At any review point, an auditor loading this card should be able to trace:
- Whether subjects are still named in the current episode's research register
- Whether Taylor's own physiological data is still present
- Whether personal details about the broken maester still appear
- Whether the surviving-Fish-Gate-subject appears in surveillance and is absent from the log (once that character is established)
- Whether any scene implies Taylor's awareness of the drift

If the records are still naming subjects in s3, that is a drift-execution violation. If the records have stopped naming subjects in s1, that is a premature-erasure violation. If Taylor is aware of the drift at any point, that is a fence violation.
