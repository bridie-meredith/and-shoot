# OQ-6 through OQ-9 — Integrated Options

OQ-6: Court-contact mechanism
OQ-7: Witness/ally architecture
OQ-8: Stopping-shape
OQ-9: Season count and macro-shape

---

## Option A — Three-Season: Reputation Arrives Before She Does

**OQ-6 — Court contact via escalation.**
Taylor never seeks the court. The court eventually cannot ignore her. S1: Flea Bottom organizing work and research run in parallel; the smallfolk disruption she enables (labor stoppages, extrajudicial restitution, a lord's men routed by insects and left unharmed to report it) generates rumor and then intelligence reports. S2: Hightower agents and a Hand-level functionary investigate independently, reaching contradictory conclusions. By late s2 the greens' intelligence apparatus has her profiled as a religious figure, a maester-gone-wrong, or a foreign asset — none correct. S3: the Dance ignites. Both factions, looking for the Flea Bottom problem, find her separately within weeks of each other. First direct court contact is coerced by circumstance — envoys arrive at her door, not the reverse.

**OQ-7 — Witness architecture: one, functional, sealed.**
One witness to the full truth: the broken maester, already established. He accumulated knowledge through proximity and his own chain-stripped disgrace makes him impossible to leverage. He functions as a brake — not an ally in the political sense, not a confidant in the emotional sense, but a live record of what she is doing, capable of naming the line when she crosses it. He does not stop her after the s4 foreclosure event; he documents it and lives with it, as she does. He is her only mirror. No one else learns the full truth (Worm origin, Gold Morning, Khepri). Tanner-elder knows something is wrong. Dock-runner knows she is dangerous. Neither knows what.

**OQ-8 — Stopping-shape: collapsed-by-demonstration.**
Late s3. Both factions send dragons to the same engagement — a critical strategic point both need. Taylor has spent two seasons establishing that she knows where every actor is at all times. She demonstrates, once, what happens when she chooses to intervene at scale. Not a massacre — a reversal. She does not kill dragons; she makes the riders unable to control them, briefly, catastrophically visibly, in front of witnesses from both factions. No faction can model the mechanism. The s4 foreclosure event established the cost she will pay; the demonstration is the moment when both sides price it in. After the demonstration, neither faction tests her again. The Dance terminates: not from diplomacy, not from exhaustion, from rational deterrence with one proof-of-concept.

**OQ-9 — Four seasons.**
S1: Flea Bottom anchor, research onset, smallfolk disruption work. S2: range expansion, candle-acquisition — the expanded operational load of holding simultaneous relay-chains across a widening range crosses the Khepri-mantle threshold in late S2; the cost is visible (behavioral change, sensory intrusion, the maester names it and is not heard); Taylor arrives in S3 already changed. S3: Dance ignites, court contact coerced by circumstance, demonstration-climax. S4: aftermath — the Dance is over, the realm must rebuild, Taylor's foreclosure event detonates here (the research cost becomes visible, the log is burned, the surviving subject lives in the Fish Gate margin). S4 ends with no clean resolution: she is intact, the smallfolk are marginally better protected, the cost is permanent.

## Option B — Two-Season: Summoning and Collapse

**OQ-6 — Court contact via summoned reputation.**
Taylor is not found by agents; she is summoned. Mid-s1 a plague or famine event in Flea Bottom kills enough people fast enough that the smallfolk reputation she has built — the woman who knows when you are sick before you do, who routed lord's men, who nobody can catch — becomes a rumor that spreads upward through beggars and servants into castle halls. By end of s1 a Hightower-adjacent lord sends a formal summons, framed as an invitation. She does not respond. S2 opens with a second summons, this time with an implicit threat attached. She treats both summons as surveillance opportunities. Court contact is established when she is present in the Red Keep — via insect-network — for a small council meeting she was not invited to, while standing in Flea Bottom. The court does not know they have made contact. She does.

**OQ-7 — Witness architecture: none who know the full truth.**
The broken maester knows about the research. Dock-runner knows about the power. Tanner-elder knows about the body and the strangeness. Nobody holds all three pieces, and Taylor works to ensure it stays that way. The dramatic cost is that she has no brake. The Khepri-weight, Gold Morning, the willingness to end the world: sealed. She is the only one who knows she would do it again. OQ-7 resolves into solitude-as-permanent-condition — the secret stays sealed, the cost is isolation, and the s4 foreclosure event has no witness who can name what it means.

**OQ-8 — Stopping-shape: replaced.**
The Dance does not require termination because Taylor removes Aegon II (greens' figurehead) in a way that looks like illness — slow, insect-administered, undeniable and untraceable. Rhaenyra's faction, without a credible opposing claimant, loses the justification for full escalation. The succession crisis is not solved but defused: neither side has a war worth fighting with the costs already visible. Taylor does not claim the act. It is never attributed. The stopping-shape is replacement-by-removal, not demonstration. The horror register is tighter: she does not perform the act publicly; she performs it and the world reshapes around an absence she created.

**OQ-9 — Two seasons.**
S1: arrival through Flea Bottom anchor, range expansion, maester observation, smallfolk disruption, end-of-season court intelligence interest. S2: candle acquisition, Khepri-mantle threshold, Red Keep penetration (insect-network only), the replacement-act, Dance termination, s4 foreclosure event (compressed into late s2, which becomes functionally the foreclosure season). Series ends at s2. The aftermath is not dramatized — it is gestured at in the final beats. Densest narrative compression; no aftermath room.

## Option C — Three-Season: Deliberate Insertion, Witnesses as Instruments

**OQ-6 — Court contact via deliberate insertion.**
Taylor decides mid-s2 that the escalating Dance cannot be managed from Flea Bottom alone. She does not seek titles or audiences. She inserts herself into the court's environment by establishing a presence in the lower reaches of the Red Keep through the smallfolk who work there — servants, rat-catchers, scullions — who are already in her extended insect-range by mid-s2. She does not approach power figures directly; she makes herself legible to them indirectly, allowing specific pieces of intelligence to leak upward through these smallfolk conduits. By early s3 she has effectively been summoned without having been sought, because the intelligence she leaked implied she already knew things no one below the Hand should know. The insertion was deliberate; the summoning feels organic to the court.

**OQ-7 — Witness architecture: two, functional roles distinct.**
First: the broken maester (full truth of the research, not Worm-origin; he knows the experiment, not the planet). He is the conscience-figure and the live record; he documents the s4 foreclosure without being able to stop it. Second: a court-side figure — not a noble, but a servant-class person elevated by the Red Keep's internal economy — who knows Taylor's power-reach without knowing its source, and who functions as a betrayer-figure in late series. Not malicious: they genuinely believe alerting a Hightower functionary will protect their family. Taylor knows this is possible before it happens; she allows it because the betrayal produces the demonstration moment she needed. The witness architecture is operational, not intimate.

**OQ-8 — Stopping-shape: exhausted-then-collapsed.**
Taylor does not intervene to stop the Dance directly. She manages it: insect-surveillance keeps her informed of every major decision by both factions; she selectively prevents resupply, routes information to prolong stalemate rather than resolution, and lets both sides bleed out to the point of internal fracture. When the demonstration comes — triggered by the betrayal event — it is not the climax of the war but the termination of a depleted conflict. The collapsed-by-demonstration happens against already-exhausted factions. This shape is darker: she did not stop the Dance; she managed the conditions under which it would be cheapest to demonstrate, which means she let people die who might have been saved if she had intervened earlier.

**OQ-9 — Three seasons.**
S1: Flea Bottom, research onset, range expansion, maester as observation target. S2: candle acquisition, deliberate insertion via servant-network, Khepri-mantle threshold (triggered by the scale of coordinating Red Keep servants through insect-relay), Dance ignites. S3: stalemate management, betrayal event, demonstration-climax, s4 foreclosure event (the managed-stalemate logic is what enables the density-saturation experiment — Taylor needed extended high-density relay operation and the war gave her the cover for it). The foreclosure is the final season's central horror.

---

## Recommendation: Option A

Option A is the only shape where the s4 foreclosure event lands with full dramatic weight. The four-season structure gives the research arc room to self-erase at the correct pace: the clinical register can genuinely deteriorate through two full seasons before the log-burn, and the audience can see the deterioration without being told it is happening. S2's peak is the Khepri-mantle threshold: pulling it here from S3 gives each season its own Faustian crossing — S2 is where Taylor first pays with herself rather than with time or safety, and the mantle cause (sustained high-density relay load during range expansion and candle-acquisition) is organically present in S2 without requiring new lore. Option (a) was chosen over (b) because a hypothesis-falsification event in S2 would duplicate the S4 foreclosure pattern too visibly, reducing S4 to repetition; option (a) instead introduces a different category of cost (somatic/identity threshold rather than research collapse), so S2 and S4 read as distinct stages of the same deterioration rather than the same event twice. The one-witness architecture (broken maester as sole mirror) is the most honest to the solitude-is-the-cost logic — Taylor has a brake, but the brake does not stop her, and that is more frightening than no brake at all. The collapsed-by-demonstration stopping-shape fits the hard constraint that fear is the load-bearing mechanism: the demonstration is public, attributed, and irrefutable, which makes rational deterrence actually function — both factions price it in because they cannot un-see it. Option B compresses too aggressively (s4 foreclosure in two seasons loses the self-erasing clinical record); Option C's exhausted-then-collapsed shape requires Taylor to be deliberately managing who dies, which is a defensible Faustian choice but risks making the s3 stalemate-management arc feel procedurally inert rather than contemplatively horrifying.
