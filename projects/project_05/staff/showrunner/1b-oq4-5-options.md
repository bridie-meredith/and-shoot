# OQ-4 + OQ-5 joint options — operational base and smallfolk slice

---

## Option A: Flea Bottom anchor, urban landless primary

**Operational base trajectory:** Taylor leaves the tanner village within the first season-quarter. The village is her origin cost surface (Tya-situation, family reaction, Stranger-leavings suspicion) but not her base. She migrates into King's Landing — specifically Flea Bottom — and stays there through s1 and s2. No relocation. The base is fixed; her range expands within it. She does not move toward Oldtown until late s2 at the earliest, and then only as a forward presence (a salvager contact), not a relocation.

**Primary smallfolk slice:** Urban landless — dock runners, tanners displaced from the Crownlands villages, the chronically evicted, Flea Bottom residents who rent a room from someone who rents a room. These are people the city generates continuously and forgets instantly.

**Secondary alliance:** Marginal-outcast — broken men and wildfire-adjacent survivors who occupy the city's literal margins (the alley-runners, the ones who sleep under the Fish Gate). They are her early-warning network because they go where no one watches.

**Supporting character archetypes implied:**
- A Flea Bottom tanner-elder who knew Tya's family by trade reputation and extends provisional acceptance to Taylor on those grounds — her first bridge into the city's labor webs.
- A dock runner (adolescent, fast, knows every gate shift and City Watch patrol route) who becomes her legs and her first test case for trust that is not bought.
- A fence operating out of a tallow-chandler's back room — the salvage channel for glass-candles. His operation moves goods that fell off ships, goods that fell off dead nobles, goods that no one is supposed to have. This is where a glass-candle surfaces.

**Broken maester access:** The observation target is a disgraced maester who was stripped of his chain and now occupies a room above an apothecary in Flea Bottom's eastern quarter — close enough to the Citadel's correspondence network to still receive letters, too disgraced to be watched. Taylor reaches him through walls within her ~300m range. His books and letters are read through insect-bodies pressed against window seams.

**Glass-candle salvage channel:** The fence (tallow-chandler's back room) is the mechanism. Specific candle provenance: fell out of a Hand's household inventory during a succession dispute — misattributed as a common glass piece. The fence doesn't know what he has. Taylor does, after six months of watching the broken maester handle his.

**Late-series flag:** No geographic relocation needed. Flea Bottom puts her six hundred meters from the Red Keep. Range expansion alone brings the Targaryen court into operational range by s3.

## Option B: Crownlands village expansion, rural tied primary shifting to outcast

**Operational base trajectory:** Taylor stays in the tanner village through most of s1, using it as cover and as a genuine smallfolk protection problem she is solving in real time. She does not migrate to King's Landing. Instead, she builds a networked presence: physically based in the village, she extends range outward through territory she walks (range is centered on her body, not on a fixed point). By mid-s1, she has a second node — a camp-follower settlement outside King's Landing's walls. She moves between them. By s2 she has three nodes: village, camp-follower camp, and a Flea Bottom contact point (not a residence). The glass-candle channel runs through the camp-follower settlement, where goods move continuously.

**Primary smallfolk slice:** Rural tied — the village people subject to corvée, conscription, and crop-seizure. Taylor's s1 problem is concrete and immediate: a lord's reeve is conscripting men for a Crownlands border garrison. She stops that. The arc is about making one village ungovernable.

**Secondary alliance:** Camp followers and war-adjacent civilians. The camp outside the walls is the crossroads where rural and urban smallfolk mix; it is where information flows; it is where the salvage channel lives.

**Supporting character archetypes implied:**
- The village headman — not the tanner-family elder, a separate figure — who is trying to negotiate with the reeve and resents Taylor's interference as likely to bring retaliation. Primary friction source.
- A camp-follower midwife who is also an informal fence: she moves goods, she moves information, she moves people. The glass-candle enters through her, provenance unclear even to her.
- A wandering hedge maester (not fully trained, chain incomplete) who camped near the settlement and stayed — the broken-maester analog. His knowledge is partial and his guilt about leaving the Citadel is the wedge Taylor uses.

**Broken maester access:** The hedge maester is already in her physical range; no walls to press against. The observation dynamic is inverted — he knows she is watching him, which is itself a dramatic difference from Option A.

**Glass-candle salvage channel:** The camp-follower midwife, via goods displaced from the capital during a lord's household dispersal. Same basic mechanism as Option A but rural-routed.

**Late-series flag:** This base requires relocation to reach Targaryen orbit. Late s2 or s3 must include a King's Landing move, which adds a transition arc. Not foreclosed — it could be a season-structure asset — but it is a required later beat.

## Option C: Flea Bottom anchor, outcast-primary with guild secondary

**Operational base trajectory:** Same as Option A — Taylor migrates to Flea Bottom early in s1 and stays through s2. The difference is her primary smallfolk embedding. She does not root with the settled working poor. She roots with the people who have no stable location at all: the broken men sheltering in Flea Bottom's margins, the wildfire-adjacent survivors from prior Targaryen disasters, the pre-conscription pool that the City Watch runs sweeps against every few months. These are people with no fixed address, no guild membership, no landlord relationship. They are off every ledger the city keeps.

**Primary smallfolk slice:** Marginal-outcast — the completely un-ledgered. Taylor chooses them specifically because her surveillance advantage is maximized by people who are already invisible. She does not protect them charitably; she organizes them as a network of bodies she can coordinate.

**Secondary alliance:** Craft guild workers — weavers and smiths in Flea Bottom's outer ring. They are organized, have communication structures already, and have leverage (a guild strike is a legible threat to the city's economy). Taylor's relationship with them is colder: she is useful to them; they are useful to her; trust is functional, not warm.

**Supporting character archetypes implied:**
- A broken man (former soldier, Crownlands border veteran) who leads an informal shelter-cluster under the Fish Gate. Taylor's first point of contact; his loyalty to her is real but his relationship to her research is the slow-building cost.
- A guild warden (weaver's guild, mid-level) who barters information access for Taylor's protection of guild workers against City Watch press-gangs. The relationship is transactional and he is aware it is.
- The fence, same as Option A — tallow-chandler's back room, glass-candle salvage channel.

**Broken maester access:** Same as Option A — disgraced maester above an apothecary, reached through walls via insect-bodies. No change to this mechanism.

**Glass-candle salvage channel:** Same as Option A — through the fence.

**Late-series flag:** Same as Option A — no relocation required. Range expansion brings the Red Keep into range by s3.

---

## Recommendation: Option A

Option A is recommended. The reason is structural compression: it puts Taylor's primary embedding with the urban landless — the largest, most visible, most politically legible slice — while preserving the fence and the broken maester as two separate character relationships with distinct functions. The dock runner gives her mobility without making her mobile. The tallow-chandler fence gives the glass-candle channel a character who does not know what he has, which is a cleaner dramatic setup than Option C's fence-identical configuration or Option B's midwife. Option B's rural-anchor has the advantage of grounding the Tya-origin cost, but it forces a relocation beat that Option A avoids — and the Tya-situation (village reaction, tanner family) can run as an ongoing cost surface even after Taylor leaves, through periodic contact and through what she does and does not do about the people she left. Option C's outcast-primary is interesting but it makes Taylor's relationship to the smallfolk colder from the start, which compresses the arc that has to pay off at s4 when she starts treating them as variables. Option A gives her a warmer initial embedding — people she is genuinely protecting, not primarily coordinating — which makes the clinical drift more visible in contrast.
