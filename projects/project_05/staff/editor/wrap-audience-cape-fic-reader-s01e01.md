## Prologue — italicised voice block

*I had won my world by becoming a hive of bugs, and I had not meant to wake. Three days later I woke anyway, in another body — a tanner's daughter dead of fever, in a village outside a king's city, the year before the Dance of the Dragons. The bugs came with me. Three hundred metres of them.*

*It has been a month since the fever. My parents have not asked me to leave and have not called me by my name. The bowls keep arriving on the table. The dogs do not come to my hand. I have spent the time learning what the body can do — what it remembers without being told, what it does not — and writing in a log nobody else can read.*

*The septon denied a miracle. My parents settled on 'falsely declared dead.' The village settled on 'Tya who came back wrong.' They weren't wrong about the wrong part.*

CLEAN. The three-paragraph structure lands. Rule-legible opening ("three hundred metres of them" — hard range stated, shard present from arrival, no re-trigger implied). Information-asymmetry framing is immediate: reader knows the log is unreadable to others, reader knows the body is not hers. "They weren't wrong about the wrong part" is the right final note — closes on Taylor's own assessment, not sentiment.

## Institutional Parenthetical Count (summary note for editor)

The episode contains four institutional parentheticals or explanatory glosses inserted into active tactical moments:
1. Reeve parenthetical — Scene E
2. Lord's-man rank explanation — Scene F
3. Maester parenthetical — Scene J
4. Gold-cloak parenthetical — Scene M

Each individual gloss is small. Together they form a pattern: the episode explains Westerosi institutions by interrupting the scene that introduces the institution. The cape-fic reader will tolerate one. Four is a texture problem. The editor should consider whether any of these glosses can be relocated to a non-active moment (the road-walk in Scene H, the perimeter-walk in Scene K) or compressed into a single sentence that rides the beat rather than stops it.
