*By the next winter the tanner-family's wage-claim was on customary record; my mother had stopped lighting the vigil candle; the broken maester had become a named entry in my log; my insects ran to four hundred metres. I kept writing.*

---

-- scene: A --

The beetles brought me the cold candle and the broken maester — the overnight watch holding the room quiet, the wall beetle already in the seam. The clerk entered the junction in full view, the morning crowd opening around him; the seam had just taken the weight of a clerk. Different rank than reeve or lord's-man, different office — his work was the entry, not the errand. He faced the tanner elder and addressed him. The elder did not lift his gaze from the ledger before he answered. "Aye, ser. She's placed here. A moon now, or thereabouts." The clerk opened the record book against the name I did not use, wrote the entry, and closed the book. He left the junction. He crossed the Fish Gate, and the file went past the seam; my body knew the shape of a file leaving its witness from before, a reader upstream of its writer, the gate carrying it past where I could reach.

The flies were on the junction and on the clerk. I opened the log. My hand stayed steady on the pen through the entry. I closed the log.

---

-- scene: B --

That night I spread the network wide. I sent the flies across the overnight network, the wasps across the dock-side alleys, the beetles across the south-wall colony, the spiders across the eastern-quarter relay, the beetles through the apothecary ground floor. I walked the perimeter — the wall's cold face at the outer arc of the district, the beetles at the far end of reach. I wrote the entry. I dropped the gaze.

I came back — first light through the window, the candle guttered, the log closed. I held the eyes. I faced the wall. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

-- scene: C --

The flies took the second clerk into the eastern-quarter adjacent street. He turned, and the apothecary door opened to him. There was more than one hand in this work: a second seam, a second clerk.

He addressed the keeper; the keeper answered twice, once for the question and once for the press.

He opened the record book, wrote the entry, closed it. He let it go.

He stepped back into the cold of the alley. I had the flies on the doorframe. I had the flies on him.

I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it. Two entries. They should not have been the same shape.

---

-- scene: D --

The elder came up the corridor through the open midday and stopped in front of me. "Dock-side wants a runner," he said. "Cluster's waiting. Half a day's work, maybe less." I faced him. My weight stayed where it was. "The dock-side," I said. "How many." "Tanner-folk in the Crownlands gave me your name," he said. "Reference is good. That's the whole of it." I'd heard the second sentence before he said it. The body counted the blank: the column where the cause should sit, left open, the event named and the reason field empty.

---

-- scene: E --

I walked into the dock-side alley. The wasps spread through the cluster ahead of me. The wasps carried the weight to me before the word reached the far end.

"There is nothing here," I said. "The birds are restless."

The wasps relayed outward and brought back the elder. The cluster thinned when I spoke. The flies pulled back.

I stepped out toward the alley mouth, where the elder stood at the threshold.

"Your cut," he said. "Same as last."

I extended my palm. He placed the coin. The body knew the denomination before my fist closed; the knowing was older than this hand. I closed my fist around it.

I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

-- scene: F --

Later that day, the maester left his rooms.

He came down the stair and out through the apothecary door. He turned into the cobbled alley of the eastern quarter and the beetles took up the footfall there. He crossed into the open row of the market and went to the stall. He spoke to the keeper. "Goodman. A measure of the dried sage, if you have it kept dry, and half of the salt-fish." The keeper answered. "Yes, yes. The sage, if I may — was it dried at the turn of the moon, or the moon before?" he said. The keeper told him. He faced the jars. He stepped out of the stall and the beetles carried him back through the apothecary door, the pen scratch coming back into range. He climbed the stair, the compound smell from the dispensary thickening against the closed upper door. He went into the upper room. The pen was still scratching; the session was still running. He set the pen down. His hand stayed on it, fingers not lifting from the shaft. The breath held one beat past the close. It was the kind of hold I'd held before, after sessions that didn't reopen.

I held the feet still. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it on him.

---

-- scene: G --

My father came to the junction at the afternoon shift. He came to the same seam the first clerk had used. He approached the elder and stood square, hands at his sides.

"Come down to see she's still placed. Aye?"

The elder faced him.

"She's placed. Earns it monthly. Same as the day she came."

My father walked off.

The elder closed the distance to the base. He came into my line.

"Your father come by. Asked after the placement. Walked off again."

I faced him. The breath left before the answer — same interval as before, the body ahead of the word.

I opened the log and wrote.

---

-- scene: H --

I sent the flies across the overnight network, the wasps to the Fish Gate margin, the beetles to the south-wall colony, the spiders to the eastern-quarter relay, the beetles around the south-wall perimeter — the far end arriving thinner than the nearer posts. I walked the perimeter at the south-wall margin where the light had crossed behind the wall. I wrote the entry. I exhaled.

I came back — the south wall still in the body, the range still open. I held the eyes. I faced the wall. The spine straightened. I opened the log. The south-wall column was the fifth count in my log. I faced the Red Keep and my interior had the count of years for what the seat above the city was going to be.

---

-- scene: I --

Morning came clear at the junction, and the flies had a third figure walking it wearing the shape of the first two. The messenger stopped at the elder's stall. He faced the old man and spoke his piece. The elder turned to him, took a breath that didn't leave fast, and said, "Aye, ser. I'll write it out. Come the morrow, by midday." The messenger left the junction. I held the feet.

---

-- scene: J --

The elder left the junction. The flies had him through the door of the writing room — enclosed, door shut, the light dropped to candle.

He wrote the account. The seal pressed down, the click of it, and the seal had closed on an account I would not read. The body had been on the other side of a seal like this before.

The middleman took the account and left the writing room. The flies had him. The account was past the seam.

I held the feet. I opened the log. I wrote the entry — my spine the same angle as the first word, the account I could not read. I closed it.

---

-- scene: K --

I left the base door at first light, and the winter cold met me at the threshold. I took the first alley and walked it, took the south alley and walked it through, came onto the Fish Gate margin where the arch's shadow lay cold on the approach. The gate was the threshold the first file had crossed. I walked the margin to the south-wall colony, where the beetles had it already, and I walked through that too. I crossed the junction's cobbled center between the stalls, took the eastern-quarter approach with the dispensary's compound smell reaching me before the door came up, the spiders had the window, and I walked that approach back to the base.

Inside, I faced the wall. The lime-wash was worn to bare stone at the height where a hand would rest, and the wall came up under my eye as two open columns on the same page — the same kind of record. The body had set this kind of angle before — that other column, the one that read no pattern identified where the cause should have sat, the record that named the event and left the convicting field blank, the word the record used in place of the name it would not write. It was older than any wall I had faced before. The body had stood here in a different room and did not need the log to know the decision was already made. I opened the log. I wrote the entry — my column and the column I would not read closing on the same hour. I closed the log.
