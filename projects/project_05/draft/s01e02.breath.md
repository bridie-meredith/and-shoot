*Through the first weeks in Flea Bottom I had walked my three hundred metres twice a day, mapped the market-side junction and the apothecary's upper room where the broken maester kept his hours, and made my first transactional pass with the dock-runner through the tanner-elder. The log was current. The tanner-village was a day's walk south.*

---

I faced the junction. The elder was already turned to the road, watching. My father crossed in from the market side, my mother a step behind him, and the elder greeted them both. I walked into the junction and pivoted toward them. My father went still. I faced him, shoulders square, weight even. "We come down with the hides," he said. "Your mother would see you." I answered, "Goodman. I have not forgotten." My father went still again, and my mother went still. He raised the trade goods. "Old Tom. A word, when you've a moment. The Crownlands lot, same as before." The elder answered him across the table. "Aye. Same slate, same cut. She's earning her place, last I heard. We'll keep it that way." My mother turned to me. My father stepped back, his weight on his rear foot, gaze on the strap at his hip. "You've grown thin, child," my mother said. "Are you eating?" I answered her, "Goodwife. The road is yours." She pivoted toward the road south, and my father with her. They walked out of the junction.

Back at the base room, I opened the log, wrote it down, closed it.

After, I let the relays run. The flies had the tanner-elder. The wasps had the Watch patrol — tempo clean, a perimeter I hadn't walked. The wasps had the dock-runner.

The daylight dropped as I stepped in. Empty room, alley-sound through the second-floor window. I wrote it down.

Out to the market-side junction. Wind off the road south, Fish Gate sound thin along the margin. The elder paused. "Off with you, then. Tell the dock-man Tom's word is good for the load. Before the bell," the elder said. The wasps brought the pass: the interval held, the question closed. He turned and faced me square, working pitch, the placement-look set on his face. The carter rolled out of the junction. I opened the log, wrote it down, closed it.

The flies spread the overnight network, the wasps the dock-side alleys, the beetles the south-wall colony, the spiders the eastern-quarter approach. I rolled my shoulders, the move clean and remembered. The flies relayed the junction return; the wasps were on the Fish Gate — the southern dock-side gate of King's Landing, the city's busy fish-trade mouth — holding the perimeter; the beetles relayed the south-wall return; the spiders the window. I exhaled. The flies spread the northern block, the wasps the eastern-quarter adjacent. I exhaled again. I walked the perimeter, wrote the entry, wrote it again, and exhaled.

I woke and held my eyes still, then opened the log, wrote the entry, and closed it.

The night alley was iron-latched and occupied when the lord's-man stepped into it, his stride loaded wrong for Eastern-Quarter ground. He spoke to the family at the door. His man broke the latch — and the alley-murmur cracked. The bar gave. His man carried possessions out. The family exited the two-room dwelling. A neighbour pressed the doorway. The flies relayed the lord's-man's man — an ear pressed to a wall; the beetles relayed the lintel. The neighbour withdrew. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

My father and mother came back into the junction. The ground between us was open. He turned to me. "There's the wage-claim, girl. Customary. Two coppers a moon till the harvest turn, and we're square," my father said. (What the Crownlands called the customary wage — the unwritten claim a family carried for the labour a returned child owed them, a tally without paper.) "Name the sum. I will see it paid," I answered. "Two coppers. That's the way of it. Same as your mother's people done for hers," he said. I let the breath out. I opened the purse. I counted the coins once across my palm, then again, and held them out to him. He closed his hand on them without looking up at my face. "We'll come again at the next moon's turn. Mind yourself," he said. I turned to him. He left the junction; she left after him. I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed the log.

The flies spread out into the autumn-density network, the wasps along the dock-side relay, the spiders into the eastern quarter. I walked the perimeter and wrote the entry — wrote it again — flexed my hand. I woke. The stylus dropped. I held my eyes still. I lowered my chin. I opened the log and wrote.

Afternoon held the side alley clear. The visitor entered the side alley, then the stairwell, then the upper room. The visitor spoke; I did not hear what. The beetles brought the register through the wall, and from the upper room the maester's voice came down: "Yes, yes. Come, sit. Let us see what you have brought." I held my feet still. He went out by the upper room, by the stairwell, by the side-alley door. I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed it on a name.

The beetles brought a rhythm. The beetles brought a phrase. The beetles brought a rhythm. I went still. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

Dusk cut the apothecary corner to a strip. The broken maester left the apothecary, took the side alley, crossed the upper-room threshold. The beetles brought the maester. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

My mother came in. The vigil candle — a Stranger-light kept burning for the dead until the family released them — was on the shelf where it had been. She faced me and sat. I faced her. "The candle's gone short. I'll let it burn what's left and trouble you no more," my mother said. "She is not here, goodwife," I answered, without raising my chin from the log. "I'll stop coming, after this one. Seven keep you. Whatever you are, you are warm — that's what I came to see," she said. She looked down. She stood. Her thumb passed once, stained crease against the edge of the bench, and her weight came off it; the chair gave, the floor gave under her. She left the room.

I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed the log.
