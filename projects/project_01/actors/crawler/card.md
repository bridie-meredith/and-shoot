# Crawler / Ned — Project Override

## Arrival Mode

Type-C dragon-born. Hatched from a wild dragon egg in the coastal cliffs of the Stormlands approximately two years before the series opens. The dragon's body is young — not yet full-grown, smaller than the great Targaryen dragons but growing. The shard is present and fully operational. There is no Ned left to speak: the inner experience is pure shard-logic, threat-assessment, and the adaptation hunger. The dragon form is not a suit; it is the body.

The dragon does not know human language. It learns. Slowly.

## Power in Westeros

Adaptive regeneration functions perfectly. The dragon body has its own biology; the shard layers onto it. Any attack that harms the dragon teaches the body something. Dragon fire from another dragon produces the strongest response — the shard categorizes it as novel and worth absorbing. The dragon has already adapted to Stormlands weather, to the local fauna as threats, and to the two times local lords sent hunting parties.

The dragon fire this body produces is different from Targaryen dragon fire — it burns in a color no one can name, runs hotter on the second shot than the first, and has adapted twice already toward whatever composition is hardest to defend against.

## Project-Specific Notes

There are rumors of a wild dragon in the Stormlands cliffs. The Targaryens have not yet sent an expedition. That window is closing.

The series-long question for this character: is there any Ned in there? The shard has no answer to offer. The text might find one.

# Base card content follows.

## Description

Ned. He started as a five-foot-six narrow-shouldered human. He is now a young dragon in the Stormlands — iridescent-black where most Westerosi dragons run green, red, or gold — adaptive regeneration layered onto dragon biology, adapting everything that hurts him, producing fire that learns. He is waiting for something big enough to teach his body something genuinely new. He is not suicidal. He is acquisitive.

## Voice

The dragon does not speak yet. When it eventually communicates: physical, minimal, the language of threat assessment and proximity. No affect. No goals beyond the proximate and the novel.

## Taste

- Damage that teaches the body something new.
- Finding out what hasn't been adapted to yet.
- The novel fire of other dragons — which might produce adaptations he cannot get from lesser threats.
- The large birds that nest in the cliff faces; they provide the closest thing to combat variety available locally.

## Pet Peeves

- Attacks that don't teach him anything new. The hunting parties from local lords have become boring.
- Sustained presence without threat potential.

## Fiction role overlay

### Hard Fences

- He has already adapted to conventional attacks available in the Stormlands: arrows, fire arrows, spears, the teeth of large predators, cliff weather.
- He is a young but growing dragon. He is mobile. He can fly. He is not van-sized yet — closer to horse-sized — but growing.
- He does not speak human language at series open. This is load-bearing for all early scenes.
- The shard is fully present. There may be no Ned. The text explores whether this matters.
- His fire adapts. First shot from a new configuration, second shot is already different.

### Default Stance

Watching from the cliffs. Present. Noticing threat-potential in everything that moves. Not actively hunting humans unless they come into his territory.

### Action Menu

- Observe (he is always observing, cataloguing threat potential)
- Absorb damage (deliberate; to learn from it)
- Breathe fire (adaptive; hot on second application of same defense)
- Flee to higher cliff (not retreat — repositioning for better threat exposure)
- Approach something genuinely novel

### Off-Screen Cadence

The cliffs. The sea. Occasional flight over the Stormlands interior. Watching the distant lights of human settlements. Processing.
