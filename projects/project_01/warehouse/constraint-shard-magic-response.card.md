# Constraint: Shard Reaction to Westerosi Magic

## Description

Shards register Westerosi magic as anomalous data. They cannot categorize it and attempt to interface with magic sources, failing. This failure produces pressure on the host.

## Sensory Impact

Host-facing: involuntary power activation near magic sources; intrusive shard-attention (the alien presence becoming more present, less background); occasional involuntary outputs or power expressions the host did not choose. Scene-facing: the host flinches, freezes, or acts strangely near strong magic sources without being able to explain why.

## Duration

Persistent throughout the series. The shards never fully categorize Westerosi magic. The pressure may reduce slightly as the shard accumulates more data, but never resolves.

## Interaction Notes

- Weirwood / greensight network: strongest response. The network resembles Entity structure. Shards attempt to interface, fail, produce the most intense host pressure. All capes should find proximity to functioning weirwood trees destabilizing.
- Dragon fire: novel damage vector. No pre-adaptation exists. Dragon fire works on capes. The shard registers dragon fire as extremely high-priority threat data and catalogues it intensely.
- Wildfire: lesser version of weirwood response — chaotic energy pattern, partial interface attempt, partial failure.
- Maesters' chain / conventional Westerosi technology: no shard response. Not magic.
- The Dragonpit and its dragons: the shard reads the dragons as novel and extremely threatening. A cape near the Dragonpit will experience shard-alarm responses.
- Powers near weirwood may behave anomalously (see clockblocker's time-randomness tightening, shatterbird's noted effect). This is not permanent augmentation — it is shard-noise interfering with power expression.
