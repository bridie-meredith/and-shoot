## Callout (a): mem:4 scaffold recurrence — CLOSED

The cycle-3 description:

> *the reading-and-the-not-reporting are running the same operation now; the architecture cycles through the body the report has filed elsewhere*

Compared against mem:2:

> *the feed returns the body she will not write down*

I am going to read this slowly.

mem:2's scaffold: **subject (the feed) + single detection verb (returns) + object (the thing the architecture refused to record — the body she will not write down)**. One event. The feed fires. The body comes back. The ledger is silent. Sentence over.

mem:4's scaffold: **paired-process subject (the reading-and-the-not-reporting) + progressive/iterative verb (are running) + shared predicate (the same operation now); second clause: architecture-as-subject + iterative verb (cycles) + object-as-filed-location (the body the report has filed elsewhere)**.

The scaffold is not the same. What changed is not vocabulary — it is the subject and the verb structure, which is exactly where the cue-mode lives.

mem:2 has one subject: the feed. One verb: detection. The event is discrete. It fires, it returns, it is over.

mem:4 has two subjects: reading *and* not-reporting — they are named as a pair, running together, which registers the simultaneity of the two processes as an ongoing fact, not as a moment of discovery. The verb is *are running* — progressive aspect, present-continuous, the thing is happening as the sentence speaks rather than having happened. The second clause does the same: *cycles through* — iterative, not punctual. The body has been *filed elsewhere* — past perfect, meaning the architecture has already committed this to a location outside the report, and the cycling confirms that routing is structural, not a one-time refusal.

This is not synonym substitution at the scaffold level. The subject changed from single-agent to paired-process. The verb changed from simple past completion to present continuous. The object changed from refusal-to-write to institutional-filing — the body is not missing from the record because Taylor refuses to write it; it is missing because the architecture has *routed it elsewhere*, which is a different shape of omission (bureaucratic routing vs. personal refusal).

mem:2: the moment of first-recognition. The feed sees what the ledger will not hold. The interior notices the gap.

mem:4: the running shape. The reading and the not-reporting are the same operation, structurally, at the chapter's close. The interior has finished noticing and is now *in* the architecture that keeps doing this. The body is not absent by refusal; it is filed in another location by the architecture's own routing.

That is the continuous-operation thesis, enacted in the description field. The stitcher has two different shapes: the first moment of recognizing the gap, and the chapter-close registration that the gap is the architecture's operating shape.

**(a) resolves. Closed.**
