# Cold-read: b01-c12 (2026-06-03)

First-time reader, text only.

## 1. EVENTS
Almost nothing physically happens. A first-person narrator who runs surveillance insects for "Otto Hightower" sits at a station and does paperwork. In order, the nameable events:
- Insects return data about lanes near the water-gate; she places a cluster of insects ("first tranche") into the upper lanes.
- Jarvis (a courier) drops a wax-sealed packet; she breaks the seal and reads a request naming two boundary walls.
- She writes a ledger entry declaring the east-water-gate lanes "not accessible," and deliberately leaves the explanation/"source" field blank.
- She seals the packet; Jarvis takes it back.
- In the afternoon she runs a second insect cluster, completing coverage of all five wards.
- The accounting reaches a "breach column"; she records a cost entry but withholds naming something (an "older word").

Long stretches contain no event at all — paragraphs 5, 9, and 11 are almost entirely interior bookkeeping metaphor with no physical action.

## 2. JEOPARDY
Effectively no jeopardy I can feel. There are gestures at risk: a "word these streets keep for women like me" (she could be named/exposed as something — a witch?), and someone named Wren whose movement she is secretly protecting by refusing to map a lane. But nothing threatens anyone on the page. No one acts against her, no clock, no consequence shown. The stakes are asserted ("load-bearing," "never been priced," "breach") but never dramatized. Closest to jeopardy: she is hiding something from the "apparatus" and it might be discovered — but the text says no one will ever see it.

## 3. CAUSALITY
Within the narrator's head, the steps chain logically: data returns → she places insects → she withholds the reason → she protects Wren's route. But almost every clause is abstraction, so I frequently could not tell *what* was happening or *why it mattered*. I could not tell: who Wren is, why protecting Wren matters, what the "older word"/"different city" is, or what the "third withhold" refers to. The causal spine ("refusing to map one lane keeps Wren free, which pays a debt") is buried under metaphor I had to excavate.

## 4. PAYOFF
The ending gestures hard at a turn — she withholds a name, recognizes a pattern from "when the world was ending," files a cost without filing the word. But it did not land, because the chapter never showed me what the word *is*, what the debt *is*, or who Wren *is*. The "earned" turn is earned only against backstory I don't have. As written, it's a character feeling weight about something the text refuses to make concrete.

## 5. CONTINUE?
No — I admired the prose texture for two paragraphs, then realized I'd read a whole chapter of a woman doing data-entry in riddles with no event, no person to hold onto, and no stakes I could feel.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
A surveillance-clerk spends a day filing reports and quietly leaves one lane off the map to protect someone, while refusing to write down why.
