# Cold-read — b01-c17 (2026-06-05)

First-time reader. Text only. Blunt.

## 1. EVENTS
- Narrator retrieves a hidden count-sheet from under a trough-stone; it holds a surveillance query about an unnamed woman crossing a gate-gap on a regular interval.
- Narrator recognizes the woman from her gait (a "second-step hold" / ankle scar) — it's someone called Wren, whom they've been deliberately routing around / shielding for months.
- Narrator walks the waterfront ("the Hook") doing mental cost-accounting: naming her = exposing her, so they consider an alternative — falsely attributing her movements to someone else.
- Narrator goes to a room above a rendering works and falsifies a coverage log: writes three lines attributing the corridor movements to "Elder Norren," who actually passes there legitimately.
- Narrator readies the falsified segment for a dead-drop and leaves.
- Three days later the dead-drop comes back quiet — the apparatus accepts Norren's name; the query is closed; Wren is safe.
- Narrator leaves the cost-ledger column blank and reflects that they've repeated, at small scale, the shape of whatever act they came here to renounce.

That's the whole chapter. Roughly seven beats. Genuinely event-bearing stretches: lines 1, 9–11, 13. The middle (5–7) is almost pure interior accounting with no physical event.

## 2. JEOPARDY
Yes, faintly. Wren is at risk of being "named, findable, usable" by some surveillance "apparatus" — i.e. exposed and presumably caught/harmed. I know this only because the narrator says naming priced out to "usable" with "no entry past usable." The threat is entirely abstract — no agent, no consequence shown, no sense of what "usable" does to a person. Norren is put at unspecified risk by carrying a false record. Stakes are asserted, never dramatized.

## 3. CAUSALITY
The causal chain itself is followable: query arrives → recognition → decision to deflect → falsification → acceptance → aftermath. That spine holds. What I CANNOT tell: who is querying and why; what the "apparatus" is; what happens to a "named/usable" person; who Wren is to the narrator and why they protect her; what "the thing I came here to set down" / "built once at scale" actually was. The narrator's *motive* is gestured at ("the shape of the act," "the prohibition I carried") but never made concrete, so the moral weight is unearned.

## 4. PAYOFF
Mechanically there's a turn — the narrator commits the falsification and lives with it. But it doesn't *land*, because the chapter never showed me the stakes it pays off. The closing "I had protected the gait the same way I built the thing I came here to set down... the shape of the act" is the supposed gut-punch, and it's a reference to events outside this chapter I was never given. So the ending gestures at a reckoning rather than delivering one.

## 5. CONTINUE?
No — I admire the control but I have no character, no concrete stakes, and no plot thread to pull me forward.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
A surveillance operative quietly falsifies a logbook to protect a woman from being identified, and realizes they've just re-committed, in miniature, the exact sin they fled.

- What is "the feed" / "ward-read" that hands back a gait? An implant? Magic? Tech? Unknown.
- Who is Wren? First named at line 7, never introduced. Why does the narrator protect her?
- What did the narrator "build once at scale" and renounce? The chapter's entire emotional payload rests on this and it's a black box.
- What is the "tether"? What is a "ward"? "Flea Bottom"-adjacent jargon ("the Hook," "stitch-house rotation," "tanner's-ward") arrives unexplained.
- The prose is so abstract ("the accounting hear itself," "named is findable, findable is usable") that I had to re-read lines 5–7 twice and still only half-followed the deflection scheme.

Re-answer Q5, strict: **No.** The confusions aren't teasers I want resolved; they're load-bearing gaps that leave the chapter feeling like a closing meditation on a story I was never told.
