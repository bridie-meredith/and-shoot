# Cold-read — b01-c09 (depth pass)

First-time reader, text only, one read at reading pace.

## 1. EVENTS
- The narrator (unnamed) walks a routine morning circuit south of the Hook. Some surveillance/sensing thing ("the insect-feed") tracks a woman named Wren walking from her stitch-shop toward a water-seller. The narrator notes her route — door, corner, lane — and "files" it.
- That evening, a second circuit runs near the Dragonpit margin / lower gate. The "insect-feed" picks up a man named Corwick standing where he shouldn't be, at the wrong hour, by a gate tied to the wrong political faction. He faces a second man and "squares his shoulders." The narrator reads this as meaningful, "prices" it, logs it, and finishes the circuit.
- The narrator returns to a "feed-station," folds and wax-seals a packet, files records (Wren's route, the courier entry, the ward notes), and the wax dries.

That is essentially all that physically happens: two walks, two observations, one packet sealed. Most of the wordcount is interior processing of those observations, not action.

## 2. JEOPARDY
Faint and inferred, not shown. Corwick is "wrong-houred and wrong-warded," squares up to face a second man, and the narrator "prices the posture-class" — this hints someone may be in danger or doing something illicit, but the text never tells me what the threat is, what happens to Corwick, or what the stakes are. Wren is being indexed/surveilled without her knowledge, which feels quietly ominous, but no concrete harm is on the table. Verdict: implied menace, no actual jeopardy I can name.

## 3. CAUSALITY
The three beats are sequential (morning, evening, return) but barely causal. The morning Wren observation does not cause the evening Corwick observation. The Corwick observation does not obviously cause the packet at the end — I can guess the packet reports what was observed, but the text doesn't confirm it. The scenes are a list of circuits, not a chain. I could not tell why any one event led to the next; they're just the day's rounds.

## 4. PAYOFF
No. The chapter ends on wax drying. The closing image (seal going firm) is meant to feel like a quiet click of finality, but nothing was set up that pays off here. I don't know what was decided, what the packet does, or why this day mattered more than any other. It reads like the connective tissue between two real chapters.

## 5. CONTINUE?
No — I have no idea who the narrator is, what they want, or what's at stake, and nothing here made me need to know.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
A nameless surveillance operative walks two patrol circuits, silently logs a woman's daily route and a suspicious man at a gate, then seals a report.

## Where I was confused
Almost everywhere. I never learned who "I" is, what "the insect-feed" literally is (drones? spies? magic?), what "the keeping," "the count," "the arrangement," "the Jarvis line," or "the Green faction" are. The vocabulary is dense and self-referential and assumes I already know the world. The prose is heavily interior — long, abstract sentences about "filing," "pricing," "posture-class," "the harm I can prevent is the only column that closes" — and I could not anchor any of it to a concrete situation. As a first chapter handed to me cold, I bounced off it.
