# Cold Read — b01-c04 (2026-05-27)

First-time-reader response. Text only.

## 1. EVENTS

In order, as best I could parse them:

1. The narrator decides, overnight, to accept some prior proposal with modifications.
2. At dawn in a cooper's yard, narrator meets a man called Jarvis (a "courier") and verbally agrees to terms: she will supply "pattern-reports" from Flea Bottom wards, on her interval, at her volume. Three days, first bell, same place.
3. Jarvis leaves. Narrator walks through three Flea Bottom wards — Pig Tallow Lane, somewhere near "stitch-house frames," and Roper's Court — using some kind of insect-based sensing ("the feed") to map them.
4. She notes two people (Oswyn Mudway, Wren Stitch-Maker) but deliberately excludes them from her written report.
5. She extends her sensing range to four wards simultaneously, noting it costs her something.
6. At first bell she returns to the cooper's yard. Jarvis shows her a folded paper (a receipt confirming "Sera was managed" and a three-month window closed). She hands him her own folded sheet of ward-patterns. He pockets it.
7. They part. She walks home through the Hook, still running the four-ward feed, passing Wren again without acting.

There is no scene where "nothing happens" exactly, but pages 22–53 (the middle walk) are nearly pure interior cartography — the only physical action is walking and looking.

## 2. JEOPARDY

Soft and offstage. The opening line ("the harm I could prevent") implies someone — "Sera" — was under threat, and this deal averts it. By the meeting Jarvis confirms "Sera was managed." So the stake was real but resolved before the chapter started; we never see Sera, never learn the threat, never feel the danger. The narrator herself bears a "cost" from the four-ward feed (saturation, load) but the text refuses to name what failure would look like — fainting? Detection? Death? Unknown. There's a faint exposure risk (handing intelligence to an upper channel she can't see into) but it's framed as accepted, not feared. Functionally: low jeopardy on the page.

## 3. CAUSALITY

The shape is legible: decide → meet → walk → write → exchange → leave. But almost every motive is referenced rather than shown. Who is "the man I serve" Jarvis represents? Who is Otto? Who is Sera and what was the threat? Why did she protect Wren and Oswyn specifically? The chapter assumes I already know. I was confused about: the nature of "the feed" (bugs? magic?), what "patron-tier"/"courier-tier" means, and why pattern-reports matter to anyone. I accepted the causal chain on faith.

## 4. PAYOFF

The ending lands a small turn — she walks away carrying both the report-just-delivered and the person-she-protected at the same count, which is a clean thematic beat. But it's earned only by the chapter's own internal arithmetic, not by anything I felt at stake. The "Sera managed" receipt is the real payoff and it arrives as a piece of paper, not a moment.

## 5. CONTINUE?

Tentative yes — the prose voice is distinctive enough to coast on for one more chapter, and I want to know who Sera is and what "the feed" actually is.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY

A surveillance-capable narrator trades ward-pattern intelligence to an unseen patron through a courier, in exchange for someone named Sera being kept safe.
