# Cold-read — b01-c18 — 2026-06-05

First-time reader, text only.

## 1. EVENTS
- Before dawn, the narrator lifts a cipher-bundle off the floor and reads an addendum: a demand for total surveillance coverage — every ward, channel, node — held for a fortnight, to complete a picture before a document moves through the Small Council. They set it back without answering.
- Before first light, they open their surveillance network (bottlefly routes, moth-corridors, feed-lines), skipping one eastern lane (Wren's). A fire creates interference; the system routes around it.
- They open ALL nodes at once for the first time, past a count they'd only reached once before elsewhere.
- Over six days the network returns surveillance on four court figures (a groom, maid, knight, septa), which resolve into one system mirroring the ward machinery.
- Days 9–12: they route the compiled picture to a dead drop in three drops; Jarvis collects it. The Greens use it to know a Small Council window is clear.
- Day 11: a return message says the document moved — the Greens now control the succession channel.
- Day 14: they shut the network down ward by ward, run the cost-ledger accounting, write the standdown, and deliberately leave one ledger column ("recognition") blank.

Genuinely event-bearing stretches are thin. Paragraphs 5, 9, and much of 11 are the narrator counting, re-reading, and naming states to themselves — almost no physical action.

## 2. JEOPARDY
Mostly abstract. Stated stakes: the narrator's deal keeps "Sera's parentage-question quiet" only while deliverables hold; Wren passes "screened" under a false name. There's a sense the narrator is expendable — "the more weight I carried, the more cleanly I could be set down." But no one is shown in concrete danger, no threat acts, nothing nearly goes wrong. The fire "did not stop anything." Closest to real jeopardy: the narrator's own disposability, named but not dramatized. Effectively: low, told-not-shown jeopardy.

## 3. CAUSALITY
The spine is traceable: demand arrives → narrator complies → picture delivered → Greens move document → narrator shut down. But it's all narrated summary, not scene. I could not tell WHO Wren, Sera, Jarvis, Norren, or "the Hand-who-was" are, or why the narrator agreed to any of this — the opening italics assume I already know. I could not tell what "the count," "the architecture," or the surveillance method physically IS. I read the whole thing not knowing whether this is magic, espionage, or metaphor.

## 4. PAYOFF
The intended payoff is the blank ledger line — the narrator earns no recognition for delivering, and knows it. As a turn it's legible IF you grant the elaborate framing. But it's almost entirely interior bookkeeping. Nothing external changes for the narrator; no consequence lands on them on the page. The ending "lands" only as a mood, not as an earned event — it gestures at tragedy (used and discarded) without showing me the cost in anything I can see.

## 5. CONTINUE?
No — I was too confused about who anyone is and what is literally happening to feel the stakes; the chapter reads as one long abstract accounting entry.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
A spy/surveillant runs their whole network at maximum for two weeks to hand an enemy faction the intelligence they need, then shuts it down and notes they'll get no credit for it.

## Confusion flagged
Heavy. Names dropped without anchoring; the central activity is never made concrete; "court apparatus," "architecture," "the count" stay abstract throughout. I never saw a room, a face, or a single line of dialogue.
