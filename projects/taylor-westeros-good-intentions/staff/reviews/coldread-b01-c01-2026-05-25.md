## 1. EVENTS

In order, as best I can name them:

1. A voice-over preface (italic): the narrator says she "won her last world by becoming a hive of bugs," woke in another body — a nameless woman in King's Landing, "the year before the year before the year the dragons would dance" — and brought "two hundred metres" of flies with her. She has been here three weeks, sleeping in a Flea Bottom alley, refusing to use the flies, paying a cost to suppress them. Her rule: be useful without taking control.
2. Scene opens in a Flea Bottom lane. A fish-cart blocks the lane. A child is on the ground, struggling to breathe ("the ground carried the child's breath"). A crowd presses in.
3. The narrator uses her insects — somehow — to part the crowd ("the nearest dozen bodies yielded; the gap propagated outward"). It is not described physically; she "tells" them to go, and bodies move.
4. She diagnoses the child: "Fever. Not the croup. She needs air. Stand back. Who knows her? Fetch them." She lifts her hands (does something — unclear what; presumably treats the child).
5. The child is cleared from the lane. The crowd disperses.
6. An elder — Oswyn Mudway — registers her, lifts his chin as if recognizing a "shape." A stitch-maker's ward (an eleven-year-old girl) also looks her in the eye. The narrator notes both have filed her in some category.

Between events 2 and 5 there are several lines I can't name as events — "the angle-wall narrowed the lane," "I exhaled," "the tallow smoke layered the lane-floor" — these are atmosphere, not action.

## 2. JEOPARDY

The child is in physical jeopardy — fever, can't breathe, on the ground. That's clear.

The narrator is in a softer kind of jeopardy: she has a rule (don't take control, don't use the bugs) and she just broke it, at least partway, in public. Two onlookers — the elder and the girl — *noticed*. The text suggests this matters ("filed me in a category"). But the consequence is implied, not shown. I infer she's now visible in a city where she meant to be invisible.

No immediate physical threat to her in this chapter.

## 3. CAUSALITY

Mostly traceable, but with a vague middle. Child can't breathe → crowd in the way → narrator parts crowd with bugs → narrator treats child → crowd disperses → two people noticed. That chain works.

What I couldn't tell:
- **How** she parts the crowd. "The insects propagated where I'd told them to go" and "the nearest dozen bodies yielded" — did flies physically push people? Did people flinch from a swarm? Did she control their minds? I don't know.
- **What** she actually does to the child after lifting her hands. The child is just suddenly "cleared from the lane." Did she heal her? Carry her? Hand her off? The treatment beat is missing.
- Why this particular emergency made her break her rule, vs. presumably other ones over three weeks. Not addressed.

## 4. PAYOFF

The ending is two strangers looking at her and her noticing they noticed. That is a turn — she came here to be invisible and is no longer invisible — and it's earned by the rule-break in the middle. So yes, technically the ending lands on a consequence.

But it lands quietly. There's no decision, no reaction, no next move. Just registration. For a chapter one of a novel, I expected a sharper hook — a threat, a choice, a closed door. I got a noted glance.

## 5. CONTINUE?

Yes, barely — the premise (a person from another world, hiding a power, in pre-Dance King's Landing) is interesting enough to buy a second chapter, but the prose style nearly cost it.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY

A nameless transmigrator hiding in Flea Bottom breaks her own rule to save a choking child with her swarm of flies, and two locals notice her for the first time.
