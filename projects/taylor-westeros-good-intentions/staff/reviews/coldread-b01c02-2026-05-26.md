# Cold-read report — b01-c02 (voice-exemplar-wired re-stitch)

## 1. EVENTS

What I can actually name:
- A narrator stands at a vantage point ("drain angle") in an alley and sends out insects (flies/beetles) to gather information about people and heat-signatures across a slum-area called "the Hook."
- She extends the range of her insect-feed past a self-imposed ceiling of four hundred bodies.
- Over repeated sweeps, the insects detect a woman who crosses the same "ward-junction" at the same hour every day.
- The woman walks down an alley toward where the narrator stands; the narrator steps aside ("yields the alley-mouth") to avoid being seen.
- The narrator files this woman in her mental "ledger" as a functional type ("connector-type") with no name.
- Late afternoon, she runs the full map: three fever-bodies, an empty corner, the woman's traffic-pattern.
- She exhales. End of chapter.

Long stretches contain no event I can name — much of this is interior bookkeeping in metaphorical prose.

## 2. JEOPARDY

Minimal and abstract. The narrator references having done something with a "stitch-maker's ward" in the past that cost her ("did not feed anyone for three weeks"). She has a self-imposed rule ("I read and did not direct") and the chapter suggests she is quietly breaking it by filing a person without consent. The stakes are moral/internal — she is becoming something she has resolved not to be. No physical danger. No antagonist on-page.

## 3. CAUSALITY

I largely could not follow it. Scenes don't connect by cause so much as by repetition of a vocabulary (drain angle, ledger, threshold, ward-junction). I cannot tell why she extends the range, why the woman matters specifically beyond being regular, or why she yields the alley-mouth at that moment. The prose substitutes ritual phrasing for causation.

## 4. PAYOFF

The intended payoff seems to be the line "the gesture of filing moved in a hand that knew the motion… the architecture was mine" — a recognition that she has crossed her own rule. It is *gestured at* rather than earned. Because the chapter never let me feel the rule as a living constraint (only as repeated assertion), the breach lands as another assertion. The final exhale is meant to be heavy; it reads as punctuation.

## 5. CONTINUE?

**No.** The prose is so opaque and self-referential that I cannot tell what world I am in, who the narrator is, what the insects are (magic? tech? metaphor?), or what she wants. I would put the book down.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY

A surveillance-capable narrator in a slum spends a day mapping foot-traffic with insects and files an unnamed woman into her private ledger, recognizing — without flinching — that she has begun doing the thing she swore she wouldn't.

## Confusions flagged plainly

- Don't know what the "insects" are (literal bugs she controls? a metaphor?).
- Don't know what "the Hook" is beyond a poor district.
- Don't know who the narrator is, what she did at the "stitch-maker's ward," or what the "line" actually forbids. The chapter assumes the reader has all of this.
