# Cold-read: b01-c05 (restitch3)

First-time reader, text-only.

## 1. EVENTS

- Two opening italic paragraphs tell me (rather than show) that the narrator has handed a "four-ward sheet" to a courier in a cooper's yard, that this protects a fourteen-year-old ward named Sera connected to Alicent's household and Otto, and that the narrator has agreed to this arrangement. No on-page action — pure recap.
- Narrator steps into a lane-cluster called the Rushwick, near the Red Keep servant-passages, and holds at a junction.
- A provisioner-train (four carts, men) passes through east.
- A lone message-runner crosses after them, exits east.
- A "courier" the narrator has flagged for three mornings enters at the same threshold.
- Three figures emerge from a side-alley, take the alley-mouth at full width, hold it.
- One of the three strikes the courier. He absorbs it silently, rises, finds his feet.
- The three leave east. The courier also leaves east, around a corner.
- The narrator files an "enforcement-incident" report mentally and adds the courier to a "body-map."
- Evening: narrator alone in a lodging room. Runs a "Hook-feed" review (clean) and a "rushwick-feed" review (does not settle — some "color" persists).
- Narrator's body reacts — hand comes off the boards — recognizing the routing "architecture" rather than the count itself.
- Narrator closes the day's filing and re-runs the rushwick flat-read twice. Both times it won't seat. Last line: "The courier-walk held the rushwick-pass."

Long stretches contain no physical event — just the narrator categorizing bodies via a "feed."

## 2. JEOPARDY

Faint. The courier gets beaten in a side-alley by three coordinated figures — that's real risk, but it's already over, happens to a stranger, and the narrator does nothing. Sera (named in the opener) is at some unspecified political risk, but I only know that because I was told, not shown. The narrator herself faces no on-page danger. The closing tension is internal: her "instrument" is refusing to stay neutral about something she's complicit in. Stakes-shaped, not stakes.

## 3. CAUSALITY

Scene-to-scene connection is loose. Why is the narrator in the Rushwick this morning specifically? "Coverage came up" — opaque. Why do the three figures attack the courier? Unknown. Who are they? Unknown. Is the narrator's "arrangement" with Otto the cause of the beating? Strongly implied but never stated. The evening scene follows the morning by simple time-order; the causal link is that her "feed" is now flagging the architecture she set up, but I had to work to assemble that.

I was confused throughout about what the "feed" actually is. Some kind of swarm-sense via insects ("Insect-bodies on lintels")? A surveillance ability? I think it's a power, but the chapter never says.

## 4. PAYOFF

The ending is the narrator's apparatus refusing to run clean — a recognition that she's implicated in what she just watched happen. As an internal beat that's earned by the morning's event. But it's abstract: "the courier-walk held the rushwick-pass" lands as a sentence I have to translate, not a turn I feel. No decision is made. Nothing changes. She closes the file.

## 5. CONTINUE?

No. The prose is dense to the point of obscurity, no character is named on-page except in the opening recap, the one concrete event (a beating) happens to a stranger and the narrator just files it, and I don't know what the protagonist wants or fears.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY

A surveillance-capable narrator watches a courier she's been tracking get beaten by three men in an alley, files a report, and that evening discovers her own monitoring system won't stop flagging the route she set up — because she's the reason it happened.

---

Confusion points: what the "feed" is mechanically; who the courier is to her; whether the three figures work for Otto or against him; why the Rushwick matters beyond "abuts the Red Keep."
