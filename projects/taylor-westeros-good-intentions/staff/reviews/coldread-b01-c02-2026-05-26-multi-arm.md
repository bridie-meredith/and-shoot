# Cold-read — b01-c02 — 2026-05-26 (multi-arm)

Reader: first-time, uninformed. One pass at reading pace.

## 1. EVENTS

I struggled to name events. What I can list:

1. Italicized prologue: narrator recalls a prior incident at a "stitch-maker's ward" where they raised their hands and afterward retreated to a "drain angle" and stopped feeding people for three weeks. They explain they now "read" via insects (flies, beetles) and keep a "coverage map" of the ward.
2. Narrator leaves the drain angle, sends insects out, takes up a position at a lane-mouth.
3. The insect feed hits a ceiling at four hundred bodies; narrator pushes the range anyway and pays a "dropout" cost.
4. Over "days," the insects map foot-traffic and return an unclassified body at a ward-junction.
5. A woman (the "ward-junction body") crosses the threshold at the expected hour; narrator yields the alley-mouth to her without looking up. The map classifies her as a "connector-type."
6. Narrator returns to the drain angle in the afternoon. Watches a shadow fill the gap.
7. Runs the map again; the ward-junction entry stalls the count. Narrator presses against the wall.
8. The accounting "closes." Narrator exhales and holds position into dusk.

Large stretches contain no nameable event — they are the narrator describing their mapping system in abstract terms. Sections 11–22 and 49–69 are essentially internal bookkeeping, not action.

## 2. JEOPARDY

Almost none that I could feel. The narrator gestures at risk — "the cost," "the bill," "the line I had drawn" — but I never learn what would happen if the line were crossed, who is watching, or what the consequence is. The three-week not-feeding suggests guilt or self-imposed penance for the opening incident, but no external threat is present on the page. The unnamed "connector" woman might be a danger or a target or nothing — I genuinely cannot tell. No jeopardy I can point to with confidence.

## 3. CAUSALITY

I could not follow it. Sentences chain by repetition and ritual phrasing ("the hands," "the accounting," "the count") rather than cause. Why does the narrator yield the alley? Because they "priced the yield days back" — but I was not shown the pricing or the reason. Why does the ward-junction entry stall the count? Unstated. Why does the narrator close against the wall at the end? Some emotional cost is implied but I cannot name what triggered it. The chapter is causal in the narrator's head and opaque on the page.

## 4. PAYOFF

The ending did not land for me. The narrator exhales, the accounting closes, dusk arrives — but I do not know what was decided, refused, or risked. The repeated "I did not give it the word" and "the cost did not appear in the closing" gesture at meaning withheld. As a first-time reader I read it as: nothing happened, but the narrator feels something happened. That is not earned; it is asserted.

## 5. CONTINUE?

No. I cannot tell what the story is about, who anyone is, or why I should care about insect coverage maps in a place called the Hook.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY

A narrator with insect-based surveillance sits in an alley for a day, watches a woman cross a threshold, and feels something about it.

## Bluntness note

I was confused for most of the chapter. The prose runs on a private vocabulary — "drain angle," "ward-junction," "the hands," "the accounting," "connector-type" — that is never grounded. I do not know what the narrator is, what insects she controls, what "the line" forbids, what the "stitch-maker's ward" incident was, or what "the Hook" is. Two paragraphs of italic preamble plus sixty lines of ritualized abstraction did not give me a scene I could see.
