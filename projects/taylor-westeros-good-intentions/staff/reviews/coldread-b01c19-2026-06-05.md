# Cold Read — b01-c19 (2026-06-05)

First-time reader, text only.

## 1. EVENTS
- Before dawn, narrator retrieves a single-sheet dead drop. It assigns a new task: read a court-tier target (a Red Keep chamberlain whose corridor patterns have run wrong for six weeks). Confirm role, map contact interval, name faction.
- Narrator runs "the accounting" (some mental/ledger surveillance system). It fits inside her existing node network.
- While running it, she recognizes the factional pattern as one she has produced before, in another place, for reasons that "did not hold."
- She marks the entry, hesitates with the stylus a beat, then files it unchanged anyway.
- Over four days she works the corridors and resolves the chamberlain as a routing node passing messages to a "Black-faction" contact in the harbor. She sends the compiled reading up the channel and closes the column.
- She brings the stylus to the *edge* of the column, not into it — a gesture about where her contempt can and can't go.
- Three days later she goes to meet Daven, a tallow contact of seven months, at the Tallow Croft. He doesn't show. A normally-open shutter is closed — a deliberate signal that he's cut her off because a "witch-label" has reached people who know her face.
- She walks past without breaking stride, returns home, drops his node from her coverage map.

Honestly, this is the whole chapter: read a memo, file a memo, get stood up. Long stretches (lines 7–15) contain no nameable event — they are one filing action narrated five times over.

## 2. JEOPARDY
Yes, but distant and abstract. A "witch-label" / name has been put to the narrator in the upper city and is "traveling" toward her. The Daven scene is the first concrete consequence — a contact pricing her as dangerous and dropping her. The risk is exposure/being hunted as a witch, inferred from the opening italics and the shutter logic. Nobody is in immediate physical danger on the page.

## 3. CAUSALITY
Scene-to-scene connection is weak-to-nonexistent. The two halves (the chamberlain filing; the Daven no-show) share a theme — her growing exposure — but the first does not *cause* the second. The chamberlain job could be deleted and the Daven beat would read identically. Within the first half I genuinely could not tell what changed: she recognizes the pattern, hesitates, and then files it unchanged, and the text insists at length that nothing about this mattered ("the reading having moved nothing"). I was confused about what the recognition *was* — what she saw, why it should have stopped her, and why it didn't. It's withheld entirely.

## 4. PAYOFF
Marginal. The Daven no-show is a real, earned turn — a relationship priced and severed, shown not told. That landed. The chamberlain half does not pay off; it deliberately resolves to "nothing happened, nothing changed," which is the opposite of a turn. The ending image (dropping his node, contempt complete, continuation unchanged) is consistent but airless — it earns a mood, not a consequence.

## 5. CONTINUE?
No — or barely. The prose is controlled and the Daven beat has a pulse, but the chapter is one woman filing paperwork and being stood up, narrated with relentless abstraction; I'd want a scene where something is actually at stake before committing.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
A surveillance-witch files a routine court report, then learns a contact has cut her off because the rumor that she's dangerous has finally reached the lanes she works.
