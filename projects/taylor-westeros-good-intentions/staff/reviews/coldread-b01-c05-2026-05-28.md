# Cold-read verdict — b01-c05 — 2026-05-28

First-time reader, single pass, text only.

## 1. EVENTS

In order, as I could make them out:

1. The narrator (italic preamble) recalls handing a "four-ward sheet" to a courier in a cooper's yard the previous day. They note the coverage now extends into a new ward near the Red Keep.
2. The narrator enters a lane-cluster called "the Rushwick," near the hill.
3. A four-body provisioner cart-train crosses a junction and exits east.
4. A message-runner double-steps across the junction and exits the far lane-mouth.
5. A recurring courier (flagged three mornings in a row, no name) enters from the same place the runner left.
6. The narrator stops at the wall.
7. Three figures come in from the east exit, turn into a side-alley, and pin the courier against the stone.
8. Sounds of effort/struggle from the alley. The narrator stops one beat. The text says "the courier found the feet" — I think this means the courier ended up on the ground or "got his feet" (unclear).
9. The narrator "files an enforcement-record" — i.e., writes it down — and delivers a report.
10. The three figures walk away unhurried.
11. The courier walks off on his original heading.
12. The narrator goes home, kneels at a door, runs an "evening review" replaying the day's feeds (Hook, Rushwick).
13. The narrator stops the replay, then runs the same replay twice more anyway. Ends with "shoulders did not reset."

The middle stretch (lines 33-55) contains almost no event — it is the narrator replaying observations they already had.

## 2. JEOPARDY

The courier is physically attacked in an alley by three men. That is the only concrete stake, and it is over in three lines. The narrator watches and does nothing — they "file the report." No risk to the narrator themselves is named. No future risk is named. There is a reference to "Sera" being protected by "court-tier" architecture, but I do not know who Sera is or what threatens her. So: low jeopardy, and the one violent moment is muffled by the prose to the point I had to re-read to confirm a beating happened.

## 3. CAUSALITY

I could not tell why the courier was attacked, who the three figures were, who the narrator works for, what the "four-ward sheet" is, why the narrator handing it over yesterday is connected to the beating today (it strongly implies they caused it, but the text never says), or why the narrator is so unmoved. The "evening review" at the end is mechanically caused (they got home and reviewed) but emotionally opaque — the "shoulders did not reset" line gestures at guilt without naming it.

## 4. PAYOFF

The ending is the narrator running the same mental replay twice and noting their shoulders are tense. That is not earned, because the chapter never let me feel what was at stake when the courier was beaten — the prose held me at the same clinical register for the violence and the bookkeeping. The intended payoff seems to be "the narrator's surveillance got someone hurt and she can't sit with it," but the chapter has to actually let the reader register the hurt for the tension to land. It did not.

## 5. CONTINUE?

No. The prose is so abstracted ("the feed filed," "the count walked forward," "the substrate was different") that I cannot find a person to follow, and the one concrete event was a beating I almost missed.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY

A surveillance-minded narrator walks a new district, watches three men beat a courier she'd been tracking, writes it up, goes home, and replays it.
