## Q1 — Voice/register
Verdict: PASS

The voice is unmistakably one narrator from beginning to end — first-person, present-tense in the italicized openings, past-tense in scene, a clipped accounting register that treats perception as ledger entries. The same idiom recurs across chapters: "held the feet" (line 19, 33, 230, 252), "the count" / "the feed," "priced," "the accounting closed." The voice deepens but does not break; even the warmer chapter 7 close ("I went out by the cross-lane mouth," line 426) sits inside the same head. The chapter-6 prose loosens slightly toward more conventional sentence rhythms ("She found me before her feet turned," line 361) but reads as the same narrator under different pressure rather than a register break.

## Q6 — Apparatus-register cumulative load (LOAD-BEARING)
Verdict: CAUTION

I did not put the book down, but I felt the strain. The ledger/count/feed/accounting vocabulary is the protagonist's defining instrument, and the prose's commitment to it is the book's argument — so the load is doing real work. It pays off at c07 when Halvard's "leaning on a crooked house" finally puts the apparatus in conversation with a non-apparatus voice. But there are stretches where the apparatus runs nearly unbroken for paragraphs — c02 lines 96-108 ("The insects returned the function-signature — the body that knotted the junction, the body the alleys gave onto, the body the map was about to name. The map assigned the connector-type") and c05 lines 332-348 (rushwick-pass / rushwick-feed / rushwick flat-read) — where I felt myself reading past sentences rather than into them. CAUTION rather than FAIL because c06 and c07 actively dramatize the cost of the register (the stylus-pause at line 373, the body's stillness at line 402), which gives a reader who's been carrying the load a place to set it down. A reader less invested in the conceit would FAIL here; I am marking it CAUTION because the payoff arrived before I quit.
