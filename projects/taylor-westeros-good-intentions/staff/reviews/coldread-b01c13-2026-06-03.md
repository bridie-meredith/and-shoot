# Cold-read: b01-c13 (2026-06-03)

First-time reader, text only.

## 1. EVENTS
- An italic frame tells us the narrator (Taylor) has been feeding insect-gathered intelligence through "Jarvis" to Otto Hightower's people, including a list of ward-elders she marked as Black-faction-adjacent.
- Via a blowfly, she watches a Green household-agent close out a tally with a salt-fish supplier on a loading platform, paring the margin to nothing. The supplier's young son loads crates. The account closes; the agent's shoulders drop. Nothing physically *happens* here beyond a bookkeeping transaction being observed — it's a read, not an event.
- Four days later, via the same fly, she watches a proceeding: Aldric, a ward-elder from her list, is tried for "receiving stolen cord." The magistrate writes the verdict before Aldric answers, sets the cord aside, and lifts a document — which the narrator recognizes as her own intelligence list. Aldric grips the table, uncomprehending.
- That evening she stops on a lane, has a realization (the word "contempt"), holds it privately, and walks on.
- Two days later she meets Halvard at a water-trough. He argues Aldric was misjudged from afar. She counters that her method keeps more people alive than his slower one. He says she'll never know what it weighs to be the name on the page. She leaves while he's still talking.

Large stretches (the tally scene, the lane scene) contain **no nameable physical event** — they are observation and interior reckoning.

## 2. JEOPARDY
Aldric is at clear risk: tried on a pretext, verdict pre-written, likely to be killed or imprisoned — Halvard says "I'll bury Aldric too, like as not." That's real and concrete. The narrator herself is at no visible risk in this chapter. The broader stakes (people being "spent" by the apparatus she feeds) are stated but abstract.

## 3. CAUSALITY
The causal spine is clear and the chapter's whole point: her list → Aldric's conviction. The tally scene connects only thematically, not causally — it's an analogue, not a cause, and on first read I wasn't sure why I was watching a salt-fish transaction for several paragraphs. The lane scene and the trough scene are reactions to the conviction, so they follow. No moment was causally confusing, but the first scene felt like it was waiting a long time to declare its relevance.

## 4. PAYOFF
Mixed. The chapter earns its central recognition — she sees her own flat sentences become the instrument that condemns a man — and that lands. But the *ending* is a deliberate non-turn: she hears Halvard's accusation, doesn't change, walks on, "neither of us stopped." That's thematically intentional (a finding, not a decision) but as a chapter close it's anticlimactic by design — nothing moves. The earned thing is internal and withheld; she files it and carries it. Honest, but flat as a payoff.

## 5. CONTINUE?
Yes, barely — the moral weight and the unresolved tension with Halvard pull me forward, but the deliberate stasis makes it a thin pull rather than a hook.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
A girl who feeds insect-spun intelligence to the powerful watches her own report condemn an innocent man, recognizes her contempt for the system she serves, and chooses to keep walking the same line anyway.
