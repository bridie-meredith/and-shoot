# Brief Expansion — Phase 2.1

## 1. Alternative Framings

1. **The prevention is prophylactic assassination.** Taylor identifies the Dance-igniter whose path she is accelerating and acts to remove them — the good intention is murder-as-mercy for ten thousand smallfolk; the retroactive bad is that the igniter was the only brake on a worse faction now uncontested.

2. **The prevention is intelligence architecture.** Taylor feeds Otto Hightower calibrated information to let the institution manage itself toward peace; the retroactive bad is that she has made him more effective at consolidating the throne against Rhaenyra — the rupture she prevented is replaced by a slower dynastic strangling.

3. **The prevention is ward-capture as hostage diplomacy.** Taylor removes the lyra-register child from a collision point to protect her; the child's absence breaks the one bond that would have made a key player negotiate in good faith — the prevention is the trigger.

4. **The prevention is shard-mediated paralysis.** Taylor uses Khepri-range (or precog-residue) to read the board and hold factions in stasis; the retroactive bad is that the stasis required her to suppress the one act of defiance that would have de-escalated — she kept the peace by removing the pressure valve.

5. **The prevention is narrative displacement — Taylor becomes the crisis.** She inserts herself as a controllable foreign variable the factions can fight over, buying them a common object instead of each other; the retroactive bad is that the common object eventually becomes the pretext for the war she delayed.

6. **The prevention is smallfolk-first triage.** Taylor's attached-smallfolk relationship pulls her toward mass-preservation decisions — evacuations, food stores, strategic retreats — that systematically weaken the Targaryen coalition she depends on for protection. The good intention is survival arithmetic; the bad act is structural betrayal of the false-ally's position.

## 2. Building Blocks

1. **Moral accounting as protagonist disease.** Cold-utilitarian Taylor tracks costs explicitly — which makes her wrong in a specific, auditable way. The text can reconstruct exactly where the ledger failed.

2. **Smallfolk-attachment as the test the theme names.** The true-ally relationship is the one item Taylor's accounting cannot absorb without breaking. The story's spine is how long she avoids doing the math on it.

3. **Intelligence-architect as the unmovable opponent.** Otto operates by the same systematic logic Taylor does, on a longer timeline, with more information about Westeros. He is not beatable by out-thinking — only by accepting a loss he has already priced in.

4. **Convergence: prevention-logic and ignition.** The opposite-number's path and Taylor's path share a causal structure. Tracing where they diverge and where they merge is the book's hidden dramatic line.

5. **The asymmetry between Worm-precog and Westerosi-prophecy.** Taylor's shard-residue (if any) reads probability fields, not fate. Westerosi prophecy is interpretive and wrong in specific ways. The collision of two broken oracle-systems is a mechanical source of irony.

6. **Lyra-cost shape.** The ward-child is the closing-image cost-bearer. The structural question is how many prior choices Taylor made that looked safe because the cost landed elsewhere — until the final trade names where the tab actually was.

7. **The patron-false-ally asymmetry problem.** Both Taylor and the patron (Rhaenys/Rhaenyra register) operate by systematic accounting. Friction requires that what each is willing to trade be genuinely different — and that the story show the gap before it uses it.

8. **Worm-residue visibility as tonal dial.** How legible Taylor's parahuman nature is to Westerosi characters determines the flavor of their fear — superstition vs. calculation vs. awe. Dark-fantasy-reader requires the wrong kind of shock to be absent: not curiosity, superstition.

9. **The naive-idealist foil as calibration instrument.** Survival or death of the foil is not twist — it is the meter reading. It tells the reader how much the world has cost Taylor without requiring Taylor to say so.

10. **Retroactive revelation as structural demand.** The end-requirement is not a surprise; it is a proof. The book must be readable backward from the revelation to every prior choice.

11. **King's Landing as hostile terrain, not backdrop.** Dark-fantasy-reader flags a world that exists only for the protagonist. King's Landing pre-Dance has its own momentum — the Blacks/Greens fault line is already forming; the Dragonpit is already a political object. Taylor enters a place that does not need her.

12. **Single-book format as constraint on cost-chain length.** One book means the full road-to-hell arc must be legible without multi-book setup. Every beat must do double work: advance the prevention-plot and lay a stone in the retroactive trail.
