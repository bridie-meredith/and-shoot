I was closing the stall when I saw Marik cross the square. He was crossing it the way he crossed it when he did not want to be seen, which meant he did not know I was looking, which meant he was coming for Asha and not for me.

I did not stop closing. The tarp goes over the boards before the wind picks up at dusk or you spend the next morning prying the boards loose from where the wind has flipped them. I had three boards left when he reached the church corner and turned out of the square.

It was not a thing I wanted to know. It was not a thing I could have unknown.

Asha would be home by now. She would have set the bread to soak and she would be in the back with the lamp turned low and the door open onto the yard. If Marik went to her tonight she would let him in. She always had. That was not a new thing about Asha and it was not a new thing about Marik. The new thing was that I had seen him from across the square and known what the seeing meant before I knew it.

I finished the tarp. I tied the cords. I went the long way home.
