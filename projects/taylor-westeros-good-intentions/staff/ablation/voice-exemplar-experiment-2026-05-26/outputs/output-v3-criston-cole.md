*The crowd had broken open around the stitch-maker's ward, and the hands had gone up before mine did. After that I went back to the drain angle and did not feed anyone for three weeks. I held the count at its edge by the cost of holding it. What I did instead was start to listen — at first because subsistence-reading is what survives in the Hook, and then because the listening had a shape I had begun to extend on purpose. The rule was the same rule. Reading was not directing. I had not crossed the line.*

What I had built I did not yet have a name for. The flies and beetles told me where heat clustered and where foot-traffic knotted. I held a count of bodies in my head — who was sick, who was hungry, which alley junction emptied before dark and which one never did. I called it a coverage map when I was being plain with myself; I called it an accounting when I was not. The ledger was where it closed at end of day. The rule was that I read and did not direct. That was the line. The instrument was harm-reduction. That was how it stayed permitted.

The flies returned the heat-signatures. The beetles returned the threshold-count. Three signatures across two alleys — the fever-cluster, the day's unresolved load. They would be priced before sundown or not at all. She left the drain angle.

From the drain angle the forty paces of lane were visible before anything arrived. She moved to the alley-mouth and held the line-of-sight both ways: into the lane, out to the foot-traffic. The insects crossed the threshold-stones. The insects held four hundred bodies in the feed. That was the ceiling. The alley-back went silent before her head turned. The dropout was the receipt — the range she had not yet committed to take had already billed her for it.

She extended the range.

The suppression-cost rose with the range. The four-hundred ceiling had just been paid for in the back of her skull. She noted that and did not retract. The insects filled the Hook — the alley-grid past the lane-mouth, the ward beyond the lane's forty paces. She drew the line there. The foot-traffic knotted the ward-junction — a corner in the Hook where three or four alleys gave onto one another under a single precinct-ward's reach.

The prohibition was the same prohibition she had set herself. The line held at the ceiling and not past it. The line drawn here was the line that would be asked of her again.

She noted that. She did not draw it twice.
