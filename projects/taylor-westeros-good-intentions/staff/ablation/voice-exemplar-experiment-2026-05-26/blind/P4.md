*The crowd had broken open around the stitch-maker's ward, and the hands had gone up before mine did. After that I went back to the drain angle and did not feed anyone for three weeks. I held the count at its edge by the cost of holding it. What I did instead was start to listen — at first because subsistence-reading is what survives in the Hook, and then because the listening had a shape I had begun to extend on purpose. The rule was the same rule. Reading was not directing. I had not crossed the line.*

What I had built I did not yet have a name for. The flies and beetles told me where heat clustered and where foot-traffic knotted. I held a count of bodies in my head — who was sick, who was hungry, which alley junction emptied before dark and which one never did. I called it a coverage map when I was being plain with myself; I called it an accounting when I was not. The ledger was where it closed at end of day. The rule was that I read and did not direct. That was the line. The instrument was harm-reduction. That was how it stayed permitted.

The flies went first each morning, returning the heat-signatures, and the beetles followed with the threshold-count, which is how I knew where bodies had crossed stone since before I arrived. I left the drain angle — the angle-gap where the two tenement walls pinch and every approach along the forty paces of lane arrives before it arrives — and let the count build. The fever-cluster was out there already, three signatures across two alleys running warmer than the ambient, which was the day's unresolved load, the thing that would be priced before sundown or not at all. The insects fanned the alleys. The ward delivered.

I took the alley-mouth with the insects already crossing the threshold-stones ahead of me, and the feed came up through the lane-mouth line-of-sight both ways: into the lane behind and out to the alley-traffic, which is the geometry that makes the alley-mouth worth holding. Four hundred bodies. The insects held four hundred bodies in the feed, which was the ceiling, and I knew it was the ceiling the way you know a thing you have decided in advance — and then the alley-back went silent. Dropped from the feed. The silence came before I had turned my head toward the question of the range, which I understood afterward to mean the dropout was not an absence but a receipt: the range I had not yet committed to take had already been billed against the back of the skull.

I extended the range anyway. The suppression-cost rose with it. The four-hundred ceiling had just been paid for and I drew the line at the ceiling and not past it, which was the same prohibition I had set myself at the alley-mouth three weeks prior, which was the line that had held every time I had held it, which I held now. The insects filled the Hook. The foot-traffic knotted at the ward-junction — a corner in the Hook where three or four alleys gave onto one another under a single precinct-ward's reach — and the Hook-wide coverage was live, the map running past the lane-precinct into the ward beyond, the feed reading the alley-grid as a single surface for the first time.
