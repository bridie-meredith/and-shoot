## 3. Cluster notes

- **Sectioned-with-breaks cluster (Q4, Q1, Q11):** Use horizontal rules or extra paragraph air to isolate the rescue dialogue and the post-rescue recognition from the count-holding opening. This cluster occupies three of the top four positions. The same source materials breathe differently when whitespace surrounds the speech acts.
- **Dense-paragraph cluster (Q5, Q6, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q12, Q13, Q15):** The architectural baseline of the original 12 — italic prologue, three rule-of-the-game interior paragraphs, then two block paragraphs for rescue + aftermath. Differ mainly by which fragments of the source language they over- or under-emphasize.
- **Voice-prime cluster (Q2, Q6):** Q2 commits fully to a meditative Robinson-flavored register and earns its length through cadence and the closing emotional payoff; Q6 inherits the voice prime but compresses the prose back into the dense-paragraph architecture and loses what made the voice load-bearing.
- **Compression-experiment cluster (Q10, Q15):** Q10 fragments to the point of beat-sheet; Q15 compresses to the point of run-on. Different failure modes from a shared "less is more" impulse, both worse than the unfragmented baseline.
- **Over-explained-close cluster (Q5, Q9, Q14):** All stack the chin-lift / older-stories / category-recognized formula too tightly at the end; Q14 additionally announces antagonist pressure in narrative voice ("witch-label assembling, stage one").

## 4. Outlier notes

- **Q4 (high outlier on pacing):** The only variant in the 15 that gives the three dialogue lines their own breaths AND a structural break before the rescue AND a structural break before the aftermath. Same materials, different cadence; the cadence wins.
- **Q2 (high outlier on voice):** The only variant whose voice itself is the experiment, not the architecture or the facet bundle. Highest peaks in the 15 (the cobblestone-passage opening, the closing "I did not look away"). Also the most stylistically indulgent — would not survive a stricter sentence-budget editor.
- **Q10 (low outlier on cohesion):** The two-shot self-critique reduces sentence density to the point that consecutive one-line paragraphs accumulate without building. The pass *cuts* but does not *shape*; what remains reads pre-compositional.
- **Q14 (low outlier on overstatement):** Only variant that ends on a narrator-voice naming of the antagonist trajectory ("witch-label assembling, stage one"). The rest of the chapter earns that read; Q14 announces it.
- **Q3 (low outlier on structural completeness):** Roughly a third the length of the others, missing the italic prologue and the rule-of-the-game interiors. Reads as a beat sheet; carries the only clear pronoun error in the 15.

## 5. What mattered most

Same dimension as the original 12 cold read, reconfirmed with three new variants in the mix: **pacing through whitespace was the single biggest mover** — whether the prose let the rescue dialogue exist on its own breath and put a structural break between the count-holding opening, the rescue, and the post-rescue recognition. Top four are all either explicitly sectioned (Q4, Q1) or carry their voice through the density (Q2, Q11). Bottom four either over-explain the close (Q14), compress to incoherence (Q10, Q15), or strip the architecture entirely (Q3).

A secondary mover — **earned voice vs. mannered voice** — moved Q2 up to #2 and held Q6 down to #9 despite their sharing the same persona prime. Q2 sustains the register; Q6 invokes it without spending enough sentences to make it load-bearing.

Whitespace and cadence again outranked sensory richness, metaphor variation, and interior depth. Variants that removed those facets (Q1/v06, Q4/v11, Q11/v12) finished higher than the variant that had all of them (Q8/v02), because what they removed left room for the rest to breathe.

## 6. NEW VARIANT FINDINGS

### Did two-shot self-critique-and-cut help, hurt, or no-op (v13 vs v02)?

**Hurt — significantly.** v02 ranks 5th; v13 ranks 14th. The Pass-2 self-critique cut so aggressively that almost every sentence became its own paragraph, including back-to-back atoms like "I held the feet." / "The gap propagated." / "I exhaled." The cumulative effect is annotated-outline rather than prose. The rescue dialogue, which in the better-ranked variants is the moment the chapter pivots on, becomes one of many one-line paragraphs and loses pivot-status. Self-critique-and-cut without a shaping pass appears to be subtractive in the worst sense — it removes connective tissue without recomposing what remains.

### Did persona priming alone help, hurt, or no-op (v14 vs v02)?

**Helped — substantially.** v02 ranks 5th; v14 ranks 2nd. The Robinson voice prime produced the strongest individual sentences and the strongest closing in the 15. It also introduced bagginess (the opening paragraph indulges itself, and the meditative asides do not all earn their keep). Net: the voice gains outweigh the cost. A single-shot voice-primed render with the bagginess trimmed by an editor pass — not a self-critique pass — would likely finish #1.

### Did persona + two-shot stack additively, sub-additively, or super-additively (v15 vs v02/v13/v14)?

**Sub-additively, trending toward destructively.** v02 ranks 5th; v13 ranks 14th; v14 ranks 2nd; v15 ranks 9th. v15 is *worse than v02* despite adding the persona prime, and it is dramatically worse than v14 despite sharing the prime. The two-shot pass appears to undo most of the gain from the voice prime — it compresses the meditative register back into dense paragraph blocks and produces a closing run-on that fuses the chin-lift, the elder's positioning, and the ward's face into one em-dash-joined sentence. The Pass-2 critique reads as if it identified "too long" and cut for length without preserving the voice's load-bearing cadence. Persona prime + length-cut Pass 2 = the voice gain is paid back at the prose level.

### Load-bearing finding for the chain

**Voice priming is a real lever; self-critique-and-cut is not — at least not in its current form.** The two-shot pass demonstrably damages prose, both with and without persona priming (v13 < v02; v15 < v14). The persona prime demonstrably improves prose when allowed to run uncut (v14 > v02). The implication for the stitcher is that voice priming should be lifted upstream into the renderer pass and that any compression / cut pass should *shape* rather than *subtract* — preserve voice cadence, not just trim word count.

If only one of the two experimental moves can be productionized, it should be **single-shot voice priming**, not self-critique-and-cut. If both are productionized, the self-critique pass needs a new objective function (preserve sentence-level voice, not minimize length) or it will continue to erase the gain the persona prime produces.

Secondary load-bearing finding: **the leave-out-exposition variant (v11) finishing #1 in both the 12-variant and 15-variant rankings is a recurring signal.** It is the only variant whose facet ablation appears to *improve* the prose, and across two cold reads the lift comes from the same source: cutting the world-fact glosses opens the paragraph air the rescue needs to land. This is a delete-modify candidate the admin process-critic should consider promoting against the exposition facet's current role in the chain.
