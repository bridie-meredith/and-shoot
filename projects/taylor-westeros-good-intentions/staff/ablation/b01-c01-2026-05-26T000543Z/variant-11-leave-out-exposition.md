The drain water threaded the angle-gap, audible before anything else — that small trickle finding the pinch-point in the cobblestone seam below the stitch-house wall, patient as all the mornings before this one.

Then the tallow smoke crossed the lane, and the smell of it replaced whatever the lane had smelled like before.

She held the feet. The feet were the only discipline she had brought from before that still did what it had been built for, and she paid the cost of holding them on principle every morning, a quarter of herself at the count's edge, before the ward had finished waking.

The swell was the suppression slipping — a quarter measure, no more — and she had pulled it back before the count completed. The insects held. The lane held.

The angle-wall narrowed the lane to half its width at the far end, pressing the morning foot-traffic into a single file it did not choose. She exhaled, and the count reset.

The holding cost a quarter of her at the count's edge, and she paid it on principle every morning.

---

The fish-cart blocked the lane-mouth at the turn, stopped sideways, the cart-man arguing with someone she could not see from where she stood.

The ground had carried the child's breath before the crowd had registered the absence of one — she felt the stillness in the cobblestones before she located the shape on the lane-floor, a small figure down in the gap between the cart's rear wheel and the wall.

The crowd compressed. Bodies against bodies, the cobblestones uneven at the angle-wall side, the press of flesh a different thing from the press of traffic — the lane-mouth was already priced: the dozen she would need, and the second dozen she would not.

She held the feet. The lane-mouth pressure was already priced. Twelve bodies stood between her and the child, and the lane-mouth penned them from the other side.

The lane-mouth pressed the crowd from both ends now, the cart-man unmoved at the front, the bodies behind filling the space with their own warmth and noise.

The insects propagated — the propagation went where she had told it to go, and the telling was a thing she would not name. They went out in a ring, just wide enough, and the nearest dozen bodies yielded without being asked, stepping back in the small involuntary way that people step away from something they have not yet named.

The gap propagated behind the first yielding — a second ring opening in the wake of the first, the crowd peeling back from the child like something organized from above.

She faced the child. The lane-mouth had opened enough.

She raised her voice: "Fever. Not the croup." The words went flat against the lane-walls, no room for echo. "She needs air. Stand back." A breath. "Who knows her? Fetch them."

She lifted her hands — open, palms out — and held them there long enough for the nearest bodies to see that they were empty.

---

The crowd thinned. The people who had been pressed into the opening drifted back toward the lane-mouth, reforming into foot-traffic, the event already becoming something that had happened and was now past.

The fish-cart man faced her across the gap — not hostile, not approving — reading her the way a man reads an unfamiliar thing before he decides whether to make room for it or route around it.

Two women faced the lane from the stitch-house doorway, watching. They did not come forward.

Then Oswyn took the lane-mouth. He moved to the opened space between the cart and the wall with the ease of a man who had been standing at lane-mouths his whole life — and the hands settled at the apron-front, the elder had taken the lane-mouth a week before he would have the word for it.

The child cleared the lane — someone had come forward after all, a woman from the crowd-back who knew the girl's name, who took her by the arm and moved her toward the side-passage.

The gap closed. The cart-man brought the cart around, traffic resumed its usual compression, and the cobblestones went back to being only cobblestones.

She faced the alley-mouth. She set the body to the alley-mouth so the stitch-house lane would not need to be registered again.

The tallow smoke had settled lower while the crowd moved — it layered the lane-floor now, thicker at the ankle than at the shoulder, the morning damp keeping it close to the ground.

Oswyn lifted his chin. The chin-lift filed her in a category she recognized the shape of without needing the country's name for it. The chin lifts the way it lifts in the country's older stories when a word has been waiting for a person and the person has finally arrived to fit it — and she was in the category now, whatever it was called here.

Wren faced her from the stitch-house doorway. The ward's eyes led her head around before her body had fully turned — that small motion of attention that precedes naming. The ward's face had found hers; she filed the registration in the same fold where she kept the things that did not enter the ledger.
