Drain water threaded the angle-gap between the houses, moving the way water always does in Flea Bottom — finding the lowest line and following it without ceremony. Tallow smoke crossed Stitch-House Lane, low and greasy, clinging to the level of a child's head. Taylor held her feet where they were. The insects swelled. The angle-wall at the far end narrowed the lane to the width of two people passing sideways, and what space remained was already contested. She exhaled.

A fish-cart had blocked the lane at the lower end, its handler not moving. Through her feet Taylor felt the child's breath — short, shallow, the particular labor of a chest working too hard against bad air. The crowd compressed into the bottleneck the lane-mouth made, and the insects propagated through it. Taylor held her feet. The lane-mouth pressed the crowd from both ends now, the fish-cart at the bottom and the angle-wall above, and there was no give in either. The insects moved faster. The nearest dozen bodies yielded a half-step each, and the gap opened — not by decision but by the cumulative arithmetic of twelve small retreats. The gap propagated outward from where it started. Taylor turned and faced the child.

She raised her voice. "Fever. Not the croup." The crowd held still, which was what she needed. "She needs air. Stand back." A beat, and then: "Who knows her? Fetch them." She lifted her hands — not a gesture of threat, just the oldest shape a body makes when it means to be believed.

The crowd thinned. The fish-cart man turned to face Taylor across the diminished press of bodies, watching her the way men watch a thing they haven't decided how to name yet. Two women stood at the edge of the lane and faced it — not leaving, not helping, just present. Oswyn Mudway moved to the lane-mouth and held it, one old woman's body occupying the threshold where the crowd wanted to reform. The child cleared the lane. The gap closed behind her. Taylor turned and faced the alley-mouth at the top of the lane, where the tallow smoke had settled into a layer just above the floor-stones, barely ankle-high. Behind her, Oswyn lifted her chin. Wren, the stitch-maker's ward, turned and faced Taylor.
