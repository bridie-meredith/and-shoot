*I had one day. The proposal sat on the night-side of the count the way an unpaid debt sits at the edge of a ledger. I did not decide twice. I decided in the hour the dark thinned and the flies came back inside the count, and what I decided was that the harm I could prevent was the only column the accounting would close. The terms I had named for myself I would name to the courier at first bell, in the yard, in the same place he had named the question. The rule I had not stated in those words now had a price tag and a destination both.*

---
The tallow-damp off the lane-caulking reached me before the yard did, which is the way of that yard in the third-bell quiet, when the smallfolk-hours murmur of the cooper's workers at the near shed had not yet broken into the daylight register and the predawn held everything at the same low pressure, and I walked into the cooper's yard and took the shed-wall at my back, putting timber behind me and the open ground of the yard between me and the lane-mouth across.

The feed had him a span before the lane-mouth did. The gait that came in through the count was the gait already mapped, settled in its weight and step-interval, and a moment after the feed returned him to me the lane-mouth gave him over to my eye also, and Jarvis Coin entered the lane-mouth.

I said, "Yes. The terms hold with two changes. You will have patterns — what moves through the streets, what does not, where the seams sit — not raw report of who said what to whom. The interval is mine to set. The volume is mine to set."

The yes went across the yard-air in the cadence of a settled count, and the modification carried only the parameter; the prohibition still held. My hand closed on the shed-wall at my back.

I held my feet.

He said, "The man I serve takes those terms. The volume yours. The interval yours. The substance pattern-reports from the wards you walk. Same place. First bell. Three days."

The cadence carried receipt without the conditional that would have left the yes open, and the receipt-form settled before my body had finished holding.

The feed held the hook-range around him.

Jarvis Coin exited the lane-mouth.

I exited the cooper's yard.

By the time the sun had cleared the rooflines and the morning-trade had moved through its first slack hour, the discard-air had begun to draw down toward the junction where the three alleys met, and what arrived in the nose was not the tallow-damp I had walked into at predawn but the heavier compound that came off middens at the hour when the carters had been working long enough for the load to begin to tell on itself.

I entered Pig Tallow Lane — the first Flea Bottom ward beyond the Hook, named for the rendering trade no other would carry, three alleys feeding a waste-middens junction at its mouth — and the lane took me in.

The range went out one ward, and the load that came back through the count was a load I had priced already on the walk down, so what arrived in me when the range came back full was not the strain of a new cost but the settling-in of a cost I had agreed to before agreeing to anything else, and the feed widened around me into the geometry of three alleys and a junction-mouth without the part of me that watched the cost saying anything about it.

I mapped the junction-agitation.

The penny-a-barrel carter parked the middens cart at the lane-mouth, and the feed registered the wheel-stop and the slacking of the harness as a single small event the count took in and held.

The insect-feed returned a body the routing had been carrying as a hollow before it returned as a name, and Oswyn of Mudway entered the feed at junction-range, and the thing that began to surface in me about what an elder at that junction at that hour meant for the shape of the coverage I had not yet finished pricing did not finish, because I would not let it.

I mapped the interval. He moved through the junction at the carter's pace.

Pig Tallow Lane returned ward-tier bodies only — no Hook-traffic carried over, no merchant-coats, no Watch.

The stitch-house frames marked the second ward at the lane's far end, the wooden uprights set against the wall in the manner of the trade.

The insect-feed returned Wren of the stitch-house at second-ward range, and the feed held her there, and the hand that had been keeping the ledger of who cost what to carry did not write her in, and the not-writing was the same hand and the same motion as the writing, and what passed through me at second-ward range was the steady recognition that she sat outside the ledger by the same discipline that had built the ledger, and the recognition arrived in the body before it arrived anywhere it could be named.

I held my feet. I did not take the stitch-house lane.

I mapped the second-ward junction.

What the early-morning grey did to Roper's Court was empty it — not in the way the night emptied the lane behind the cooper's yard, which was an emptiness held against the lamp-fall and the carter-work that started before the first bell, but the proper emptying of a craft-court at the hour before its trade had begun to run, when the central court sat sight-clear to the mouths of its tributary lanes and the carter-work of the surrounding wards arrived as a low ambient pressure from beyond the rooflines rather than as anything moving in the court itself.

I entered Roper's Court — a Flea Bottom craft-trade ward turning on rope-making, a central court with tributary lanes radiating off long and linear enough to serve as rope-walks — and the court took me in.

The range went out a fourth ward, and the feed came back full, and the load that arrived in me when the fourth ward opened was a load that had gone past the load I had carried before, and the part of me that watched the cost watched the cost cross the threshold of every prior carrying and did not speak.

I ran the four-ward feed.

The cooper's yard at first bell held the same low pressure the court had held at predawn, and the shed-wall took my back as it had taken it the day before, and a moment after I had taken the wall the lane-mouth gave Jarvis Coin over to the yard-air with a half-yard of open ground between the lane-mouth and the shed.

He took the note from inside his coat and held it where the yard-light could fall on it, the seal turned to face me, the seal-mark a small fixed point in the yard between his hand and my eye, and the note stayed in his hand and the seal stayed in my eye long enough for the receipt to be a thing he had given and I had taken.

I delivered the report-sheet.

Folded twice along the same crease, the sheet went across the half-yard of yard-air between my hand and his coat, and the half-step that closed around the sheet's last contact with my hand closed before the part of me that watched the cost had registered the cost crossing, so that what arrived in me when the sheet left was not the strain of the cost but the after-image of a cost already paid by a body that had paid it before the watching had caught up.

Jarvis Coin pocketed the report-sheet.

What the sheet carried in its two folds was the four-ward map at the level the terms had set — which lanes ran heavy in the third hour and which sat slack, where the seams between the wards thinned to a single body's passage and where they widened to admit a cart, the count of bodies that took the junction at the carter's pace and the count that took it at the merchant's, and nothing about a name and nothing about a word said in any doorway in any of those wards.

What had filled the sheet had filled it at the source-tier the terms had set also — what a ward-walker at ward-level could see and price and carry out without the carrying showing on the carrier, which in Flea Bottom meant the rendering trade and the stitch-trade and the rope-trade and the middens and the foot-traffic that moved between them, and nothing from the Hook above and nothing from the merchant-coats that passed through the Hook on their way to anywhere else.

The arrangement that had set the terms held its shape across the handoff also — Jarvis the conduit from the wards up the line to whatever sat at the line's far end, the wards the source he drew from through me, and me the ward-walker who supplied the conduit without standing anywhere along the line above the supply.

Jarvis Coin exited the cooper's yard.

The sheet had crossed into a channel the feed could not reach into, and the operation that had been a held shape across the prior day and the predawn and the first-bell had cycled now for the first time at scale, and the registering of the cycling arrived in me not as relief and not as cost but as the steady recognition that the shape was a shape that would hold the next cycle and the cycle after that.

I ran the ward-feed.

The morning had moved into the trade-hours by the time I reached the second ward at the lane's far end, and the tallow-lamps were still on against the stitch-house frames the way they had been the day before, and the insect-feed returned Wren of the stitch-house at second-ward range, and what passed through me when the feed returned her was the same reading that had passed through me at second-ward range the day before and the same not-writing that had followed the reading, and the reading and the not-writing arrived as a single motion that had begun to run through the body in the same channel the four-ward feed ran through, the architecture of the one cycling through the architecture of the other without the part of me that named things needing to name what was cycling.

I exited the stitch-house lane.

My stride held the four-count past the stitch-house frame, and the north-end lane-mouth opened ahead into the alley that would carry me back into the Hook, and the frame went past at the count's pace, and the lane-mouth took me through.
