# Pig Tallow Lane and Courts — Waste-Processing Ward, Adjacent to the Hook

## Geography

A ward of Flea Bottom immediately adjacent to the Hook, one lane-network over from the Hook's curved waterfront alleys. Pig Tallow Lane is the spine of the ward — a longer-than-average lane for Flea Bottom, running roughly north-south, with three connecting alleys branching off it at irregular intervals and converging at the ward's mouth in a waste-middens junction.

The name defines the trade: pig-tallow rendering, which requires the rendering of pig fat into tallow for chandler-feed. This is labor that other wards don't want — the smell of hot rendering fat carries on still air for considerable distance, and the lard-press and cauldron work produces both grease residue and a persistent low-grade smoke. The ward occupies this function because it is downwind of the Hook (relative to the prevailing southwest off the Blackwater Rush) and because the Hook's population is not powerful enough to displace it further.

The waste-middens junction at the lane-mouth is the ward's defining geographic fact: three connecting alleys converge here, and it is the collection point for what other wards in Flea Bottom discard. Carter work happens here — penny-a-barrel labor shifting the accumulated middens waste to the Mud Gate approaches and eventually to the river. The junction is never clean and never quiet during working hours.

## Layout

**Pig Tallow Lane (spine):** The main lane is wider than a typical Flea Bottom alley — wide enough for a dray-cart to pass, which is necessary for the rendering trade's supply chain (live pigs in, barreled tallow out). Cobbled on the main stretch; the secondary alleys are packed earth.

**Three connecting alleys:** Branch off the lane at roughly equal intervals. The first, closest to the middens junction, is primarily foot-traffic — residents and workers shortcutting between the lane and the adjacent ward network. The second is where the rendering work happens — cauldron stations, lard-presses, the equipment that produces the ward's characteristic smell. The third alley, at the far end of the lane, is quieter — smaller tenements, residents rather than workers.

**Courts:** Small open spaces along the lane where the buildings step back from the street line. The Pig Tallow Lane courts are irregularly shaped, used for barrel storage, middens accumulation, and the carter staging area. The middens court (at the lane's junction-end) is the largest and the most active.

**Waste-middens junction (lane-mouth):** Where the three connecting alleys converge and meet the main lane at its southern end, before it opens into the broader Flea Bottom alley network toward the Hook. This is the collection point: barrels, handcarts, and the penny-a-barrel carters who move them. Mid-day is the peak for carter work; the junction is navigable but crowded. Pre-dawn and post-dusk it is quieter but not empty.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** The ward's dominant sensory characteristic. Multiple layers: pig-tallow rendering produces hot fat smell during cauldron work (morning through midday); cooling tallow from overnight batches produces a flatter, greasier note at dawn; the waste-middens contribute a compound organic smell that the ward has become the designated carrier for. The air here carries "the particular weight of a ward that processes what other wards discard" (b01c04 s02 chunk). It is not an overwhelming smell to people habituated to it; to someone entering from outside Flea Bottom, it is the first thing that stops them.

**Sound:** Carter work is noise: the clatter of barrel-rolling on cobbles, the grunt of heavy lifting, the mule-and-cart sounds of the dray runs. The rendering cauldrons produce a wet boiling sound during work. The lane has more horizontal communication than a quieter ward — carter-work requires coordination, and the ward's workers communicate across the junction at raised-voice volume. At night: considerably quieter. What remains is the same nighttime Flea Bottom ambient as the Hook, but with the residual fat-smell thicker.

**Light:** The lane is slightly better lit than the Hook's more contorted alleys — the lane's relative width allows more sky, more midday sun on the cobbles. The secondary alleys are as dark as anywhere in Flea Bottom. The middens court is open enough for adequate light during the day.

**Texture:** Grease residue on the cobbles of the main lane near the rendering alley — the ground near the cauldron stations is permanently coated, and foot-traffic distributes it. The middens court floor is compressed organic matter layered over old cobble. A person moving through the ward accumulates a faint tallow-grease smell on their boots and lower clothing from the lane surface.

None card-level. The rendering equipment (cauldrons, lard-presses, barrel stacks, handcarts) is ward property and runtime state.

## Exits

- **South to Hook alley network** — the lane-mouth opens toward the Hook and its connecting alleys.
- **North along the spine lane** — continues further into Flea Bottom, connecting eventually to the interior ward network.
- **Secondary alley exits** — each connecting alley has its own mouth opening to other Flea Bottom lanes.
- **Stitch-house ward connection** — via the third connecting alley, running east from the far end of the spine lane.

## Hazards

- **Tallow-grease underfoot** — the lane near the rendering work is a slip hazard in wet weather.
- **Middens accumulation** — the waste-middens junction's organic matter accumulates disease substrate; higher-than-average baseline illness in this ward.
- **Carter congestion** — during peak carter hours (midday), the junction becomes difficult to navigate quickly.
- **Rendering cauldron proximity** — open-flame heat sources in the second connecting alley; smoke carries into the alley even in mild wind.
- **Watch-avoidant labor** — the carters and some rendering workers notice strangers more attentively than the Hook's fluid population does.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A carter blocking the middens junction mid-turn with a badly loaded dray — foot traffic forced around the obstruction
- Cauldron work running late — the ward's active-rendering smell extends into early evening, unexpected
- A rendering accident — spilled hot tallow, a worker burned, the ward's activity momentarily reorganizing
- The Watch making a pass through the spine lane — rare enough to cause visible behavior change in the carters
- Oswyn Mudway's carter-run taking him through the middens junction on a route that overlaps with a character's path
- A sick child left in one of the courts while a parent does carter work — the same fever-and-overcrowding dynamic that brought Taylor's triage to the Hook, now visible in the adjacent ward
