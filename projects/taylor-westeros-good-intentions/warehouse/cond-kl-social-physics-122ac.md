# KL Social Physics — Currency, Class, and Street-Level Authority (122 AC)

## Description

The operative social and economic physics of King's Landing at 122 AC as they apply to Taylor's operational context. Currency, class stratification, authority by class, and the specific texture of how each class is treated and treats others in the KL urban environment. This card is the KL-specific version of the existing Riverlands-calibrated social physics cards — same feudal structure, different urban density and different authority profile.

## Sensory Impact

Social physics manifest in the body: the threshold-pause when someone of higher class enters a room, the speed at which street-vendors clear their stalls during a Watch sweep, the specific quality of invisibility a smallfolk person achieves when they lower their chin and move at consistent pace through a street. Taylor's parahuman background means she reads these social signals with analytical clarity but lacks the embodied automation that makes deference instinctive for people raised in this world.

## Duration

Persistent throughout the project. The class structure does not change within the book's scope.

## Currency

**Gold dragon:** Standard noble-and-above currency unit. A gold dragon is a significant sum for smallfolk — approximately a laborer's monthly wage, or a craftsperson's week. Taylor does not handle gold dragons in act one. Their presence in a scene signals that a transaction is at noble-layer scale.

**Silver stag:** Mid-tier currency. A silver stag is several days' labor for a skilled smallfolk worker. Landed knights and prosperous merchants count in stags. Taylor may handle stags once she has an established position or patron relationship.

**Copper penny:** The smallfolk unit. Most Flea Bottom transactions are in pennies or in kind. A penny buys a small meal or a night's rough shelter. Taylor's initial economic position is copper-penny level.

**Copper star:** A fraction of a penny. The smallest unit. A star is a crumb, a cup of water, a small courtesy payment. Flea Bottom's most marginal residents count in stars.

**Currency as social indicator:** What currency a character handles instantly signals their social position to any Westerosi observer. A smallfolk person producing a gold dragon is an immediate anomaly requiring explanation. A well-dressed merchant producing coppers suggests disguise or deliberate self-humbling.

## Class Stratification

**Smallfolk:** The base layer. Flea Bottom is the most marginal smallfolk zone in KL — below even the stable-hands and household servants of the noble quarter. Taylor's entry point is the Hook, which is marginal even within Flea Bottom. The smallfolk social world is horizontal: reputation, favors, and information travel through informal networks rather than formal authority chains. Taylor's insect-network maps well onto this structure — she is naturally working in the same register.

**Landed knights:** Minor landed gentry; above smallfolk but below the noble houses. In KL, a landed knight from outside the city is often here on specific business (court attendance, petition, debt collection) and has household staff in temporary lodging. A landed knight is an authority figure to a Flea Bottom smallfolk person but not to a lord or council member.

**Lordly houses:** The major and minor houses with representatives in KL. Their household staff (stewards, captains, servants) are themselves a social layer above common smallfolk. House livery is visible authority in KL streets; Watch officers defer to household staff in livery when the matter is within the lord's jurisdiction.

**Royal household:** The Targaryen family and their immediate household. A category apart. Contact between smallfolk and royal household figures is extremely rare and is always mediated by layers of intermediaries. Taylor does not have direct royal household contact at story open.

**The Faith:** Septons occupy a horizontal authority lane distinct from the feudal vertical. A septon does not command armed men; a septon does command spiritual and moral categories. In KL, the Faith's institutional scale is significant — the Grand Sept on Visenya's Hill is the center, with a network of smaller septs and chapel-rooms throughout the city. A septon who decides Taylor is a spiritual concern is a different problem than a Watch officer who decides she is a security concern.

**Maesters:** The Citadel-trained administrators and healers. In KL, maesters are attached to noble households and to the Red Keep. They are not a political faction (the Citadel's institutional posture is neutral service) but individual maesters have their own loyalties and information-network connections. A maester who notices Taylor is more dangerous than a septon who notices Taylor, because the maester's reporting channel reaches the administrative level.

## KL-Specific Authority Texture

The Riverlands-calibrated social physics cards describe a rural authority landscape where the primary authority figures are wandering men-at-arms and occasional maester census-takers. KL's authority landscape is denser:

**The City Watch:** A standing force numbering in the hundreds. They patrol specific districts on schedules that locals know. The Watch's primary concerns in Flea Bottom are: civil disorder, theft that reaches complaint level, and persons-of-unknown-provenance who appear repeatedly. Taylor's foreign-national status is a Watch concern if she accumulates enough visibility. Watch officers report to the Gold Cloaks' command structure; serious anomalies reach the Red Keep.

**Household servants as watchers:** The servants of noble households move through KL on daily errands. They are practiced observers of the people around them — their household's safety depends on knowing who is in their district and what those people do. A Hightower household servant who sees Taylor's animal-uncanny behavior twice and reports it is not performing unusual surveillance; it is ordinary household intelligence practice.

**Otto's network (specific to this project):** Hightower intelligence specifically captures persons-of-unknown-provenance in KL. This is not the Watch's formal mechanism; it is an informal network of informants, household staff, and paid observers that Otto built during his tenure as Hand. The Hightower network is faster and more targeted than the Watch's general-purpose reporting. It is also less visible — Taylor cannot see it operating the way she can see a Watch patrol.

## Taylor's Operational Position Within This Structure

At story open, Taylor is: a foreign-national smallfolk woman, unknown provenance, no institutional cover, no patron relationship, no documentation. She is in the most vulnerable social position available in KL. Her liabilities:

- No documentation means no verifiable identity
- Foreign markers in speech (she speaks the Common Tongue, but not with the accent of any identifiable Westerosi region; her speech patterns are wrong in ways that are hard to place)
- No visible economic activity (she has no established trade or service)
- No household or patron affiliation

Her assets at story open:

- Flea Bottom's relative social opacity (strangers are not remarkable if they keep their heads down)
- The Hook's fluidity (waterfront-adjacent; transient population makes one more face unremarkable)
- Her analytical capability (she reads social signals fast and can adjust behavior to reduce conspicuousness)
- Her insect-network (intelligence asset before it becomes a liability)

The path from story-open position to act-one patron relationship is Taylor moving from maximally-exposed to patron-protected within the existing class structure — trading her visibility (the witch-label, her capability) for institutional cover she cannot generate herself.
