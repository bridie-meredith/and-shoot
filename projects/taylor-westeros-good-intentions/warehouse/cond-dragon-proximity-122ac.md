# Dragon Proximity — Backgrounded with Instrumental Pressure (122 AC)

## Description

The operative rule for dragons in this project. Dragons exist, are present in King's Landing, fly overhead, and exert political and atmospheric pressure on every scene set near the Dragonpit or under an aerial approach. Taylor does not ride a dragon, does not directly engage a dragon, and does not attempt to bond or claim one. Closer in background presence than the HOTD-show framing; not on-stage as scene partners.

## Sensory Impact

**Aerial passage:** Dragons fly over KL on patrol or in transit. The shadow is the first sign — an eclipse-quality dimming moving across the district, followed by the sound (a rhythmic wing-stroke low enough to feel in the sternum, distinct from wind or thunder). Heat shimmer follows if the dragon has been flying in direct sun. Flea Bottom does not stop; dragons over KL are not unusual enough to interrupt work. But eyes go up. Children point. Old men make the sign of the Seven.

**Dragonpit proximity:** Within a few blocks of the Dragonpit, the ambient sensory signature shifts. The smell is the first indicator — sulphur, animal heat, something that reads as wrong to Taylor's insect-network because the insects avoid the immediate Dragonpit perimeter (heat, smell, and proximity to predators). The passive insect feed has a shadow-gap around the Dragonpit — a dead-zone in Taylor's awareness that is itself an information signal.

**Ground-level encounter:** If a dragon is moved through city streets (transport, training exercises near the gates), the street clears. This is not panic — it is practiced city-behavior. People stand aside, face away, and do not make eye contact with the rider. The only people who approach deliberately are dragonkeepers and their apprentices.

## What Taylor Does Not Do

**Does not ride.** Taylor is not a Targaryen, does not carry Valyrian blood, and has no mechanism for initiating a bond. This is not a project where that boundary is tested or approached. Any scene implying Taylor is attempting to bond or claim a dragon is a structural violation.

**Does not directly engage.** Taylor does not use her insect network to gather intelligence from within the Dragonpit interior (the insect dead-zone makes this operationally impossible at story-open range). She does not approach a dragon on foot. She does not use her network to distract, calm, or communicate with a dragon.

**Does not position dragon interaction as a plot lever.** Dragon capabilities — fire, flight, physical scale — do not resolve any plot problem Taylor faces in this project. Dragons are political objects and atmospheric pressure; they are not tools.

## What Dragons Are in This Project

**Political objects.** The Dragonpit's population and the identities of bonded riders are the strategic inventory both factions are managing. Taylor can read the political-weather significance of dragon-sightings (Aemond's Vhagar overhead means Aemond is in KL or has recently departed; an unbonded dragon's behavior change suggests attempted claiming) without direct engagement. This intelligence value is real; it comes from observation at range, not from proximity.

**Atmospheric pressure.** Dragon presence heightens every scene's stakes by ambient reminder that this world operates at a scale Taylor's insects cannot directly address. She can organize smallfolk intelligence networks; she cannot stop a dragon. The asymmetry is load-bearing: it establishes the ceiling of her capability in this world clearly and repeatedly.

**Behavioral indicator.** Taylor's insect network's dead-zone around the Dragonpit is an involuntary, continuous reminder of what is beyond her operational range. When political events involve the Dragonpit — a new rider, a contested claiming, a dragon unaccounted for — Taylor registers this as a gap in her network before she registers it as news. That gap is the narrative texture of dragon proximity.

## Taylor's Insect Network and Dragon Biology

Taylor's insect network avoids dragon-proximate zones by behavioral suppression, not by Taylor's directive. Insects do not willingly approach dragons; heat, smell, and predator-proximity keep them at distance. Taylor cannot override this with effort — her control operates on insect cognition, and the avoidance behavior is below the level she can countermand.

**Practical result:** Taylor has a permanent dead-zone of approximately 50-100m radius around any actively present dragon (smaller for sleeping dragons, larger for active/heated ones). She cannot gather granular intelligence from within that zone. She knows the zone exists. She can use its shape (a negative space in her network) to infer a dragon's presence, location, and activity level from outside it.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-dance-faction-state-previserys`:** Dragon-asset inventory changes (new bonds, rider deaths, claiming events) are major faction-state events. Taylor reads these through her network's dead-zone behavior and through street-level information flow, not through direct observation.

**With `cond-khepri-residue-122ac`:** The insect dead-zone is a KL-specific feature of Taylor's operational environment. The base fauna-control rules do not describe it because it is specific to environments with large predatory fauna that actively suppress insect populations (dragons, secondarily the larger hunting birds kept in noble mews).

**For auditor use:** Flag any scene where Taylor attempts to gather intelligence from inside the Dragonpit insect dead-zone. Flag any scene where Taylor uses her network to directly interact with (calm, distract, communicate with) a dragon. Flag any scene where dragon capability is positioned as a tool Taylor can deploy or leverage through any intermediary mechanism.
