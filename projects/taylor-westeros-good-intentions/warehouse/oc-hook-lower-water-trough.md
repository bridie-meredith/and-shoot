# The Hook Lower Water-Trough — Public Water-Point, Chandler's Row Junction, Lower Hook

## Geography

A public water-point at the lower end of the Hook ward, where the chandler's row meets the small open space before the fishmongery lane. The trough is a standard Flea Bottom infrastructure piece: a stone or heavy timber basin fed by a slow pipe or periodic municipal fill, used by ward residents, tradespeople, passing animals, and anyone who needs water and does not have house access to a private source. In 122 AC the Hook's lower end is the ward's commercial-functional cluster; the trough is at the approximate center of this cluster, which makes it a point that ward residents pass through as a matter of routine business, not a destination requiring deliberate travel.

Septon Halvard uses the trough as a regular stop on his sick-tending circuit — the water-carrying function is explicit: he fills a skin here for his rounds. The trough is not, for Halvard, a social meeting point by design; it is a functional stop on a predictable route. This predictability is what makes an encounter here ordinary and unengineered.

## Layout

**The trough itself:** A rectangular stone basin at waist height, long enough for two people to draw water simultaneously without crowding. The basin holds enough water for steady use through the day; it is refilled by the ward's water-carrying arrangement (the city's water distribution at ward level is loose and practical rather than piped-and-formal in 122 AC Flea Bottom). The trough's exterior stone is worn smooth where people grip the rim to draw.

**Open space before the fishmongery:** The trough sits at the edge of the small open area where the chandler's row terminates and the fishmongery lane begins. This is not a plaza — it is an accidental widening where two lane geometries meet at a slightly offset angle, producing a clearing large enough for a handcart to turn. The trough occupies the corner of this clearing.

**Chandler's row face:** The buildings on the trough side are chandlers — wax-and-wick shops, with display items in the ground-floor windows or set on boards outside during business hours. In the evening or off-hours the boards are in; the storefronts are shut. The chandler's row extends back toward the Hook's upper network.

**Fishmongery approach:** The lane opening to the fishmongery begins just past the open space. The fishmongery itself is an interior space; the lane here carries the fish-smell from the operation even when the shop is not actively displaying.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Fish is the lower Hook's dominant note — the fishmongery's operations saturate the air in this part of the ward. Brine, gutting residue, the cold-salt smell of yesterday's stock in the stall corners. The chandler's row contributes tallow-wax — a dry, faintly sweet note that sits underneath the fish smell. The trough adds its own water smell: wet stone, the algae that grows in the trough's grout-lines, the faint mineral note of whatever the water source carries. Together the lower Hook smells like work.

**Sound:** Daytime: the ambient commercial noise of the lower ward — fishmongers calling stock, the chandler's wood-on-counter sounds, handcarts on the cobble. At the trough: the sound of water drawn — the scoop or skin against the basin edge, water running, the soft impact of a filled vessel set back on the rim. In the evening or off-hours: quieter but not quiet; the residential floors above are still active and the ward does not silence early.

**Light:** The lower Hook's open space receives more sky than the tight upper Hook lanes — the wider geometry at the junction opens the overhead view. In the evening: the sky is still reading above but the lane surfaces are in shadow. The chandler's row has firelight in the windows that pushes into the lane marginally.

**Texture:** The trough rim — worn smooth on the draw-side, slightly rough at the ends where hands do not grip. The lane surface at the clearing: compacted with fish-scale and brine runoff from the monger's work, slightly slicker than dry-earth lane in the upper Hook. The open space has more debris underfoot than a purely residential lane — dropped scales, fish-wrap refuse, the material residue of a working junction.

## Exits

- **Chandler's row (north, toward upper Hook):** The row continues northward through the Hook's commercial layer. This is the direction Taylor arrives from on the route from the hook-lane.
- **Fishmongery lane (east/southeast):** The lane that leads to the fishmongery's front. Not typically a through-route for pedestrians not patronizing the monger.
- **Lower ward exits (multiple, west and south):** The lower Hook's exits toward the broader Flea Bottom network, the street-level connections to the adjacent wards and the lower city approaches.

## Hazards

- **Witness geometry:** The trough is in the clearing, not hidden. A conversation at the trough is visible to anyone in the open space or at the lane-mouths — not to the whole ward, but to anyone who happens to be present. The ward's ambient observability applies: nobody is watching deliberately, but nobody is invisible.
- **Fish-smell concentration:** The lower Hook's fish smell is heavier than the upper Hook ambient. A character sensitive to smell has a persistent background input here that does not diminish during a conversation.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A third party arriving at the trough for water during a conversation — the shared infrastructure makes trough-use a public act, not a private one
- A fishmonger's apprentice running past on an errand, moving through the clearing without slowing
- The water-level in the trough low enough to require waiting — the basin not yet refilled from the municipal cycle, other users already waiting or having recently drawn the level down
- A cart from the fishmongery beginning to load in the clearing — noise, movement, the practical intrusion of commerce
- The evening light failing faster than expected — the sky's transition from grey to dark changing the clearing's ambient register mid-conversation
- An animal (dog, pig) at the trough edge — routine ward fauna at a water-point
