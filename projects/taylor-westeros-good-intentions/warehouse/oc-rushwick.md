# The Rushwick — Court-Adjacent Ward, Lower Slope of Aegon's Hill

## Geography

A working-class ward pressed between the hill's stone skirt — the irregular lower slope of Aegon's Hill where the Red Keep's foundational stonework meets the city's accumulated density — and the upward lean of the city's higher tenement district. The Rushwick sits at the transition seam between Flea Bottom and the court's logistics infrastructure: it is not Flea Bottom (the rental stock is marginally better, the residents marginally more stable) and it is not court territory, but the servant passages that run beneath the lower Red Keep approach pass under or behind its eastern wall-line. Bodies on legitimate court business — provisioners, message-runners, couriers, minor household staff — transit the Rushwick as a matter of functional geography. It is the lowest point in the city where court gait appears without ceremony.

The ward name is vernacular and old. It does not appear on any official map of King's Landing. In 122 AC it is inhabited primarily by craftsmen, minor traders, and the residential overflow of adjacent wards. Its transit population is higher than its residential population in any given morning-bell window, because the ward's junction sits on a natural shortcut between the lower servant-passage exits and the city's main eastward throughfares.

Taylor's insect-feed extended into the Rushwick for the first time five days into b01c05, making it the first ward in her operational coverage that returns court-tier body signatures on a regular basis.

## Layout

**Main junction:** The ward's functional center — a wider-than-average meeting of three lanes at a point where the hill's natural topography created a small depression that construction filled irregularly. The junction is large enough for two handcarts to pass without coordination. It is the primary node for through-transit bodies (provisioner-trains, couriers, message-runners taking the shortcut) and for the ward's own resident foot traffic. Visibility from the junction to each lane-mouth is partial; the hill's stone skirt creates a broken sight-line on the north side.

**Lane-mouth (west):** The junction's primary approach from the lower city. Wide enough for a provisional-train with loaded carts. The lane-mouth opens onto a broader east-west lane that connects the Rushwick to the adjacent ward network and, further west, to the Hook's outer ring. Through-transit enters and exits here.

**East lane:** The natural transit corridor toward the servant-passage exits — a lane of moderate width running northeast from the junction, following the hill's stone skirt. The east lane is the direction provisioner-trains and institutional-gait bodies favor. It narrows as it approaches the wall-line.

**Side alleys:** Two confirmed side alleys opening off the junction-east axis. Both are too narrow to sell from and too narrow for loaded carts — foot traffic only, and only one person wide at their narrowest point. Wide enough, however, to pass through at a run. The side alleys connect to secondary lanes in the ward's interior. Their mouths are visible from the junction; their interiors are not.

**Wall-line (east):** The interior boundary of the ward, where the buildings have been built flush against the lower slope of the Red Keep approach. The wall-line is not a true fortification — it is a compression artifact, buildings pressed close to the hill's base because the hill's stone skirt limits expansion. A person standing at the wall-line has a partial sightline across the junction. The insect-feed anchors Taylor's observation position here during the b01c05 coverage window.

**Alley-mouth (side-alley off east exit):** The mouth of the primary side alley that opens off the east lane, approximately forty feet northeast of the main junction. This is the scene-B enforcement geometry: approach from the junction, containment in the alley-mouth, sound does not carry back to the junction at low register.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Cool stone is the Rushwick's dominant register — the hill's stone skirt keeps the north side of the junction in shade for most of the morning and into midday, and the stone retains cool damp from the previous night. The tallow-smoke from the neighboring stitch-house ward (one lane southwest) drifts east and settles near the wall-line when wind is light; it is not the Rushwick's own smell but it arrives as a consistent ambient note during morning and early-evening still-air windows. The ward's own occupants run minor craft and food-stall activity on the main lane; their contribution is secondary to the stone-cool base and the borrowed tallow-smoke.

**Sound:** Junction acoustics are the defining feature for the insect-feed's auditory layer. The junction's depression and the surrounding wall geometry creates a mild sound-collection effect: raised voices at the junction are audible at the lane-mouths and at the top of the east lane; low-voiced conversation at the junction does not project. The side alleys carry sound differently — narrow stone walls return sound inward. A low, effortful sound from a body inside a side alley does not reach the junction at any register the human ear recovers. The insect-feed has no field for this gap; it returns the absence of recoverable sound as silence, not as evidence of sound.

**Light:** The north side of the junction (hill's stone skirt side) is in morning shadow until the sun clears the Red Keep's lower tower-line. The east lane gets morning light from the gap between tenement roofs; the side alleys are dim throughout the day. The wall-line position is in shade from the hill's proximity. Late afternoon: the junction receives full light briefly before the tenement overhangs cut it.

**Texture:** Hill stone underfoot on the east lane and near the wall-line — older paving than the junction, which has been relaid in packed earth over old cobble. The side alleys have the stone-and-mortar feel of original construction: rough walls, uneven floor, the give of old mortar under boot.

## Fixed Props

None card-level. The Rushwick's cart-staging and stall activity is runtime state.

## Exits

- **Lane-mouth (west)** — the primary through-transit exit toward the lower city and Hook ward network. Natural direction for outbound institutional traffic.
- **East lane (northeast)** — toward the servant-passage exits and the lower Red Keep approach. Preferred by court-logistic transit bodies (provisioner-trains, message-runners, couriers).
- **Side alleys** — secondary connections to the ward interior; foot traffic only; no cart access.
- **Wall-line continuation (east)** — follows the base of the hill; connects further east toward the ward's narrower residential section and eventually to the outer Sept-approach road.

## Hazards

- **Enforcement geometry at side-alley mouths:** The side alley off the east lane's mouth creates a natural containment point — two bodies at the alley-mouth control all egress; a third body pins the target against the alley's interior stone. This geometry was enacted at b01c05 scene-B and is repeatable. The alley-mouth is not visible from the junction at distance.
- **Sound gap in side alleys:** Low-register sound (effortful, involuntary) produced inside the side alleys does not reach the junction. The insect-feed logs silence rather than event; a human observer at the wall-line receives the same gap. Incidents in the side alleys are invisible to the feed's auditory layer.
- **Court-tier transit observation window:** The Rushwick is the city's lowest-elevation point where institutional-gait bodies move without escort or ceremony. A sustained observer (insect-feed or otherwise) learns provisioner-train schedule, message-runner intervals, and courier routes within a week of coverage. This intelligence value also makes the ward a site where faction enforcement of courier bodies is geographically convenient — contained, below court notice, transit route confirmed.
- **Coverage novelty registration:** For the insect-feed, the Rushwick is a novel substrate. Court-tier body categorization is a new classification class — the feed can register gait-signatures but has no prior pattern-set for institutional hierarchy markers. Novel substrate means the categorization-layer is working harder than it does in known wards, which may produce affective residue even under factual-observation discipline.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A provisioner-train arriving early or late relative to the established schedule — feed anomaly flag before the cause is visible
- The east lane blocked temporarily by a cart turned sideways (delivery to a wall-line adjacent address) — through-transit backing up at the junction
- A message-runner taking the side-alley shortcut instead of the east lane — shortcut use indicates time pressure or route-avoidance
- A courier pausing at the junction corner rather than continuing on heading — hesitation that reads as a recurring-body anomaly in the feed
- Rain shifting the tallow-smoke layer from the stitch-house — the Rushwick's cool-stone register temporarily replaced by wet-stone-plus-tallow, a sensory combination the feed has no prior record of
- Two institutional-gait bodies crossing the junction simultaneously from different headings — categorization demand spikes if both require simultaneous tracking
