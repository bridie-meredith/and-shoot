# Alicent Hightower — Queen Consort, 122 AC

## Description

Queen Consort of the Seven Kingdoms. Wife of Viserys I Targaryen, who is aging and increasingly infirm by 122 AC. Daughter of Otto Hightower. Mother of Aegon, Aemond, and Helaena Targaryen. The institutional center of the Green faction.

She is approximately thirty-five years old in 122 AC, though she has been aging in public performance rather than private time — the specific quality of a woman who has managed her presentation for dynastic purposes since she was a girl. She is not old. She looks precisely as old as it is useful for her to look.

The Green faction is her faction in the way that a household is a woman's domain: she did not build its institutional architecture (that is Otto), but she is its social fact — the person in whose name the succession argument is made, the person whose children are the argument. She executes what her father plans, and she has been doing this long enough that the execution is seamless and the planning seam is invisible to most observers.

She was raised devout and remains so. The Faith is not performance for her; it is the register in which she understands herself as a moral actor. The specific quality of her piety is useful: it gives her an internal language for the succession question (my children's right is God's order) that lets her hold the political calculation at one remove from its instrumental nature.

Taylor observes her through insect-feed across three to five sequences: the managed-affect register of court performance, the dynastic-maternal warmth deployed at specific occasions, the specific invisibility of smallfolk in her peripheral vision. The contempt Taylor accumulates begins with what the compound-eye returns: not cruelty, not calculation on display, but the specific natural entitlement of a woman for whom the smallfolk burning in a dynastic war are a weather event — present, unfortunate, not requiring her attention.

## Voice

**Register:** managed-formal, dynastic-maternal. She is warm in the specific way court warmth works: warmth as a resource deployed toward particular ends, not absent, but not spontaneous. She speaks in complete sentences. She does not hedge in public. In private, she may be less composed, but Taylor never has access to the private register — only the public face.

**What Taylor observes through insect-feed:**
- Her voice does not rise. When she is displeased, the register tightens; the temperature drops a fraction; she does not perform anger in court spaces.
- She defers to Viserys in public and manages the outcome in private. The deference is not servile — it is the specific political grammar of a woman who holds institutional power through the husband's name.
- Smallfolk are linguistically absent from her register. She does not speak about them, to them, or around them in ways that register their presence as significant. Taylor notes this as a specific observable data point: the feed returns Alicent moving through a corridor of serving staff without any signal that she has noticed them as persons.

## Taste

- Institutional order. The specific preference for things being in their correct places — the succession resolved, the succession question not raised in her presence, the smallfolk managed at a distance by the mechanisms that exist for that purpose.
- Devotion as self-understanding. She finds the Faith register genuinely satisfying; it provides internal coherence to the political calculation.
- Her children's advancement. This is not cold calculation; she genuinely loves them and the love is the mechanism by which the political calculation is held as something other than calculation.
- Competent staff. She does not like being surprised by operational failures in her household.

## Pet Peeves

**The succession question raised as an open question**
In her framing, the succession is not open. It was opened by Viserys I's sons' existence and it was closed by his decisions about his children. Anyone who speaks about it as still open is either uninformed or acting in bad faith. She does not argue this; she expresses it as categorical correction.
Severity: strong.

**Smallfolk brought into court discussions as moral considerations**
The specific irritation of a woman who manages the realm's practical welfare through intermediaries and does not appreciate being presented with the individual human cost of institutional decisions. Not cruelty — inattention. The smallfolk burning is a war-cost, which is a political cost, which belongs in the category of things that institutions manage.
Severity: soft to the external observer, strong in her internal register.

**Her father's plans going sideways due to human sentiment**
She has watched Otto's network function well and malfunction when assets carry sentiment. She is more tolerant of this than he is — she has her own sentiment and considers it a manageable variable — but she shares his taxonomy: sentiment is a factor to be managed, not a moral argument.
Severity: soft.
