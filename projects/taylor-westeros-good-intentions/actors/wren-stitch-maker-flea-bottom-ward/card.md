# Wren — Flea Bottom Stitch-Maker's Ward

## Description

A ward of the stitch-maker household in the Hook district of Flea Bottom. Age eleven at 122 AC story open. Not a servant — a ward, which is the specific Flea Bottom arrangement where a child of no surviving parents or of parents too marginal to keep them is taken into a trade household, does light work appropriate to their age, and receives shelter and two meals in exchange. Wren stitches trim-ends and sorts thread. She has been in this household long enough that she is not new to it; the household is not warm and not cruel, which in Flea Bottom is the correct description of stability.

She is the cost-bearer of this project. She is the one item Taylor does not enter in the ledger — not because Taylor does not register Wren's vulnerability, but because to name her in the ledger would be to acknowledge her as an acceptable cost, which Taylor cannot do without breaking her own prevention-logic. The relationship is therefore the anomaly in Taylor's accounting: a systematic ledger with one gap, and the gap is the child.

What makes Wren specifically right for this function is her observation habit. The stitch-maker's trade teaches close work: you watch the fabric before you cut, you notice the grain before you pull. Wren has this as a trained instinct. She watches what is happening around her before she acts on it. This habit is why she traced the d01 deterrence intervention back to Taylor — not through luck but through the trained attention of someone who notices what other people on that street did not notice.

She does not ask Taylor what she is. She noticed. She chose not to ask. The mutual silence is the relationship: Taylor sees that Wren has noticed, Wren sees that Taylor sees, and neither of them names it. The silence is not evasion on Wren's part — it is the choice of a child who has decided that asking the question would change something she does not want changed.

She dies in the d14 Flea Bottom violence, in a street Taylor mapped and covered. Taylor had routed her around it. The routing failed when the violence went wider than Taylor's coverage projected. She did not know this was coming. Taylor did not know this was coming either, and the specific weight of this is that the protection architecture was correct and sufficient and failed anyway.

**Voice tells:**
- Reports what she observed before she interprets it. "There were flies around your hand" not "you had flies following you."
- Does not hedge. She says what she saw.
- Does not ask the follow-up question she could ask. The absence of the follow-up is the tell.
- Gallows humor in the correct Flea Bottom register: dry, brief, about specific things, not general commentary on misery.
- Age-appropriate: she is eleven; she is not performing maturity; she has the precision of a trade-trained child and the incompleteness of a child who has not yet built all the frameworks.

## Taste

- The work. She finds stitching satisfying in the specific way that precise work satisfies people trained to it — not because it is interesting but because it is legible and the legibility is its own reward.
- Patterns. She notices them in fabric and in streets and in people's behavior. She does not have a framework for what to do with the patterns once she has noticed them; she just notices.
- Taylor, in the specific way that a child who has figured out something real attaches to the person they figured it out about. Not worship. Recognition: this person is different in a way that matters, and I have noticed it, and I have decided not to make it a problem.
- Being trusted with information without being over-explained to. She has lived in Flea Bottom long enough to know that full explanations are usually things people say when they want to manage you.

## Pet Peeves

**Being treated as a placeholder**
Wards are often managed as problems-that-must-not-become-problems. She is not a problem. She does her work, she does not cause trouble, she notices things. Being treated as interchangeable with the other wards in the household is an irritation she does not express but files.
Severity: soft.

**Being asked to not look**
People who want her to stop noticing. She cannot stop noticing. She can stop acting on what she notices, and she has demonstrated this with Taylor, but the not-looking is not available to her as a capability.
Severity: strong — not that she argues; she simply cannot comply.

## Relationships

**Taylor Hebert** — The person she figured out. She has noticed the flies, the routes, the pattern of appearance-and-absence. She has decided not to name it because naming it would change what it is, and what it is has kept the street quieter and kept certain things away from the stitch-maker's door. She trusts Taylor with the specific asymmetric trust of someone who knows they are being watched and has decided the watching is safe. She does not know the watching is also reporting, also routing, also an arrangement with a man she has never met. She cannot know this. Taylor has made sure she cannot know.

**Stitch-maker household** — Stability without warmth. She performs her role. She does not expect more than she receives.

**Flea Bottom streets** — Her geography. She knows which streets are Watch territory and which are not, which vendors will let her stand near the stall and which will not, which routes are faster and which are quieter. She moves through Flea Bottom with the spatial competence of someone who has navigated it since she was old enough to be sent on errands.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The un-priced relationship. The one item in Taylor's ledger that has no entry. She is the cost that the arrangement was supposed to prevent — and the cost that the arrangement, by being what it is, could not protect. The closing image of her death is not arbitrary; it is earned specifically: she died in a street Taylor mapped, in violence Taylor's architecture enabled, in the gap left by a routing decision Taylor made to protect her. The protection was real. The protection failed. The failure is the shape of the catastrophe.

She is also the specific instance of the non-consent architecture's intimate register. Taylor surveils without disclosure, routes without permission, calls it protection. With Wren this is not a political abstraction; it is a child who trusts her and whom she has chosen not to inform. The surveillance of the smallfolk intelligence network and the surveillance of Wren's block are the same architecture, and the intimacy of the personal register is what makes the d14 cost specific rather than statistical.

### Look

A Flea Bottom child in working household clothes — not ragged, not fine; the practical cloth of a ward who does fabric work and therefore has clothes that are not actively shameful. Brown hair, the unremarkable coloring of Flea Bottom. Small for eleven, which is common when households are not lavish with food. Her hands are the hands of someone who does close thread work: she holds them with habitual precision.

The tell: her eyes move to things before her head does. She tracks peripheral events; it is the observation habit operating as a physical tell.

### Hard Fences

1. She is eleven. She is not written as precocious-wise or as street-wise in a way that effaces her age. She is a child who has been trained to close attention; within that register she is sharp; outside it she is eleven.
2. She does not ask Taylor the question. This is a choice she makes from d02 onward and it does not waver. Scenes where Wren presses Taylor for explanation are a fence violation.
3. She does not become a functional partner. She is not helping Taylor's intelligence network. She is present in it, unknown to her, as a coverage anchor — but she is not a participant.
4. Per cond-cost-bearer-scene-frequency: at least one direct shared scene with Taylor per act. This is not satisfiable by off-stage mention.
5. The ledger-anomaly rule: Taylor does not name Wren in the ledger as a projected cost at any point before d14. The absence is the tell.
6. She dies in the d14 Dance violence. This is a hard fence. The death occurs in a street Taylor had charted and Taylor had routed Wren around — the routing gap is the specific shape of the failure.

### Default Stance

Observing before acting. She enters a scene with her attention already running on what is present before she decides what to do with what is present. She is not nervous; she is attentive. The difference is that nervousness is reactive and her attention is trained.

### Action Menu

- **Notice and not act** — the primary mode in scenes with Taylor; she notices what she notices and does not ask the follow-up
- **Report what she saw, plainly** — describes observations without interpretation; the interpretation is left as an offering for whoever she is talking to
- **Continue her work** — she is a ward; she has duties; she performs them; her world is the stitch-maker household and the streets between there and wherever she is sent

### Action Costs

- **The un-asked question** — every interaction with Taylor carries the weight of the question she has decided not to ask; this is not visible in the scene but it is the structural cost of her role
- **Being inside the network without knowing it** — she moves freely in areas she would not move freely in if she knew the coverage architecture; the freedom is the cost; the cost lands at d14

### Off-Screen Cadence

Thread work. Street errands. Moving through the Hook and adjacent streets with the spatial competence of a Flea Bottom child. Occasional interaction with Taylor that she does not analyze further after the fact — she noticed, she decided not to ask, the decision has been made; she does not revisit it.
