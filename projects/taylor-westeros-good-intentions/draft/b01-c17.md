The count-sheet was under the trough-stone where it always was, the agreed mark on the third column. I lifted it and unfolded it against the low grey light coming flat off the waterfront, the kind of end-of-day light that the wall-shadow had already started to eat. The query sat third from the bottom. A height, a gait, six observation points, eleven days — a woman moving the east-of-water-gate gap on a regular interval, observed twice in proximity to my known ward-contacts. The apparatus wanted the figure named. The sheet held no name of its own; the pattern was the whole of it.

I stilled. The feed handed the gait back before the apparatus had a word for it — the second-step hold off the scar across the left ankle, the interval that ran the stitch-house rotation. The coverage log matched the eleven-day cluster against the record I already kept. I had been reading this gap in my own feed for months and let it stand nowhere a record would hold it, the one figure I routed around on purpose, the cost of the keeping never written down. The question had come in from the channel's far side. The answer was already in my hand. I folded the sheet again and kept my hand flat on the trough-stone and did not shift my weight toward the next leg of the circuit. The question existed whether or not I answered it. That was the part that did not pause.

I crossed the Hook's southern edge with the sheet folded in my coat. The stone underfoot had gone to its night-cool, not day-warm, not quite wet, the ward's density legible through the soles of my feet, and I let the accounting run. The name-option priced out to one terminus: named is findable, findable is usable, and the cost-sheet had no entry past usable. So the ledger opened the other entry. Route a different figure through the gap documentation. Attribute the movements to a ward-elder whose own passage closed the question — and what that option cost was not the gait's exposure but a person who had not chosen this corridor.

That was the thing that cracked. Not a feeling I would have called by a name; the accounting itself, recognizing what the option was. The ledger held both entries open and I kept walking, the night quieting around the work until the ward was only a far ember-glow and the occasional footfall, the kind of quiet that lets the arithmetic hear itself. Wren's second-step hesitation surfaced in the feed unsummoned. I set the two entries side by side and the screen-option read identical to the architecture I carried the prohibition against — not a feeling about it, the match itself, returned the way the feed returns a gait. The work I had built once at scale and named the thing I would not do again, come back now at the size of one corridor. I did not open the recognition the rest of the way. I extended the walk and held it at the edge.

Before the Hook turned north I had decided; the accounting had run long enough. The room above the rendering works smelled of cold tallow soaked into the stone, the day's render long done. I pulled the coverage log out onto the working surface and it returned the eleven-day cluster in my own hand. Elder Norren was a tanner's-ward contact whose own twice-weekly passage through the east-of-water-gate corridor I already had on record — legitimate, regular, his own. I opened his entry-cluster.

I wrote the first line. A presence in the corridor, the date, attributed to a body that had not been in it. The pen added two more — a supplementary observation, a second, the cluster closing on itself, dated backward, indistinguishable from a gap that had healed. The ledger that never carried names now carried one I had put there. The record stopped being a thing I was doing and became a thing that sat. Extraction would now mean resolving what was written; the false entry was a structural element of everything the tether stood on. The protection and the trap were the same three lines. I set the pen down. The shape of it was the shape I had carried the refusal of across into this place — smaller in count, identical in kind. I readied the segment for the dead-drop and left the log open on the surface, the entry unclosed, and went out.

It was three days before the dead drop came back quiet. No follow-up query; the channel handed back nothing, the apparatus's question shut against Norren's name. The picture had resolved the attribution — Norren's movements logged, the slot filled, the gain and the cost arriving as one entry, Wren safe inside a record that was false. She crossed the east-of-water-gate corridor that morning the way she always had, and the ward-read gave me the second-step hesitation again, and the false record now sat in the picture saying the interval belonged to a man who had never run it.

I lifted the pen off the cost-ledger. The column for this stayed open, the slot blank, and I kept the accounting in my head where it would carry no name and route nowhere a record would hold it. The coverage log keeps the attribution; it will accumulate every future observation under Norren's name now, permanent. I had protected the gait the same way I built the thing I came here to set down — not the scale, not the count of bodies, the shape of the act. The column held the blank. Both were true.
