*I had kept the eastern boundary eleven months — clean coverage west of the water-gate, nothing east of it, the gap held as deliberately as the ground I covered. The arrangement still ran: the count routed through Jarvis to Otto's people, the quiet on Sera's question paid for by what the feed could deliver. This morning the passage-adjacent ward returned a boy.*

---

I held two flies on the stone-lip above the outer-court approach and a third in the corner-gutter of the passage-arch — a three-station read on coverage terrain that abutted the court's traffic without being the court itself. A working morning. Then the master-at-arms led a boy across the outer-court ground, and the boy on the court was Aemond Targaryen — the prince, twelve, the blue stone in the left socket. Off a page until now, the name behind a handler's note; here he was a body. The dragon he rides was not with him. The habit of it was.

He halted at the court-center. The sapphire caught the morning light and held it flat, and the stone did not move with the eye behind it; the boy accommodated the master-at-arms the way an animal accommodates weather, and the stone did not accommodate at all. He checked the yard-ground before the first engagement, a wide low sweep, more ground than a body his size would need. Then he opened the approach angle and raised the blade past the master-at-arms' guard at a clearance no twelve-year-old's reach accounts for — the arc was sized for a body that was not on the court, and he kept the spacing for it as if that body were already there. The master-at-arms reduced the drill pressure at the shoulder-line. The boy did not take the gift of it. He reset the foot-position, and the master-at-arms yielded the shoulder; the correction landed, and the yielding happened in the older man's frame, not the boy's. He reset and waited for the world to adjust to the reset, and the world adjusted. I returned the feed to routine scan density. Then he repeated the correction, and the second run fit the first — one of those I could have read wrong, two was a finding, and the finding fit a specimen I already held. I routed nothing of him anywhere.

The master-at-arms walked him up the passage-slope, and the compound eyes lost him into stone. What came next was not an image. The arch-flies juddered in a warm airstream, and the warmth in the air was the dragon — Vhagar, resting on the Red Keep's eastern slope, too large to be anywhere without the stone carrying her heat. The flies did not see her. They labored in the changed air, and the feed read the weight of her at distance, off the warmth alone. The flight-muscles worked against air that had changed weight, and the change was not weather; two months of this read, bay-warm off the morning stone against the hill-warm with the load behind it. The arch-flies took the differential at the passage-arch edge. The eastern-slope stone channeled the added weight down into the substrate, and the warm stone radiated the difference into the gutter-flies — I could feel it through them, the way you feel a hand near a wall in the dark, the stone giving back a heat it had not had an hour ago. The feed-record took the site-condition. I marked the thermal-rise as site-condition and did not mark what it confirmed: that the channel had found the architecture on its own, last chapter, from the far side — and a channel found from both ends does not unmake. The read that grounded me here was the same one that had closed the way out. The thermal-rise normalized across the passage-arch gutter.

The eastern-fringe flies carried the thermal-noise, and at the eastern boundary they dropped the image-resolution. I had run degraded coverage before; I knew the signature. What was different was what the degradation lit up. East of the water-gate the lanes returned nothing — not unclear, empty. I had left that ground uncovered eleven months on purpose, the one place I would not put the feed, because Wren moved there. The silence was the shape of a choice, not a failure of reach. The lanes did not read as covered-but-unclear; they returned silence to the feed, no noise, no interference, no dropout, the clean silence of ground the coverage had never reached, and clean silence read louder than the fringe-noise around it. The gap-lane opened a hole in the feed-image. The feed-noise framed the gap the way a lit wall frames a shadow — the absence was the shape, and the shape was perceptible; the noisy fringe gave it an edge, and the edge was person-sized, the clearing I had kept blank for eleven months perceptible for the first time. The eastern-boundary edge sharpened against the fringe-noise. My hand stopped on the stylus and held above the record. I exhaled one breath above the feed, the single sound in the room against the feed-interior silence, and let the gap hold its shape one more beat. Then I wrote "eastern-boundary, backwash-effect" in the coverage record. I did not note what it looked like.

The arch-flies settled at the passage-arch gutter, and the fringe-interference cleared at the eastern edge; the hill-warm fell off toward the stone-morning baseline, and the eastern boundary went back to its ordinary state — clean coverage west, clean gap east, the two stone edges holding. The negative shape had gone. It had been visible for the duration of the backwash and no longer. I closed the morning circuit and ran the coverage-record entries through the pass. What the morning had produced: a boy shaped like the thing he would become, the sapphire holding flat light, the master-at-arms yielding without being asked, a thermal weight behind the stone, and — for the length of a fringe disruption — a gap with a person-shaped quality. I wrote the final notation across the coverage-record. Aemond's arrival changed nothing I was doing; I was not routing a twelve-year-old to a patron. I closed the coverage-record and pulled the feed back to routine density. The category had been eleven months old; what had changed was that it had a shape now — perceptible as a deliberate thing, a structure of not-covering held with the same discipline as the covered ground, the one ground I had built to read nothing so the one who moved there moved unseen by it. My hand crossed the field where a name would go and found the line below it without a catch. I lifted the stylus past the name-field, set no name down, and ran the afternoon circuit. The arrangement continued.
