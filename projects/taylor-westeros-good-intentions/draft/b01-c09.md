*I knew the stitch-maker's days now the way I knew the lanes. Wren kept the Hook's children fed and clothed, and the feed had been threading her street since the boundary moved, and I had not once had to ask it to — the keeping had indexed her on its own, the way it indexed a gate or a supply cart, and that was the part I did not look at straight. The count ran the Hook and four wards past it. The courier I had carried without a name had one now, filed last circuit; I had not asked whose errands he ran.*

---

I entered the lane south of the Hook, and for a moment the morning let me be nothing else: I pressed the lane-stone underfoot, and the stone was cold up through the boot-sole, and for the length of the press I was only a body standing in a cold lane, which is the last thing the morning lets me be. The bay-damp had beaded the lane-stone, a cold wet film resolving on the dry surface where the sun had not yet reached it. Then the insect-feed threaded the stitch-shop lane, and the stitch-shop door stood open at the lane-mouth, and Wren walked the shop-to-water-seller lane — the route has waypoints now, the door, the corner, the lane that takes one entrance, and a thing with waypoints is no longer a body that happens to be in range. My gaze held on the bread-seller's corner a beat before she reached it. She reached the corner. I filed her route. The cold had stiffened my fingers, and they filed the route anyway, the same fold and set the hand gives a supply cart or a gate, and nothing in the set of the hand marked that this one was Wren — and the keeping has indexed her without once asking. The ward-coverage notes received the boundary geometry.

That evening, the second circuit ran the Dragonpit margin — the outer lanes south, toward the hill. I entered the dragonpit-margin lane, the hill-lane air gone cool where the lower wards still held the day's warmth. The supply cart marked the lower-gate road — the lower gate, the Dragonpit's court-margin side, under Rhaenys's Hill, where bodies on the heir's business pass and not the Queen's: not a Green-faction gate. The stone-post marked the lower-gate side-exit. Then the insect-feed returned Corwick — a body resolving out of the empty evening distribution, a man standing where a man at this hour should not be standing, wrong-houred and wrong-warded. The cold had settled its weight on my shoulders where I held still at the feed-edge. He went still in me too as his body turned. Corwick faced the second man. He squared the shoulders. I filed the posture-class — the wrong hour and the squared shoulders and the gate that backs onto the wrong faction resolved to a direction, not a name, and I read the gate's loyalty the way a person reads a thing already written, the split's ending held at a remove no one standing in this lane could hold, a direction I did not infer so much as recognize; I priced the posture-class and the price was small. I closed the observation-entry. I completed the circuit.

I took the feed-station. I folded the packet. The wax came down under my hand at the weight I had already settled; I sealed the packet, and what the packet held was the only thing the channel would ever see. To the left of it the ward-coverage notes marked the station I had left. The internal map held Wren's route. The feed-record closed the courier entry. The seal dried, the wax gone from soft and warm under the thumb to set firm.
