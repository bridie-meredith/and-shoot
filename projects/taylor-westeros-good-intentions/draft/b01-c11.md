*The arrangement was a named thing now — a function written into the channel, owed against the quiet over Sera — and naming it had changed nothing in how the mornings ran. Corwick was a closed entry in the ledger: the thread cut at the lower gate, the detention the feed had returned two days after I routed his corridor up the line. One man priced and paid. There was still one observation I had not routed.*

---

Jarvis took the packet off the station surface where it sat at the edge waiting for his hand, and his hand closed on it the way it always did now, without the small ceremony of a thing being handed over — just the close, and the covering-sheet folded down over the seal in the same motion, and then he was already turning, already most of the way back to the door, gone before the morning bell over the lane had finished losing its sound. There is a kind of work that looks like nothing is happening, and that is the work running at full load. I did not have to tell him it was full. He knew the weight of the packet from the way it sat.

Out at the grain-measures junction Oswyn had the cart-frame under both hands, holding it square between two carters who would otherwise have argued the order of the morning into the middle of the day, and he held it without knowing he was a held thing himself, a place in a pattern, the junction I needed kept open kept by a man who thought he was only keeping the peace over grain. And the wool-dyer, who I had asked for nothing, returned the cart-timing observation across to him unprompted — the kind of small true account a person gives back when an earlier kindness sits unpaid between them — so that the thing I would have had to reach for arrived on its own, carried up the line by a debt I had not designed and could not have designed, only set the conditions for and waited under.

I set the stylus to the source-field. The lane-pattern went down clean — the route the timing implied, the corridor it opened, all of it legible in the column where it would do its work. The source-field stayed where it was. The lane-pattern goes down and the name that returned it does not go down with it; the count carries the routing and not the man, and the man stays out of the record not because I weighed the question and answered it but because the hand came off the surface with the field still bare. I lifted the stylus. That was the whole of the omission — a hand off a board, the work going out built, as I had built every quiet machine of mine, to run on exactly what no one had chosen to give it.

The arrangement is a different shape now, a named function written into the channel and owed against the quiet over Sera, and I closed that entry too, later, though it changed nothing about how the morning had run. The merchant's shop opened on the Hook's south end with no relation I could see to any of it. The cloth-merchant — six months a steady square in the coverage, a man who squared bolt-tickets and went home — drew back the narrow worktable at the rear of the shop in the low rushlight, and that small act of opening was the only thing it seemed to be until the messenger crossed the threshold at an hour no order of cloth is placed. He lifted a folded paper from the table. He burned it.

The heat of it reached the worktable's edge before any of it was meant for me — heat first, then the curl of the smoke turning up off the iron dish, a thing already finished by the time I had it. The thread that had run through that shop ran to Dragonstone: the island seat across the bay where Rhaenyra keeps her court and the heir's faction holds, a half-day's water from the city. Something on that side had learned the channel was cut, and the answer was a burned page and not a rerouted one — the enemy done before I was in it, the world already running this ahead of me. The iron dish held the burn after the paper was gone, the residual warmth sitting in it the way warmth sits in a thing that has done its one job, and through the few of mine in the worktable's ambient air the char came up in a thin curl I had through them and not through any window of my own. Then the merchant squared the bolt-ticket on the table edge, the way a man squares a thing he has squared a thousand times, and went back to being a man who sells cloth.

I marked the timestamp. The thread I cut was read somewhere I do not route to, read before I had finished knowing it was cut — and the mark is what I did instead of the thought, the small thing the hand does so the larger one does not have to settle. It went down on something already finished a long way off, a smallness I would once have set against a larger ending and found small, and the measure is wrong the way it has always been wrong, because the smaller wars still burn the people standing under them. I made the mark. I moved on from it.

By early afternoon the soap-lane contact came across the cross-lane at the rendering-lane mouth, open to the approach, and put the nighttime-visitor report into the channel — the observation already shaped to move. The packet came back into my hands with the weight of it, the cloth of the covering coarse against my fingers, and I opened it. I wrote the precinct-pattern sourcing. The sourcing wrote itself in three motions — the observation set, the pattern drawn from it, the route assigned — and there was no fourth motion. The cycle closed on three and did not feel short. I sealed the packet. Nothing in the three steps had asked the older question, and the question had not come to stand in their way; the thing was sealed before there was a place in the sequence for it to be.

I came into the feed-station at the end of the day with the surface bare and the ledger open, the count already moving in me before I sat to it, and I ran it in motion and closed the arms one at a time. The Jarvis arm closed: the packet sealed, the channel carried everything upward, nothing snagged at the conduit. The Oswyn arm closed: the junction clean, calibrated, the grain holding the peace it thought it was holding. Third arm — the contacts — and as it shut the load that had been distributing itself across the whole of the day arrived all at once in the column, three threads confirmed and the whole tether sitting there counted, not declared, only counted, and heavier counted than it had ever felt carried. The arrangement arm closed last. The cover held; neither end could see the shape of what it was not being handed; two omissions sat in the record where nothing in any packet would ever name them, and I closed the entry over them without giving either a name. I set the stylus down. My hand stayed on it a beat after it was down, after the rhythm that had been under it all day had gone to nothing, and then I took the hand away.
