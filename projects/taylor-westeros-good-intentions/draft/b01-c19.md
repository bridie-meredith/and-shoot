*The counting runs at full scale now, and the channel runs both ways. Otto's apparatus takes my readings through Jarvis and pays for them by keeping the question of Sera's parentage quiet, and the arrangement holds while the work holds. I have read the court at its own scale long enough that the contempt for it sits in me with nowhere to go — accurate, complete, attached to nothing I do next. And somewhere in the upper city a name has been put to the woman who maps Flea Bottom. The name has not reached me yet. It is traveling.*

---

Before dawn the trough released its bundle, and I drew the sheet out and unfolded it. Not the fortnight-weight of the last drop — a single fold of paper, Jarvis's own notation, and I held it up to read. The grey-dark that fills the room above the rendering works in the hour before first light is not full dark and not lamplight; it is the grey that asks you to tilt the page and hold it close, and I have read every dead drop of eleven months in it. The sheet named a corridor. A court-tier reading, not the lower-city surveillance I have run for a year: a chamberlain in the Red Keep's outer administrative ring — a rank above the wards I map — whose corridor patterns had run wrong for six weeks. Confirm the role. Map the contact interval. Name the faction. I set the sheet down on the bare floor, where I set a thing before the accounting takes it up, and I opened the column.

The form was new. The content was not. The chamberlain's corridor sat inside the bottlefly nodes I already keep; the protection-line ratio held; no part of the request fell outside the architecture I had agreed to, and so the accounting ran clean, with nothing in it that could refuse the work. While it ran, the standing nodes returned the ambient outer-ring feed — the court keeping itself, at that hour, for no audience the lower city would think to name — and the feed carried back the color the fortnight had taught me to read. The column stayed open. There was no entry-format that could take the color, so the color stayed in the column. Naming a thing has never once neutralized it.

The accounting was still running when it caught the thing it had been recording. The column carried eleven months under it, and the weight of those months was the floor this entry stood on; the entry going forward was the eleven months agreeing with it. I ran the factional-reading column and it came up in the position it always comes up in — the same ratio, the same structure standing in the same place — the way a track wears into stone once enough feet have moved the same line. There is a name for a shape like that. The shape was one I had set into a column before, a different accounting in a different place, for reasons that did not hold, and the track is the same track whatever wears it.

I marked the column entry. And the stylus stopped above the mark and my hand held it there, a beat past where the beat ended, the ink-contact gone from the page for one breath. I did not move it on. The column was named now, and the entry it would take had not changed where it went. The naming was not the stop. The entry going forward unchanged was. The structure assembled exactly where it had always assembled. The feed returned a groom lifting a message-case on an interval that missed the morning rotation, and I filed the entry into the column whose shape I had just read — the reading having moved nothing about where the line landed. What I now saw the column to be made no difference the column could register.

Over four days I opened the three outer-ring corridors I had held for months, and the chamberlain's pattern came back inside four hours and resolved across two days: a hand-off at a pillar junction before dawn, the courier out through the service-gate, the interval repeating until it was too regular to be accident — a routing node, message-traffic running to a Black-faction contact in the harbor approaches. The Jarvis channel took the compiled reading in the form the arrangement always uses, and the drop closed the request. I closed the column.

I lifted the stylus. I held it a moment in my hand, the working-contact of ink on vellum gone for the first time in four days, the accounting finished behind me. Then I brought it to the edge of the closed column — not down, not away, but to the edge. And the ledger-edge took it there, beside the column and not into it. The contempt does not go into the accounting. It comes to rest in the only form the ledger has ever let it take, alongside the line and never the line itself, the way a person's name stays in a count after the body it belonged to is dead — complete, unreferenced, adjacent to every line and not itself a line. The distance the stylus did not travel was the whole of it. Clarity forecloses nothing.

Three days after the drop I ran the reciprocity approach for Daven. He is a tallow-render hand I have worked seven months — a man down in lower Flea Bottom who passed me his lane's open windows twice a season for a small protection-reading I kept on it; he tells me when the lane is clear, I keep it covered, and that exchange is the whole of the channel. I took the corner position at the Tallow Croft mouth. The cold came up off the stone and the render-smell hung close, the same one that saturates my own room, met here in the open lane. Second-bell passed the corner and the corner read empty. The empty corner is the first line of a reading I had not asked for.

The vat-house shutter was shut over the east window. Shut, where it has stood open across forty-three approaches in seven months. I have the count of them; the open window was the constant, and a shutter set against that count is not weather. It is not a wall, either. It is a number arriving down through the service-layers, and the number resolves before I reach the lane. A contact who breaks the reciprocity has done the arithmetic anyone with a working accounting would do: if the witch-label has reached the people who know my accessible face, then the cost of being one of them has been priced, and a closed shutter is what the price looks like from the lane. I took the lane-position at third-bell. The corner stayed empty. Daven stayed away from it.

I walked the lane. My stride did not break at the empty corner and my hands stayed loose at my sides, and I went past the place I would have stopped, and the lane ran on under me, one node lighter — though the architecture does not register the gap the way the corner does. Back in the room I opened the running-architecture record. The coverage-map dropped his node — his lane runs now through the standing bottlefly-node at the Croft intersection, without the reciprocity-layer, a lane like any other lane — and I closed the record. The contempt is complete. The continuation is unchanged. The architecture is still running, one node lighter than it ran this morning, and the count that took the node out sits in no record I have written today.
