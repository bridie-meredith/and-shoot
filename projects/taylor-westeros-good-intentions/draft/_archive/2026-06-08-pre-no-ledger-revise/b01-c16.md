The storehouse eaves laid their shadow across the cleared ground, and the corner was the corner it always was. The reed-mat for the dying lay rolled against the storehouse wall, no one on it today. Two crates stood stacked at the wall's foot where the kneelers used them as benches. An old man knelt at one of them and straightened his back as I came on; a woman stood near the wall with a child who had stopped growing, and the child pressed itself into her side. I came in on the diagonal I always came in on, west-precinct coverage, the corner one more point on the pass. I was not there for Halvard. The flies on the eaves returned me the three bodies and the two adjacent lanes before I had crossed half the ground — I had everyone placed while the old man was still finding his back. Then Halvard finished whatever low thing he had been saying to the old man and lifted his head, and the morning became the thing it became. The clearing-bodies were in the feed before he looked up. I had them counted while he was still deciding whether to speak.

He fell into step with me. Not in front of me, not blocking — beside me, matched to my pace, walking the counter at my own speed where I could not set it down and move past it. He named the ones who had lived through the autumn.

"The ones who lived through the autumn. The woman with the chest-complaint in Muggers' Row. The boy from the fishmonger's lane — he's still on it, still gutting fish, I saw him a sennight past. I'll not claim them. I don't know what you did or didn't do, and I'm not asking. I have not counted them, either. It is not my count to run. The count I run is what it costs them, after. What they understand about how they survived. Some of them understand things about this precinct that I did not think anyone who arrived the way you arrived would know."

The counter landed clean and posted to the wrong account. It answered the contempt and not the continuation, and the continuation was older than any naming of it; I had had the answer to it since the third time he found me at this corner, the words changing each time and the account they posted to never changing at all. The rendering-yard fence ran along the clearing's far margin, the scene's edge and its way out. The old man rose and planted himself at the clearing-edge near the fence, a body where a body would be. I set my hand to the fence-rail and pressed. The iron held the cold the way the stone under it held the cold, and it took the weight while the arithmetic finished — what the position was worth to me now against what it cost to hold, and the number on the right had moved, not by a margin that triggered some other accounting, only by enough to notice. Worth less to hold than it cost.

Halvard stopped. He turned and faced me, his weight even on both feet, his hands open at his sides. I closed my hand on the rail.

"I am not asking you to do differently. I've no better way to give you, and I'll not pretend I have. I am asking you to know what you're doing. That's the whole of it."

It was the only honest thing he had said, and it was not live. Knowing what I did had never been the variable that stopped me. I let the rail go.

I stopped. Not a pause — a stop, my feet settling even on the dirt, the standing finishing the way a thing stands when it has stopped weighing. There was no answer that was also a continuing, and so there was no answer. The flies on the eaves returned him to me, standing where he stood, mid-wait, the stillness of a man who has said his thing and waits on it; the distance to him grew in the count and the count kept him, a body that stayed where I had set it, still numbered behind me. I turned. Not away from the argument — away from the man — and I walked back across the cleared ground toward the rendering-yard approach at the pace I had come in with. The reed-mat lay rolled against the wall. The old man stood facing the clearing-edge as I crossed. I reached the rendering-yard fence at the gap. Halvard cleared the eaves-line and passed out of the feed; he kept his face to the eaves and did not turn his head after me, and he did not call out. I ran the next circuit-segment. The count picked up where the circuit always picked it up.
