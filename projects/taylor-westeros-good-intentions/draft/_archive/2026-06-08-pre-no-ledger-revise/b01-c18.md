*The wards are mapped now, and the channel runs both ways. The Hand-who-was still works through Jarvis: he takes my counting and pays for it by keeping Sera's parentage-question quiet, and that holds only while my deliverables hold. East of the water-gate there is the one lane I have never covered, because it is Wren's, and Wren still moves through it screened — her passage written into the record under Norren's name, a man with a real reason to be there. And this, now, is the call on the arrangement at its full agreed scale: not a ward, not a cluster, but all of it.*

---

Before dawn I lifted the cipher-bundle off the floor, the paper cold and stiff in the dark, and read the margin-cipher addendum that had come with it. It was written in the addendum hand, which meant the request had not originated with Jarvis; it had only passed through him. The cipher opened onto the succession-mechanism column, and what stood there was not a query but a specification — full coverage, every ward, every channel, every feed-node at once, held for a fortnight, the lower-city picture made complete before a document moved through the Small Council. I reopened the addendum and read it again. The arrangement could not proceed otherwise; the mechanism needed the picture, and I had agreed to it at the price I agreed to it. So I folded the bundle and set it back on the floor without answering, and stood over it with my weight even on both feet, and named to myself the count the addendum called for. It was a size I had held in my hand before. The number stood in the room, and the answer was deferred, not absent.

Before first light I opened the bottlefly routes under the Fishmonger Gate, and the moth-corridor through the chandler quarter, and the feed-lines reaching out into the outer-gate corridors where the stone went grey before the grey of dawn. Not gate by gate. The one lane I skipped was the eastern one, because the gap was Wren, and Wren crossed it screened — and the rest of the field ran at maximum density on every side of her, so the blank was not the architecture's weakness but its most precise instrument. The moth-corridor returned noise: a smelt-fire in the chandler quarter bleeding a low, acrid interference into the channel. The architecture routed around it. The friction was real, and it did not stop anything.

This time it opened all of it at once — not ward by ward, one cluster at a time the way I had run it for months, but every node together. I opened the nodes and the count crossed the threshold I had set for myself, and the configuration changed from a thing I carried to a thing that held the city, and the shape of it was older than this work. The count ran past the number I had reached once before, in a different architecture, in a different place, for reasons that did not hold; and the line was behind me now, and the architecture did not register the crossing the way I did. I extended the count into the fortnight, and the cipher-bundle dropped.

What the network returned over six days at full density was the court apparatus entire — not specimens but the machinery whole. A groom whose saddlebag-hour did not match the tilting-yard schedule. A maid whose irregular corridor indexed to three contacts, two Green and one not. A knight whose left-hand pommel-grip belonged to a method for enclosed spaces the tilting yard does not teach. A septa whose daily timing mapped to a ward-elder handoff interval I knew from Flea Bottom — the court machinery in the ward machinery's shape, only the cloth was finer. The four resolved into one system. I filed the court-entry, and then the opening-entry, and the feed returned the apparatus complete, the same shape as the wards at every scale at once. The contempt arrived not as something I felt but as the picture completing: I was reading it, for it, while it read nothing of me at all, and there was no exit attached to knowing it. On the seventh day I filed the court-entry again. It was the same act it had been on the first, the same hand, the same column — and the sameness was the whole of it.

From the ninth day through the twelfth I routed the compiled picture through the dead drop in three drops over a single day and a night, the packet left clear in the trough, and Jarvis collected it. The document the Greens were moving needed a particular window open in the Small Council to pass — a specific composition on a specific day — and my fortnight-picture was what told them the window was clear. On the eleventh day the counter-bundle came back with a single margin-cipher line. I lifted it and read it: the document had moved. The window my picture had named had cleared, and the succession channel was the Greens' now, and the move did not come back. I closed the record without writing the line into it. The thing existed whether I named it there or not, and the ward was still running at full density while I did not.

On the fourteenth day I closed the network down ward by ward, the way I had built it — the ward-elder routes first, the eastern gap last, still blank, held that way through the whole fortnight under a name I had set down myself that had never been the true one. The chandler-quarter moths settled back into the eaves, the sustained hum of two weeks of coverage thinning down into the buildings the network lived in. I opened the cost-ledger column and ran the accounting against the drawdown. The protection-delivered line closed the column at the edge where the contempt stopped being a thing that could be added to. The column received the contempt-entry, complete and named, with no mechanism for refusal attached. I wrote the standdown line in the same hand that had opened the deployment, because once the picture was delivered, the position that had held the whole city's coverage had nowhere left to stand. The ledger closed the disposal-calculus entry: the more weight I carried in the work, the more cleanly I could be set down when the work was done. Then I passed the column where the recognition would have gone, and left it blank — set down every other entry true and left that one empty, and the blank was the most load-bearing line on the page. I set the stylus beside the closed ledger, not away from it. The deployment was the irrevocable one, and the ledger closed clean, and both were true.
