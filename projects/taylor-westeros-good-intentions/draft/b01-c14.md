*The wards were mapped and the channel ran both ways now. Otto's apparatus — the Hand-who-was, still working through Jarvis — took my counting of the lower city and paid for it by keeping Sera's parentage-question quiet, and that holding lasted only as long as my work was good. East of the water-gate the lanes still ran a Black-faction courier I had watched eleven weeks and named to no one. And there was Wren, whose route I covered and had never once let myself reckon — a woman I kept off every page on purpose.*

---

The fly settled on the corner-shelf above the stall-door, low and back, and the door stood open to the lane-light. The oil-cloth square lay flat on the counter where it always lay, the working-surface set out for a day's trade. The bolt-end samples hung from the hook. The bag-peg on the lane-side wall faced the lane and held nothing. One thing missing against a full array of staged goods, and the missing one was the cured-leather work-bag a man takes when he means to go and leave the stock he means to sell.

I swept the fly across the lane-mouth and sent the insects east along the approaches, through the net I had run him in four weeks — a counter, not a runner, a man who held the shapes of who walked where and carried no names in his mouth. He was nowhere the round put him. Not the round shoulders, not the red stripe on the collar. Not at the chandler's two stalls down where he sometimes stopped of a midmorning. Nowhere. I lifted the fly off the corner-shelf.

The apparatus does not strip its own people bare, so the thing that had moved him came the other way. Someone on Dragonstone's side had read that the Green count was live in these lanes, and the reading had reached him, and he had run before the second bell of the day it reached him. The Dance had come into the lane the way pressure always comes to a man who holds shapes and not names — not as a knock, but as an absence: a bag gone, a stock left, a stall that would not open again. I had read this shape before, in a war whose dates I carry and do not say.

Withholding was a real road here. But the apparatus had the figure either way; only Sera's guarantee turned on which road I took.

Jarvis set the packet at the channel-station before the second bell, earlier than his schedule, and the early hour was itself a thing to read: the request had been moved up the line before I had so much as broken the seal. The wax cracked dry under my thumb. The sheet named the east-of-water-gate gap-lanes by their designation and asked me to confirm a counter-adjacent courier the apparatus had partly traced — a man I had tracked eleven weeks, whose route ran the gap-lane to the tallow-chandler's back approach and north along the rendering-yard margin, where the overhangs come close and the insects stop being insects.

He came round the water-gate corner in my reading of him and lifted his chin, and there it was, the left eye set lower than the right, the small asymmetry that made him look forever like a man weighing something. He paused at the second junction the way he always paused, and shifted the weight of the satchel before he turned north. Eleven weeks of that face, of that pause, none of it on the sheet. The sheet had not given him to me. I had given him to myself, in weeks of coverage I never wrote into anything a courier carried out, and I had held his face and his route since the fifth week and told no one. I set the sheet flat on the working surface. The apparatus had named him through some channel that was not me. Which meant it could move without me, and the man was a dead road whichever way I turned.

I set the packet beside the blank response-sheet and held the stylus over the empty page. The road that did not write him was a real road — I would not let it read as closed before I had honestly stood in it — and standing in it I could see where it went: the lane shut to the Black faction regardless, the man traced by other means in his own time, and Sera's guarantee stepped down from a thing that held to a thing that might. Both roads took the man. Only Sera stood between them. I lowered the stylus to the working surface and set my hand on the response-sheet.

I named the harm to myself before I wrote a stroke of it, the way I made myself name it every time. One man, courier-adjacent, taken — taken, not a worse word, because the apparatus runs takings and not killings for his sort, and I had the record that said so. I held the harm in the open long enough to read all of it: a face with a low-set eye, a route, a habit of pausing to shift his weight, eleven weeks of small bodies in the overhangs above a head that did not know my name, a man doing the ordinary work of carrying word through a city where carrying word for the wrong people is a thing they can charge you for. I did not let myself take less than the whole of it. Then I named the gain. Sera's guarantee, lifted from contingency back to active, one woman kept clear of a parentage-charge she could not lift off herself by anything she did. The two were not close. The harm was real and entered and the gain was real and entered, and the weight went where weight goes.

I wrote the lane-designation. I wrote the weight-adjustment, the shift at the second junction that crossed my tracking against the apparatus's partial trace. I wrote the low-set left eye — the most particular thing I had kept for eleven weeks, set down now as the instrument that fixed him. I did not write his name. I never knew it. I wrote what I knew. The wax gave warm under my thumb where the surface had been firm, the one soft give in the room, and I set the response-sheet at the station edge for Jarvis. Then I held the stylus over the page a moment and added no line. The reckoning was exact. The exactness was the part I could not put down.

The following evening a bottlefly gripped the overhang timber above the second junction, set there for pattern-coverage and not for this, and it caught the thing the way a fly catches anything in front of it. Two men came into the lane from the south approach. The man rounded the water-gate corner at his usual time. He lifted both hands to the satchel-strap the way he always did, the way I had watched him do it eleven weeks of Tuesdays and Thursdays — and the two men were at the junction before his hands had finished. He walked between them, back toward the south, the satchel still on his shoulder, the route he had walked all those Tuesdays ending at the junction where he always stopped. The lane emptied behind them.

I lifted the bottlefly off the timber and brought the record back to the working surface. The man was a finished thing now, taken and accounted, and finishing him put me back among the others I keep in the same hand — and there was the one I keep open. A route I track, a route I cover, a route I have never once carried out into anything for anyone: Wren, running the same logic as the man just closed, and not on any page. I held the two of them in one look, the closed one and the open one, the named harm I had paid for and the harm I would not let myself name; and behind them, for one breath, a different city, a different count, a different person left un-named at the foot of the sum the war needed — the same arithmetic at another scale, and that older one had never gotten a name either. I held the breath through the look and opened nothing for Wren. I let the breath go and took my hand off the surface.

I knew the recognition was there. I have had the architecture of it before, and I did not let the shape settle, because if it settled it would be as accurate as everything below it, and there is no page in me that can carry that. So I left it un-settled and ran the rest of it out, the part that needs no hand: there was no road that did not go through the apparatus, and no thing I could do that cleared the confirmation already given. The sum had been waiting at the foot of the column all along, and I had been keeping the word off it. Now the lane at the second junction stood empty of him and the word stood plain. Non-extractable. It was in me without my writing it. I set the stylus down.
