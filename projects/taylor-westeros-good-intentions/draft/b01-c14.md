*The wards were mapped and the channel ran both ways now. Otto's apparatus — the Hand-who-was, still working through Jarvis — took Taylor Hebert's counting and paid for it by keeping Sera's parentage-question quiet; that arrangement held only while her deliverables held. East of the water-gate the lanes still ran a Black-faction courier she had watched eleven weeks and never entered into anything. And there was Wren, whose route she covered and had never once priced — a person-shaped item the ledger had simply left open.*

---

The fly took the corner-shelf above the stall-door, low and back, and the stall-door stood open to the lane-light. The oil-cloth square was still on the counter where it always lay, the working-surface set out for a day's trade. The bolt-end samples hung from the hook. The bag-peg on the lane-side wall faced the lane and held nothing. One missing object against a full array of staged goods, and the one that was gone was the cured-leather work-bag a man carries when he means to leave and not the stock he means to sell.

I swept the fly across the lane-mouth and sent the adjacent insects east along the approaches, through the lane-net I had run him in for four weeks — a counter, not a runner, a man who held shapes and not affiliations — and he was nowhere the passive circuit put him. Not the round shoulders. Not the red collar-stripe. Nowhere. I released the fly from the corner-shelf. The read was done. The apparatus does not expose its own nodes, so the thing that had moved him was not the apparatus he served but the inference coming the other way: a reading, Dragonstone-side, that the Green count was active in these lanes. The Dance had reached the merchant and the merchant had run, and I closed the passive-count entry in the same flat hand I used for any coverage loss. A node foreclosed. The Dragonstone inference registered. The coverage paid for with exposure, and the stall that would not reopen. I had read this shape before, in a war whose dates I carry and do not say aloud.

Withholding is a real choice here — but the apparatus has the figure either way; only Sera's guarantee turns on which path I take.

Jarvis set the packet at the channel-station before the second bell, and the early hour was itself a figure: the request prioritized, the timing a data point before I had broken the seal. The seal broke under my thumb with a small dry crack. The sheet named the east-of-water-gate gap-lanes by their designation and asked confirmation on a counter-adjacent courier-figure the apparatus had partially traced — a man I had tracked eleven weeks, whose route ran the gap-lane to the tallow-chandler's back approach, who paused at the second junction to adjust the weight he carried. The figure rounded the water-gate corner in my reading of him and raised the chin, and it was the left eye, set lower than the right; eleven weeks of that face, none of which the request had given me. He paused at the second junction. He adjusted the satchel-weight.

I set the packet beside the response-sheet. I lifted the stylus above the sheet and held it there, above the blank, the alternative open and genuine: the lane closed regardless, but Sera's guarantee stepped from active to contingency if I let it. Then the stylus came down to the working surface, because the door had never been on the figure. The apparatus had him through its own channel; it could act without me. Only the protection-column distinguished the two roads now. I set my hand on the response-sheet.

I brought the stylus to the left column and marked the left margin — the deliberate opening of the cost-column, the same gesture I made every time. The left column took the harm. One gap-lane figure, courier-adjacent, detained; not a worse word than detained, and not a better one. I set the wrist down on the left column and held it there and let the entry take its full weight — a face, a route, a pause-habit, eleven weeks of overhang-feed above a head that did not know my name — and I did not let it take less. Then I brought the stylus across to the right column, and the stylus stopped above the closed entry, above Sera's guarantee elevated from contingency to active. The columns were not close.

I wrote the lane-designation. I wrote the weight-adjustment detail, the adjusted load at the second junction that cross-validated my tracking against the apparatus's partial trace. I wrote the low-set left eye — the most specific thing I had withheld for eleven weeks, set down now as the instrument that confirmed him. The wax gave under my thumb as I pressed the seal, warm and yielding where the surface had been firm, the give of it the one soft thing in the room, and I set the response-sheet at the station edge for Jarvis. I entered the breach-column record. Then I lifted the stylus above the breach-entry and held it there and added no line. The accounting was exact, and the exactness was the part I could not set down.

The following evening a bottlefly gripped the overhang timber above the second junction, placed for pattern-coverage and not for this, and it caught the thing as found geometry, the way a fly catches anything. Two men came into the lane from the south approach. The figure rounded the water-gate corner at his usual time. He lifted both hands to the satchel-strap the way he always did, the way I had watched him do it eleven weeks of Tuesdays and Thursdays, the same gesture firing once more at the one time it was the last — and the two men were at the junction-vertex before his hands had finished. He walked between them, back toward the south, and the lane emptied.

I released the bottlefly and closed the cl04 outcome-entry, in the format the ledger keeps for finished things. And the format went back to the column it shared and found the open one written in the same hand: a route tracked, a route covered, never once entered into a deliverable — running the same logical function as the entry just closed. I lifted the two column-positions into one look, the closed one and the open one, one route entered and one route never entered, a count in a different shape; and behind them, for one breath, a city, a different kind of count, a different kind of named-and-unpriced, the same arithmetic at another scale, and the older one did not acquire a name. I held the breath through the look and did not open the second entry. I let the breath go as the hand came off the surface.

The exit-column ran where it always ran, down to the bottom: no path that did not go through the apparatus, no mechanism that cleared the confirmation already given. This was not new arithmetic. It was the sum I had been not-writing, the one I had let stand at the foot of the column without the word on it. The second-junction lane went silent, the lane where he always paused now empty of him. Non-extractable. The word was in the record without being written. I released the stylus.
