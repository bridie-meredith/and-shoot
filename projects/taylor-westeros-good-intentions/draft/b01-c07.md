*Two months on, and the accounting has gone quiet — not because it stopped costing, but because the cost had become the shape of an ordinary morning, and a cost you no longer feel is the one that has finished its work on you. The bay-wind had turned damp in those two months; the carter-breath stood visible over the shafts and the sept-bells kept the long count of the season the Faith calls the Crone's stretch. Nothing on the feed told me an argument was waiting at the sept corner. The feed could not read the kind of thing that was.*

The morning circuit returned the four absences before it returned anything else. The Hook feed came in at its standard density and the count opened against its slots the way it opened every morning, and four of the slots did not fill — four recurring bodies the count had logged a hundred-and-eighty mornings running did not cross their thresholds. The feed filed the gap as filled-slot-pending and went on. I did not name what I knew. The count carried the absence forward into the body of the morning and the morning closed around it the way mornings close around any entry the ledger has already paid for.

The substrate had shifted at the rendering-yard junction. New bodies were in the lanes the four had held — a different forward-lean at the carter's near side, a patrol-pattern through the Hook coming back at a frequency the count had not read in the prior two months, the count filing them at filled-slot replacement without giving the replacement a name. The morning's coverage held to its standard density because the slots had been filled. The thing that had filled them was a different shape under the same density, and the count filed the shape and did not write what it filed.

At the chandler's-storehouse corner the smallfolk murmur ran one beat off the rhythm it kept on a Crone's-stretch morning. A word about a thing-uphill-in-the-night carried at corner level, short and unfinished, the way a thing carries when no-one in earshot can put a name to it but the absence of the name does not stop the carrying. I filed the murmur without engaging. The Septon's corner was past the cart and the count knew he was on it; the feed gave no reading that the murmur had reached him, and the count filed his hearing of it as the kind of thing the count cannot read into a man at distance.

I closed the ward-coverage circuit at the top of the morning and came down toward the corner the way I came down every day, and the way was blocked. A handcart sat across the passage where the chandler's storehouse abuts the sept, its bed wide enough that the choke narrowed to a single cart's width and the lane could not pass. The man who owned it stood at the shafts, and facing him, hearing him out, was Septon Halvard — the Faith's man for this stretch of the Hook, no formal sept to his name, just a corner of a chandler's storehouse and the sick he tends; he has held the precinct seven years and asks plainness of people, not agreement.

He was naming the man's child. A fever, he said, the way one says a thing that is simply true and known between them — a boy named Derry, up the lane, two days into it. He said it to me as much as to the carter, plainly, the way you acknowledge someone who belongs where they are standing. I took the acknowledgment and gave nothing back to it. The insect-feed had him placed and had placed him since the first week — a fixed point on the corner, a node the count had logged a hundred times. I knew him the way I knew the cold stone and the candle-smell off the storehouse: as a feature of the ground.

I came in past the cleared shaft of the cart, into the sept corner, where the sept-bay's shadow lay across the crossing stone and the lane-cold met the corner-cold at the threshold. I gave him the nod a person gives a fixture they have decided to greet.

He went on with the boy's fever — how far it had run, what it had taken, where it sat now. He named what the maester would cost to call, plainly, no appeal in it. Then he turned off the account of the sick child and faced me square, and his voice did not change register, which was how I knew the next part was not about the child at all.

"There's a man up the Lane runs errands for a house that pays him in bread, not coin," he said. "Good bread. His children eat. I've no quarrel with a man feeding his children. But the house pays him that way because it suits the house, and every loaf he takes, he takes on the house's terms, and the terms don't get smaller for the taking. They stay the size they were. A thing built crooked doesn't come straight because you lean on it gently. It grows crooked at its own rate. The slow way costs him more now — he'd go hungry a while, refusing. It costs him less after. The debt doesn't sit there gathering."

I went still. Not deliberating — the body simply arrested, the way it does when a thing you have been carrying gets set down in front of you in another man's hands. A crooked house does not come straight by leaning it gently; the leaning compounds at its own rate. That was the architecture of the thing I had built. He was reading my own ledger back to me in a stranger's mouth and did not know it, and the figures matched.

I faced him. The resentment I had filed for two months as a kind of weather had carried no object until that turn, and facing him handed it one — not the man, the structure he had just described. I had tracked his circuit for those two months as a node on a map and was looking at him as a man for the first time.

I planted my feet. The body's instinct was to route around the obstruction and close the circuit and be gone; staying was the more expensive option, and I paid it. He saw a woman who had decided not to leave the conversation, and what I gave him was the staying and not the rebuttal.

He let a breath out into the cold and did not press.

The ground cold came up through the soles where I stood, the corner stone gripping at the planted weight, firm and unwarmed.

I named the body count. My breath went out before the name did.

"Then the account has to carry the interval," I said. "Your grave-count comes in at the end. There's one in the interval. The Cobb girl — Wenna, six years old, Pig-Tallow Lane in the Hook. The fever season, two years back, before there was anyone here who could route the maester-call in time. By the time someone was found to carry it, it was a burial, not a call. The slower way kept its principles intact for the eleven days it took her to die. She's the first name in the count."

The name went down the way an entry goes into a ledger. A child, a lane, a fever two seasons back my coverage had not yet been here to route against — the founding entry, the one the slower way had left unpaid and I had not, which is the whole reason the count is mine to keep. The person was not a category. The precision was the entire cost of keeping the count honest. And under the entry, an older shape: a smaller door that had stayed shut on someone I could not reach in time, the same arithmetic in another tongue.

His hands went still on his knees.

Two accountings stood in the cold and neither was paid. His slower way carried a body it could not answer for. Mine carried a coverage that answered for that body and compounded the corruption at its own rate to do it — and what the engagement cost me was the clean knowledge of the road I had chosen not to take, which I let cost that much and did not write down.

"I know the slow way has a cost," he said. "I'll not pretend I don't. You've named it, and I'll carry the name. I've buried by the slow way before. What I can't do is the other thing — take the quick way and watch the debt go on growing past where I can see the end of it. I don't know where it stops. That's the truth of it. I haven't an answer that makes your dead breathe. I've only the one I can live beside."

My feet had begun to shift toward the lane, and I settled the heel back instead, the weight pressing down at the cobble's edge. The natural close was departure and I declined it. He read the staying the way he had read the planting — two people who had taken each other's measure seriously and resolved nothing, which was the thing the architecture I ran was built to prevent and could never have produced.

Then I left, before a resolution that was not coming. The leaving was the filing: the argument left available, not closed, and I knew I was leaving it available the way I knew the range limit on the count. This was the road I was not taking, and the knowing was not the same as the taking. I went out by the cross-lane mouth, the exit that cleared his sight line.

Behind me the lane-cold settled back into the corner stone where it had held all morning. I cleared the Hook and went up into the rest of the day.
