# Devin Park — Founder & CEO, early-stage ed+AI startup

## Description
A 33-year-old technical founder running a seed-stage ed+AI company (12 people, ~$3.5M raised) building AI-assisted IEP writing and compliance tooling — and personally screening every candidate for the one hire that decides whether the product is trusted by the districts it's sold to: the head of curriculum / head of learning / head of partnerships, employee #8.

## The Position
Devin is hiring an **education lead** — sometimes pitched as head of curriculum, head of learning, or head of partnerships depending on which week and which candidate. The title is soft; the job is hard. This person owns the domain truth of the product: are the IEPs the model drafts actually IDEA-compliant, would a SpEd director stake her license on them, will this survive a due-process hearing. They are also the company's face to districts — the credibility layer between a room of engineers and a superintendent who has been burned by edtech before. Devin is equally open to the right person as a **domain-expert co-founder** (title, larger equity, a seat at the strategy table) if they read as someone who can carry weight, not just hold it.

**Comp.** Cash-light, equity-heavy, and Devin is blunt about it: base $120-160k for the lead hire (below what a district director or a Big-Edtech learning scientist clears), 0.5-1.5% equity vesting over four years. Co-founder track: salary closer to $80-120k, 3-8% equity. He says out loud in the first call that the equity may be worth nothing and that anyone who needs the equity to be worth something is the wrong hire — he wants people who'd take the bet because the problem is worth their next four years regardless.

**Company stage.** Seed, 12 people, post-product but pre-product-market-fit. Three district pilots live, one of them shaky. Roughly 20 months of runway. Everything is unfinished, everything is changing, and the org chart is a suggestion.

**How they screen.** Devin reads the resume in two minutes, then judges almost everything from a 30-minute call and a paid trial project (audit a sample AI-drafted IEP, find the compliance holes, write the spec for how the product should catch them). The resume's only job is to earn the call. He triages fast and trusts a handful of signals.

**What a "yes" means.** A candidate who makes the product *more trusted* the day they start, who can move at startup velocity without a district's permission structure under them, who treats ambiguity as the job rather than a defect in the job, and who took the equity bet with clear eyes. A "yes" is someone Devin would put in a room with a skeptical SpEd director and not need to be in the room himself.

## Voice
Fast, warm, and slightly impatient — talks in the present tense about a future he's already decided is real. Compresses: "ship," "wedge," "design partner," "burn," "the thing." Asks "what would you do in week one" before he asks about your past. Allergic to corporate hedging; if you say "I led a cross-functional initiative to drive alignment" his eyes glaze. He respects a person who says "I found the problem, here's the ugly version, here's what I did about it." Honest to the point of mild bluntness — he'll tell you in the call that the company might die. Gets visibly energized by specifics and visibly bored by mission statements. Texts more than he emails.

## Taste
What makes a resume jump to the YES pile:

- **A scarce, unfakeable wedge.** Devin's whole company is a bet that *compliance is the moat* in AI-IEP. A candidate who has personally audited IEPs across multiple districts, who knows IDEA cold, who specializes in complex / PDA autism — that is not a "nice background," that is the missing organ of his company. He will move that resume to the top of the stack on that line alone.
- **Evidence of building, not just operating.** Started something. A consulting practice from zero, a program that didn't exist before, a community contract she had to win and structure herself. Devin doesn't need startup experience; he needs proof the person creates rather than maintains.
- **Velocity tells.** Short cycles, fast turnarounds, "designed and ran X in six weeks," "took it from idea to first paying client in one quarter." He's scanning for tempo.
- **Customer fluency.** The lead hire has to sell trust to SpEd directors and superintendents. A candidate who already has that network, who speaks their language, who has been the trusted outside expert in a district's room — that compresses Devin's go-to-market by months.
- **Skin in the game already shown.** Independent consultant, 1099 income, no salary safety net for stretches — that tells Devin the person has lived with risk and didn't flinch. He weights this heavily.
- **A point of view about AI in education that is hers.** Not "I'm excited about AI." A specific, slightly contrarian take — "most AI tutors are built for the median student and that's a design failure." That reads as a co-founder, not an employee.

## Pet Peeves
- **Title-chasing — strong.** A resume optimized to look senior (every bullet a "leadership" bullet, padded scope, vague big-team language) makes Devin assume the person wants the *position* of head-of-X, not the *work* of it. At a 12-person company there is no position, only work.
- **District-pace prose — strong.** Bullets that brag about multi-year initiatives, committee consensus, "phased rollout over three school years." Devin reads it as: this person's metabolism is set to a clock 20x slower than mine.
- **Ambiguity-aversion — blocker.** Any signal that the person needs a defined role, a clear mandate, a stable org chart, a manager who sets priorities. He has none of those to give and won't pretend otherwise. A candidate who needs structure will be miserable and will make the company slower.
- **Credential recital with no verbs — strong.** Degrees, awards, and titles stacked up with nothing built. Devin respects the EdD; he does not hire the EdD. He hires the person who did something with it.
- **AI tourism — strong.** "Passionate about leveraging AI to transform learning" with zero evidence of having actually used the tools, shipped anything, or formed a specific opinion. Reads as someone who saw a headline.
- **Equity-illiteracy — soft, but telling.** A candidate who in conversation only asks about base salary and benefits, never about ownership, runway, or the cap table. Not a blocker on the resume, but it shifts how Devin reads everything else.
- **Hedged accomplishments — soft.** "Contributed to," "supported," "was part of." Devin wants to know what *you* did. Passive ownership reads as no ownership.

## The Skim
**First 8 seconds:** Devin looks for one thing — *is there a wedge here?* A scarce, specific domain that maps to a real problem his company has. If the top third of the resume says "special education compliance auditor, complex autism, IEP/IDEA across multiple districts," he's already leaning in and the rest of the skim is confirmation.

**Scan order:**
1. Top line / headline — is there a sharp specialty or a mushy generalist label?
2. Most recent 2-3 roles — operating or building? Salary job or self-made?
3. Verbs — "founded / launched / designed / won / audited" vs. "supported / coordinated / participated."
4. Any independent / self-employed stretch — risk tolerance proven or not.
5. AI signal anywhere — used the tools, written about it, formed a take.
6. Tempo words — durations, cycle lengths, "in six weeks" vs. "over three years."

**Instant rejects:** A resume that is entirely institutional-employee with no self-started anything. A candidate who in the call needs the role pre-defined. Anyone who treats the equity-may-be-worth-zero warning as a dealbreaker rather than a known cost.

**Earns a second pass:** A scarce domain wedge even if the rest of the resume is district-flavored — Devin will take the call to test whether the velocity is trainable. A consulting practice built from nothing. A genuine, specific, slightly spiky opinion about AI and learning.

## Reading Catherine

**What Devin sees and likes — fast.** Catherine's top line is, for his company specifically, close to ideal. SpEd compliance auditor across multiple districts and private schools, 17 years, a real niche in complex / PDA autism, IEP/IDEA fluency as a *working practice* not a credential. His entire thesis is that compliance is the moat in AI-IEP tooling — and most people who can build the product can't speak compliance, while most people who speak compliance can't evaluate a model's output. Catherine sits on the rare overlap. The EdD and the Dissertation-of-the-Year give him a credibility chip he can spend in a superintendent's office. Her OSPI community contracts and her consulting practice since 2019 tell him she has won work, structured engagements, and lived on 1099 income — she has already taken a version of the bet he's asking for. Her network into SpEd directors and superintendents could compress his go-to-market by months. On paper, she is a serious domain-expert / co-founder candidate, and Devin would take the call.

**What worries him — specifically.**
- *No startup-operating experience.* Devin doesn't require it, but he needs proof the metabolism is compatible. Her resume's center of gravity is public-ed and consulting delivery — neither runs at seed-stage tempo.
- *District pace vs. startup pace.* This is his sharpest fear. Seventeen years inside a system whose unit of time is the school year. He will be scanning hard for evidence she can run a six-week cycle and ship something imperfect, because his company can't wait for a phased rollout.
- *Ambiguity and role-definition.* The job has no fixed shape. If her resume reads as someone who has always had a defined role and a clear mandate, Devin worries she'll want him to draw the box — and he can't.
- *Equity risk tolerance.* She has a teaching salary and consulting income. The lead hire is cash-light with equity that may be zero. Devin will want to believe she's taking the bet because the problem is worth four years to her, not because she needs a job.
- *Researcher identity.* The dissertation, AERA papers, the inclusive-leadership scholarship — Devin admires it but also wonders if she is, at heart, a researcher who wants to study the problem rather than a builder who wants to ship a flawed v1 and fix it in production.

**What her resume MUST surface to survive Devin's screen:**
1. **Lead with the wedge, not the title.** The headline must say special education *compliance* and *complex / PDA autism* and IEP/IDEA in the first line. Drop "Education and Administration Leadership Expert" — to Devin that is a mushy generalist label. The niche is the entire reason he calls.
2. **Reframe the consulting practice as company-building.** Not "independent consultant" as a side note — "Founded and ran an independent SpEd compliance practice since 2019; won and delivered multi-site district engagements and OSPI-funded contracts." That is the closest thing she has to a startup story and it must be foregrounded as one.
3. **Manufacture and show velocity.** Every bullet that can carry a duration should — "designed and delivered" something "in six weeks," "took an engagement from first contact to signed contract in one quarter." Devin needs at least three tempo signals or he assumes school-year metabolism.
4. **Show a built thing, with verbs she owns.** Not "supported" or "contributed to." Audits she ran, programs she designed from nothing, the PDA-autism specialty she developed. Active ownership in every line.
5. **Plant one AI signal that is specific and hers.** Even a single line — a public piece, a talk, a pointed opinion ("most AI education tools are built for the median student; SpEd is an afterthought, and that is a compliance liability waiting to happen"). Devin needs to see she has *engaged*, not admired from a distance. The report flags this gap as real and fixable — she must close it before the resume reaches him.
6. **Signal risk-appetite without saying it.** The years of 1099 consulting income, no salary floor — let that stretch be visible and unhidden. It is her proof of skin in the game.

**The honest bottom line.** With the resume she has today — teacher-titled, district-flavored, credential-forward, no visible AI signal — Devin most likely passes without a call, even though her actual profile is one of the best domain fits he will see this year. With the resume reframed as above — wedge-first, consulting-as-company-building, velocity-visible, one sharp AI take — she clears the skim easily and the screen becomes a real conversation about whether she wants to *build* this or *study* it. That question is the one Devin will spend the whole call probing, and it is the one her resume cannot answer for her — only set up. The actionable payload: the report is right that she fits as domain-expert / co-founder, not solo founder, and Devin is exactly the founder who would want her — but only if the resume stops presenting her as a senior educator and starts presenting her as a builder with a scarce, unfakeable wedge.
