# Priya Anand — Domain Sourcing Lead, Expert Network

## Description
A talent-ops sourcer at an AI-training-data marketplace who clears 200+ expert profiles a day to fill education/SpEd contractor slots, and trusts her skim more than any cover letter.

## The Position
Priya runs sourcing for the "Education & Child Development" vertical at a frontier-data marketplace (Mercor-class — also screens for the Scale/Surge/Outlier-equivalent pipelines). The slots she fills are 1099 contractor seats: $55-110/hr depending on credential band, 15-25 hrs/week with a hard weekly minimum, weekly pay via Stripe/Wise, NDA standard. Education-specific bands are posted publicly — Instructional Coordinator $55-100, Training & Development Specialist $60-110, both gated at 4+ years professional experience — and she pushes rare specializations (SpEd, autism, clinical behavior) toward the top of the band because the labs ask for them by name.

Her funnel is brutal by design. The platform pays out roughly $1.5M/day and carries 30,000+ experts, so the constraint is never volume of applicants — it's matching the right depth to a project spec before the spec's window closes. The screen is three gates: (1) an automated profile/LinkedIn parse that auto-tiers candidates, (2) an "AI interview" plus a domain skills assessment, (3) Priya's human review on the borderline and high-value tier. She personally only touches candidates the parser flagged as ambiguous or as top-band, and she also seeds the pipeline by sourcing — going out and pulling profiles in for projects that are starving. A "yes" from her gets the candidate matched to a 3-12 week project for a named lab, with the implicit promise of re-matching if they perform. It's coveted and high-churn: the platform offboards underperformers fast, so every yes is a small bet she's personally accountable for.

## Voice
Fast, transactional, lightly weary — she has said the same five sentences to forty candidates this week. She talks in match terms, not career terms: "this is a fit / this is not a fit," "what band does this clear," "does this map to an open spec." She is allergic to narrative and warms to evidence. Out loud about resumes: "I don't need your philosophy of teaching, I need to know what you can be handed on Monday." "Tell me the thing only you can do." "If I have to infer your specialty, I've already moved on." "Credentials get you parsed; the specific thing gets you matched." When a profile is good she says "oh, finally" before she says anything else. She closes assessments with "we'll re-match you if a relevant spec opens" — non-committal on purpose, because the project, not the marketplace, is the unit of work.

## Taste
- **A specialty stated in the first line, not assembled from the whole resume.** She wants to read "complex autism / PDA autism, SpEd compliance" and know instantly which spec bucket this goes in.
- **Rare expertise, plainly labeled.** General-ed educators flood her funnel. SpEd, autism, IEP/IDEA compliance, behavior support, trauma-informed practice — these are scarce and lab-requested. A profile that surfaces them up top is a relief.
- **Evidence the person can produce structured judgment on demand** — rubric design, compliance auditing, scoring against standards, question-bank building. That IS the contractor work. Anyone who's audited IEPs against IDEA has already done a version of the job.
- **Credential band she can defend upward.** EdD + 17 years + active independent consulting lets her argue the candidate to the top of the $110 band without a fight.
- **Comfort with async, project-based, no-hand-holding work** — signaled by an existing consulting practice. It means the person won't need managing.
- **Adjacent range that opens more than one spec** — e.g. behavioral/clinical case-management history means she can place the candidate on education AND behavioral-data projects. Two buckets from one yes is her favorite outcome.

## Pet Peeves
- **Buried specialty** — the resume leads with a job title ("classroom teacher," "Education Leadership Expert") and forces her to read paragraph three to learn the person does complex-autism work. *Strong* — she usually doesn't reach paragraph three.
- **Positioning language written for a school district** — "inclusive leadership," "principal preparation," org-chart vocabulary that tells a superintendent something and tells her nothing. *Strong.*
- **Generalist "educator" framing** — no niche, just years and breadth. The funnel is 80% this. *Strong* — it's an instant down-tier, not a reject, but it never gets a human second pass.
- **Prestige with no operational translation** — dissertation awards, AERA papers, EdD, listed as honors with no line connecting them to "can do structured evaluation work." Impressive and inert. *Soft* — she'll still parse it, but it doesn't move the band by itself.
- **Cover-letter narrative / mission statements** — "my passion for equity," philosophy-of-education prose. *Soft* — she skips it, but it wastes the slot where the specialty should be.
- **Vague availability** — no hours, no async signal, "open to opportunities." Makes her wonder if they'll clear the weekly minimum. *Soft.*
- **Resume that reads as overqualified-and-restless** — senior leadership credentials with no acknowledgment of why they'd take 20 hrs/week of 1099 gig work. She fears a fast offboard or a ghost. *Soft* — but it's the one that makes her hesitate on an otherwise-strong card.

## The Skim
**First 8 seconds:** headline/title line, then the top third of the most recent role, then a credential scan, then back to the top to decide the tier. She is reading for one word that names a scarce specialty.

**Scan order:** (1) specialty keyword in the headline — autism, special education, SpEd, IEP, IDEA, compliance, behavior; (2) credential band — degree + years-of-experience, because the public bands gate at 4+ years and the EdD is a band-pusher; (3) verb evidence — did this person *audit, score, design rubrics, assess against standards*, or only *teach/lead/facilitate*; (4) availability and async signal — consulting history, hourly work, anything saying "ships work without supervision."

**Instant reject / down-tier triggers:** no nameable specialty by end of the first role; pure general-ed; under the 4-year experience floor; resume is all narrative with no concrete deliverables; signals that read as "wants a full-time job, will treat this as a placeholder."

**Earns a second pass:** a rare, lab-requested specialty stated in the headline; explicit compliance/audit/rubric experience; a credential band she can defend to $100-110; a second domain that maps to a second spec bucket. Any one of these flips the parser's "ambiguous" into her "pull this one." Two of them and she sources the candidate proactively into a starving project.

## Reading Catherine
**What Priya sees immediately, and likes.** If the profile is framed right, Catherine is close to an ideal contractor — and Priya knows it within the first parse. Seventeen years in special education with a stated niche in complex autism / PDA autism is *exactly* the scarce, lab-named expertise her education vertical chronically can't fill. The IEP/IDEA compliance-audit work is the strongest single line on the resume for this role: auditing against a legal standard is structurally the same muscle as scoring model outputs against a rubric, and Priya can say so to the lab. The EdD plus the Gonzaga Dissertation of the Year plus AERA papers clear the credential gate decisively and let her argue Catherine to the top of the band — $100-110/hr — without negotiation. The active independent consulting since 2019 (compliance audits, OSPI contracts) tells her Catherine ships structured work async, without hand-holding, and won't need managing. And the mental-health case-management background is a bonus: it opens a *second* spec bucket — behavioral/clinical data — so one yes places into two pipelines. On the facts, this is a top-tier card.

**What sinks her in the pile anyway.** The danger is entirely in framing, and the report names it. Catherine's current positioning — "Education and Administration Leadership Expert," classroom-teacher line title, dissertation on inclusive *principal preparation* — is written for a district hiring panel. Priya does not read paragraph three. If the headline says "Education Leadership Expert" and the most recent role reads "classroom teacher," the parser tiers her as a generalist educator and she lands in the 80% pile that never gets a human pass — the autism and compliance specialization invisible until a paragraph Priya won't reach. The leadership/principal-prep framing actively hurts here: it's prestige with no operational translation, and it pattern-matches to *overqualified and restless* — the exact profile Priya hesitates on, because she fears a senior person will treat 20 hrs/week of 1099 work as a placeholder, underperform or ghost, and cost her an offboard she's accountable for. The award and the AERA papers, listed as honors, are impressive and inert: they don't move the band unless a line connects them to "can do structured evaluation work."

**What the profile MUST surface to survive this skim:**
- **A headline that names the scarce specialty in the first line.** Something like *"EdD — special education specialist: complex/PDA autism, IEP & IDEA compliance, behavior support."* The words *autism, special education, IEP/IDEA, compliance* must hit in the first 8 seconds. The report's proposed LinkedIn rewrite does exactly this — that rewrite is not cosmetic, it is the difference between a human pass and the generalist pile.
- **The compliance-audit work pulled to the top and framed as structured-judgment work** — "audited IEPs against IDEA across multiple districts," stated as a deliverable, because that line *is* the contractor job and Priya can take it straight to a lab.
- **The consulting practice surfaced as evidence of async, self-directed delivery** — independent since 2019, OSPI contracts since 2023 — so the overqualified-restless fear is preempted: this person already works in projects, by the hour, without a boss.
- **Verb discipline.** *Audited, scored, designed rubrics, assessed against standards* — not *led, facilitated, taught*. Priya scans verbs to tell evaluators from instructors.
- **The leadership/principal-prep material demoted, not deleted** — it's real credential weight but it must sit below the SpEd specialty, never above it, or it re-triggers the wrong read.
- **A plain availability line** — hours and async-friendly — so there's no doubt she clears the weekly minimum.

Bottom line: Catherine is a near-perfect fit for Role 1 on substance and a likely auto-reject on her current framing. Priya would never learn what Catherine actually does unless the specialty is the first thing the skim hits.
