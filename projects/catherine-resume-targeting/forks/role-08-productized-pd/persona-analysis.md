# Role 8 — Productized PD / Online Courses / Paid Community — Persona Analysis

Role 8 has no hiring manager. The "resume" is a capabilities artifact / pitch that
two distinct gatekeepers screen:

- **Camille Duarte** — Head of Instructor Partnerships at a Maven-tier cohort-course
  platform, who also reads nonfiction proposals. She decides whether to spend the
  platform's launch machinery (and a revenue share) on Catherine. She is a
  **platform gatekeeper** betting on distribution.
- **Denise Carver** — Director of Special Education in a 4,100-student Ohio
  district. She is the **end buyer** deciding whether to spend her discretionary PD
  line and her scarce time enrolling in Catherine's cohort.

They want opposite-feeling things from the same document, which is why this is
hard. Camille wants proof an audience already exists. Denise wants proof the
product is concrete, practical, and cheap on her time.

---

## Persona 1 — Camille Duarte (productized-PD gatekeeper)

### What she is looking for — the bar for a YES

A "yes" from Camille is **not** "your expertise is valid." A yes is: *"I believe
you can fill a cohort of 30-80 paying adults, twice a year, for at least three
runs — and I believe it mostly because you can already prove people are following
you."* She backs points of view that **already have an audience pulling on them**,
not credentials waiting to be discovered. She evaluates on four axes, in order:
**distribution → point of view → proven demand → content stamina.** A course slot
is a launch bet; a book is a two-year bet. She does not bet on potential.

To clear her bar Catherine needs four of five: a crisp contrarian thesis + a named
buyer with a deadline + any real audience signal (even small — ~800 of the *right*
subscribers) + evidence of paid demand + proof of consistent output (12+
consecutive weeks of publishing).

### Instant-reject triggers

- **No audience and no audience plan** — the controlling blocker. A course slot is
  a distribution bet; nothing to distribute to means no.
- **Credential-as-platform** — assuming an EdD, dissertation award, and AERA record
  substitute for reach. They are trust, not reach. *Blocker.*
- **"I'll build the audience after we launch"** — treating distribution as the
  platform's job. The platform amplifies an audience; it does not manufacture one.
  *Blocker.*
- **No receipts** — "huge demand for this" with no waitlist, no paid pilot, no
  sold-out workshop, no list. *Blocker.*
- **The syllabus pitch** — a Week-1/Week-2 outline instead of a thesis and a buyer.
  A fully built syllabus with zero demand evidence is *worse* than an honest sketch.
  *Strong.*
- **Cadence-untested expert** — never published consistently, yet pitching weekly
  cohort + newsletter + book. She has watched that stack collapse by week 6.
  *Strong.*
- **Flinch-from-commerce** — academic discomfort with pricing and the word "buyer."
  *Strong.*

### Top questions Camille will be asking

1. **"What's your list size?"** Is there a subscriber / follower / waitlist number
   anywhere in the first screen? (The Skim, step 2 — "If there is no number
   anywhere in the first screen, her interest drops by half.")
2. **"Show me the receipts — has anyone paid for a version of this?"** Consulting
   revenue, OSPI contracts, paid speaking all count as demand validation. (Taste:
   "Receipts of paid demand"; The Skim, step 4.)
3. **"Where's the cadence archive — can you ship every week for a year without
   burning out?"** Twelve weeks of dated, consecutive posts beats one brilliant
   essay. She is buying a habit, not a sample. (Axis 4: content stamina; Pet
   peeve: cadence-untested expert.)
4. **"What's the wedge — is there one sharp, slightly contrarian thesis I can
   paste on a landing page?"** A list of competencies reads "service provider"; a
   claim reads "creator." (Axis 2: point of view; Pet peeve: the syllabus pitch.)
5. **"Who's the buyer — named, with budget and a deadline?"** "SpEd directors at
   districts under a state AI mandate" beats "educators"; a buyer with Ohio's
   July-2026 deadline beats a buyer with an interest. (Taste: "a specific, named
   buyer.")
6. **"If this cohort is this fully designed, why hasn't it run once? Where are the
   people?"** The Round-2 feedback's central new risk — a polished named cohort
   with zero enrollment history sharpens the demand-gap question. (The question
   under the question.)
7. **"Is this a repeatable franchise or a one-off?"** Newsletter → cohort 1 →
   cohort 2 → book on one spine. She backs IP backbones, not single products.
   (Taste: "a repeatable franchise.")
8. **"Are you comfortable selling — do you flinch at pricing and the word
   'buyer'?"** If embarrassed to charge, the instructor undersells and quits.
   (Pet peeve: flinch-from-commerce.)
9. **"Did the conference talks generate pull — room counts, DMs, follow-up
   requests?"** "Accepted" is not "delivered"; she needs evidence anyone leaned in.
   (Round-2 fix: add room/engagement counts.)
10. **"Can you borrow an audience if you don't own one — a partner list, a
    co-host, a guest channel?"** Distribution is the one thing the platform cannot
    give; a credible borrow plan is the fallback to owning a list.

## Persona 2 — Denise Carver (cohort-PD buyer)

### What she is looking for — the bar for a YES

A "yes" from Denise is **not** "this looks interesting." A yes is *"I will block
the calendar, defend the line item, and tell my coordinators to clear
Thursdays."* She says it only when she can already **picture the specific artifact
she'll walk out with** — a policy template, an IEP-team script, a compliance
checklist — and when the instructor reads as someone who has **actually sat on her
side of an IEP table.** She is a tired buyer, not a tech buyer; time, not money, is
her binding constraint (~2 hours/week ceiling, fragile). She buys two or three PD
things a year out of a $9-14k discretionary line, and trusts a forwarded
recommendation from another SpEd director over any landing page.

To earn enrollment: SpEd named in the headline + a nameable verb-and-object
outcome + an instructor who has sat at the IEP table + honest small time math + a
concrete deliverable list + one peer she could phone + a sample she can inspect
before paying + a visible price.

### Instant-reject triggers

- **Generic-AI-with-a-SpEd-slide** — an "AI in Education" course where special
  education is one optional module. *Blocker.*
- **The hours lie** — "2 hours/week" when the work needs six, or "self-paced" with
  hard live deadlines. Discovering it mid-cohort makes her cancel and never trust
  the creator. *Blocker.*
- **Hidden / buried price** — a "discovery call" gate to learn the cost. She closes
  the tab. *Strong.*
- **No instructor track record on the actual job** — a bio that is all consulting
  buzzwords or all academic, nothing showing they ran an IEP team or owned
  compliance. *Strong.*
- **No deliverable** — all "frameworks," "mindset shifts," "rich discussion,"
  nothing she walks away holding. *Strong.*
- **Hype vocabulary** — "transformative," "revolutionary," "game-changing." Each
  lowers trust. *Strong.*
- **Stale AI claims** — 18-month-old screenshots, advice that ignores her state's
  actual mandate. *Strong.*
- **Niche stated as breadth** — "for all educators" / "school leaders" instead of
  "SpEd directors." Her interest halves in the first eight seconds. *Strong.*
- **The unknown creator with no proof of practicality** — first-time course-maker,
  no sample lesson, no peer to call. Not auto-no, but raises the bar steeply.

### Top questions Denise will be asking

1. **"Is 'special education' or 'SpEd directors' in the headline — do I see
   myself named?"** If it says "educators" or "school leaders," her interest halves
   before she reads further. (The Skim, step 1.)
2. **"What will I actually be able to *do* Monday — can I name the outcome with a
   verb?"** "Write the SpEd section of your district AI policy" / "audit an
   IEP-writing tool for IDEA compliance" beats "explore" or "understand." (The
   Skim, step 2; Taste: "a named, specific job-to-be-done.")
3. **"Who's teaching this — have they sat on my side of the IEP table?"** She
   scrolls straight to the bio for "ran IEP meetings," "compliance auditor,"
   "17 years in SpEd." Degrees alone do not clear it. (The Skim, step 3.)
4. **"What's the honest time commitment — stated, specific, and survivable in my
   two-hour week?"** "90 minutes/week, recordings posted within 24h, homework
   capped" earns trust; silence loses it — she assumes the worst. (The Skim, step
   4; Pet peeve: the hours lie.)
5. **"What's the price — is it on the page, and is there a team/PO-bundle rate?"**
   One seat is discretionary; two-plus needs a purchase order and a justification
   memo. She cannot start that math without a number. (The Skim, step 5;
   Round-2: "now the largest gap for her.")
6. **"What do I keep — is there a deliverable noun-list?"** A policy template, an
   IDEA-compliance checklist, a vendor-evaluation rubric, an IEP-team script. The
   noun list is what makes the price feel paid-for. (The Skim, step 6.)
7. **"Can I name one peer in a district like mine who did this and found it worth
   the time?"** A "SpEd coordinator, Westerville" beats any superintendent or
   vendor quote. (The Skim, step 7; Taste: "peer proof.")
8. **"Can I taste it before I pay — is there a free sample lesson or download?"**
   The way an unknown creator overcomes the no-track-record problem. (Reading
   Catherine, payload item 7.)
9. **"Does this know what year it is — does it cite my state's actual AI
   mandate and current tools?"** Stale advice is worthless to her. (The Skim,
   step 8; Pet peeve: stale AI claims.)
10. **"Is this genuinely SpEd-deep, or general-ed with a SpEd slide — and is there
    a real refund/drop policy?"** IDEA timelines, FAPE, behavior support, PDA
    autism depth is the whole reason she'd pay; a drop policy signals the creator
    isn't afraid of the product. (Pet peeve: generic-AI-with-a-SpEd-slide; Taste:
    "a real refund/drop policy.")

## Synthesis — what the two personas jointly demand

**They converge, from opposite directions, on one missing thing: proof that
someone has already gone through this cohort.** Camille needs it as demand
validation for a distribution bet; Denise needs it as the peer-she-can-phone that
an unknown creator must supply. A single edit — a **named pilot cohort,
participants listed by role and region** ("Pilot cohort, Spring 2026: 7 SpEd
directors and coordinators, WA/OR districts") — serves both at once and is the
highest-leverage line on the page.

**Beyond the pilot, the document is doing two jobs and pulling apart.** The same
confidence that moves Denise toward "yes" (a named, fully built, four-week cohort)
moves Camille's blocker into sharper relief ("if it's this finished, why hasn't it
run?"). The structural truth: this should ideally be **two artifacts** — a
cohort-facing pitch for Denise (price, deliverables, time math, pilot proof up
front) and a creator/platform pitch for Camille (audience numbers, cadence
archive, franchise spine). Held as one document, it must surface both.

**The shared non-negotiables both screeners enforce:**

- A sharp, one-sentence, contrarian thesis ("AI in schools is built for the median
  student; SpEd is the 14% it fails") — Camille's wedge, Denise's proof it isn't a
  generic deck.
- The **practitioner identity leading**, the EdD demoted to a credibility layer —
  Denise buys the auditor, not the academic; Camille reads credentials as trust,
  not reach.
- **Receipts of paid demand** — consulting since 2019, OSPI contracts since 2023.
- **Honest, modest framing** — no hype words (Denise's blocker), no overclaiming a
  product or audience that doesn't exist (Camille's "syllabus with extra polish"
  risk). Both reward honesty; both punish inflation.

**The timeline split they jointly imply:** Denise's gaps are copy-and-product
fixes closable in one revision pass — she is the **reachable buyer now**. Camille's
gap is a real-world action gap (an audience, a 12-month cadence archive) — she is
the **12-18-month play**. The next-steps and projected artifact treat Role 8
exactly as the opportunity report frames it: a slow compounding supporting move
whose near-term job is to lay the audience/IP foundation, not to launch a
profitable course.
