# Persona Analysis — Role 1: AI Training Contractor

The two gatekeepers for this role sit at different stages of the same funnel.
Priya Anand sources and tiers the profile; if she advances Catherine, Ravi
Okonkwo reviews the paid sample task that decides whether she stays on a
project and at what pay band. Catherine must clear both — Priya's 8-second
skim and Ravi's 15-25-minute artifact review.

---

## Persona 1 — Priya Anand (Domain Sourcing Lead, Expert Network)

Priya runs sourcing for the "Education & Child Development" vertical at a
Mercor-class marketplace. She clears 200+ profiles a day, only personally
touches the borderline and top-band cards, and trusts her skim over any cover
letter. Her unit of accountability is the match: every "yes" is a personal bet
that the contractor will perform, because the platform offboards
underperformers fast.

### What Priya is looking for — the bar for a YES

A YES (a "pull this one" / proactive source) requires the scarce, lab-named
specialty to be **legible in the first 8 seconds**, before she has to infer
anything:

- A specialty named in the first line — `autism`, `PDA`, `special education`,
  `IEP`, `IDEA`, `compliance`, `behavior` — not assembled from the whole
  resume.
- A credential band she can defend upward to the $100-110/hr ceiling: EdD +
  17 years + active independent consulting.
- Verb evidence that the person produces *structured judgment* — audited,
  scored, designed rubrics, assessed against standards — not just taught or
  led.
- An explicit availability line that clears the hard weekly minimum and
  signals async, no-hand-holding work (an existing consulting practice).
- A bonus second domain (behavioral / clinical case management) that opens a
  second spec bucket — "two buckets from one yes" is her favorite outcome.

### Instant-reject / down-tier triggers

- No nameable specialty by the end of the first role.
- Generalist "educator" framing — years and breadth, no niche (80% of her
  funnel; an instant down-tier that never gets a human pass).
- District / leadership positioning ("Education Leadership Expert,"
  "inclusive leadership," "principal preparation") — vocabulary written for a
  superintendent that tells her nothing.
- Pure narrative / mission statements with no concrete deliverables.
- Senior credentials with no acknowledgment of why the person would take
  20 hrs/week of 1099 gig work — reads as overqualified-and-restless, the
  profile she fears will ghost or be offboarded fast.
- Vague availability ("open to opportunities," no hours).

### Top questions Priya asks while screening Catherine

1. **What is this person's specialty, and can I see it in the first line?**
   Does the headline name a scarce, lab-requested niche, or do I have to read
   to paragraph three (which I won't)?
2. **Which spec bucket does this go in?** Education? Behavioral/clinical? Can
   one yes feed two pipelines?
3. **What credential band can I defend this to?** Does degree + years clear
   the public 4-year floor and let me argue the top of the $110 band without
   a fight?
4. **Has this person done structured-judgment work I can take straight to a
   lab?** Have they audited, scored, designed rubrics, assessed against a
   standard — or only taught and facilitated?
5. **Will this person ship async without management?** Is there a consulting
   practice or hourly-work history proving self-directed delivery?
6. **Will they clear the weekly minimum?** Are hours stated, and is the
   availability async-friendly?
7. **Is this a senior person who'll treat 20 hrs of 1099 work as a
   placeholder?** Does the resume read as a current, deliberate contractor
   profile — or as a restless academic's CV?
8. **Is the expertise current or archival?** Is this person still in
   practice, or coasting on a degree earned years ago?
9. **Is the AI-familiarity claim modest and credible, or a fake "AI expert"
   reframe?** (Note: she does not gatekeep on tech literacy — but an
   overclaim would worry her.)

## Persona 2 — Ravi Okonkwo (Education-Domain Technical Lead, Frontier Lab)

Ravi is on the lab's side. He writes the project spec, the rubric, the
calibration set, and the offboarding criteria. He reviews the paid sample task
every matched contractor submits — typically 3-6 items: write scenarios to
spec, score model transcripts against his rubric, draft one rubric, sometimes
a red-team probe. He compares the submission against gold answers and measures
*agreement*. He runs a small project (6-20 slots), so the bar is comparative,
and he decides keep / promote / offboard fast — first-sample quality predicts
steady-state quality. He does not care about the LinkedIn or the resume; he
cares about the artifact.

### What Ravi is looking for — the bar for a YES

A YES is "keep at the entry band." A STRONG YES ("promote" — routed the
better-paid rubric-authoring, red-team, and calibration work where the
$90-110/hr end lives) requires:

- **Calibration over confidence** — scores that land tight against the gold
  answers; where they diverge, the divergence is *flagged and reasoned*, not
  silent.
- **Scenarios with a real decision point** — a tempting wrong answer, not an
  obvious right one. The contractor builds the trap, not just the question.
- **Observable rubric criteria** — phrased so two reviewers score the same
  transcript the same way ("acknowledges the stated constraint before
  offering an alternative," not "is appropriate").
- **Domain depth used as a precision instrument** — depth that produces
  *specific failure modes the model actually exhibits* (e.g. conflating an IEP
  accommodation with a modification).
- **Adversarial instinct** — catching the plausible-but-non-compliant answer
  and naming the exact violated clause.
- **Reads the spec and writes to it** — internalizes the project's definition
  of "good," not an imported outside framework.

### Instant-reject / offboard triggers

- **Credential-as-argument** (blocker) — defending a divergent score by
  appeal to authority ("in my 17 years...") instead of reasoning from the
  rubric. The fastest route to offboarding.
- **Silent divergence** (strong) — scoring differently from the gold answer
  without flagging it or why. He can coach wrong-but-transparent; he cannot
  trust wrong-and-quiet.
- **Scenario with no failure mode** (strong) — a quiz question with one
  obvious answer; generates no training signal.
- **Unobservable rubric criteria** (strong) — adjective soup ("thoughtful,"
  "student-centered") that poisons inter-rater agreement.
- **Best-practice import** (strong) — scoring to IDEA / field best practice
  instead of the project rubric without noticing they differ. Named as
  *Catherine's exact risk*.
- **Ignoring an explicit spec instruction** (wrong format/length/axis) — a
  reading-the-spec failure, and the spec is the entire job.
- **Essay instead of a score** (soft) — three paragraphs of nuance where the
  task wanted a score plus one line.

### Top questions Ravi asks while reviewing Catherine's sample task

1. **Did her scores land tight against my gold answers?** What is the
   inter-rater agreement number — and where she diverged, did she flag it?
2. **When she disagreed with the rubric, did she say so?** Did she write
   "scored per the rubric; note field best practice would weigh this
   differently" — or did she silently score to her own standard?
3. **Did she score to the project rubric or to IDEA?** Is she calibrating to
   the spec's explicitly scoped target, or importing statute and field best
   practice the project didn't ask for?
4. **Do her scenarios have a real decision point?** Is there a tempting wrong
   answer where the model can plausibly fail — or are they teaching items
   with one obvious correct answer?
5. **Can her authored rubric be handed to the next contractor blind?** Are
   the criteria observable, or adjective soup?
6. **Did her domain depth produce a specific failure mode?** Did she write a
   scenario that hinges on a real, named model weakness (accommodation vs.
   modification; a fluent answer subtly harmful for a PDA learner)?
7. **Did she catch the subtle non-compliance in a transcript?** Did she flag
   the plausible-but-wrong answer and name the exact violated clause?
8. **Did she follow the spec's format and length exactly?** Or did a format
   miss signal a reading failure?
9. **Did she commit a judgment under the spec?** A score plus one tight line
   — or three paragraphs of qualitative nuance that won't commit?
10. **Does she defend her reasoning from the rubric, or from her resume?**
    Does her justification cite the criteria — or her years of practice?

## Synthesis — what the two personas jointly demand

Priya and Ravi guard two sequential gates, and they reward almost opposite
postures of the same expertise.

**Priya rewards the specialty being loud.** She wants the scarce, lab-named
niche — autism, PDA, IEP, IDEA, compliance — and the structured-judgment verb
evidence surfaced *in the first 8 seconds*, plus explicit async availability,
so she can tier the card high and source it without a human cost to herself.
Her gate is a *framing* gate: the substance is already strong; the question is
whether the page makes it legible fast.

**Ravi rewards the expertise being disciplined.** Once the work reaches him,
he wants the domain depth turned into a *precision instrument* — calibration
to *his* rubric over Catherine's own professional standard, transparent
flagging of every divergence, scenarios that build a genuine trap, and
observable criteria. His gate is a *behavior* gate: the resume can only
pre-load the right dispositions; the proof is the sample task.

The through-line both demand: **the same compliance-auditing muscle, pointed
two ways.** Auditing an IEP against IDEA is structurally identical to scoring
a model transcript against a rubric — Priya needs that equivalence stated as a
deliverable on the page, and Ravi needs it executed against *his* standard,
not IDEA. Catherine's single biggest joint risk is that her professional
reflex — authority and statute as the argument — reads to Priya as
district-panel positioning and to Ravi as credential-as-argument and
best-practice import. The resume must therefore do two things at once: name
the specialty loudly enough for Priya's skim, and signal explicitly enough
(spec-over-standard, transparent divergence, trap-building) that Ravi walks
into the sample task expecting the right instincts. The current resume already
does both; the projected resume below tightens the last residue and adds
direct proof for every open question.
