# Role 6 — Solo Consulting to Districts: Catherine's Next Steps (1-3 Months)

Each step below is achievable while Catherine keeps her teaching job, is drawn from the
opportunity report's Section 4 (Weak Spots) and Section 6 (Recommended Next Steps), and is
tied to a specific buyer question or screen-out risk it neutralizes. Steps are sequenced
into three windows.

The strategic frame from the report: consulting is Catherine's north star. The point of
these 1-3 months is to convert *credible-but-buried* into *buyer-ready* — close the
artifact gaps the round-2 critic flagged as operational, and the positioning gaps it
flagged as editable.

---

## Weeks 1-2 — Foundation: positioning, fluency, the AI point of view

### Step 1 — Rewrite LinkedIn + one-pager positioning to fuse the niche
Replace "Education and Administration Leadership Expert" with a line that fuses SpEd +
compliance + AI in the first 8 seconds, and adds a "practitioner" beat to the tagline so a
phone-skimming principal never hesitates.
- **Neutralizes:** Renata Q1 (does the headline name my problem?), Renata's
  generic-AI-consultant instant-reject, Eleanor's generic-flood reject, Joanne Q1
  (does this person teach?). Report Weak Spot 3 (positioning language).
- **Realistic:** an afternoon of editing; no new credential required.

### Step 2 — Build genuine LLM/power-user fluency on SpEd-relevant tools
Spend 5-8 hrs/week using Claude, ChatGPT, NotebookLM, and one AI-for-teachers tool
(MagicSchool, Brisk, or SchoolAI) specifically on SpEd workflows: drafting IEP-progress
language, summarizing behavior data, building differentiated materials.
- **Neutralizes:** Joanne Q5/Q6 (is the AI session concrete; has she actually used these
  tools?), Renata Q6 (AI literacy). Report Weak Spot 1 (LLM/prompting fluency).
- **Realistic:** evenings/weekends; this is the prerequisite for Steps 4 and 8.

### Step 3 — Write the AI risk-literacy point of view (one sharp paragraph)
Draft a clear, opinionated statement of what AI gets *wrong* for neurodivergent learners —
the median-student assumption, bias against disabled students, where the FERPA/IDEA
exposure actually lives. This becomes a one-pager line and the seed of the first public
piece (Step 5).
- **Neutralizes:** Renata's REMAINING risk (no point of view on AI failure modes for
  neurodivergent learners — the one thing between "call" and "call first"), Eleanor Q5
  (defensibility frame). Report Weak Spot 2 (public-facing AI artifact, content seed).
- **Realistic:** a few hours; draws directly on her existing PDA/complex-autism expertise.

## Weeks 3-6 — First public signals and the policy artifact

### Step 4 — Pilot one AI-tools-for-teachers session (low-stakes building)
Deliver a 60-90 minute AI-literacy segment to a teacher/para audience — a neighboring
principal's staff meeting, an OSPI session, or a building Catherine already works with.
Road-test the "three tools they open Monday" content so it becomes a *delivered* workshop,
not a *new* one.
- **Neutralizes:** Joanne's lone call-stage no-buy risk (has she delivered *this* content?),
  Joanne Q6. Critic synthesis "operational note": pilot the AI session before leaning on
  the bullet.
- **Realistic:** one session; uses Step 2 fluency and her existing facilitation skill and
  WA network.

### Step 5 — Publish 2 short public AI artifacts
Publish two pieces on LinkedIn or a Substack: (1) "What I Wish AI Education Products
Understood About PDA Autism" (built from Step 3), and (2) "Five Compliance Questions Every
District Should Ask Before Buying AI Tools."
- **Neutralizes:** Renata Q6/Q7 (converts claimed AI competency into demonstrated
  judgment), Eleanor's generic-consultant reject, the "no public AI artifact" gap. Report
  Weak Spot 2.
- **Realistic:** two posts over four weeks; content already exists from Steps 2-3.

### Step 6 — Publish the model district AI policy as a genuinely public document
Take the existing "model AI policy + administrative-regulation template" and publish it
openly under Catherine's name (a website page or PDF), not "available on request." Frame it
explicitly as adoption-ready and whole-district: acceptable use for all staff/students,
academic integrity, vendor data-privacy standards, with SpEd/accessibility as the section
most consultants get wrong.
- **Neutralizes:** Eleanor's hardest screen (no board-adopted artifact — this is the honest
  public substitute), Eleanor Q1/Q3 (whole-district coverage). Critic operational note;
  Round-1/2 Fix on the artifact gap.
- **Realistic:** the template largely exists; this is publishing + reframing it, ~1 week.

### Step 7 — Soft-launch the consulting offer to 20 WA contacts
Email 20 superintendents, deputy superintendents, and SpEd directors Catherine knows or can
be warm-introduced to (OSPI network, prior engagement sites). Offer a free 30-minute
consult on their AI policy, and explicitly mention the published model policy and the
piloted AI session.
- **Neutralizes:** Renata Q8 / Eleanor Q8 (warm path, references), seeds anchor clients.
  Report Next Steps "soft-launch consulting offer."
- **Realistic:** outreach over two weeks; uses her existing network.

## Months 2-3 — Anchor client, scope, website

### Step 8 — Land a first AI-policy or pilot-adoption engagement
Convert one soft-launch contact into either (a) a small-WA-district AI-policy engagement
aimed at a *board adoption* — even a small district counts — or (b) a paid AI-literacy
workshop or compliance audit at $3-15k. Prioritize an engagement that produces a
named, board-adopted policy proof point.
- **Neutralizes:** Eleanor Q1 (board-adopted artifact — the ceiling on her verdict),
  Renata's "audition tier" → project conversion. Report Next Steps "land first paid
  consulting engagement."
- **Realistic:** the report's own timeline puts a first engagement at months 2-3; one
  client is the target, not a full calendar.

### Step 9 — Cite WA's specific AI mandate and build a phased fixed-fee scope
Research and name WA's actual AI guidance and timeline on the one-pager and in proposals.
Build a reusable two-page, phased, dated, fixed-fee scope template (in the $25-75k band)
that can be dropped into a board consent agenda.
- **Neutralizes:** Eleanor Q7 (names *my* mandate — "written for me"), Eleanor Q8 (fixed-fee
  board-packet-ready scope). Critic Fix 3 and Fix 4 for Eleanor.
- **Realistic:** half a day of research plus a template; no engagement required.

### Step 10 — Build a simple one-page consulting website
Stand up a template-based (not custom) website: positioning line, three engagement areas
with named deliverables, rate bands, the published model policy (Step 6), links to the two
public articles (Step 5), and named references.
- **Neutralizes:** all three buyers' "can I verify / picture this" questions; consolidates
  Steps 1, 5, 6 into one discoverable place. Report Next Steps "build a simple website."
- **Realistic:** a weekend on a template; report explicitly says template, not custom.
