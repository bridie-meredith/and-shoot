## 2. Role-by-role analysis

### Role 1 — AI training contractor (Mercor, Scale, Surge, Invisible, Outlier)

**What the work is.** You sign onto a marketplace, get matched to a project for a frontier AI lab, and produce structured training or evaluation data: writing scenarios a model should handle, scoring model outputs, building question banks in your area of expertise, red-teaming responses, designing rubrics. Work is asynchronous and project-based, typically 3-12 week engagements.

**Comp.** Mercor pays an average around $85-95/hr across all domains, with bands for education-specific roles posted publicly at **$55-110/hr** (Instructional Coordinator roles $55-100, Training and Development Specialist roles $60-110, both requiring 4+ years professional experience). High-credential domains (medicine, law) go higher. Specialized SpEd / autism expertise is rare and should command the top of the band.

**Obligations.** Contractor status (1099). Typically 15-25 hrs/week, often a hard minimum. Weekly pay via Stripe/Wise. NDAs are standard. Quality bar is enforced — Mercor offboards underperformers quickly. No benefits, no PTO.

**What's needed to land it.** A LinkedIn that clearly shows the expertise, an "AI interview" or skills assessment, and willingness to do a paid sample task. Not gatekept by tech literacy — Catherine's profile is exactly the kind they recruit.

**Pros.**
- Fastest cash in tech. Realistic to land in 2-6 weeks from cold.
- Lets her keep her teaching job (asynchronous, evenings/weekends).
- Direct exposure to how frontier models are actually built and evaluated — this is the LLM literacy gap in the project summary, but *paid* and *hands-on*.
- Builds a portfolio narrative: "I've helped train models at three of the top labs."
- No interview gauntlet, no leadership-track gatekeeping.

**Cons.**
- Gig economy. No security, no benefits, no long-term arc.
- Project quality varies wildly. Some projects are tedious rubric work; others are genuinely interesting.
- Some platforms have reputation issues (long onboarding, opaque pay decisions, sudden offboarding).
- Not prestigious in a traditional sense — you can't lead with "Mercor contractor" on a résumé the way you can with "Anthropic education lead."

**Catherine's fit.** **Very strong.** Her profile (EdD, 17 yrs SpEd, conference speaker, active consultant) is well above the typical contractor caliber and exactly the kind of "deep domain expertise" these platforms pay premiums for. She'd likely qualify for both education-specific projects *and* general behavioral/clinical work given the mental health case management background.

**Verdict.** This is the obvious first move. Low risk, fast cash, builds the LLM exposure she needs for everything else in Path 1.

### Role 2 — Frontier-lab full-time (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI evals/human-data/policy)

**What the work is.** A handful of role families exist:
- **Evals / model behavior**: design tests for how models handle education contexts, safety in education, age-appropriate content, accessibility, etc.
- **Human data / training program design**: define what good training data looks like for education domains, manage contractor workflows, write specs.
- **Policy / trust & safety for education**: shape how the company's products serve K-12, what age gates exist, what data practices apply.
- **Customer / field education** (covered in role 5 separately).

**Comp.** Scale AI median total comp is ~$224k (Levels.fyi, 2026). Frontier-lab non-engineering roles for senior individual contributors typically run **$160-250k base + meaningful equity**. Director-level $250k-400k+ base. Equity at pre-IPO labs is genuinely valuable but illiquid.

**Obligations.** Full-time W-2. Hybrid (most labs require 2-4 days/week in SF, NYC, or London office, with limited remote exceptions). Heavy IC role with high autonomy; little hand-holding. Cross-functional with researchers and engineers who speak a different language.

**What's needed to land it.**
- A public artifact showing you've thought about AI + education seriously (essay, eval design, public talk)
- Network into the lab (these jobs are largely hired through warm intros, not cold applications)
- Comfort writing structured documents and specs
- Some baseline LLM/prompting fluency

**Pros.**
- Highest prestige in this list. "Education lead at Anthropic" or equivalent is a career-defining title.
- Direct influence on products that touch millions of learners.
- Mission alignment — most labs publicly care about education and accessibility.
- Strong compensation and benefits.

**Cons.**
- Slow hiring. Roles are scarce and competitive. 3-9 months realistic.
- Geographic friction. Most require relocation or hybrid presence. Catherine is in WA — Seattle has Anthropic presence; SF/NYC does not.
- Culture shock. Tech-company velocity is different from district timelines.
- Education is one priority among many at these labs, not the center of gravity.

**Catherine's fit.** **Strong but with gaps.** Her credentials are excellent and the SpEd/accessibility angle is genuinely valuable to labs (they get few candidates who can speak to IDEA compliance and AI together). Gaps: no public artifact yet showing she's engaged with AI; no obvious warm intro path; would need to be comfortable being the "education person" in a sea of engineers.

**Verdict.** Realistic in 6-12 months *if* she does Path 1 contracting first and writes 1-2 public pieces about AI + special education or AI + inclusive leadership. Don't apply cold today.

### Role 3 — Learning scientist / instructional designer at an edtech (Khan, Duolingo, MagicSchool, Quizlet, Newsela, Brainly, etc.)

**What the work is.** Two distinct flavors:
- **Learning scientist / efficacy researcher**: runs studies on whether the product actually teaches, designs A/B experiments on learning outcomes, analyzes in-app data, publishes white papers. Heavy stats and data tooling.
- **Learning designer / content lead**: designs curriculum within the product, owns scope and sequence, partners with engineering on what features support learning.

**Comp.** Duolingo Learning Scientist (Efficacy Research): **$117-158k base + equity** (current posting). Duolingo Director of Learning Design: **$190-284k**. Head of Duolingo Academy: **$228-342k**. Glassdoor data shows Learning Scientist average at Duolingo ~$160k total comp, with the 75th percentile around $220k.

**Obligations.** Full-time W-2. Most edtechs are remote-friendly but some (Duolingo Pittsburgh, Khan Mountain View) prefer hub presence. Standard tech benefits.

**What's needed to land it.**
- For learning scientist: Ph.D. or EdD ✓, *plus* R or Python for data analysis, *plus* publication record ✓
- For learning designer: deep curriculum experience, product sense, comfort with experiment-led iteration

**Pros.**
- The most direct "use your education expertise in tech" path.
- Strong comp.
- Stable employment with benefits.
- Many roles are explicitly designed for ed.D./Ph.D. candidates.

**Cons.**
- Learning scientist roles want **quantitative chops** Catherine doesn't currently have. Her work is practitioner + qualitative + dissertation. Duolingo's posting explicitly requires R or Python and large-dataset analysis.
- Learning *designer* roles want product experience iterating with engineers — different muscle than designing district PD.
- Edtech companies have heavy overrepresentation of math/language/literacy domains. SpEd-focused edtech (e.g., Goalbook, IEP-writing tools, AAC apps) is smaller market with fewer roles.

**Catherine's fit.** **Mixed.** EdD ✓, publication record ✓, instructional design experience ✓. But the data/stats requirement is a real wall for learning scientist roles, and her domain (SpEd, autism, early childhood) is a smaller subset of the edtech market than math/language/reading.

**Verdict.** Best target inside this category: **SpEd-focused edtech companies** (Goalbook, IXL's SpEd line, n2y, Frontline Education) or **early childhood edtech** (Lovevery has an instructional design org, Begin, Khan Kids). She'd be a very strong fit there and the smaller market means less competition. General-purpose learning scientist roles at Khan/Duolingo are a stretch without picking up data skills.

### Role 4 — L&D / instructional design at non-edtech companies

**What the work is.** Designing internal training for a tech company's employees: onboarding programs, manager training, sales enablement curricula, technical certifications. The "ed in tech" path that has nothing to do with student learning.

**Comp.** Senior IC L&D / Senior Instructional Designer at tech companies: **$110-160k base**. Director of L&D: **$180-280k**. Equity varies; usually meaningful at pre-IPO companies.

**Obligations.** Full-time W-2. Mostly remote-friendly. Heavy stakeholder management — partnering with sales, eng, customer success leaders to figure out what training they need.

**What's needed to land it.**
- Instructional design portfolio (ideally with adult learning, not just K-12)
- Familiarity with tools like Articulate Storyline, Rise, Camtasia
- Track record of designing programs at scale (not 1:1 coaching)

**Pros.**
- Massive market — every tech company has L&D.
- Generally lower bar than learning science roles (no stats requirement).
- Very transferable adult-learning principles.
- Stable, well-compensated, sane hours.

**Cons.**
- Furthest from Catherine's identity. Designing sales enablement for a SaaS company is a long way from inclusive leadership research.
- Her experience is K-12-flavored; she'd need to repackage as adult learning.
- The "AI angle" is weaker here — fewer roles are explicitly about AI.

**Catherine's fit.** **Decent but not optimal.** She has the design and facilitation skills (she literally runs PD for 60-person groups, designs curriculum, manages stakeholders). But the work feels mission-misaligned for someone whose dissertation is on inclusive principal prep. She'd be employable here, but probably not happy long-term.

**Verdict.** Backup option if Path 1 contracting plus targeted edtech applications don't pan out. Not a first move.

**Cons.**

**Catherine's fit.** **Surprisingly good for AI-company customer education specifically targeting education customers.** If Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google has a team teaching school districts how to deploy their AI products responsibly, Catherine is one of the few candidates who can credibly speak to both sides. **Verdict.** Worth a targeted search for "education customer success" / "field engagement, education" / "partner education, K-12" at AI companies. Probably only 10-30 such roles exist in the US but they're under-recruited.

### Role 6 — Solo consulting to districts (AI policy, SpEd + AI, inclusive leadership PD)

**What the work is.** Districts hire her on engagements ranging from 1-day workshops ($3-8k) to full AI policy development (3-6 month engagements, $25-75k) to ongoing fractional advisor work ($3-8k/month retainer). Specific products she could offer:
- **District AI policy authoring** (Ohio, California, and 33 other states now require formal AI policies)
- **AI literacy PD for teachers** (44% of teachers report no AI PD; the market is acute)
- **AI compliance audits** for special education programs
- **Inclusive AI implementation reviews** (does this AI tool work for SpEd students? IDEA compliance?)
- **Train-the-trainer programs** for instructional coaches

**Comp.** Education consultants with senior credentials and a niche typically command **$1,500-3,000/day for workshops** and **$15,000-50,000 per engagement** for multi-week projects. Top-tier consultants with national reputation hit $5,000/day. A full calendar at modest rates is $150-300k/yr; at premium rates with retainers, $400k+ is possible.

**Obligations.** All overhead is hers: LLC, insurance, taxes, contracts, invoicing, lead gen, travel. Highly cyclical (school-year-driven, with summer being PD high season and fall being policy season). Income unsteady year 1-2.

**What's needed to land it.**
- Clear positioning: who, what problem, what offer
- 2-3 anchor clients to validate
- A website / one-pager / LinkedIn that ranks for the niche
- Speaking engagements at conferences (she has this already)
- Referral network into superintendents / SpEd directors (her OSPI work + her network)

**Pros.**
- **This is the closest fit to who she actually is on paper.** She's already doing it part-time and on OSPI contracts. Going full-time is an *expansion*, not a pivot.
- Highest income ceiling if she nails it.
- Full autonomy over what she works on.
- Builds equity (brand, audience, IP) instead of just earning wages.
- Genuinely addresses a market that needs serving — schools are scrambling for this.
- Most states now mandate district AI policies. Ohio's mandate took effect July 2026; California, Idaho, Utah, Tennessee, Virginia have comprehensive policies; 35 states + Puerto Rico have official AI guidance.

**Cons.**
- Income variability. First 6-12 months are usually thin.
- All sales work is hers. She'll spend 30-50% of her time on lead gen and proposals, not delivery.
- Lonely. No team, no salary, no benefits.
- Easy to plateau as a "speaker who does workshops" instead of building something bigger.

**Catherine's fit.** **Outstanding.** Look at the resume: OSPI consulting since 2023, private school compliance audits since 2019, regular AERA presentations, Dissertation of the Year, multiple guest lectures and panel appearances. She's *already a consultant*. The pivot is just to (a) add AI to the offer mix and (b) raise her rates and shift from public-ed work to district work.

**Verdict.** This should be her primary long-term path. Path 1 contracting and any tech role should be evaluated against "does this strengthen the consulting practice or distract from it."

### Role 7 — Joining or founding an ed+AI startup

**What the work is.** Either joining an early-stage company as employee #5-50 (typically a head of curriculum / head of learning / head of partnerships role) or starting her own.

**Most promising white spaces for someone with her profile:**

- **AI-driven behavior support tools** (BIP/FBA workflows, data collection)
- **Trauma-informed AI design** (what does it mean for an AI tutor to be trauma-aware?)
- **District AI governance tooling** (policy management, vendor evaluation, teacher training tracking)

**Comp.** As an early employee with a meaningful title: $120-180k base + 0.5-2% equity. As a founder: salary often $0-80k in year 1-2, equity is everything.

**Obligations.** Long hours. High risk. Equity may be worth nothing.

**Pros.**
- Highest upside.
- Most leverage on the world if it works.
- Builds something that could outlast her career.

**Cons.**
- Highest risk. Most startups fail.
- Demands skills she doesn't currently have (fundraising, product, hiring engineers).
- Hard to do part-time.

**Catherine's fit.** **Best as a domain expert / co-founder, not solo founder.** She has the niche, the credibility, the network, and the research. She doesn't have the technical chops to build product or the startup operating experience. The right path is to find a technical co-founder or join an existing 0-1 stage startup as the education lead.

**Verdict.** Don't target this directly yet. Let consulting (Role 6) flush out which problem is most painful and most repeatable. When she sees the same problem at 5 districts in a row, *that's* the startup idea. Many education startups are founded by consultants who got tired of solving the same problem manually.

**Obligations.** Heavy upfront content creation. Ongoing audience-building (email list, social, speaking). Customer support.

**Pros.**

**Cons.**

**Catherine's fit.** **Good as a supporting move, not a primary one.** She has the credentials to be taken seriously and a clear point of view. But productized education is a long compounding game; it shouldn't be the first move.

**Verdict.** Start a Substack or LinkedIn newsletter on inclusive leadership + AI within month 1 of the pivot. Don't try to monetize it for 12 months. It's the marketing layer for consulting, and the IP backbone for everything else.

She also needs to start showing up where the people are: AI for Education community, ISTE's AI strand, Stanford Accelerator for Learning events, Khan Academy's Khanmigo educator events.

A fifth nice-to-have, not blocker: **basic data fluency** if she wants edtech learning scientist roles. R or Python literacy at a "can clean a CSV and run a regression" level. Not needed for any other role.

## 5. The market context

Three trends are converging right now that make this an unusually good moment for Catherine specifically:

1. **State AI mandates are creating forced-buyer markets.** 35 states + Puerto Rico now have official AI guidance for education. Ohio mandated district AI policies by July 2026. California's AB 2876 requires AI literacy in K-12 curriculum. Districts must comply but most have no internal expertise. This is a 2-3 year window where consulting demand will outstrip supply.

2. **Frontier labs are spending billions on human training data.** Mercor pays out ~$1.5M/day to contractors and has 30,000+ experts on its platform. The bottleneck is no longer compute; it's high-quality expert-generated data. Education and child development are explicitly named domains.

3. **Special education is structurally underserved by ed-tech.** Most edtech is built for the median student. SpEd students are 14% of US public school enrollment but capture less than 5% of edtech R&D dollars. AI changes this, but the people building those products mostly don't have SpEd backgrounds. There is a real talent shortage.

Working against her:
- Public education hiring is slow and conservative — this is what she's experienced.
- Many ed-tech companies are still recovering from the post-pandemic correction.
- Generalist "AI in education" consultants are flooding the market; she needs to differentiate fast.
