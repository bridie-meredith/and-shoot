# Resume 03 — Edtech Learning Scientist — Critic Feedback

The "edtech learning scientist / learning experience designer" title spans a wide market with internally incompatible requirements. At the mainstream consumer-edtech end (Duolingo, Khan Academy), the role is a causal-inference research position — quantitative first, domain second, practitioner background tolerated only when it comes with a methods toolkit. At the SpEd-focused edtech end (Goalbook, n2y, Frontline, Begin), the role is a domain-and-design position — rare subject-matter expertise first, product instincts second, stats optional. Early-childhood edtech (Lovevery, Khan Kids) splits the difference but skews toward developmental curriculum design and family-facing craft. Catherine Olver's candidacy looks entirely different depending on which door she's standing in front of. The three screeners below represent those three distinct bars.

---

## Screener: Hannah Brooks — Senior Manager, Efficacy Research (general-purpose edtech, quantitative bar)

**Verdict: SCREEN-OUT**

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.** Eyes go straight to the profile block hunting for a statistical method or a language name. What Hannah sees: "Comfortable reading research," "qualitative inquiry," "data-informed." She circles all three mentally. No R, no Python, no SQL, no regression, no named dataset. In eight seconds she has already made her call; she finishes the scan only to confirm. The EdD and the Dissertation of the Year register — proof of stamina and research bar — but they immediately trigger the credential-shield read because there is nothing quantitative underneath them. She stops reading around the third line of the profile.

**Strengths she sees.** The EdD is real and the AERA/UCEA papers tell her Catherine can survive peer review. Seventeen years of domain depth in SpEd, autism, and early childhood is uncommon and Hannah knows it. The 60% referral-reduction metric is the single most interesting line on the resume — but she needs to know whether that was measured, by whom, with what design, over what time period. As written it reads as a practitioner outcome, not a study. The Dissertation of the Year means genuine research quality by academic standards, which she respects but cannot weight.

**Screen-out risks.** Fatal on this req. The entire research record as presented is qualitative and practitioner-facing: "qualitative inquiry translated into iterative program improvements," "disability categories," "MTSS/RTI data review," "outcome measurement." These are practitioner verbs and practitioner data, not researcher instruments. The posting requires R or Python and large-dataset analysis as a requirement, not a preference. That gap is not a soft gap — it is the instant-reject condition. The credential-shield peeve fires hard: the profile leads with EdD, dissertation award, and AERA papers, all of which substitute for the missing quantitative signal rather than pointing toward it. "Data-informed practice" appears in the profile and triggers the specific pet peeve ("the phrase that exists precisely to paper over the absence of statistics") without any named method, tool, or dataset behind it.

**Specific resume fixes.**

Current line: *"Data-informed practice: weekly RTI/MTSS data review, intervention tracking, outcome measurement across multi-site programs."*
Problem: This is a practitioner data workflow, not a research methods claim. Hannah reads "RTI/MTSS data review" as monitoring a dashboard, not running an analysis. The phrase "outcome measurement" is vague enough to mean anything.
Fix (only if honest): If the dissertation or consulting work included any quantitative analysis — even descriptive statistics, survey analysis, or quasi-experimental design — name the tool and the method explicitly: *"Analyzed pre/post intervention data using SPSS across N participants; mixed-methods dissertation includes quantitative phase (survey, n=XX)."* If no such work exists, delete this bullet entirely. An empty quantitative claim is worse than silence.

Current line: *"co-introduced 6-8 week documented intervention reducing SpEd referrals by 60%"*
Problem: The most compelling metric on the resume has no methodological frame. How was the 60% measured? Compared to what baseline? Hannah's first question is "what's the counterfactual?"
Fix: *"Co-designed 6-8 week RTI intervention; district tracking data showed 60% reduction in SpEd referrals vs. prior-year baseline (N=XX students, XX-XX cohort)."* Even if the study design is weak, naming the comparison makes it a data claim rather than an anecdote.

Current line: *"Applied research grounding (EdD, Dissertation of the Year, accepted AERA papers 2024-2026)"*
Problem: This is the credential-shield in its purest form — the award standing in directly for the missing skill.
Fix: Move all credential mention to the Education section. In the profile, replace this sentence with whatever quantitative work Catherine actually has. If she has none, the profile should not claim "research grounding" at all — change it to "practitioner-research bridge" or "applied program design" and stop there.

**Honest bottom line for this screen.** The resume cannot be written to beat Hannah without misrepresentation. The take-home is a messy CSV and a causal-inference prompt; no resume edit changes whether Catherine can pass it. The advice is: do not target this variant of the role until one genuine quantitative artifact exists — a regression run, a dataset cleaned and analyzed, a pre-registered pilot with a comparison condition. Then return.

## Screener: Renée Acosta — Director of Learning Design, SpEd-Focused EdTech

**Verdict: BORDERLINE (advances if two targeted fixes land; screens out as currently written)**

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.** Renée reads the headline first. What she sees: *"EdD with 15+ years of direct K-12 teaching and program design."* Her eye is already moving, mildly interested. Then the Areas of Expertise line names "Complex autism (PDA)" and "IEP/504/IDEA compliance" and she slows. PDA autism is specific enough to be unfakeable — she knows it, it's hard, and almost nobody in the applicant stack can claim it. She keeps reading. But then the experience block reads as practitioner → consultant → speaker, and her "PD-deck-as-product" peeve starts pinging. She is not out, but she is on the fence.

**Strengths she sees.** The SpEd depth is exactly what this screen exists to find. PDA autism expertise, multi-site compliance auditing, IEP/IDEA work across multiple districts — these are load-bearing signals that the general-ed applicants flooding her stack cannot fake. "Program audits across private schools and EC settings" from the Olver Consulting entry is close to what she wants: it suggests Catherine has looked at programs from the outside, not only delivered from the inside. Caseloads of 35-51 students, supervising teams of up to 8 — these tell Renée this is someone who has managed real complexity at real scale, not just run a lovely classroom. The OSPI contract work (state-funded, presumably involving accountability structures) reads as something more rigorous than an independent keynote. The EdD and peer-review record are genuine tailwinds for selling the hire to a data-leaning leadership team.

**Screen-out risks.** Two. First: the product-iteration gap. Nothing on the resume shows Catherine designing inside a team, against a roadmap, shipping something at product scale, and revising on user feedback. The Olver Consulting bullets — *"speaking/writing on inclusive leadership... compliance and program audits"* — read as consultant output, not product output. The PD-facilitation record (10-60 person groups, OSPI contracts) reads as service delivery. Renée's peeve *"a PD deck is not a product"* applies here. Second: the headline and the profile's framing. The profile's first sentence ("EdD with 15+ years of K-12 teaching and program design") buries the SpEd niche under a generalist educator frame. Renée decides in 8 seconds and the 8-second read does not give her the SpEd signal loudly enough.

**Specific resume fixes.**

Current headline/profile opener: *"EdD with 15+ years of direct K-12 teaching and program design, focused on what works for learners with disabilities and the educators who serve them."*
Problem: "K-12 teaching and program design" is a generalist frame. "Learners with disabilities" is the soft version of the SpEd-specific claim she needs in the first clause.
Fix: *"Special education specialist with 15+ years in complex autism, early-childhood SpEd, and IEP/IDEA compliance — practitioner-researcher designing for the learners mainstream edtech leaves behind."*

Current line (Olver Consulting): *"speaking/writing on inclusive leadership, complex autism, trauma-informed practice; compliance and program audits across private schools and EC settings"*
Problem: This reads as independent-consultant/speaker framing — Renée's "pure consultant" soft peeve. The compliance audit work is the most valuable signal here and it is buried at the end of a list with keynote speaking.
Fix: Split the entry into two bullets. Lead with: *"Conducted IEP/IDEA compliance audits across multiple private school and early-childhood settings; identified systemic gaps and authored remediation frameworks adopted by programs."* The speaking/writing becomes a secondary bullet.

Current line (Auburn SD): *"co-introduced 6-8 week documented intervention reducing SpEd referrals by 60%; helped move 88% of students receiving services into gen-ed majority of day"*
Problem: This is the strongest outcome claim on the resume, but the verb "co-introduced" undersells the design work, and the framing is all practitioner outcome. Renée wants to see the artifact verb.
Fix: *"Co-designed and piloted a 6-8 week structured intervention (documented framework, replicable across classrooms) — district outcome data: 60% reduction in SpEd referrals; 88% of students served moved to gen-ed majority placement."* The word "replicable" is load-bearing: it signals she built something others could use, which is the product-analog Renée is hunting.

Missing element — explicit product pivot bridge. The current resume has none.
Add (to profile or as a closing line): *"Now targeting curriculum and content design roles in SpEd-focused edtech — bringing domain depth that takes years to build, a practitioner's feel for what actually works with real kids, and a researcher's frame for knowing why."*

**Honest bottom line for this screen.** The domain fit is the strongest in Catherine's edtech stack. Renée is the right screener to beat. But the resume must move the SpEd specificity to the front, recast the compliance work as a craft rather than a consulting line item, and add one honest pivot-bridge sentence. Without those changes the "PD-deck-as-product" read wins and Renée passes.

## Screener: Grace Tan — Head of Curriculum, Early-Childhood EdTech

**Verdict: BORDERLINE (close to advance; one framing fix is critical)**

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.** Grace reads the headline and sees "EdD" and "K-12" in the same breath and her first instinct is *district administrator, wrong building*. Then her eye catches the first entry in the experience block: *"SpEd Teacher Early Childhood — Kent SD (2025-Present): design/run EC SpEd programs ages 3-5."* She stops. Ages 3-5, current, daily practice. That is the hook that keeps her reading. But she has to work to get there — the headline and profile framing make her do unnecessary labor before she finds what she actually cares about.

**Strengths she sees.** The current early-childhood SpEd role is a genuine peer credential on her stack. Ages 3-5, diverse developmental, behavioral, and communication profiles, co-regulation strategies, family and bilingual-specialist partnerships — this is birth-to-six developmental work done every day. Grace reads complex/PDA autism depth as developmental range expertise, not a niche credential: a curriculum head who understands how language, motor, and social-emotional development can diverge designs better content for all kids. The trauma-informed and co-regulation work maps directly to the gentle-by-design sensibility her product needs. Years of IEP/family communication is years of translating developmental complexity to anxious parents — which is the 11pm-parent-tip job Grace's product does at scale. The training and PD design (10-60 person groups, OSPI contracts) reads as program design and structured knowledge delivery, which is adjacent to curriculum design in her view.

**Screen-out risks.** The product-iteration gap is real and the strongest reject trigger. *"No evidence of working with designers/engineers, shipping versions, responding to user feedback, or iterating content"* is Grace's most common rejection reason for otherwise-strong candidates. Nothing on this resume signals a sprint, a roadmap, a version cycle, or a piece of content that was revised based on use. The closest analog Catherine has is classroom-level iteration — adjusting instruction based on individual kids' responses — and designing PD programs revised based on participant feedback. That analog is honest and defensible; it is simply not on the resume. Second risk: the headline and profile's K-12 / administrative framing. "K-12 teaching and program design" reads as school-age center of gravity; "Administration Leadership Expert" (if carried from a previous version) reads as district-org ambition, wrong building. Third risk: compliance and legal vocabulary leading the experience. The Areas of Expertise block lists "IEP/504/IDEA compliance" as a named area — Grace reads "services-and-paperwork" and has to work to see the developmental curriculum underneath it.

**Specific resume fixes.**

Current headline/profile opener: *"EdD with 15+ years of direct K-12 teaching and program design, focused on what works for learners with disabilities and the educators who serve them."*
Problem: "K-12" centers school-age. "Learners with disabilities" is compliance framing. Grace needs the early-childhood and developmental signal in the first clause.
Fix: *"Early-childhood educator and curriculum designer — developmental learning, ages 0-6, play-based and inclusive practice, with 15+ years building programs for children who learn differently."*

Current Kent SD bullet: *"design/run EC SpEd programs ages 3-5 across diverse developmental/behavioral/communication profiles; build behavior support systems, co-regulation strategies, instructional routines; partner with families and bilingual specialists"*
Problem: "EC SpEd programs" and "behavior support systems" are compliance-adjacent; "instructional routines" is generic. Grace wants milestones, play-based language, developmental specifics.
Fix: *"Design and run early-childhood programs for ages 3-5 — building individualized, play-based developmental sequences across motor, language, and social-emotional domains; adapt based on each child's emerging skills and family input."* Add: *"Partner daily with families to translate developmental progress into concrete, actionable home strategies — accessible language, not clinical jargon."*

Current Areas of Expertise block: lists "IEP/504/IDEA compliance" prominently.
Problem: Compliance vocabulary as a headline area signals services-and-paperwork to Grace.
Fix: Remove "IEP/504/IDEA compliance" from the Areas of Expertise block. It can live in the experience bullets where it belongs. Replace with: *"Developmental milestone sequencing · Play-based and naturalistic instruction · Family communication and parent coaching."*

Missing element — product iteration signal.
Add a bullet to the Kent SD or Auburn SD entry: *"Revised instructional routines based on observed child response and family feedback — tight iteration cycles across 35-51 children per year with diverse developmental profiles."* This is the closest honest analog to content iteration. Grace knows it is not a product sprint; she will accept it as proof of a feedback-loop instinct.

Missing element — complex-autism depth framed as developmental range.
Current phrasing: "Complex autism (PDA)" appears in Areas of Expertise but with no framing.
Fix: Add one sentence to the profile: *"Deep fluency in atypical development — including complex autism/PDA, sensory and motor divergence — means I design curriculum that holds up across the full range of how children actually grow."*

Optional high-leverage add: Grace's product roadmap has an AI thread. Catherine's resume says nothing about AI.
Add (if honest): *"Tracking AI applications in early-learning content personalization and parent-guidance tools — interested in how developmental expertise shapes responsible use."* One sentence. It signals she will keep up with where the product is going without overclaiming technical skill she doesn't have.

**Honest bottom line for this screen.** Grace is a real possibility — closer than Hannah, roughly equivalent to Renée, with different fixes needed. The developmental work is native and current; the gap is framing and the absence of any product-iteration signal. The two critical fixes are the headline (she decides in 8 seconds) and the product-iteration bullet (she rejects otherwise-strong candidates on this specifically). With those in place, Catherine earns the call.

## Synthesis

**Where the three screeners agree.** All three find the same structural problem: the resume is organized around identity and credential (EdD, Dissertation of the Year, speaker/consultant/leader) rather than around craft signals calibrated to the target role. The profile block leads with the doctorate and the award, which reads as a credential shield to Hannah, a credential-as-whole-pitch soft peeve to Renée, and an academic-register red flag to Grace. All three would move faster to YES if Catherine's domain-specific craft were front and center before any credential appears.

**Where they diverge.** Hannah is a screen-out regardless of resume edits — the gap is the work itself (no quantitative methods, no programming language) and no framing fixes that. Renée and Grace are both genuine borderline-to-advance opportunities, but they want different things surfaced: Renée wants the compliance audit work stated as a craft and the SpEd specificity loud in the first clause; Grace wants the early-childhood developmental practice framed as milestone fluency and play-based pedagogy, with compliance language pushed off the lead. A single resume cannot fully satisfy both without sacrificing one for the other. Two distinct variants (SpEd-edtech and early-childhood-edtech) are the correct tool here.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits across both viable targets:**

1. **Rewrite the headline and profile opener to name the specific domain first, credential second.** The current opener (*"EdD with 15+ years of direct K-12 teaching"*) triggers the wrong read in the first eight seconds for both Renée and Grace. For the SpEd variant: name complex autism, IEP/IDEA compliance design, and practitioner-researcher bridge. For the early-childhood variant: name ages 0-6, developmental sequencing, play-based practice. One change, applied twice, fixes the highest-leverage failure point for both viable screeners.

2. **Add one honest product-iteration signal — a bullet framing classroom or PD iteration as a feedback loop.** Both Renée and Grace reject otherwise-strong candidates specifically on "no evidence they can iterate content." Catherine's years of adjusting instruction based on child response and revising PD programs based on participant feedback is a legitimate analog. It must be written into the resume with iteration-framing language ("revised," "adapted based on feedback," "tested and adjusted") rather than delivery language ("facilitated," "presented"). This is the single change that moves both borderline verdicts toward advance.

3. **Lead the Olver Consulting entry with the compliance audit work, not the speaking record.** Currently: *"speaking/writing on inclusive leadership, complex autism, trauma-informed practice; compliance and program audits."* The speaking/writing comes first, triggering Renée's "pure consultant" soft peeve and burying the compliance-audit signal she values most. Swap the order, expand the compliance bullet to name scope (how many districts, what kind of gaps found, what remediation designed), and make it the lead artifact claim for the SpEd-edtech variant.

**Which door is strongest and whether "learning scientist" is a stretch.** The SpEd-focused edtech door (Renée's screen) is Catherine's strongest fit inside the edtech market — it is the subset where her specific domain expertise (complex autism, PDA, IEP compliance, early-childhood SpEd, multi-district audit experience) is genuinely rare and genuinely valued, and where the quantitative bar is low enough that her research record is an asset rather than an exposure. Early-childhood edtech is a close second and requires a distinct resume variant built around developmental fluency and family-facing communication rather than compliance expertise. As for the "learning scientist" title: at a general-purpose edtech company running a Duolingo-shaped efficacy-research org, it is a stretch that the resume cannot honestly support right now — the title implies causal-inference methods and large-dataset fluency that Catherine does not yet have on paper, and a resume engineered to clear Hannah's screen would fail the take-home. At SpEd-focused or early-childhood edtech companies, the title "learning experience designer" or "curriculum lead" is the better-fit descriptor, and Catherine's actual credentials land it without overreach.
