# Resume 07 — Startup Head of Education — Critic Feedback

## Role Context

The target role is Head of Education (or domain co-founder) at a seed-stage AI startup building IEP-writing and SpEd compliance tooling. The screeners are Devin Park, a technical founder who has staked his company on compliance being the moat and is hiring the person who embodies that moat, and Arjun Mehta, a staff-level engineer running a near-identical company who is courting a co-founder rather than posting a job. Both need the same thing — a working IEP/IDEA compliance specialist with an independent operating history and at least one concrete signal of AI engagement — but their stakes differ: Devin is making an early hire decision; Arjun is making a decade-level co-founder bet. The resume must earn a call from Devin and earn a co-founder conversation from Arjun. Those are related but not identical asks.

---

## Screener: Devin Park (edai-startup-founder)

**Verdict: BORDERLINE — earns a call only with the revised framing below; fails the skim on current copy.**

### First-8-Seconds Skim Reaction

The top of the resume is doing the right thing structurally — a "why I'm the hire" narrative block rather than a job-history list — and the SpEd / IEP / compliance content is there. But Devin's 8-second scan stalls on the headline: "Head of Education | Special education • Compliance • Trusted by districts." That middle dot cluster reads like a LinkedIn summary, not a wedge. He wants to see "IEP/IDEA compliance auditor, complex autism specialist, 15+ years multi-district" in the first line — words that map to the exact thing his company cannot fake. "Trusted by districts" is mission-statement language. It tells him nothing about the domain and reads exactly like the mushy generalist framing he auto-skips.

The "WHY I AM THE EDUCATION LEAD YOUR STARTUP NEEDS" section rescues this — fast. Once he gets there, he's in. Lines like "most edtech is built for the median student" and "most ed-AI startups die on a compliance objection" land because they're specific and slightly adversarial. But he shouldn't have to survive a soft headline to get to the real content. Half the candidates who would benefit from reading further won't.

### Strengths Seen

- The compliance fluency is named with specificity: "I know IDEA, IEP, 504, and state SpEd regulations cold." That is exactly Devin's wedge, said plainly. He will note it.
- The OSPI contracts and the consulting practice signal 1099 operating history — she has lived with financial risk, which is skin-in-the-game proof he weights heavily.
- "Co-designed a 6-8 week intervention process that reduced SpEd referrals 60%" is the best velocity-with-outcome line on the resume. Six-to-eight weeks, a metric, owned verb ("co-designed"). Devin responds to that tempo signal.
- "Run multiple competitive contracts concurrently on tight deliverables" is the sentence from the OSPI section that does the most work. It directly counters the district-pace fear.
- The "WHERE I WANT TO BUILD" section functions as the product thesis. "AI-assisted IEP writing and compliance tooling for SpEd teams" is Devin's exact company. She named his thesis before he said it. That earns attention.

### Screen-Out Risks

**1. No AI signal anywhere — the hardest gap.**
The entire resume reads as an excellent educator with deep compliance experience who has formed opinions about AI in education at a general level. "Most ed-AI startups die on a compliance objection" is a business insight, not an AI engagement signal. Devin needs proof she has used the tools, formed a specific take on where current AI-IEP products break on compliance, or published anything about it. Without this, his gut read is: researcher who wants to study the problem, not someone who will open a draft IEP in ChatGPT, find the IDEA violation, and write the spec for how to catch it. This is fixable before the resume reaches him — but it has to be fixed.

**2. The headline undersells and the verb structure is inconsistent.**
"Head of Education | Special education • Compliance • Trusted by districts" — "Trusted by districts" is a claim, not a descriptor. The headline slot should carry the unfakeable niche. The working experience bullets mix owned verbs with hedged ones. "Supervised/trained teams of up to 8" is fine; "restructuring/PD moving 88% of students into gen-ed" needs a subject — who did the restructuring? The slash construction throughout the operating-track section reads like note-shorthand, not ownership language.

**3. Researcher identity signal not addressed.**
The resume closes on "AERA 2026/2025, UCEA 2024 sessions; guest lectures Pacific Lutheran, WWU, Kent SD 2024-2025." These are the last lines Devin reads, and they leave him with an image of someone who presents and lectures, not someone who ships. This is a sequencing problem, not a content problem — but where items appear signals identity, and ending on the academic conference list tips the identity toward researcher.

**4. The EdD as a timestamp problem.**
"EdD Gonzaga 2025" correctly placed. But "Dissertation of the Year (Gonzaga 2025)" as a standalone award line immediately after reads as credential recital. He respects the distinction; he does not hire for it. This needs to be attached to a sentence about what the research *does* for the product — not listed as an award.

### Specific Resume Fixes

**Line:** `Catherine Olver, EdD — Head of Education | Special education • Compliance • Trusted by districts.`
**Rewrite:** `Catherine Olver, EdD — IEP/IDEA Compliance Specialist | Complex autism • SpEd regulatory auditing • 15+ years, multi-district`

Rationale: Replaces a job-title-seeking headline with the scarce domain niche. "Trusted by districts" is a marketing claim; the specific audit history is the proof.

Most AI-IEP tools miss [specific mechanism]. I have written about this at [link/venue] and can bring that diagnostic lens to product development from day one.`

Rationale: Devin runs a paid trial where he asks candidates to audit a sample AI-drafted IEP. A resume that shows she has already done this — unprompted — tells him the metabolism is right. If she does not have a public piece yet, she should write one paragraph and post it before submitting.

## Screener: Arjun Mehta (startup-technical-cofounder)

**Verdict: BORDERLINE — strong enough to earn a co-founder conversation, not strong enough to close it. The domain wedge stops him cold; three structural gaps prevent a fast yes.**

### First-8-Seconds Skim Reaction

Arjun's first-pass check is three questions: (1) is there a domain moat he structurally lacks, (2) has she operated outside an institution, (3) any sign she has touched AI. On question one, Catherine answers his prayer — SpEd / IDEA compliance auditing across multiple districts is precisely the moat he chose his wedge around. He does not have this and cannot fake it. On question two, the consulting practice since 2019 and the OSPI contracts are visible — she has operated on 1099. On question three, the resume is silent. That third check produces a pause, not a no, but it slows him down in a way Devin's equivalent pause does not — because Arjun is making a co-founder bet, not a hire bet, and silence on AI in someone pursuing an AI company is a more expensive signal here.

What catches him further on the skim is the "WHERE I WANT TO BUILD" section. She has a product thesis. A co-founder candidate who arrives with a market map is different from one who arrives with a resume. But the thesis is framed as three separate directions ("AI-assisted IEP writing," "AI tutors for neurodivergent learners," "district-side AI governance") rather than a single pointed stake. Three theses where one is needed makes him wonder if she is scoping a research agenda rather than naming a company.

### Strengths Seen

- The compliance-auditing track record is the single most valuable line on the document for Arjun. "Compliance audit work across districts and private schools" tells him she has been paid, repeatedly, specifically to find where compliance breaks. That is not adjacent to the moat — that is the moat.
- The OSPI-funded contracts section is co-founder evidence more than employee evidence: she won competitive grants, structured engagements herself, managed legal and financial requirements without an employer's scaffolding. He will note this. "Independent Consultant — OSPI-Funded Contracts" is the correct framing; it must stay visible.
- "Built a six-year part-time consulting practice alongside full-time teaching" — this sentence quietly says: she has run lead-gen, closed clients, delivered without institutional support, and done it while teaching full time. That is a high-functioning independent operator. For Arjun this reads as: she might be able to own GTM from day one.
- "Research, curriculum, facilitation, evaluation" under the OSPI section signals that she carries full delivery cycles end-to-end. This is not a specialist who hands off.

### Screen-Out Risks

**1. Three product theses, not one — reads like research scoping, not founder commitment.**
The "WHERE I WANT TO BUILD" section lists three separate directions. Arjun needs to hear the specific bet, not the option space. A co-founder who arrives with three theses is still shopping. A co-founder who arrives and says "the bet is AI-IEP compliance tooling, here is specifically why, here is what I have personally audited that confirms it" has already started the company. The multi-thesis framing signals researcher mode — the person who wants to study the landscape before picking the peak. He will not say this kills the conversation, but it is the first thing he will challenge in the call.

**2. No AI artifact and no co-founder identity claim.**
Catherine does not state, anywhere, that she is a builder — only that she wants to build. "WHERE I WANT TO BUILD" is future-tense aspiration. Arjun needs at least one sentence that reads as present-tense identity: "I am building this, and here is the smallest version of what I have done." That can be a teardown essay she wrote, a prototype she critiqued, a compliance gap she documented in a public place. Without it, the resume proves she is an excellent domain expert who has ideas about AI — not a co-founder who has started.

**3. The CEO/GTM seat is implied but never claimed.**
"Trusted by districts" and "the trust signal your product needs" positions her as a credibility layer, not as a company operator. Arjun is reading for someone who will own sales, fundraising narrative, design-partner relationships, and the public face of the company. The consulting practice implies she can do this — she closes her own clients, she runs proposals, she does her own lead-gen. But the resume frames it as consulting experience, not as "I have run a sales pipeline and closed five-figure engagements, and that is the muscle I am bringing to this company's go-to-market." That reframe is the difference between an advisor and a co-founder. She needs to claim it.

**4. The equity/runway number is absent.**
"Willing to take a bet" is implied by the consulting history. It is not stated. Arjun wants a number — not "I'm comfortable with risk" but a month count. This is not something that can go on a resume as-is, but the consulting practice section should be written in a way that makes the financial-risk experience explicit: she operated for six years on unsteady 1099 income alongside a teaching salary. That is the data point. It must be surfaced, not hidden behind "independent consultant."

### Specific Resume Fixes

**Line:** `WHERE I WANT TO BUILD: Three theses — AI-assisted IEP writing and compliance tooling for SpEd teams; AI tutors and behavior-support tools designed first for neurodivergent learners; district-side AI governance and vendor-evaluation infrastructure.`
**Rewrite:** `WHERE I WANT TO BUILD: The bet is AI-assisted IEP writing and compliance tooling — the wedge every AI-IEP product gets wrong because the builders don't know IDEA. I have personally audited compliance failures across [N] sites; the same three gaps appear every time. That pattern is the company. The other directions (neurodivergent-first AI tutors, district AI governance) are adjacent markets, not the founding bet.`

Rationale: Collapses three theses into one named stake. Names the founding logic in one sentence. Relegates the other directions to adjacencies rather than competing priorities. The bracketed [N] is a real number Catherine should fill in from her auditing history — if she has audited at four sites, she should say four.

**Rewrite (add under WHAT I BRING or as a new section titled "HANDS ON AI"):**
`I have [audited AI-generated IEP drafts / written about AI-IEP compliance gaps / reviewed [specific product] against IDEA standards] and found [specific finding]. Most AI-IEP tools fail on [specific mechanism]. I have documented this at [link]. I am not studying this problem — I am past the point of studying it.`

Rationale: The last sentence is the one Arjun is actually listening for. It signals identity. A researcher studies; a co-founder has moved past studying. The specificity of the finding is the proof the depth is real.

Ran a sales pipeline and closed five-figure engagements without institutional support while teaching full time.`

Rationale: Converts "consulting experience" into "I run a business" language. "Sales pipeline" and "closed engagements" are the phrases that tell Arjun she can own GTM. The specific engagement count makes it concrete.

**Line:** (missing — the founder identity claim is absent)
**Rewrite (add as closing line of the WHY I AM block, or as the first line after the header):**
`I spent 17 years finding what is broken in special education. I am done writing about it. I want to build the fix and sell it — and I have the compliance auditing history and the district relationships to make that company defensible from day one.`

## Synthesis

### Where Devin and Arjun Agree

Both screeners respond immediately to the same core signal: SpEd / IEP / IDEA compliance auditing across multiple districts is the unfakeable wedge their companies are built on, and Catherine has it. Both weight the OSPI contracts and the six-year consulting practice as proof of independent operation and financial-risk tolerance. Both identify the same three gaps that prevent a fast yes: (1) no AI artifact or specific AI engagement, (2) no present-tense builder identity claim, and (3) the consulting practice is framed as service delivery rather than as company-building. These three gaps are the highest-leverage editing targets.

### Where They Diverge

Devin's primary concern is pace — she will read as district-metabolism, not startup-metabolism, unless the resume surfaces velocity signals aggressively. His is a hire decision at 12 people; if she's wrong it is expensive, not fatal. He will take the call on domain alone and probe pace there.

Arjun's primary concern is identity — is she a researcher who wants to work near a startup, or is she committing to the builder role with a co-founder stake in the outcome? His is a co-founder decision, potentially 40-50% of his cap table; if she's wrong it is company-ending. He needs the resume to prove the identity, not just the domain.

These concerns are not incompatible — the same edits address both — but they produce different thresholds. Devin is BORDERLINE and tips to YES with the AI signal added. Arjun is BORDERLINE and does not tip to YES without the builder-identity claim and the single-thesis pivot in WHERE I WANT TO BUILD.

### Top 3 Highest-Leverage Edits

**1. Add one concrete AI artifact or engagement, placed prominently.**
This is the single change that moves both screeners from "interesting" to "call her." It does not need to be a product or a prototype. It can be a one-paragraph post, a talk, a documented teardown of a real AI-IEP tool's compliance failure. The content matters less than the fact of it — it proves she has put her hands on the future. Without this, the resume answers "does she know the domain?" (yes) but not "will she be able to engage with an AI product at the speed we need?" (unknown). Both screeners read that unknown as a risk.

**2. Rewrite "WHERE I WANT TO BUILD" as a single named bet, not three theses.**
The three-thesis framing reads as option-scoping — the work of a researcher who hasn't committed, or a consultant who is keeping doors open. Both screeners, but Arjun especially, need a single named stake: "the bet is AI-IEP compliance tooling, here is why, here is what I have personally audited that confirms it." Collapsing this from three to one — with the other two named as adjacent, not co-equal — signals that she is already running toward the specific company, not shopping for the right one.

**3. Reframe the consulting practice as company-building with GTM language.**
The line "built a six-year part-time consulting practice" is doing enormous work but is buried and under-claimed. It should read: "Founded and operated Olver Consulting; solo lead-gen, client acquisition, closed [N] multi-site district engagements, ran $X in five-figure contracts without institutional support." That is a sales pipeline. That is founder evidence. Devin needs to see she can move at velocity without a permission structure; Arjun needs to see she can own GTM. The evidence already exists — the framing is hiding it.

### On "WHERE I WANT TO BUILD" — Helps or Hurts?

The section helps, with surgery. A candidate who arrives with a product thesis is demonstrating product thinking — Devin and Arjun both respond positively to a co-founder who has already started thinking about the company rather than waiting to be told what to build. But the three-thesis format converts this strength into a liability: it reads as a researcher's agenda, not a founder's conviction. The fix is not to remove the section — it is to collapse it to one named bet and demote the others. The section, revised, helps significantly.

### On the Lack of Any Tech-Company or Startup Experience

Not disqualifying for either screener — but it is the thing that makes both slower to close. Devin explicitly does not require startup experience; he requires proof the metabolism is compatible. He will probe pace on the call if the resume gives him enough velocity signals to get there. Arjun does not require startup experience either — he has explicitly noticed that great domain co-founders often come from consulting practices, not from prior startups. But neither screener can assume the metabolism without evidence, and the resume's current framing (15 years in a school system, one consulting practice) gives them nothing to grab. The velocity signals — "designed and delivered X in six weeks," "took Y from first contact to signed contract in one quarter," "ran three concurrent contracts with two-week delivery cycles" — are what substitute for startup experience on paper. If Catherine can surface three to four of those, the lack of startup history becomes a talking point on the call, not a screen-out risk before it.
