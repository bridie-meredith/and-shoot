# Resume 05 — Customer Education at an AI Company — Critic Feedback

The target role is a Customer / Partner Education Manager position in the K-12 segment at an AI company — a rare, cross-functional seat that demands simultaneous credibility with skeptical district leadership and with a commercial, product-fluent internal team. The candidate must be able to stand in front of a SpEd director and answer a hostile IDEA compliance question, then turn around and demo an AI platform to a district buyer without hesitation. There are roughly a dozen seats like this in the country at any given moment. The screen is therefore unusually brutal: education-only candidates fail on product fluency, AI-only candidates fail on institutional credibility, and the resume has to prove the dual-fluency visibly — in the first eight seconds — or it does not survive the sort.

---

## Screener: Sofia Mendel (ai-co-customer-education-lead)

**Verdict: BORDERLINE — strong lean toward NO without a revision**

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.**
My eyes go to the headline. *"K-12 customer & partner education | Translating product to district practice."* That is actually the right framing — she's already using the phrase "customer & partner" and "product to district practice," which tells me she's thought about bridging. I keep reading. Most recent role: SpEd Teacher, Kent SD. That is a classroom title, and it immediately raises my fear. I sweep for AI signals. Nothing in the top third. I hit "AREAS OF STRENGTH" — no AI anywhere in that list. I flip to speaking record — AERA, UCEA, WWU, named venues, that's real. This is a genuine MAYBE: the speaking track is the strongest in my stack, the compliance credentials are rare and valuable, and the consulting history is the daily texture of this job. But I cannot find a single artifact that proves she has touched an AI product. My fear is a brilliant SpEd expert who will learn our product on customers' time. Right now this resume confirms that fear rather than dispelling it. She survives the skim on speaking and compliance, but she will not survive my second pass unless I find AI evidence — and there is none.

**Strengths Sofia sees.**
The conference speaking record is the single most powerful positive signal on any resume I read this cycle. AERA 2025 and 2026, UCEA 2024, WWU Forum panelist, guest presentations at Kent SD and Pacific Lutheran — these are named, real, verifiable. This person can be in the room; the proof exists. The compliance and SpEd depth — IEP/504/IDEA auditing, compliance monitoring across three sites, translating federal requirements into practice — is extraordinarily rare on the AI-company side of this hire and it is the thing that makes or breaks a K-12 AI deployment conversation. I know that; almost no other candidate in my stack knows it from lived practice. The independent consulting history since 2019, OSPI contracts since 2023, multi-site audits, district-facing presentations to Kent SD leadership — that is client-facing, stakeholder-managing, walk-in-cold experience. She has run engagements. The EdD plus Dissertation of the Year plus Emerging Scholar signal credibility I can put in front of a superintendent without coaching her. These are real assets. They are enough to make me want this candidate to be a yes.

**Screen-out risks.**
The blocker is absolute: there is no AI product fluency anywhere on this resume. Not a tool named, not a session delivered, not an article written, not a demo given. The headline says "translating product to district practice" but the resume body never mentions what products she has translated or whether she has engaged with AI at all. When I search for an artifact to click — something that says "I think about AI publicly" — I find nothing. The entire speaking and publication record is education-side: inclusive leadership, complex autism, trauma-informed practice. All of it excellent; none of it AI. This is my exact failure mode: the brilliant educator who arrives on day one and needs a quarter of ramp time before she can stand in front of a district buyer and demo without flinching. I cannot take that risk at this comp band, and this resume gives me no evidence that the risk is unwarranted. The second risk is framing. The SpEd/IDEA/compliance angle — the thing that makes her rare and valuable — is buried as a job duty ("compliance monitor across 3 sites") rather than stated as the customer-facing asset it is. I need to read it as "can lead the district compliance and accessibility conversation that every K-12 AI deployment depends on." Right now I have to do that translation myself, and in a stack of forty I mostly won't.

**Specific resume fixes.**

*The headline positioning line is close but incomplete.*
Current: `"Educator and consultant with 15+ years inside K-12 and the credibility to be heard by superintendents, principals, SpEd directors, and classroom teachers alike."`
Problem: no AI in it anywhere. A profile that never says "AI" in the first paragraph tells me she's not sure she belongs in this lane.
Rewrite: `"EdD in inclusive leadership and complex autism | 15+ years building compliance fluency and district trust that AI deployments in K-12 actually depend on | practitioner of AI-assisted SpEd and disability-rights-aligned product deployment."`
Or, if she has used specific tools: `"EdD specializing in inclusive leadership | helping K-12 districts and the AI products they're adopting navigate the compliance, accessibility, and SpEd questions that kill rollouts | currently using [Claude / MagicSchool / Brisk] to design and deliver AI-integrated PD."`

*The WHY I AM EFFECTIVE block lists compliance but buries it as one item among five.*
Current: `"Compliance literacy — comfortable on IEP/504/IDEA, data privacy expectations, accessibility, equity; these objections kill many product rollouts."`
This is the most valuable line on the resume and it is positioned as the fourth bullet in a supporting section. Move it up and make it the explicit claim: this person can lead the district compliance conversation that every K-12 AI company fails at. Consider pulling it into the profile paragraph explicitly: `"One of a very small number of candidates who can answer the hostile district compliance question on AI and IEP documentation, data privacy under FERPA/COPPA, and IDEA accessibility requirements — live, in the room, without flagging a lawyer."`

*The speaking record is named but not quantified enough.*
Current: `"AERA 2026/2025, UCEA 2024 sessions; guest presentations Kent SD, WWU, Pacific Lutheran 2024-2025."`
Rewrite: `"AERA 2025 (presenter, AI and inclusive education), AERA 2026 (presenter); UCEA 2024 Emerging Scholar panel; WWU Forum panelist; guest lecturer Kent SD district leadership, Pacific Lutheran School of Education — all since 2024."`
Add session titles where AI is mentioned. If any session touched AI even tangentially, name it. If none did, that is the gap to close.

*Add an AI-fluency line — the blocker requires a direct response.*
There is nowhere on this resume to add it gracefully yet. The fix must be real before the resume fix can land. A single line in the current role or consulting history that says, for example: `"Design and deliver AI-literacy PD for educators using Claude, ChatGPT, and MagicSchool; advise districts on responsible AI policies under FERPA and COPPA"` changes the entire read. If that sentence is not currently true, making it true — even by designing and piloting one session — should be the priority before applying, not a resume edit.

## Screener: Marcus Hale (ai-co-gtm-partner)

**Verdict: SCREEN-OUT as written — winnable with reframe**

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.**
Headline, positioning line — OK, she says "customer & partner education," that's buyer language. Most recent role — SpEd Teacher, Kent SD, 2025-present. That's a classroom title. I sweep for a number. Nothing in the top half. I hit "AREAS OF STRENGTH" — seventeen items, not one of them reads commercial. "Trauma-informed practice." "Family and community engagement." These are admirable and they don't tell me she's ever been on the hook for an account. I find the consulting work — Olver Consulting 2019-present, OSPI contracts 2023-present — that's the strongest line on the page for me. But it's described as "speak/consult to private schools, districts, university audiences." That reads as bespoke service delivery, not a client business. No engagement count, no renewal, no repeat-client signal. Dissertation of the Year up top. My margin note is forming: lovely educator, wrong seat. She might not be — the raw material is there — but the resume is written as if she is.

**Strengths Marcus sees.**
The independent consulting practice is the best thing on the page, commercially. A person running Olver Consulting since 2019 has sold work, scoped engagements, delivered for institutional clients, and presumably been asked back. The OSPI-funded contracts since 2023 tell me she has secured multi-year publicly-funded work — that is a renewal pattern, even if she doesn't name it as one. I also clock the district-network credibility: Kent SD, OSPI relationships, WWU and Pacific Lutheran — that is not a speaker's bio, that is a pipeline. She can walk into a district account and be trusted by the buyers I need her to reach. Her conference-speaking tells me she can carry a room in front of a crowd. And the multi-site compliance audit experience — three sites, cross-stakeholder — is exactly the muscle for managing a demanding institutional customer across a buying team. These are real commercial signals. She just hasn't written them in my language.

**Screen-out risks.**
The resume has no commercial framing anywhere. Not one mention of adoption, retention, renewal, account health, or customer outcomes. The consulting work — which is a genuine operator track record — is written as service descriptions: `"speak/consult to private schools, districts, university audiences on inclusive leadership, complex autism, trauma-informed practice."` That tells me what topics she covers. It does not tell me she has clients who renewed, engagements that expanded, or districts that kept paying. I need to see the word "retained" or "renewed" or "multi-year" at least once. I also need a number — any number — tied to an outcome she owned. She trained groups of 10-60; she certified 250 staff in Alaska; she managed multiple concurrent OSPI contracts. These facts exist in the resume. None of them are stated as owned outcomes with a metric attached. `"Certified ~250 staff"` is close; it needs one more clause: to what outcome, at what scale, with what follow-on. The deeper problem is that her current role is classroom teacher and her dominant verbs are teach/instruct/facilitate. I read the current title as the answer to "what does this person do most recently" and the answer is: teaches children. That is not the profile of someone I trust to hold a district account under pressure. Add to all of this: no AI product fluency. She cannot demo. I will not put someone in front of a district buyer who freezes when the buyer asks "can you walk me through how your platform handles a student with an IEP." That is close to disqualifying alone.

**Specific resume fixes.**

*The consulting description is the biggest missed opportunity on the page.*
Current: `"speak/consult to private schools, districts, university audiences on inclusive leadership, complex autism, trauma-informed practice; conduct compliance audits at private schools and EC programs; translate federal/state requirements into actionable practice; advise on documentation, accessibility, policy implementation."`
Problem: reads as a services menu, not a client business.
Rewrite: `"Operate independent K-12 compliance and inclusive-leadership practice since 2019 — 30+ engagements across private schools, districts, and early-childhood programs; multi-year OSPI-funded contracts (2023-present); repeat district clients including [name if permissible]; designed and delivered compliance training reaching [N] educators across [M] sites."`
Make the renewal visible. Make the repeat-client pattern visible. These facts almost certainly exist; they just need to be stated.

*The OSPI contracts entry is buried and under-described.*
Current: `"manage multiple concurrent contracts with cross-sector partners; facilitate community learning sessions; design family-facing training programs; translate qualitative findings into program-design recommendations."`
Rewrite: `"Hold concurrent OSPI state-funded contracts (2023-present) — secured renewals across [N] contract cycles; facilitate multi-site community learning sessions reaching [M] participants; design family-facing and educator-facing training programs implemented across [X] districts."`
"Secured renewals" is the phrase I need to read. It converts a job description into a retention signal.

*The positioning line speaks to credibility but not to outcomes or customers.*
Current: `"Looking to bring practitioner-fluent, research-grounded credibility to a K-12 customer or partner education function at an AI or edtech company."`
Problem: "bring credibility" is input language. I need output language — what happens to the customer account because she is in it?
Rewrite: `"Bringing district-trusted compliance fluency and a proven institutional-client track record to a K-12 customer education role — accelerating AI product adoption by solving the compliance and SpEd objections that stall most deployments."`
"Accelerating adoption" and "stall most deployments" are my language. Those words tell me she understands the commercial problem her role exists to solve.

*Stakeholder altitude must be made explicit.*
The resume mentions "superintendents, principals, SpEd directors" in the profile but then buries them. I need to see: `"routinely engages superintendent, SpEd director, and procurement-level stakeholders in compliance and product-adoption conversations"` — stated plainly, not implied. That is the altitude I need to know she can hold.

*AI product fluency — same blocker as Sofia, same hard gate.*
There is nothing to fix here with a rewrite alone. The experience must exist before the line can be written. If it does not exist yet, a single verifiable use case — one AI-literacy session delivered, one tool named and demonstrated, one district AI-policy consultation — is the minimum required before this resume can pass my screen.

## Synthesis

**Agreement between Sofia and Marcus.**
Both screeners agree on the structure of the verdict: the raw material is genuinely strong, the resume as written does not surface it correctly, and the AI-fluency gap is a hard blocker that no amount of reframing resolves. Sofia and Marcus converge on the same core finding from different directions — Sofia because she is terrified of the brilliant educator who learns the product on customers' time; Marcus because a customer-education hire who cannot demo is a liability in front of a buyer. Neither will advance the resume as written. Both believe the candidate is more advanceable than the resume shows.

**Divergence.**
Sofia's screen tilts on the AI artifact: she is more likely to advance on the strength of the speaking record and compliance depth if she sees even one clickable AI-facing piece. Marcus's screen tilts on commercial framing: even with AI fluency added, he will reject a resume that reads as an academic CV with no client-retention signal, no numbers, and no buyer-facing language. Sofia can sometimes do her own translation; Marcus will not. The threshold for Marcus's yes requires more structural changes to the resume frame.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits.**

1. **Close the AI-fluency gap with a real use case, then name it explicitly in the top third.** This is the single non-negotiable. The resume cannot survive either screener without it. The edit is: name one or more AI tools used in actual work (Claude, MagicSchool, Brisk, NotebookLM, ChatGPT), attach a concrete deployment context (PD session designed and delivered, district AI-policy consultation, AI-literacy curriculum piloted), and place that statement in the profile or the first consulting role bullet — not buried. If the experience does not yet exist, building it (even one session, documented and deliverable) is the prerequisite before applying.

2. **Reframe the consulting practice as a client business with renewal signals and engagement-count metrics.** The current description is a services menu. It needs engagement counts, repeat-client language, and at least two numbers tied to scale or outcomes — educators reached, sites served, contracts renewed. "Secured renewed OSPI-funded contracts (2023-present)" is a two-word fix that converts a job description into a retention signal Marcus can use.

3. **Surface the SpEd/IDEA compliance angle as a customer-facing asset, not a job duty.** This credential is rare and valuable and it is currently invisible as a strategic asset. It must be stated in the profile and in the "Why I Am Effective" section as a direct claim: *can lead the compliance and accessibility conversation that every K-12 AI deployment depends on.* That reframing is what turns the deepest credential into the reason both screeners make a call.

**On the SaaS/tech gap.**
The absence of SaaS or customer-facing-tech experience is coachable, not fatal — provided the AI-fluency gap is closed with real practice, because the compliance depth and client-facing consulting history give Catherine a more credible path into this role than most candidates coming from the AI side of the hire.
