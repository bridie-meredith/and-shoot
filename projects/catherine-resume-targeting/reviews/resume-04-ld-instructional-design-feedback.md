# Resume 04 — L&D / Instructional Design — Critic Feedback

Catherine Olver, EdD is a 17-year adult-learning practitioner — PD facilitator, curriculum designer, independent consultant, academic speaker — applying for a Senior Instructional Designer role at a ~900-person Series-D SaaS company. The req is workforce training: onboarding, manager enablement, sales-enablement courseware. The screeners are a first-pass ATS-oriented recruiter (Tyler Brennan) who decides in eight seconds whether a document reads as a tech-company resume, and a Director of L&D (Dana Whitfield) who is looking specifically for a builder who ships courseware for adult employees at scale. Catherine's underlying qualifications are real and meaningful. Her current resume, as submitted, does not reach either screener's threshold — not because her experience is insufficient but because the document speaks the wrong genre entirely.

---

## Screener: Tyler Brennan (saas-talent-recruiter)

**Verdict: SCREEN-OUT** (does not survive ATS + first-pass skim in current form)

**First-8-seconds skim reaction:**

My eyes go to the top line and I get: "Catherine Olver, EdD — Learning & Development | Program design, facilitation, capability-building at scale." Okay, the words "Learning & Development" are there, that's something — but "EdD" is the first credential she leads with, and "capability-building at scale" is not a title. I'm already doing interpretive work, which I don't do. Then I scan the most recent job title: "SpEd Teacher Early Childhood — Kent SD (2025–Present)." Teacher. School district. That's my title-proximity fail right there. I look for a tools strip — nothing. I see a "Selected Speaking & Publications" section, and at that point I've clocked "academic CV" and I'm onto the next resume. She never made it to a phone screen.

**ATS keyword read:**

The saved scorecard for this req is: *instructional design, adult learning, ADDIE, Articulate Storyline, Rise, eLearning, LMS, onboarding, sales enablement, stakeholder, SCORM, learning experience.* Against that list, the current resume hits maybe two: "adult learners" (in the Capabilities block) and "stakeholder" (also in Capabilities). "Instructional design" does not appear. "ADDIE," "Storyline," "Rise," "eLearning," "LMS," "onboarding," "SCORM," "sales enablement" — all absent. The parser scores her in the bottom third of 200 applicants. She may not reach my pile at all. The keyword problem isn't about stuffing a skills bar; it's about whether the accomplishment bullets can carry those terms honestly. They currently cannot because they describe a different vocabulary set entirely.

**Strengths seen:**

The consulting line is real — "Independent Consultant — OSPI-Funded Contracts (2023–Present)" combined with "Olver Consulting (2019–Present)" is an employer-shaped adult-training entry. If I reach the phone screen, her scale bullet ("groups of 10-60") is exactly the program-design number I want. Her tenure reads as stable — multi-year stays, no suspicious gaps, the teaching job she's now leaving after 12 years is a coherent story arc. If she gets on the call, I'll like her. That's the problem: my screen is what's between her and that call.

**Screen-out risks:**

- **No title match anywhere in the top third.** "SpEd Teacher Early Childhood — Kent SD (2025–Present)" is her most recent line. I cannot map "Special Education Teacher" to "Senior Instructional Designer." That's a blocker. The headline partially helps ("Learning & Development") but doesn't complete the job because the most recent title immediately contradicts it.

- **Academic CV format — hard blocker.** The resume contains "AWARDS: Dissertation of the Year (Gonzaga 2025)," "SELECTED SPEAKING & PUBLICATIONS: AERA 2026/2025, UCEA 2024 sessions; guest lectures Pacific Lutheran, WWU, Kent SD 2024-2025," and an "EdD" foregrounded in line one. To me this is the single loudest "not a tech resume" signal in the document. The Publications section alone is an instant reject cue.

- **Zero authoring-tool keywords.** Nowhere in the document does Storyline, Rise, Camtasia, Docebo, Cornerstone, Lessonly, or any LMS appear. The parser penalizes her before I see her; my tools-strip glance confirms the gap. I read "has never built eLearning in a corporate stack." That's a strong screen-out.

- **K-12 institutional framing throughout.** "Kent SD," "Auburn SD," "OSPI-Funded Contracts," "SpEd," "IEP" (implied by "SpEd" context), "families and stakeholders on regulation, child development, trauma-informed care." Every employer is an institution. None is a company training its workforce. I worry the Director bounces it back to me.

- **Mission-language residue.** "Trauma-informed workplace practice · DEI & inclusive practice" in the Areas of Focus block, "inclusive leadership and trauma-informed practice" in the consulting bullet. This is soft on its own but it sharpens every other doubt — I start reading "this person wants a mission job."

**Specific resume fixes:**

Line: `Catherine Olver, EdD — Learning & Development | Program design, facilitation, capability-building at scale.`
Rewrite: `Catherine Olver — Instructional Designer & L&D Consultant | Curriculum Design · Adult Learning Programs · Workforce Enablement`
Why: "EdD" is an academic credential flag, not a title. "Capability-building at scale" is not a role title. The rewrite names the actual target job family in the headline, which is step one in my eight-second skim.

Line: `SpEd Teacher Early Childhood — Kent SD (2025–Present): lead classroom teams of educators and paraprofessionals; deliver training and continuing education to staff.`
Rewrite: Demote this entry entirely or retitle it. If the staff-training dimension is real, surface it: `Staff Training Lead & Classroom Instructor — Kent School District (2025–Present): designed and delivered continuing-education programs for classroom teams of 6-8 adult professionals; developed training materials for onboarding new paraprofessionals.`
Why: "SpEd Teacher" is a title-proximity fail. The staff-training work inside the role is the actual L&D content — lead with that.

Line: `AWARDS: Dissertation of the Year (Gonzaga 2025)` and `SELECTED SPEAKING & PUBLICATIONS: AERA 2026/2025, UCEA 2024 sessions; guest lectures Pacific Lutheran, WWU, Kent SD 2024-2025.`
Rewrite: Delete both sections entirely. If you must keep the award, fold it into one credential line in the Education block: `EdD, Gonzaga University, 2025 (Dissertation of the Year Award)` — one line, no section header, no AERA list. Conference talks become at most a single bullet in the consulting role: "Speaker and workshop facilitator at national professional conferences."
Why: Publications and conference presentation sections are academic CV markers. They are instant-reject cues in my skim.

Line: `Areas of Focus: Adult learning principles · Curriculum & program design · Manager & leader development · Cohort and PD facilitation · Coaching & mentoring · Onboarding & ramp programs · Trauma-informed workplace practice · DEI & inclusive practice · Stakeholder management · Cross-functional program operations`
Rewrite: Replace with a compact tools-and-methods strip that the parser can score: `Skills: Instructional design · Curriculum development · Adult learning · Onboarding program design · ADDIE methodology · eLearning development · Stakeholder management · LMS administration · Facilitation (10–60 learners) · [Articulate Rise / Storyline — if any exposure; if none, build it before applying]`
Why: "Trauma-informed workplace practice" and "DEI & inclusive practice" read as mission-job signals. The strip also needs tool keywords or the parser scores her low regardless of everything else.

## Synthesis

**Agreement between Tyler and Dana:**

Both screeners agree on three things without hesitation: (1) the current document is an instant screen-out; (2) the CV apparatus — awards section, publications section, EdD as the lead identity marker — is a hard-reject signal for both, Tyler because it breaks his business-resume pattern-match and Dana because it reads "thinker, not builder"; (3) the absence of authoring tools is a compound problem, hitting both the ATS parser (Tyler) and Dana's tiebreaker question simultaneously.

**Divergence:**

Tyler's primary failure mode is genre: the document doesn't read as a business resume and her most-recent title is "Teacher." Fix the genre and the title proximity and he advances her. Dana's failure mode is proof: she needs a portfolio link and named tools backed by actual samples. Tyler cannot evaluate whether her instructional design is any good — that's not his job. Dana can, and she will not advance someone whose work she cannot see. This means the fixes operate at different depths: Tyler's problems are surface-level rewriting problems; Dana's most important fix (build a portfolio, acquire Storyline/Rise fluency) requires new work, not new words.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits:**

1. **Build and link a portfolio — immediately.** This is Dana's near-blocker and Tyler's tools-strip gap rolled together. Repurpose two or three existing PD programs into self-paced Rise modules, publish to Articulate Review, put the URL in the header. Every other fix compounds on this one; without it, a rewritten resume passes Tyler and stalls at Dana's first click.

2. **Kill the CV apparatus entirely and reformat as a business resume.** Delete "AWARDS," delete "SELECTED SPEAKING & PUBLICATIONS," bury the EdD in one credential line, strip narrative paragraphs, convert to tight reverse-chronological bullets (verb → scale → outcome). This is Tyler's blocker and Dana's strong-signal problem at the same time. One reformat clears both.

3. **Retitle and reframe the consulting practice as the lead entry, in business-register language.** "Olver Consulting (2019–Present)" is her strongest single asset for both screens — adult-facing, deliverable-oriented, client-work at scale. It currently reads as "Independent Consultant, Speaker & Author" with bullets that speak educator-dialect. Rename the role, retitle it toward instructional design, and recast every bullet in the language of "scoped, designed, delivered, partnered." This is the one entry that can carry the resume for both screeners if it's written correctly; in its current form it cannot.

**Does the K-12-to-corporate-L&D translation land, or does it read as a career-switcher gamble?**

With the resume as submitted, it reads as a career-switcher gamble — and a half-committed one, because the document doesn't even attempt the translation; but with a portfolio link, named tools, the consulting practice repositioned as the lead adult-learning entry, and the K-12 vocabulary stripped from the top half, Catherine's underlying profile — 15 years of program design at scale, doctoral-level credibility, a running consulting practice, proven stakeholder fluency — is exactly the substance a SaaS L&D director needs, and the translation becomes credible rather than aspirational.
