# Resume 02 — Frontier Lab Full-Time — Critic Feedback

## Role Context

Catherine is targeting full-time IC roles at a frontier AI lab in the education research, policy, and inclusive systems family — a cluster of positions that includes model-behavior evals leads, education Trust & Safety policy writers, and human-data / compliance-framework designers. These roles are scarce (10-30 seats across all labs combined), pay $160-250k base plus equity, sit in SF/NYC/London hybrid, and draw hundreds of applicants. The three screeners who matter are a first-pass recruiter running an 8-second cold-pile skim, a technical evals lead reading for structured-assessment rigor, and a T&S policy hiring manager reading for evidence of authored binding policy. All three operate on the same failure mode: a resume that is substantively strong for a domain expert but packaged for a district hiring panel, not a lab.

## Synthesis

**Where the three screeners agree.**

All three land on the same two verdicts simultaneously: the underlying substance clears the bar, and the current packaging does not. Every screener independently flags: (1) no public AI artifact, (2) the compliance and audit work described as duties rather than authored instruments, (3) district vocabulary that does not translate for a non-K-12 reader. These are not three different complaints — they are the same complaint read through three different lenses. The resume is a strong domain-expert document presented to screeners who are not domain experts and who will not do the decoding work.

Maya and Devang land on nearly identical screen-out mechanics — both go to the URL first, both find nothing, both stall on the classroom-teacher title. Naomi's failure mode is slightly different — she gets further into the resume because she reads for document nouns, and she finds them in degraded form ("produce detailed findings reports" rather than "authored the protocol"). She is the most likely to advance Catherine even from the current resume, but only to "maybe," and she will argue herself out of it on the second read without the AI-policy bridge.

Devang is the hardest gate on the cold pile without an artifact — his blocker is more mechanical and less arguable than Naomi's.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits, in priority order.**

1. **Publish and cite one AI-education artifact above the fold.** This is not a resume edit — it is a prerequisite to the resume being functional for this target. A 600-word policy piece on what AI tutoring products miss about IEP compliance, posted to LinkedIn or Substack, named and linked in the header. All three screeners independently identified this as the single change that converts the application from "park it / set it down / adjacent" to "second pass / yes pile / maybe with upside." No resume rewrite substitutes for it. Without it, Maya and Devang soft-no the cold apply; Naomi advances it to "maybe" only. With it, all three engage.

2. **Convert the compliance-auditing work from duty to instrument.** The Olver Consulting bullet is the most important line on the resume for both Devang and Naomi. "IEP/504/IDEA compliance audits across private schools and EC settings; produce detailed findings reports" must become "Authored multi-site IEP/504/IDEA compliance audit protocol used across [N] settings; translates federal statutory obligations into site-specific remediation requirements, findings defensible to state review." That is a rewrite of roughly 25 words, and it converts the resume's single most load-bearing line from "duty" to "built instrument." Devang will say "this one's real." Naomi will find the document noun she's hunting for.

3. **Rewrite the positioning line to be legible to a recruiter, an engineer, and a lawyer simultaneously.** The current PROFILE opening — "Researcher and practitioner at the intersection of inclusive K-12 leadership, special education compliance, and equitable systems design" — is correct but inert. It names domains but does not make the transfer legible. Replace it with something like: "Special education compliance specialist and IEP/IDEA auditor translating federal accessibility and child-protection law into enforceable practice — now applying that framework to how AI education products handle students with disabilities." That sentence: names the rare domain (Maya's forward phrase), uses instrument language (Devang's "rigor signal"), and names the job she is applying for in recognizable terms (Naomi's legibility test).

**Is full-time frontier-lab a realistic target for this profile?**

Yes, with a clear role family caveat: the T&S / education policy path (Naomi's req) is the most realistic door, because Catherine's compliance-auditing work is structurally identical to what that role requires and her child-development depth is precisely the scarcity Naomi cannot otherwise hire for — but it becomes accessible only after the AI-policy artifact is published, the compliance work is reframed as authored protocol, and the positioning line is translated out of district vocabulary; the evals path (Devang's req) is the second-best door and shares the same artifact prerequisite; the cold-recruiter path (Maya) is the weakest channel and should be backstopped with a warm referral from any researcher or policy person inside a lab, because without a referral tag this profile will not clear the cold-pile skim reliably regardless of resume quality.
