# Resume 02 — Frontier Lab Full-Time — Critic Feedback (Round 2)

## Role Context

Catherine is targeting full-time IC roles at a frontier AI lab in the education research, policy, and inclusive systems family — model-behavior evals leads, education Trust & Safety policy writers, and human-data / compliance-framework designers. Scarce seats (10-30 across all labs), $160-250k base plus equity, SF/NYC/London hybrid, hundreds of applicants per req. Three screeners: a first-pass recruiter on an 8-second cold skim, a technical evals lead reading for structured-assessment rigor, and a T&S policy hiring manager reading for authored binding policy.

This is the **second round**. Round 1 produced a unanimous BORDERLINE from all three screeners, with one shared diagnosis — strong domain substance, district packaging — and three named fixes: (1) publish and cite a real AI artifact, (2) convert audit work from duty to authored instrument, (3) rewrite the positioning line to be tri-legible. The revision was made with one new fact in hand: Catherine has completed a "Generative AI for Educators" PD course (Google/ISTE) and now routinely uses Claude/ChatGPT in her consulting workflow. She has no deeper AI experience. This round judges whether the revision banked the round-1 fixes, and whether the modest AI step is surfaced credibly — neither inflated nor buried.

The PROFILE then gives him the sentence he was hunting for in round 1: "reading a statute, turning it into a repeatable check, and holding institutions to it." The first experience bullet now says **"Authored a multi-site IEP/504/IDEA compliance audit protocol"** with "traceable criteria and follow-up verification." That is the instrument-language sentence. AREAS OF FOCUS leads with "Special education compliance" and "IEP/IDEA audit protocol design" / "Compliance audit framework design" — exactly the nouns he stops on. He reads "this one's real."

- A clickable artifact exists. He will open it; if it is good, the resume is done. The title — compliance questions to ask AI *vendors* — is squarely on his domain.

For his specific req — turning "should the model treat a 7-year-old with an IEP differently" into a testable spec — a second piece that sketches an actual eval or rubric would convert ADVANCE into a strong, unarguable yes. Not blocking; the compliance-protocol framing already carries most of the weight.

- RESOLVED — *Compliance work framed as duty, not instrument.* "Authored a multi-site... compliance audit protocol" with "traceable criteria" is the exact rewrite round 1 asked for.
- RESOLVED — *Residual leadership framing.* "Inclusive K-12 leadership" and "equitable systems design" are gone from the tagline and AREAS OF FOCUS. The framing is now instrument-first.

**New problems introduced.**

## Synthesis

**Where the three screeners agree.**

All three round-1 BORDERLINEs were driven by the same three deficits: no AI artifact, audit work framed as duty rather than authored instrument, and district-coded positioning. **The revision banked all three.** A named, clickable, on-domain artifact now sits in the CONTACT line above the fold. The Olver Consulting bullet now opens "Authored a multi-site IEP/504/IDEA compliance audit protocol" with traceable-criteria and statutory-translation language. The tagline and profile are written in plain, tri-legible English with no leadership cosplay and no adjective inflation. Every screener's single highest-leverage round-1 ask has been satisfied. The verdicts moved accordingly: Devang and Naomi both move from BORDERLINE to ADVANCE; Maya moves from "soft-no, parked" to "second pass" but stops short of a clean advance for one reason outside the resume's control.

All three also agree on the **remaining shared dependency**: the application now stakes its survival on the cited LinkedIn article being genuinely substantive. Every screener will click it. If it is a real piece — a policy memo with judgment in it, on-domain — it converts all three. If it is thin or SEO-flavored, the gain reverses into a credibility hit. The resume has done its job; the artifact must now do its job.

The AI-familiarity step is handled **correctly and honestly by all three readers' standards.** It is surfaced (certificate in EDUCATION & RECOGNITION; concrete tool use in the profile), it is not buried, and — critically — it is not inflated. The profile describes "use Claude and ChatGPT routinely to draft, summarize source documents, and stress-test rubrics" — concrete workflow, not claimed expertise. Devang, whose blocker is "AI claimed with nothing to verify," gets both a verifiable artifact and an honest, modest tool-use claim. Naomi, who screens out overclaiming instantly, finds nothing to screen out. This was the highest-risk part of the revision and it landed on the right side of the line.

**Where they diverge.**

Maya is the one screener who does *not* reach a clean advance — not because of any resume defect, but because her card makes a cold apply to a scarce req without a referral a practical blocker regardless of resume quality. For her, the resume is now as good as a resume can be; the bottleneck has moved entirely to the warm-intro channel.

Devang and Naomi both ADVANCE, but probe different residual gaps downstream. Devang wants a second, *eval-flavored* artifact (a rubric or test sketch) to make his yes unarguable for his specific req; the existing piece is policy-flavored. Naomi is satisfied by a policy-flavored artifact but will test the genuine depth of the AI/tech-policy thinking in the writing exercise — a limitation the resume honestly cannot paper over and correctly does not try to.

One shared minor flag: "AI education product policy [in development]" sits in the AREAS OF FOCUS competencies list. All three read the bracket as honest, but a competencies list is normally for things held, not things begun. It is acceptable as-is; it would be marginally cleaner relocated to the profile, where the "moving into" framing already makes in-progress status expected.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits still worth making.**

1. **Verify the cited artifact is genuinely substantive — and if it is a listicle, deepen one item into a worked policy tradeoff.** This is no longer a resume edit; it is the load-bearing dependency. All three screeners will click the LinkedIn piece, and Naomi's standard for it is high. The resume now promises a real artifact; the artifact must be a real policy memo, not five thin bullet points. This is the single highest-leverage remaining action.

2. **Secure and surface a warm referral.** This is the only thing standing between Maya's "second pass" and a clean forward, and her card is explicit that she will fight for a referred borderline candidate and merely glance at a cold strong one. The resume cannot fix this — Catherine or the user must source one name inside a lab and get it into the recruiter's system. Without it, the cold-pile channel stays unreliable no matter how good the resume is.

3. **Publish one eval-flavored second artifact and relocate "AI education product policy [in development]" to the profile.** A short piece sketching how she would *test* a model's handling of an IEP-protected student converts Devang's ADVANCE into an unarguable yes for his specific req, and gives Maya a second link. Relocating the bracketed competency tidies the one minor placement flag all three noticed. Lower-leverage than #1 and #2, but both are clean, low-cost gains.

**Is the resume submission-ready?**

**Effectively yes — the resume itself is submission-ready.** It banked every round-1 fix, surfaces the modest AI step honestly without inflating or burying it, and earns an ADVANCE from two of three screeners and a "second pass" from the third. The two remaining gates are no longer resume problems: the cited artifact must be genuinely good (verify before submitting), and a warm referral should be secured to make the recruiter channel reliable. Submit the resume as-is to the policy and evals reqs once the artifact is confirmed substantive; pair every submission with a referral attempt. Edit #3 is a worthwhile but optional upgrade, not a blocker.
