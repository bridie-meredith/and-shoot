# Resume 06 — Solo Consulting to Districts — Critic Feedback (Round 2)

## Context

This is the second-round review of the buyer-facing consultant one-pager. The artifact was revised after round-1 feedback; this pass judges whether the revision landed. The three reviewers are district buyers — not hiring managers — deciding whether to spend district money on Catherine Olver's services. They read defensively, fast, under time pressure, looking for a reason to discard. Verdicts are purchase decisions with political and professional skin in the game.

The revision proceeds on a stated assumption: Catherine has taken one modest, credible AI step — a "Generative AI for Educators" completion certificate plus routine use of ChatGPT/Claude in her own consulting and PD-prep workflow. No deeper AI experience. The revision reordered the page to lead with compliance and treat AI governance as an *extension* of the compliance practice. This review judges whether that strategy worked for each buyer.

Round-1 verdicts, for reference: Renata WOULD EXPLORE, Eleanor WOULD PASS, Joanne WOULD EXPLORE.

Feedback is organized by buyer, then synthesized.

## Buyer: Renata Okafor — Director of Special Education

**Verdict: ENGAGE** (clears the skim, earns the call — moved up from round-1 WOULD EXPLORE)

**First-8-seconds skim reaction.**
Her eye lands on the tagline: *"Special education compliance consulting | IEP/IDEA audits • AI governance for SpEd • Inclusive practice PD."* That is her exact problem in the first line — *special education* and *compliance* fused, with *AI* present and explicitly subordinated ("AI governance **for SpEd**," not "AI in education"). She does not get dropped into the forty-emails pile. She skims to ENGAGEMENT AREAS and the first bullet is "Special education compliance audits" with a named deliverable; AI governance is bullet two. The page now sequences the way her shortlist filter is built. She keeps reading because it rewards reading, not in spite of itself. This is a clean pass through the 8-second gate — a meaningful change from round 1, where it was "a near miss."

**What builds trust.**
- **The tagline fusion is fixed.** SpEd + compliance + AI, visible in 8 seconds, no hunting. Round-1 Fix 4 fully delivered.
- **"OSPI-funded contracts, 2023-Present"** still the hardest-working line on the page. State-agency contract work tells her this person operates at the altitude where compliance is decided.
- **The compliance-audit bullet now names deliverables in procurement nouns** — "a compliance audit report, a prioritized findings list with IDEA-exposure flags by site, and a board-ready summary structured to survive state monitoring." This is exactly the noun-string her round-1 feedback demanded. She can picture the purchase order.
- **The AI governance bullet now carries the risk-literacy signal.** "Integrating FERPA, LRE, and IDEA obligations into vendor evaluation and ongoing governance" — this is the line whose absence was a near-blocker in round 1. AI is now framed as a compliance extension, not a vendor pitch. The framing sentence in WHAT I DO ("AI governance there has to be compliance-grade, not just pedagogically sound") reinforces it.
- **Rate bands are now on the page** — "$3,500-7,500" workshops, "$15-65k" projects, retainer available. She can scope before the call instead of during it.
- **PDA autism, complex autism, trauma-informed practice** still signal she is not selling a generic deck.
- **The EdD + Dissertation of the Year + AERA/UCEA record** still reads as board armor. The AERA line is now reframed as "the evidence base underlying my compliance and policy work" — load-bearing, not decorative.

**What raises doubt — remaining risks.**
- **AI competency is now declared, but the evidence is thin and she will clock it.** The credential line reads "Generative AI for Educators completion certificate; a routine user of general-purpose AI assistants in my own consulting and PD-prep workflow." Renata's round-1 risk was "AI as a claimed competency with thin public evidence." This is *honestly* thin — a completion certificate plus self-use is not a demonstrated point of view about what AI gets wrong for SpEd kids. She will partly discount the AI-authoring claim and may still fall back to reading Catherine as "an excellent compliance auditor who could probably also do the AI policy." That is a *call*, not a *reject* — but it is not the unambiguous win the compliance side now is. The risk is downgraded from round 1, not eliminated.
- **The risk-literacy signal lives in the AI bullet but not in a SpEd-specific claim.** Renata's deepest AI-specific want is a consultant who can say what AI gets *wrong* for her highest-need kids — bias against disabled learners, AI tools that assume the median student. The current page integrates FERPA/LRE/IDEA into *governance process* (good) but never states a *point of view* about AI failure modes for neurodivergent students. A single sentence — "AI tools are built for the median student; for a kid with a PDA profile or a complex communication need, that assumption is where the IDEA exposure lives" — would convert the last of this risk.
- **"Model AI policy ... available for review on request" appears twice** (engagement-areas bullet and Recent Engagements). It is a real artifact substitute, but "on request" is softer than "here it is." For Renata this is minor — she is not the policy-artifact buyer — but the doubling reads slightly anxious.

**Round-1 risk disposition.**
- RESOLVED — AI-first positioning with no risk framing. The engagement list now leads with compliance; AI carries FERPA/LRE/IDEA language.
- RESOLVED — no deliverable named in procurement nouns. Compliance bullet now names report + findings list + board-ready summary.
- RESOLVED — no rate/scope signal. Bands are on the page in her exact ranges.
- RESOLVED — headline buries the niche. Tagline now fuses the three words.
- RESOLVED — "leadership development" diluting the headline. Third pillar is now "Inclusive practice PD," which is squarely her world.
- PARTIALLY RESOLVED — AI as claimed competency with thin evidence. Honestly disclosed now, but still thin; she discounts it somewhat.
- REMAINS — no stated point of view on what AI gets wrong for neurodivergent learners specifically.
- NEW — none material.

**Specific further fixes.**
1. Add one sentence of AI risk-literacy *opinion* to the AI governance bullet or WHAT I DO — what AI specifically gets wrong for complex/PDA profiles. This is the cheapest remaining upgrade and it is the one thing standing between "call" and "call first."
2. Optional: collapse the duplicated "model AI policy ... on request" to a single appearance, and consider "available for review" without "on request" — it reads as more confident.

## Buyer: Eleanor Voss — Deputy Superintendent / Chief of Staff

"Special education compliance consulting" leads — and her round-1 problem returns in a softer form: the *first* word she reads is "special education," and she is commissioning a *whole-district* governing document. The framing sentence ("which is why I am also the right author for AI policy") is the bridge, but it is asking her to follow an argument rather than handing her the conclusion in the headline. Eleanor's round-1 Fix 5 (her numbering: Fix 5) asked her to *cite the specific state mandate and deadline* — naming WA's actual guidance/timeline. The revised page names no mandate at all. For a forced buyer on a clock, a consultant who names her deadline signals "wrote this for me." The current page does not. This is a small, cheap, unaddressed miss.

**Round-1 risk disposition.**

## Synthesis

**Where all three buyers now agree.**
The revision worked. All three verdicts moved up. The round-1 diagnosis — "not failing on content, failing on sequence" — was correct, and the resequencing fixed the thing it was supposed to fix. Specifically, all three buyers agree on what is now *right*:
- The tagline fuses SpEd + compliance + AI and survives the 8-second skim.
- Deliverables are named in concrete nouns at every engagement level — board-ready summaries for Renata, board policy + administrative regulation for Eleanor, three-tools-Monday + protocols/templates for Joanne. The verb-phrase fog is gone.
- The practitioner-first opening line ("17 years as a special education practitioner") simultaneously kills Joanne's ivory-tower risk, reassures Renata of real SpEd depth, and gives Eleanor a credibility frame.
- Rate bands are on the page.
- The compliance-as-spine / AI-as-extension reframe is honest to Catherine's actual expertise and is the correct strategic choice given the thin AI credential.

The single AI-familiarity step — completion certificate plus routine self-use — was integrated honestly and without overclaiming. It does not pretend to be more than it is. That honesty is the right call: an inflated AI claim would have tripped every one of these buyers' generic-consultant detectors. The cost of the honesty is that the AI claim is genuinely modest, and two of the three buyers price that in.

**Where they diverge.**
The same reorder that won Renata and Joanne is the one that holds Eleanor at BORDERLINE. Leading with "special education compliance" is exactly right for the SpEd director and the principal — it is their world. For the deputy superintendent commissioning a *whole-district* governing document, leading with SpEd keeps her pattern-matching to "program specialist," and the page repays that deficit only through an argument she has to follow in body copy rather than a conclusion handed to her in the headline. Round-1 Fix 1 for Eleanor explicitly asked Catherine to "lead with policy authorship, not special education"; the revision chose the opposite, and chose correctly for two buyers out of three. This is a genuine, unavoidable divergence — one page cannot lead with two different identities.

The deeper divergence is artifact-readiness. Renata buys on compliance-audit proof, which Catherine *has* — so Renata is now a clean ENGAGE. Joanne buys on practitioner credibility and a right-sized concrete offer, which Catherine now *shows* — so Joanne is a clean ENGAGE, with one call-stage caveat. Eleanor buys on a named board-adopted policy artifact, which Catherine *does not have* — and no amount of editing produces one. Her verdict is capped by an operational gap, exactly as round 1 predicted.

**Is it submission-ready?**
For Renata and Joanne: effectively yes. The page would not be embarrassing to send to a SpEd director or a principal today, and both would likely call. The two further fixes for them (a line of AI risk-literacy opinion; a "I've run this AI session" reference) are upgrades from "call" to "call first," not blockers.

For Eleanor: the page is submission-ready as a *first-contact* document but is mis-targeted if it is aimed at the top of her $25-75k band. She is a BORDERLINE — a scoping-call or co-author buyer, not a lead-policy-author buyer — until a board-adopted artifact exists. The honest framing is: this one-pager is ready to *open* a conversation with a deputy superintendent, not to *win* a whole-district policy engagement outright.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits still worth making.**

1. **Add one whole-district clause to the AI governance bullet.** State plainly that the board-adoptable policy covers acceptable use for all staff and students, academic integrity, and vendor data-privacy standards — with SpEd/accessibility as the section Catherine is *strongest* on, not the section she is *limited* to. This is the single edit that most helps Eleanor without costing anything with Renata or Joanne: it converts "program specialist" into "whole-district author with the rarest possible bench on the hardest section." Highest leverage because it is the cheapest move against the one buyer still at BORDERLINE.

2. **Add one sentence of AI risk-literacy opinion — what AI gets wrong for neurodivergent learners.** Something like: "AI tools are built for the median student; for a child with a PDA profile or a complex communication need, that median assumption is exactly where the bias and the IDEA exposure live." This converts Catherine's thin-but-honest AI credential from a *claimed competency* into a *demonstrated point of view* — the one thing that survives a thin certificate. It helps Renata most (clears her last AI doubt), helps Eleanor (shows judgment, not just process), and costs nothing with Joanne.

3. **Restore the state-mandate signal and name a specific deadline.** The round-1 "35+ states have published guidance" stat was dropped; for Eleanor, a forced buyer on a clock, naming WA's (or the target state's) actual AI guidance and timeline is the cheapest single way to signal "this was written for me, not blasted to fifty districts." Re-add it to the AI governance bullet.

**One operational note that is not an edit.** The board-adopted-artifact gap is the ceiling on Eleanor and cannot be written around. As round 1 said: piloting a small-WA-district policy adoption from the OSPI/superintendent network, or publishing the model policy as a genuinely public document (not "available on request"), is what moves Eleanor from BORDERLINE to ENGAGE. Until then the one-pager is correctly built — it just should not be pointed at the top of her band. Similarly, if Catherine has not yet delivered an AI-tools session to a teacher audience, piloting one before leaning on the AI-literacy bullet closes Joanne's lone call-stage risk. Both are things to *do*, not things to *write*.

**Bottom line.** The revision resolved the structural problem round 1 identified. Two of three buyers move to ENGAGE; the third moves from PASS to BORDERLINE and is capped by an operational gap, not a copy gap. The page is submission-ready for the SpEd-director and principal audiences now, and ready as a first-contact document for the deputy superintendent — with three low-cost edits that would tighten all three reads and one operational step (a board-adopted artifact) that would lift the last buyer.
