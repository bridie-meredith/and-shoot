# Resume 04 — L&D / Instructional Design — Critic Feedback (Round 2)

This is the second-round review of the revised Resume 04, targeting a Senior Instructional Designer (L&D) role at a ~900-person Series-D SaaS company. The revision was made in response to round-1 feedback from two screeners: Tyler Brennan (first-pass ATS-oriented recruiter) and Dana Whitfield (Director of L&D, portfolio-first hiring manager). Round 1 returned SCREEN-OUT from both.

The revision made four visible moves: (1) replaced the academic headline with a clean instructional-design tagline; (2) added an ATS-keyword CORE SKILLS strip; (3) dropped the AWARDS and SELECTED SPEAKING & PUBLICATIONS sections, folding the dissertation award into one education line; (4) retitled the teaching roles toward training functions and led with consulting. It also added a modest, credible AI-familiarity signal — a completed "Generative AI for Educators" PD course (2026) plus routine ChatGPT/Claude use in the consulting workflow. This review judges whether those moves clear each screener's threshold.

## Screener: Dana Whitfield (saas-ld-director)

**Verdict: BORDERLINE** (no longer an instant reject; earns a real read — but stalls at the portfolio click)

**First-8-seconds skim reaction:**

Header. Title line says "Instructional Designer & L&D Consultant." Good — that's not "teacher," my default verdict starts neutral-to-positive. Eyes flick top-right of the header for a portfolio link. Contact line: Algona WA, phone, an `.edu` email, LinkedIn, and — there it is — "Portfolio: catherineolver.com/portfolio." A portfolio URL is present. That buys her a real read; round 1 she'd have been NO by now. Most recent role, first bullet: "Design and deliver adult learning programs for professional audiences (cohorts of 10-60)." Adults, program, scale. Tools strip: Storyline, Rise 360, Camtasia, LMS, SCORM — named, specific. Scale scan: "10-60," "~250 staff," "60% referral reduction." This is not "another teacher." This is a credible MAYBE. She gets the full document read and a portfolio click.

And then the portfolio click is where my verdict actually gets decided — and that's the problem. The resume has done its job; it can't do the portfolio's job. Everything past this point is conditional on what `catherineolver.com/portfolio` actually contains.

**Strengths seen:**

The repackage is genuine, not cosmetic. The consulting practice leads, retitled "Instructional Design & Training Consultant," with deliverable-verb bullets — "design and deliver," "develop," "scoped and managed." The teaching roles are retitled toward the training function and the K-12 dialect is mostly drained from the lead bullets. ADDIE is named ("ADDIE-informed design process" in the profile, "ADDIE methodology" in the strip) — that costs nothing and signals method, and round 1 explicitly asked for it. The 60%-referral-reduction metric is a real business-style outcome. Scale is quantified throughout (10-60, ~250, 4-6 staff supervised, $350K budget). A portfolio URL is in the header. The AI line is modest and honest — "completed a course, uses ChatGPT and Claude to draft and stress-test rubrics" — which is exactly the right altitude; it signals current-practitioner without bluffing depth she doesn't have. I don't penalize it and I mildly credit it.

**Remaining screen-out risks:**

- **The portfolio is a URL, not yet a proven asset.** This is the whole ballgame. A link in the header gets her the click; what's *behind* it decides YES vs NO. My standard: I need to see 2-3 sample modules built in Rise or Storyline that are credibly hers and credibly adult-facing. The resume cannot tell me whether that exists. If `catherineolver.com/portfolio` is live with real Rise/Storyline artifacts, she's a YES to a recruiter screen. If it's a thin page, a list of PD topics, or a 404, she's a NO the instant I click — and a dead portfolio link is *worse* than no link, because it reads as carelessness. **My BORDERLINE verdict is genuinely suspended on this.** *Near-blocker until the portfolio content is confirmed.*

- **Authoring tools are named but unbacked.** Round 1 said: named tools *with samples to back each*. The revision did the first half. Storyline, Rise, Camtasia, SCORM appear in the CORE SKILLS strip but no bullet anywhere says "built X in Rise" or "authored a SCORM module in Storyline." My tiebreaker question is "but does she know Storyline?" and the resume still doesn't answer it — it just asserts it. Combined with a 17-year history that is entirely facilitation and curriculum-design (live, instructor-led) with zero eLearning-authoring evidence, my honest read is: she may have *touched* these tools via the AI course or self-study, but she has not *shipped* in them. The strip risks reading as the "heard the tools matter" bluff I screen hard against. *Strong — and it's the seam between BORDERLINE and SCREEN-OUT.*

- **No completion/adoption/Kirkpatrick metric for any training program.** The 60% referral reduction is a good outcome but it's a *program-intervention* metric, not a *learning* metric. I look for "completion rate," "adoption across the org," "ramp-time lift," "Kirkpatrick Level 2/3." "Curricula adopted across the organization" gestures at adoption but isn't quantified. *Soft.*

- **The work is all instructor-led facilitation, not self-paced courseware.** Every bullet describes designing and *facilitating* — live cohorts of 10-60. The req owns "onboarding curriculum, manager-enablement tracks, and sales-enablement courseware," much of which is asynchronous self-paced eLearning. The resume proves she can design and run live training; it does not prove she can build a self-paced Rise module a new hire takes alone. The CORE SKILLS strip claims "eLearning development" but no experience bullet demonstrates it. This is the substantive gap behind the tools-not-backed risk. *Strong.*

- **NEW — the AI line, judged on my screen: net-neutral-to-mild-plus, correctly scoped.** It does not overclaim. It does not pretend to be "AI-powered instructional design." It reads as a working professional who uses current tools — which is table stakes, not a differentiator, and the resume treats it that way. I neither reward it heavily nor distrust it. Correctly handled. No fix needed.

- **K-12 background still legible — but no longer disqualifying.** Employers are districts and OSPI; "classroom," "First Aid/CPR," "child development" appear. Round 1 this was a strong screen-out. The retitling and consulting-led order have downgraded it to "non-traditional background I'll forgive *if the portfolio proves the person already does the job*." That conditional is the same conditional as the portfolio risk above — they're the same gate. *Downgraded; conditional on portfolio.*

## Synthesis

**Where the two screeners agree:**

Both agree the revision is a large, genuine improvement and that the round-1 instant-reject is gone. Both confirm the same fixes landed cleanly: the headline now names the target role, the CV apparatus is gone, ADDIE is named, mission language is stripped, and the consulting practice leads as an adult-facing entry. Both also agree the AI-familiarity line is correctly scoped — modest, honest, not overclaimed — and neither penalizes it. Tyler advances; Dana gives a real read where round 1 gave none. The genre-translation problem — "this doesn't read as a tech resume" — is solved.

**Where they diverge:**

The divergence is the same structural split as round 1, and it's now the *only* thing separating their verdicts. Tyler's job is genre and keywords; the revision fully satisfies him, so he flips to ADVANCE. Dana's job is *proof* — she will not advance an instructional designer whose design work she cannot see and whose authoring-tool fluency is asserted but never evidenced. The revision gave her enough to grant a read (BORDERLINE) but not enough to grant a YES. Tyler's remaining concerns are soft and phone-screen-deferred (comp, an `.edu` email). Dana's remaining concerns are substantive and require *new work, not new words*: a real portfolio with real Rise/Storyline artifacts behind the URL, and ideally one self-paced eLearning deliverable she can point a bullet at. The resume is now as good as a resume alone can get her for this role. The gap that remains is not a writing gap — it is an evidence gap, and it lives behind a hyperlink.

**Top 3 highest-leverage edits still worth making:**

1. **Confirm the portfolio at `catherineolver.com/portfolio` is live and contains 2-3 real, self-built Rise or Storyline modules — before submitting.** This is Dana's entire suspended verdict. A working portfolio with credible adult-learning artifacts flips her BORDERLINE to a YES; an empty page, a topic list, or a dead link flips it to an instant NO that is *worse* than having shipped no link at all. The resume has done everything it can; this is the one action that converts the BORDERLINE. Highest leverage by a wide margin — and it is build-work, not edit-work.

2. **Back at least one authoring tool with a real accomplishment bullet.** Right now Storyline/Rise/Camtasia/SCORM/eLearning live only in the CORE SKILLS strip — the layer parsers count and hiring managers distrust. Add one bullet to the consulting entry that names a tool inside a deliverable: e.g., "Built a self-paced onboarding module in Articulate Rise 360, packaged to SCORM for LMS delivery." This closes Dana's "but does she know Storyline?" tiebreaker and converts the strip from an asserted claim into an evidenced one. (If she genuinely has not built in these tools, this is a reason to build one module now — which also doubles as the portfolio artifact in fix 1.)

3. **Add one learning-specific metric and lightly signal self-paced/asynchronous capability.** The resume quantifies headcount and the 60% referral reduction, but has no completion rate, adoption figure, or ramp/Kirkpatrick number, and every bullet is live instructor-led facilitation. Add one figure ("training curricula adopted by X teams" / "Y% course-completion") and ensure at least one bullet describes a self-paced or asynchronous deliverable, since the req's courseware is largely async. This is the smallest of the three but closes Dana's last two soft-to-strong doubts.

**Is the resume submission-ready?**

As a *document*, it is nearly there — Tyler advances it today, and most of his residual notes are cosmetic or phone-screen-deferred. It is **not yet submission-ready as a candidacy**, because Dana's verdict is BORDERLINE and explicitly suspended on the contents of the portfolio link, which the resume cannot supply. The blocking item is no longer rewriting — it is fix 1. Confirm a live portfolio with real authoring-tool artifacts behind it, fold in fixes 2 and 3, and the resume converts a two-persona split (ADVANCE / BORDERLINE) into a clean two-persona pass. Submitting before the portfolio is verified risks the worst outcome on Dana's screen: a dead link read as carelessness. Hold the submission until the portfolio is real.
