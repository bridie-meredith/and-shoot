## Voice-Fidelity Systemic Notes

### What the writer-fork is doing well

**Pre-calc tense.** Entries 4, 5, 8, 9, 14 all deploy past-perfect correctly and consistently. "She had already counted," "the calculation had set them," "the cognition has already cleared" — these are the diagnostic rhythm of Taylor's interiority and the fork has internalized the pattern.

**Cost-language as resting register.** Across the 18 entries, cost-language appears without being forced: "exposure," "priced," "budgeted against," "threshold," "his to spend," "shifted to Mira's side." This is Taylor's native unit of measure and the fork uses it as such — not as ornament, as the actual accounting register.

**Semicolon usage (mostly correct).** The canonical habit-to-consequence chain fires cleanly in entries 4, 8, 9, and 16. Entry 1's use is slightly off-model (parallel readings rather than habit→consequence) but close enough that it does not reach fail.

**Refusal-to-look construction.** Entries 6 and 17 both execute the gap-narration pattern correctly. The "what is on [the] other side stays the size [X]" formulation is an effective Taylor-register rendering of the refuse-to-name move, and both instances hold the doubled register without naming the Earth-Bet monument underneath.

**Trailing fragment on "whether" (entry 14).** This is the strongest cadence move in the file. The sentence ending on the withheld word, with the withheld content being the foreknowledge-clamp, is precisely Taylor's closing-clause-twist adapted to interiority.

**Specificity.** Counts and measurements appear throughout: "two beats," "two paces," "two strokes," "by one." These are not decorative — they are the tell.

### What is slipping

**Author-voice reaching through the register (entries 10, 18).** The two fails both result from the writer-fork switching from character-vocabulary to author-vocabulary under pressure. Entry 10's "heavier than what it brought" is a poetic weight-metaphor — it names the emotional significance of the transaction rather than the transaction itself. Entry 18's "register" and "the voice underneath" are narrative-craft terms, the vocabulary a writer uses to describe what a character is doing, not the vocabulary the character uses to describe what they are experiencing. Both entries know the right content but render it in the wrong idiom.

**The fix pattern for both:** stay on the fauna or the body. What are the beetles doing when the cost-transfer happens? What does the eleven-year-old body do when the mask thins near Aldric? The base card is explicit: under fear or pressure, she narrates what the bugs are doing instead of what she is feeling (§"Voice tells" inventory-tell). The same rule applies to mask-thinning — it should surface through the inventory or the body, not through naming the layer.

**Structural echo in the refusal-to-look entries (6 and 17).** Both use "stays the size [X]." Entry 6: "the size she will not name." Entry 17: "the size it has been." The syntactic skeleton repeating across two entries 31 proto-lines apart is noticeable. Each instance is individually valid, but the repetition makes the construction feel like a formula rather than a voice. One revision to entry 17's formulation in Phase 4 would dissolve this.

**Entry 13 cadence tail.** "Along the dirt" appended after the load-bearing measurement ("by two paces") is a small cadence problem. Taylor ends on the load-bearing word. The location-descriptor should precede the measurement or be dropped.

**Entry 15 syntax tangle.** "And she carries with it" is the weakest grammatical execution in the file. The cost-doubling impulse is correct; the syntax strains. A cleaner version: "two strokes; the determination is now a thing the record carries, and she carries it."

## Specific Worm-Canon-Pedant Concerns

WCP is the primary calibrator on voice-fidelity grounds. Their dissents follow.

**Entry 10 — REJECT.** "What the hand carries away is heavier than what it brought." This is the most significant voice-fidelity failure in the file. Taylor does not render consequence as weight-metaphor. When something costs, she names the cost in ledger terms or in body-terms or in fauna-terms. "Heavier" as emotional accounting is the prose doing the feeling-report she refuses to do. Per base card §"Voice tells": "When grief surfaces, it is in the form of an inventory item." The watch-cost returning with more weight than it came with is the right content; the expression of that content through figurative weight is the wrong channel. WCP demands: name what the hand carries away (the record, the officer's receipt, the specific document) and name what that carries forward (the exposure is now on the record, the cost is now in his possession and will return). Do not weigh it.

**Entry 18 — REJECT.** "Inside the frame the inventory thins by one register; the voice underneath is closer to the surface." The tell-phrase "the voice underneath" is the most significant vocabulary violation in the file. Taylor does not have a term for her own doubled-register structure. She experiences the mask as performance (when it surfaces consciously) and as absence (when it slips and she notices she is not performing). She does not name the thing under the mask as "the voice underneath" — that is the author naming it for her. Per base card §"Vocabulary" §"Reaches for": her operational nouns are "status, inventory, assessment, scope, framing, parameters, variable, gap." "Register" is not in this set. WCP demands: render the mask-thin through what is actually happening in her perceptual layer. If the ravens are closer-present (Septon Aldric's proximity changes how she is running the roost), name the ravens. If the body stops performing the child-fidget, name the stillness. If she catches herself on the verge of a long planning sentence she would not normally voice, name the catch. Do not name the layer-structure.

**Entry 1 — semicolon concern (not reject).** "The beetles hold the seam steady; the wheel-tremor has not arrived." The base card licenses semicolons for habit-to-consequence chains ("She did this automatically; she was often operating on that information before anyone else knew she had it"). This entry's semicolon joins two parallel passive-feed observations rather than a habit→consequence chain. The function is closest to two simultaneous register-readings stacked. WCP accepts this under a broad reading of the base-card rule but marks it: if the writer-fork is using semicolons freely between any two independent clauses, that is pattern-creep. The rule is specific and should be enforced specifically.

**Entry 2 — register-height concern (not reject).** "Gate-line of egress" is reading at negotiation-register height, not at resting-interior register. The base card notes that formal/Latinate vocabulary appears "under negotiation pressure" (§"Register markers"). Interior prose is not under negotiation pressure. WCP would prefer "the exit-line" or "the egress-slot" or simply "the gate" with a count — blunter, more resting-register.

**Entry 17 — structural echo concern (not reject).** WCP clocked the "stays the size" repetition between entries 6 and 17. A pattern that fires twice in 18 entries is on the verge of becoming a formula. Taylor's voice has several signature constructions but they are not formulas — each surfaces under specific pressure and does not repeat mechanically. WCP would revise entry 17's formulation to avoid the echo.
