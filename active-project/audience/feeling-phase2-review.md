## Entry @6 — mira-stonefield: "her gaze tracks the yard before her mouth opens"

### Per-Persona Verdicts

**worm-canon-pedant:** VOICE-OK

Mira is not a Worm character and no canon-match gate applies. Checking against the persona card exclusively. The tell directly instantiates two named features of the card. First: §Look — "Eyes that move slightly faster than her face does." The phrasing "her gaze tracks the yard before her mouth opens" is a direct enactment of this: the eyes have moved, the face has not yet caught up (her mouth has not opened). The sequencing in the tell captures exactly what the Look note describes — the eyes are ahead. Second: §Signature Moves §1 "The stillness beat" — "Before she responds to anything she has assessed as significant, there is a pause." Here the "before her mouth opens" is the pause rendered somatically, named as a gaze-action rather than as stillness-named. This is a correct re-description of the stillness beat through her card's specific eye-movement vocabulary rather than generic-watchful. No register violation. No synonym-ladder for a forbidden feeling. Verdict: this tell could not belong to Edric or Taylor. It belongs to Mira's card. Tracks.

**dark-fantasy-reader:** VOICE-OK

Mira's card establishes her as a woman who assesses before she speaks, whose assessment is visible as a particular kind of stillness, and whose eyes are structurally faster than her face. "Her gaze tracks the yard" has the flavor of a person who reads space as a survival input — not a person who pauses because they are cautious in general. "Tracks" is a specific verb: directional, continuous, disciplined. Generic-watchful would give us "her eyes moved over the yard" or "her eyes swept the space" — the formulation that could belong to any character who is careful. "Tracks" implies the gaze has a working purpose and follows a logic. Combined with "before her mouth opens," the sequencing tells us the space-read has to complete before she commits to speech. This is Mira's specific economy — §Voice "Economical. She does not explain her reasoning... You get the conclusion; the reasoning is hers." The reasoning here is the yard-tracking; she does not explain it; it happens before the output. No register-leak. Not generic-watchful.

**pulp-enthusiast:** VOICE-MIXED

The tell is correct for the card. I have no complaint about fidelity. Light flag: "tracks the yard" is a slow beat. The gaze-tracks-then-speaks is a competent character note but it does not announce that a specific Mira is doing something only Mira does — it is more visible as a skilled survivor character tell than as a Mira-specific tell. The audience can read it and clock it as Mira if they already know Mira, but on a first-encounter beat it risks reading as genre-capable rather than character-specific. The "slightly faster than her face does" language from the card gives the tell its Mira signature; the written somatic tell captures it but the distinctiveness is slightly lower-contrast than the card vocabulary implies. Minor note. Not a FAIL. Voice register holds; this is texture-softness not registration error.

### Aggregate Verdict: ACCEPT

2 VOICE-OK (worm-canon-pedant, dark-fantasy-reader) + 1 VOICE-MIXED (pulp-enthusiast, minor texture note). Threshold: accept with light note. The mixed note names a contrast-softness on a first-encounter beat but does not identify a register violation.

**Light note on @6:** The tell is card-faithful and not generic in register. The pulp reader's note is advisory: the "tracks" verb is doing signature-carrying work; the stitcher should let it be the precise word, not soften it or swap it for a more generic eye-movement verb at render time.

## Entry @39 — taylor-hebert-westeros: "her shoulders go down and back"

### Per-Persona Verdicts

**worm-canon-pedant:** VOICE-OK

Hard-fence check on Earth-Bet specifics: none present. "Her shoulders go down and back" contains zero Worm-specific vocabulary, no Earth-Bet proper nouns, no cape-register diction. Clear.

Where most people draw their shoulders up under fear, hers go down and back. She has trained this — being a smaller target is the wrong instinct for someone who has to deploy a swarm." The tell in the fire is word-for-word the canonical body behavior: down and back. The base card is the authority for this. The somatic tell is not an interpretation of the card; it is a direct rendering of the documented tic.

The worm-canon-pedant's gate for the Westeros variant is whether the tic still makes sense to carry. The base card says the shoulder-set is trained — it is a behavioral artifact of cape-discipline, ingrained enough to be automatic under fear/high-alert. The Westeros variant card (§Non-verbal tics) preserves the base-card tic set with only additive modifications; nothing in the variant card overrides or revokes the shoulder-set. The variant card explicitly notes "Base-card tics (`taylor-hebert` §Non-verbal tics) all carry." The cape training does not disappear because the body is eleven years old. Trained tics do not require the original training context to fire; they fire because they were trained. This tracks against Worm mechanics (conditioned body responses from parahuman context).

Final check: the worm-canon-pedant reads "trained cape-body-discipline showing through the sept-ward mask." The base card says she trained this; the variant card adds "Body softened to read as child... Under pressure the layer thins and the base stillness reappears." The shoulder-set is that base-layer showing under pressure. This is canonical and coherent. The base card explicitly names this tic as trained, persistent, and the opposite of the untrained shoulder-raise — that behavioral inversion is a voice-distinctive marker. Verdict: VOICE-OK. Canon faithful. Base card cited correctly.

**dark-fantasy-reader:** VOICE-OK

Mira has a gaze tell. Edric has a hands-and-weight tell. Taylor's tell is the shoulder-set. The three-fire vocabulary stays in different somatic registers — no overlap. On this entry specifically: the shoulder-set that goes down rather than up is the kind of detail that belongs to a character who has been shaped by something the reader does not know the full shape of yet. It reads as the body of a person who has been trained to control fear rather than perform it. For the Westeros variant this means: under the sept-ward mask, the cape-discipline fires. The character's body is ahead of the performance she is maintaining. This is character-as-consequence — the body carries history the world cannot see. "Down and back" is distinct from generic: the generic fear-under-control tell is stillness. This is the opposite-of-the-expected-direction. No Westerosi characters share this tell; it is hers because of what made her.

Shoulders down and back. Quick, specific, unmistakable. No character confusion. The inversion (down not up) is a fast signal that something in this person has been modified — that she has not just controlled fear but trained around it. Reads as a character beat with forward momentum: what was she trained for? This one does not blur or slow. It is a body action that names itself and its strangeness in the same motion.

## Entry @57 — edric-cray: "his hands settle at his sides and his weight finds the back foot before the step"

### Per-Persona Verdicts

**worm-canon-pedant:** VOICE-OK

Edric is not a Worm character; the canon-match gate is inapplicable. Checking against persona card. The tell has two components: "his hands settle at his sides" and "his weight finds the back foot before the step."

Card authority for hands: §Look — "He is always slightly aware of where his hands are." The hands settling to his sides is the somatic enactment of this — not the hands moving in a reaction but the hands finding a deliberate resting-position, which is the outward form of someone who tracks where their hands are. The placement "at his sides" is specific: not behind, not in front, not clasped. At his sides means: accessible, contained, not displaying. This is the hands-awareness tell made visible.

Card authority for weight-and-foot: §Signature Moves §1 — "The exit check first. In any new space, before anything else, his eyes find the door." §Look — "It is not stillness for effect. It is the stillness of someone running calculations." §Default Stance — "Wherever there is a clear sightline to the exits." The weight going to the back foot "before the step" is the body version of the exit-check: before committing to forward motion, the weight draws back. This is the posture of someone who does not commit before they have assessed. The "before the step" clause names the timing — the calculation happens in the body before the action executes. This is "stillness of someone running calculations" rendered as weight-distribution rather than as facial stillness.

Both components are card-grounded. No register violation.

**dark-fantasy-reader:** VOICE-OK

The tell has two distinct actions which stay in two distinct somatic registers (hands, weight/foot) but they belong to the same behavior logic: a man who is very aware of where his body is positioned before he commits to anything. The card describes this as "the stillness of someone running calculations" — the tell renders this as something the body does at the moment of transition (preparing to step). Generic-calculating would give you a jaw-tightens or a long look at the door. This tell is in Edric's physical register: hands placed, weight drawn back, then the step. It is the body of a man who has been assessing exits his entire life making a decision. The double-action tell is controlled: both halves are his. Not Taylor's shoulder-set; not Mira's gaze-track. This is the weight-and-hands vocabulary of someone built for sustained physical labor and sustained situational awareness, who knows exactly where his mass is before he moves it.

**pulp-enthusiast:** VOICE-MIXED

The tell is correct and I can track it to the card. My light flag is about compounding: "his hands settle at his sides and his weight finds the back foot before the step" is a two-clause somatic tell where each clause is doing card-faithful work. The slight softness is that the tell, at this density, slows the beat a degree — the two clauses arrive one after the other and the second one requires the reader to track a weight-shift and a pending action simultaneously. One-clause version would land harder. The card note "always slightly aware of where his hands are" plus the exit-orientation gives the writer material for either clause as a standalone tell. As a combined tell the register is right but the pacing cost is light. Not a FAIL — this is the pulp reader noting that the tell is doing two things that could have been one. Voice fidelity holds. The hands-clause and the weight-clause both belong to Edric specifically. Advisory only.

### Aggregate Verdict: ACCEPT

2 VOICE-OK (worm-canon-pedant, dark-fantasy-reader) + 1 VOICE-MIXED (pulp-enthusiast, pacing note on two-clause density). Threshold: accept with light note. The mixed note is about delivery compactness, not register error.

**Light note on @57:** The tell is card-faithful across both clauses. The pulp reader's note is advisory for the stitcher: at render time, if the two clauses produce a density problem in prose flow, the stitcher may opt for the more weighted clause ("his weight finds the back foot before the step" alone carries both his exit-orientation and his calculation-stillness). The hands-settle clause is also independently card-faithful. Neither is wrong; the stitcher has clean options.
