## Attack vector

Earned consequence and whether the world pushes back. My read on zero-metaphor chapters is: does the absence feel like restraint or like the chapter forgot to pay its bill? A figurative silence that reads as cold and deliberate is different from one that reads as the author not having anything to say. ## Primary attack: does the chapter's darkness earn silence?

The chapter's two peak bones — @17 (filing Wren as ward-junction contact, unnamed, without approach) and @24 (Taylor's recognition-and-suppression of the surveillance architecture she built) — are the chapter's load-bearing moments. These are the beats where consequence propagates.

The question is whether that silence reads as the cost of the cold-utilitarian register (which is correct for this character and this story) or as a chapter that let its peak-bones pass without paying the bill.

The vibes cluster at @17 (7 entries, including vibes:9 "inside-coverage-without-crossing-any-threshold / the-map-is-already-around-her") and mem:2 @18 ("the absence has the shape of one person and the shape is the architecture she came here to refuse to build") carry the consequence-register at these beats. The reader knows what just happened. The world — or at least the accounting of it — has already named the cost. The silence at the metaphor layer is not the chapter dodging the bill. The bill is documented. The filing IS the cost.

This is the texture I wait for. Not the moment the character acknowledges what they're doing. The moment they don't — and the chapter does it for them, from the outside, through the bones of the scene. The cold-utilitarian register without figurative overlay is correct for this story-type (slow-prevention-tragedy) and this character (affect suppressed not absent; moral ledger explicit; the theme never spoken on-page).
