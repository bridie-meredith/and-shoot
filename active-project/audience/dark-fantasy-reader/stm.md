## Round 2 — OQ-2 Plan Review (2026-05-17, /and-project Phase 1b)

Topic: Taylor Hebert arrival circumstance and identity-handle. Three options (block-capped, insects-only, ambient power-state settled from OQ-1).

Verdicts:
- Option 1 (Flea Bottom, "vagrant"): REVISE. World-invisibility is not world-hostility. A protagonist the world doesn't notice is not the same as a protagonist the world opposes. Early wins unrecorded means no friction — the world is indifferent, not dangerous. Indifferent worlds do not cost anything to move through.
- Option 2 (Dragon Gate, "witch"): ACCEPT. World makes its move before she does. Septon calls witch — that is Westerosi-specific superstition, not generic authority. Gold cloaks release her as lunatic — she survives by being dismissed, not rescued. The world's hostility is immediate, culturally grounded, and the resolution (dismissal) compounds rather than resolves: she is now marked and loose in the market block. Earned.
- Option 3 (Maegor's Holdfast, "spy"): REVISE. Two days detained, released as not-worth-paperwork. After detention, release reads as reprieve. The adjacent-to-power positioning feels author-placed — the world drops her near the machinery she needs. That is set dressing for the protagonist, not a world that existed before her.

Pattern updated: The arrival must be the world's first move, not Taylor's. Option 2 is the only arrival where Westeros acts first and the resolution (dismissal-as-lunatic) is not a softening. The witch-label compounds — it is not neutralized when she walks free, it follows her. That is the correct shape of earned bleakness: the first hostile contact leaves a mark that doesn't come off.
