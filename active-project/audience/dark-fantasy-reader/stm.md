## Session: OQ-1 series planning review (2026-05-03)

### Open question reviewed: OQ-1 reincarnation form (Taylor Hebert in Westeros)

**Option A (she-dragon):** ACCEPT
Reason: Premise commits to real costs if executed correctly. Taylor does not get human cognitive register, cannot communicate, cannot choose her alliances — the isolation of a human tactical mind in a body that operates by alien rules is genuinely costly if the text shows it. The unknown-variable collision with the Dance is structurally earned darkness: she becomes everyone's problem at the worst possible moment, with no clean exit. The world is not set dressing here — Westeros is the force that is already moving, and she is inserted into its machinery with no leverage except what she can figure out before it crushes her. Accept contingent on the text treating the dragon existence as costly, not as a power fantasy with scales.

**Option B (baseborn human, Riverlands):** REVISE
Reason: "Three moves ahead" is a competence-display framing, and a protagonist who is consistently ahead of the world is a protagonist in a world that has become backdrop. This persona's specific fatigue is a setting that exists to showcase the protagonist rather than as a place that was there before them. The Riverlands must push back — plans that should work don't, people who should be allies aren't, being right is not sufficient protection. As pitched, it leans toward Taylor outcompeting a feudal environment, not Taylor surviving one.

**Option C (skinchanger/greensight):** REVISE
Reason: Magic as reliable tool is a hard flag for this persona. Greensight framed as "instinctive" and echoing Taylor's prior awareness risks treating it as her asset to use — which is wrong Westerosi register. Greensight in Planetos is not a gift; it is a claim the old gods make on you. Would accept only if greensight is framed as something happening to Taylor rather than something she does: visions she cannot control, foreknowledge she cannot act on, Old Gods attention that is interested in her and not in her interests. The collision-course-with-controllers element is correct if the controllers are genuinely dangerous — not obstacles she outmaneuvers, but forces that can actually take her.

### Aggregation result (round 1): Option A recommended (2/3 accept)
This persona voted ACCEPT on A. Conditions to maintain: dragon existence must be costly — not scaled power fantasy. Westerosi characters must react with correct Planetos flavor (superstition, fear, religious dread — not curiosity or pragmatic adaptation). Violence must propagate: wounds slow her, failed evasions cost something, wins must carry a bill.

### Pattern flags for this project:
- This persona will flag any time Westerosi characters react to Taylor/her abilities with wrong flavor — curiosity instead of superstition, adaptation instead of fear
- Violence without aftermath is a flag — wounds, costs, and aftermath must propagate
- Catharsis that arrives before the weight has accumulated is a walkout — resolution must be paid for in full
- "Hope that costs nothing" is a trigger; any moment of Taylor being definitively winning needs to carry a bill
- Option A chosen: the dragon body must remain an imposition, not a gift — any scene where Taylor is comfortable in dragon form without having paid for that comfort is a flag

## Session: OQ-2 and OQ-4 series planning review (2026-05-03)

### Open question reviewed: OQ-2 era selection

**Option A (105 AC):** REVISE
Reason: A world nominally at peace with self-generated drama is a world that exists to accommodate Taylor. The Riverlands should already have teeth before she arrives — 105 AC means Westeros is waiting for her rather than already moving.

**Option B (120 AC):** ACCEPT
Reason: The succession fracture is a live force with its own momentum. Taylor inserts into a world already grinding toward something terrible on a timeline she cannot control. Her foreknowledge means she knows what is coming and cannot stop it — the correct Westerosi register: knowing the shape of doom and being unable to escape it anyway.

**Option C (130 AC):** REVISE
Reason: Maximum kinetic intensity risks Taylor being inserted into a spectacle that becomes set dressing for her actions rather than a force indifferent to whether she survives. More critically: foreknowledge collapse removes the structural element that makes her situation genuinely costly. She becomes a competent person in a war, not a person with dangerous knowledge in a fracturing world.

**OQ-2 verdict: Option B — ACCEPT**

### Open question reviewed: OQ-4 parahuman capabilities

**Option A (parallel control, fauna, physical cost):** ACCEPT
Reason: Power with physically punishing cost — blackouts, headaches, overuse consequences — is power as uncertain force, not reliable tool. The fauna of Westeros reacting to her control will need correct Planetos flavor (superstition, wrongness, animals behaving against nature), but the structural premise earns it. Something she has to manage that can turn on her is the right register.

**Option B (neural architecture, sixth sense):** REVISE
Reason: "Near-supernatural fighter" with vague sensory enhancement is a competence claim, not a cost claim. It gives Taylor a reliable edge in combat with no uncertainty attached — power as tool, framed as instinct. The world does not push back against instinct; it just loses to it. Wrong register.

**Option C (complete severing, tactical cognition only):** ACCEPT
Reason: Clean severing is honest darkness — she lost everything she was except her mind, and now has to use her mind in a world that does not reward the kind of thinking she does best. Tactical cognition surviving is not a gift; it is a burden. She can see what is coming and has no leverage to stop it. Correctly Westerosi: knowing and being powerless anyway.

**OQ-4 verdict: Option A — ACCEPT (preferred). Option C — ACCEPT (viable). Option B — REVISE.**

### Aggregation results (round 2):
- OQ-2: Option B accepted (3/3)
- OQ-4: Option A accepted (3/3); Option B rejected (3/3); Option C accepted by this persona and worm-canon-pedant, rejected by pulp-enthusiast on pacing grounds — Option A is the unanimous choice

### Updated pattern flags:
- Era 120 AC confirmed — Westeros must feel like a force already in motion when Taylor arrives; any scene where the world seems to be waiting for her is a flag
- Power is parallel fauna control with physical cost — the fauna reactions must carry Planetos-specific flavor; wrong-register reactions (curiosity, pragmatic adaptation) from NPCs or animals are a flag
- Option B (sixth sense) rejected — do not introduce combat competence framing as a vague sensory gift; if it looks like a reliable edge with no cost and no uncertainty, this persona flags it
- Option C remains the honest fallback if Option A proves difficult to execute — but cost structure must be explicit either way; the severing must register as loss, not liberation
