# b01c10 chunk review — dark-fantasy-reader

## The three questions answered directly

**(a) Does Corwick land as a real person becoming a closed ledger entry, or as abstraction?**

Mostly real — but the weight is unevenly distributed. The body-map detail (months of observation, errand-corridor, lower-gate faction-inference, the second man at the heir's-business gate) is the strongest material in the draft. That accumulation makes him someone who has been watched, not merely a node label. The phrase "he did not consent to the observation, he did not consent to the body-map, he did not consent to the delivery" is blunt in the right way — it names the three-step instrumentalization without asking the reader to feel bad about it on Taylor's behalf. That is the correct register. What I'm less certain of: s02's handling of the surrender. "She provides it" — one clause — is where the act happens, and then the chunk moves immediately to the translation mechanics (name, errand-frequency, faction-contact inference). The surrender itself has almost no weight at the scene level. A body-mapped person becoming a deliverable needs a beat that isn't the mechanism of the delivery. The mechanism is there; the weight of the act in the moment of commission is thin.

**(b) Is the suppressed-recognition crack in s04 FELT?**

The "face stays in the feed-record / the ledger does not ask her to stay with it" line is the strongest single beat in the chapter. It earns its keep. The image is doing real work: the ledger closes; the face doesn't comply with the closing. That is the correct dark-fantasy shape of a suppression that costs something — the mechanism works and something persists anyway. What concerns me is that the crack is named explicitly in the [image:] tag before the reader encounters it as experience. "corwicks-face-in-feed-record-after-ledger-close" is authoring notation leaking into the material's logic. The chunk should earn the image through what precedes it; the preceding material (accounting-runs-on-corwick, gain column accurate, cost column accurate) is accurate but flat. The precision of the accounting is what makes the face's persistence sting — but the accounting as written runs too cleanly as procedure. It needs one moment where the procedure hesitates, however small, before it files. The crack lands because the line is good, not because the preceding four lines earned it.

**(c) Is the systematic-override-rationalized threshold (moral_framework −1.0) EARNED?**

Yes, across the chapter as a unit. The two-tranche structure (s02: surrender; s04: filing-after-consequence-confirmed) is the right architecture. Seeing the detention confirmed in the feed (s03) before Taylor opens the ledger is load-bearing — she is not running an abstract cost-benefit calculation, she is calculating with the consequence already visible. That earns the threshold. My concern is not whether the −1.0 is deserved but whether it will read as earned at bone level. The chunk currently treats the accounting in s04 as a completed procedure that closes cleanly. The threshold being crossed should feel like a threshold — not a dramatic event, but a one-way door that the reader recognizes as one-way even if Taylor does not. The ledger-close needs to register as foreclosure of something, not just completion of something.
