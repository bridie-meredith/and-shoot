## Round 3 — OQ-3 Plan Review (2026-05-17, /and-project Phase 1b)

Topic: Taylor Hebert time-of-arrival within pre-Dance window. Three options (Dragon Gate arrival, witch-label, block-cap, settled from prior rounds).

Verdicts:
- Option A (126 AC late summer, ~3 years before Viserys dies): ACCEPT. Three years of administrative normalcy means the foreclosure must be inscribed in daily operational texture, not announced by political crisis. That is the correct shape: the reader watches each door close individually — the witch-label hardening, the block-cap narrowing operational logic, the slow accumulation of decisions that cannot be undone — without a dramatic hinge telling them "now the bad part starts." The war off-page at close means the first irrecoverable atrocity functions as the book's foreclosure made visible. The reader has been watching it approach for three years of prose. They cannot say they did not see it coming. That is the road-to-hell shape inscribed into the reader's experience of the book, not into the prose's commentary about it.
- Option B (127 AC early winter, ~18 months): REVISE. "Two-act symmetry" is a structural convenience, not a structural argument. Viserys dying at the midpoint hands the reader a labeled hinge — "here is where the book changes." The last viable pivot becomes a chapter marker rather than a thing the reader feels closing. The foreclosure is announced by the plot's shape; it should be dramatized by the prose's accumulation. This does not do that.
- Option C (128 AC autumn, ~6-8 months): REVISE. War as majority of book inverts the problem: political crisis is doing the structural work the block-cap and witch-label pairing was supposed to do. The last viable pivot (Viserys at Act 1/Act 2 boundary) is assigned by the timeline, not dramatized in prose. The reader does not watch foreclosure happen — they arrive when it is already operating at scale. Theme is delivered by events, not inscribed into the reader's reading experience. This reader does not accept delivery by event.

Pattern updated: Structural foreclosure requires the reader to accumulate the evidence themselves — not be shown a pivot labeled as such, not arrive after the pivot has fired. Option A is the only timeline where the whole book is the accumulation. The atrocity at the close lands as consequence the reader assembled, not as war-event the plot provided. Future iterations will be read against whether the pacing continues to let the reader do the structural work, or whether the plot starts doing it for them.

## Round 5 — OQ-4 Plan Review (2026-05-17, /and-project Phase 1b)

Topic: Taylor Hebert first faction-gravity at story open. Three options (Dragon Gate arrival, witch-label, block-cap, 126 AC, grief-recovery Act 1 with granular street friction settled from prior rounds).

Verdicts:
- Option A (Watch-track, Gold Cloak captain): ACCEPT. The dramatic-irony density is structural, not announced. Taylor thinks she is building an intelligence network on neutral ground. The reader can read, in the operational texture — captain's channel, Hightower patronage, promotion paths — that she is constructing Green infrastructure scene by scene, without a single line of exposition naming it. The faction-character is legible in the mechanics of who gives and who receives. The foreclosure shape is inscribed in the infrastructure itself: the network becomes most valuable to her at exactly the moment it becomes irrecoverable. She cannot dismantle it without destroying what she built; she cannot keep it without being what it is. Foreclosure is not argued — it is visible in the operational record from Act 1 onward.
- Option B (Faith-track, Dragon Gate septon): REVISE. "Tolerates Faith-framing because useful — teaches the reflex of letting misreadings stand" is narration doing structural work the prose should be doing. The patron-relation here tells the reader what Taylor is learning. Option A's irony is that the reader sees the faction-gravity in the texture while Taylor does not; Option B announces the reflex she is developing. That is theme-by-statement. This reader does not accept theme-by-statement.
- Option C (Velaryon merchant-track, Lyseni factor): REVISE. "Longest invisible fuse" is precisely the wrong shape. Invisible means the faction-character cannot be read in operational texture — the dramatic-irony gap between what Taylor thinks and what is true exists only retrospectively. Irony-as-reveal is a plot device; irony-as-accumulation is a structural property. This reader requires accumulation. A fuse the reader cannot see until it fires does not inscribe foreclosure; it delivers surprise. These are not the same thing.

Pattern updated: The faction-gravity must produce readable dramatic-irony density in the operational texture from the first scene it appears — not a reflex the narration describes, not a revelation delivered at Act 2. Option A is the only option where the reader can watch the faction-character build in the mechanics of the network itself: who the captain reports to, which channels the promotions travel, whose patronage the infrastructure runs on. The irony gap between Taylor's model of what she is building and the reader's accumulating read of it is present on every relevant page. That is the doubled-accumulation structure this book requires: Taylor's understanding and the reader's understanding diverging in real time, with the reader always ahead. No other option produces this. Options B and C either announce the gap (B) or withhold it until revelation (C).
