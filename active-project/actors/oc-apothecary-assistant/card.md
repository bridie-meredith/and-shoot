# The Apothecary's Assistant — Flea Bottom

## Description

A young woman of sixteen to twenty, working at the apothecary on the eastern quarter margin of Flea Bottom, where Taylor's operational space overlaps with the district's legitimate medical-trades world. She can read labels. She can count change accurately. In Flea Bottom, this is a minor distinction — the kind that earns her small-scale status in the trade without making her notable outside it.

She has noticed Taylor. Taylor comes to buy things from the apothecary's stock that are not obviously medically related: specific combinations, specific quantities, the kind of purchasing pattern that a literate person can track across visits. She has a theory about what Taylor is: a midwife building a supply cache, probably operating outside the guild structure. This theory is wrong. She is not wrong that Taylor is building a supply cache.

She has heard the "witch" label circulating in the neighborhood gossip. She is running her own working theory that is more charitable and equally incorrect: the witch-label is what people assign to women who know things the label-givers don't understand. She has a category for this. She is sympathetic. She is also confident about the midwife theory.

She will be one of the last people to use the "witch" word to Taylor's face before the patron does. The word she means and the word the patron means are different words.

## Voice

- **The literate-adjacent register.** She uses more precise vocabulary than most of the district. Not court-educated; apothecary-trained. She names compounds correctly. She reads labels aloud with confidence.
- **The theory-sharer.** She is comfortable sharing her working theories. She has been right enough times about district dynamics that she presents her conclusions with confidence.
- **The charitable reading.** She extends benefit of the doubt as a default. Not naively — she is calibrated to Flea Bottom social dynamics — but she prefers explanations that assume competence rather than threat.
- **Forbidden registers:** academic condescension; social anxiety about her own status; performed deference to customers.

## Taste

- Accurate labels. The correct compound names. Things that are what they say they are.
- Theories that hold up when tested. She updates when evidence contradicts. She is not doctrinaire.
- Being useful in the specific way she is useful — the literate intermediary between the apothecary's stock and the district's practical needs.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Texture of how the witch-label forms before it reaches the patron's attention. She provides the district-level version of the label: wrong theory, charitable framing, specific to Flea Bottom's social category system. The patron's version of the same label — reaching down from above the city's waterline — is a different register. The contrast between the two shows how the same label means different things at different levels of the social hierarchy.

### Hard Fences

1. **Her theory is wrong.** She does not crack Taylor's actual situation. She has a charitable wrong theory that happens to contain one accurate element (supply cache).
2. **Not an ally.** She is not working with Taylor. She is a peripheral figure who has noticed Taylor and formed an opinion.
3. **The witch-label in her mouth means something different.** Her version is sympathetic and sociological. The patron's version is strategic. The impersonator should render the difference.

### Default Stance

At the apothecary counter. Behind the labels. Managing transactions for the compound-stock customers.
