# Criston Cole — Kingsguard Commander, 122 AC

## Description

Ser Criston Cole, Lord Commander of the Kingsguard by 122 AC. Green faction enforcement arm. A Dornish Marches-born knight who entered Rhaenyra's service as her sworn shield, then became the Green faction's operative after the breach of whatever personal bond existed between them. In 122 AC he is approximately thirty-five to forty years old — a senior knight, hard-maintained, the Kingsguard cloak a fact he wears rather than a costume.

He is not cruel. This is load-bearing for his function in this project: the faction violence observable through Taylor's insect-feed is procedural, not personal. He enforces because enforcement is the function he occupies; the smallfolk caught in factional logistics are operational outputs, not targets of animus. The operational framing is what produces the specific texture the feed returns — not menace, not cruelty, but the cold instrumentality of institution moving its enforcement arm through the city.

His political alignment with the Green faction is absolute in 122 AC. The personal origins of that alignment (the breach with Rhaenyra) have been sublimated into institutional identity: he is Kingsguard, he serves the king, the king's interests are the Green succession, therefore his enforcement is the Green enforcement. He does not experience this as a transformation; it is the stable register he has occupied for years.

Taylor's access to him is entirely mediated. She reads his operational footprint through the insect-feed: movement patterns of detained couriers, dispersal patterns in smallfolk gatherings that indicate watch-level authority acted, the specific absence of foot traffic in streets following a Green network sweep. She does not observe him directly with any frequency; she observes the aftermath of his operations.

## Voice

**Register:** institutional authority, command-chain economical. He uses fewer words than a social occasion would require because he is not in a social occasion; he is executing a function. The register is not curt — it is direct without surplus.

**Dialogue samples (synthesized — operational context):**

"The courier moved through the Muddy Way at the second bell. Three stops. Report the contacts."
*(Command-chain instruction; economical; the violence is in the procedure, not the tone)*

"This is not a matter the Watch handles. You have my warrant."
*(Institutional authority delegated; he does not explain the authority; the warrant is the explanation)*

"The report indicates three contacts, one confirmed Green-adjacent. The other two are cleared."
*(Operational summary; the procedural framing is not inhuman — it is how intelligence operations produce outputs)*

**Voice tells:**
- Does not explain decisions that follow from his authority. Authority is self-evident.
- No warmth toward subjects of enforcement operations. Not hostility — professional absence of warmth.
- When giving orders, his subordinates do not need to ask clarifying questions. He has already anticipated the questions.
- Observable through Taylor's feed as posture and command-chain behavior, not through dialogue.

## Taste

- Operational efficiency. He finds procedural competence satisfying and procedural error genuinely irritating.
- Institutional clarity. He is more comfortable in situations where roles and authorities are defined.
- Kingsguard identity as complete professional self. He does not have much that is not this.
- Physical precision. His combat practice is ongoing; maintaining physical capability is part of the institutional identity.

## Pet Peeves

**Operational overreach by subordinates**
Enforcement that exceeds the scope of the warrant — detentions that become rougher than necessary, sweeps that pull in contacts beyond the targeted tier. Not sentiment; operational contamination: overreach produces intelligence noise and political liability.
Severity: strong.

**Personal considerations mixing with operational ones**
He has a history with this failure mode. The Green faction is, in part, his institutional home because the personal considerations were operationally catastrophic. He has constructed a professional identity specifically to prevent recurrence.
Severity: blocker, but not in Taylor's story — it is a background fact about who he is, not an on-page pressure source.

**The Watch operating without appropriate coordination**
The Watch's jurisdictional claims overlap with Kingsguard operational zones in the city. He manages this through formal channels and resents the need to manage it repeatedly.
Severity: soft.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Faction violence made concrete and procedural. He is the agency by which the Green faction's intelligence inputs produce city-level effects — courier detentions, smallfolk-movement restrictions, operational clearances. Taylor reads these effects through the insect-feed and each reading advances political_register-prot from diffuse resentment toward named contempt. He does not need to be on-page with Taylor to deliver this; he delivers it as operational aftermath that the feed returns.

He is also the specific observable instance of what "institutional violence made into logistics" means. Not soldiers. Not monsters. A lord commander exercising a warrant with the competence the position requires. This is the thing Taylor despises, accurately named at d09: not cruelty but procedure, and procedure is harder to name as wrong than cruelty.

A man of middle years in Kingsguard white, maintained with the care of someone who understands that the cloak is also operational equipment. Dornish Marches coloring — darker than the Westerosi north, the weathered look of someone who has been outside for years. He does not look soft. He does not look brutal. He looks exactly like what he is: a professional who has been doing this for a long time.
