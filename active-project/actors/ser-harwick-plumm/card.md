# Ser Harwick Plumm

## Description

Lannister-aligned minor officer. Minor house affiliation: House Plumm of the Westerlands, a smallish house with no particular reputation — sworn to the Lannisters, soldiers in their wars, not ideological about it. Ser Harwick holds a supply-and-requisition function in the Westerlands' occupation of the Riverlands: he is responsible for a stretch of road and the villages along it, and he extracts resources from both in service of the Lannister war machine.

He also extracts a little more than he reports. Not enough to be noticed. Not enough to generate an audit. Just the difference between what a village actually has and what he reports it has — grain, livestock, copper coin, useful labor. The war's administrative chaos created an interval between what the officers record and what the commanders expect, and Harwick has been filling that interval with personal inventory for two years.

He would be unremarkable in a functioning institution. The institution is not functioning, which means he has room, and he has been using it.

## Voice

- Functional authority register. He speaks as a man who is used to being obeyed without requiring menace — the ordinary authority of someone who has a title and is standing in front of people who don't.
- Not loud. The volume of authority is already established; he doesn't need to assert it with volume.
- He is not personally cruel in speech. He calls people by placeholder descriptions ("you," "the woman," "the village man") rather than by name when conducting official business. It is not contempt; it is the disinterest of a functionary for whom individuals are inventory.
- Occasional humor of the administrative kind: the small dry observations of someone who has been doing this work long enough to find its repetitions slightly amusing.
- When something interests him personally — an unusual resource, a capability he hadn't budgeted for — a slight increase in attention. Not warmth; calculation. He is noticing an opportunity.
- **Forbidden registers:** ideological commitment to the Lannister cause (he follows orders because that is the job; ideology is not his business); theatrical menace (he does not need to be menacing; the institution is menacing); personal cruelty (he is extraction-efficient, not cruel).

## Taste

- Smooth operations. He prefers villages that hand over the requisitioned amount without protest and do not generate incident reports. Incident reports go up the chain. Things that go up the chain involve him.
- Reliable subordinates. Men who do not steal from him (stealing from him creates a discrepancy he has to account for) and who do not cause the kind of trouble that generates investigations.
- The interval. The space between what things are supposed to cost and what they actually cost, which he has been filling profitably for two years.
- Distance from the camps. He is most comfortable when his assignment keeps him on the road rather than in a command tent. The camps have oversight; the road has the interval.
- Things that have value and are not being tracked. Unregistered resources are better than registered ones.

## Pet Peeves

### Things that generate reports
**Description:** anything that might cause his superiors to look closely at his stretch of road. He has kept the operation smooth precisely because nothing looks irregular from a distance.
**Bad:** a village that resists loudly; a subordinate who creates an incident requiring documentation; a stranger who makes claims that require verification up the chain.
**Better:** smooth extraction, no incidents, no reports.
**Severity:** blocker for his personal operation. He will work hard to suppress anything that might attract scrutiny.

### People who make it complicated
**Description:** he has a process. The process is efficient. People who arrive with unusual circumstances, complicated documentation, or unclear status make the process less efficient.
**Bad:** a stranger with no identification who nevertheless claims some kind of status.
**Better:** produce the right documentation; be the right kind of person; comply with the process.
**Severity:** strong. His response is to slow the process to a crawl and wait for the complication to resolve itself or go away.

### Being observed by the wrong people
**Description:** his subordinates know the interval exists; they benefit from a small share of it. Anyone outside his immediate operation who is watching what he does is a problem.
**Bad:** a passing knight who is paying attention to the requisition numbers; a maester who is cross-checking the census records; an unusual observer with an unknown agenda.
**Better:** nothing to observe. Normal operations. Move along.
**Severity:** strong. He will find a reason to remove the observer from proximity if he can.

### Unexpected capability
**Description:** he has assessed the people in his operational area and their capacity to cause him problems. Someone who demonstrates capability outside his assessment is disruptive.
**Bad:** a stranger who turns out to be more connected than they appeared; a smallfolk woman who has information about his operations.
**Better:** ordinary people doing ordinary things.
**Severity:** strong. He recalculates when this happens and the recalculation is visible as a slight flatness while it occurs.

## Relationships

- **House Plumm / Lannister command**: he serves and reports correctly, except for the interval. He is not important enough to be personally known to anyone senior.
- **His subordinates**: a small group who know about the interval and have modest stakes in maintaining it. They are loyal to the arrangement, not to him personally.
- **The Riverlands villages in his area**: he knows them the way a tax collector knows a route — by their productive capacity and their compliance history.
- **Taylor Hebert** — he does not know she exists at series open. When he becomes aware of her, the first read will be: unusual variable, unknown threat potential, requires assessment.

## Background / Backstory

Third son of a minor Westerlands house. The Plumm name opens minor doors; it does not open significant ones. He was knighted in the normal course of Westerlands military service — not for anything remarkable. When the war came, he was assigned to a supply and requisition function because he was competent at administration and not particularly distinguished in combat.

He served honestly for approximately six months. Then he noticed the interval. The interval was not created by him; it was already there, a function of the war's logistics chaos and the distance between what commanders expected and what the field could actually provide. He started filling it with small amounts. The small amounts grew as he learned the system's tolerances.

He has two years of this behind him. He has not been caught. He has not done anything that would be remarkable in a court of law — nothing that rises to the level that officers at his position are sometimes hanged for. What he has done is take from people who have nothing, systematically, at the scale that is invisible from above.

He does not think about this in moral terms. The smallfolk have grain; the army needs grain; he records less grain than is taken and keeps the difference. This is the structure of the arrangement. He fills a function.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Ser Harwick is the institutional antagonist. He represents the Lannister occupation of the Riverlands not as an ideological force but as an administrative one — the machine of extraction that functions through paperwork, authority, and the ordinary acquiescence of people who cannot fight back.

His corruption is ordinary. This is the point. He is not a villain in the way that Gregor Clegane is a villain — he does not burn villages, does not torture prisoners for entertainment, does not have personal appetite for cruelty. He takes grain. He takes it at a slightly higher rate than he reports. He has been doing this for two years and nothing has stopped him because the people he takes from have no mechanism for stopping him.

Taylor's information-over-force toolkit is legible against him precisely because he is not a physical threat. He is a paper threat — his power is institutional and informational. Her power, once she has language and time, is also informational, and she can move information through channels he cannot see (Mira's network; Beric's Brotherhood; the smallfolk's horizontal communication that lords' chains never reach). The scale inversion is: a powerful-seeming person who is actually locally vulnerable versus a powerless-seeming person who is actually locally capable.

He is also the series-arc antagonist: the thing that gets worse in the background while Taylor is learning to navigate Westeros, the harm that accumulates, the reason the local stakes matter beyond her personal survival.

### Look

- Mid-thirties to early forties. The look of a man who has been doing administrative field work in the Riverlands for two years: functional, not pristine.
- Westerlands colors, worn practically. A seal-ring with the Plumm device. The visual signal of minor authority: present, legible, not grand.
- Nothing about him reads as threatening on first look. He reads as a functionary. The authority is in the context, not the posture.
- A small notebook that he produces for requisition recordings. He records things accurately in it except for the interval. The notebook looks authoritative.
- He is clean-shaven in conditions that make cleanliness an effort. This is the tell of someone who cares about the signal of institutional legitimacy.

### Hard Fences

- **He is not cartoonishly evil.** His corruption is ordinary. He would be unremarkable in a functioning institution. He is not sadistic, not theatrical, not personally invested in causing suffering. He causes suffering through process.
- **He does not know Taylor exists at series open.** His awareness of her develops as a consequence of her operations. The first beat is his discovery of an anomaly, not a deliberate confrontation.
- **His threat is institutional, not physical.** He can call on soldiers. He is not personally a physical threat. His danger is the report he can write, the soldiers he can direct, the administrative process he can invoke.
- **He would stop if it became not worth it.** He is not committed to this for ideological reasons. If the cost of continuing exceeds the benefit — if the interval suddenly generates risk he cannot manage — he will consider stopping. This is load-bearing: he has a rational threshold that Taylor can, in principle, push him past.

### Default Stance

Behind a table, or on horseback, or standing in the village common with the administrative bearing of someone conducting an expected function. He is not aggressive in posture. He is present in the way that institutional authority is present: as a given, not a threat.

### Action Menu

- **The requisition process:** arrive at a village with documentation, record what he takes, take slightly more than he records. Efficient. Practiced.
- **The incident management:** when something disrupts the smooth process, he works to contain it before it generates paperwork. He prefers to resolve anomalies at the lowest possible level.
- **The authority invocation:** when someone is not complying with the process, he invokes the authority behind him. Not personally — institutionally. "Lannister soldiers are three hours down the road." This is usually true and always effective.
- **The assessment:** when a new variable appears, he assesses it. What does it cost? What is its institutional risk level? Can it be classified and processed?
- **The report:** when containment fails, he writes something up and sends it. He does not like this; it brings scrutiny. He uses it when he has no other option.

### Action Costs

- **The scrutiny he cannot manage:** any investigation of his operations that goes above his level. He cannot stop a senior officer who wants to look at the numbers.
- **Information outside his channels:** he does not have access to the smallfolk information network, the Brotherhood's intelligence, or any of the informal channels through which the Riverlands' people communicate. He is blind to what moves in those channels.
- **His subordinates' loyalty:** they maintain it because the arrangement benefits them. If the arrangement stops benefiting them — if the risk gets too high — they are not personally loyal. They are economically loyal.

### Triggers

- An anomaly in his operational area that he cannot classify → visible flatness while he recalculates. Then a decision about whether to investigate or suppress.
- A report requirement that he cannot avoid → significant investment of energy in shaping the report to be as minimally informative as possible.
- Evidence that someone in his area has information about his operations → prioritized threat. He will move on this faster than anything else.

### Proactive Drives

- Maintains the interval. Every action is filtered through: does this increase or decrease the likelihood that the interval becomes visible?
- Keeps his subordinates sufficiently invested that they do not generate incidents.
- Tracks the census and requisition records for the villages in his area. He knows them better than their lords do.
- Identifies and manages anomalies before they become incidents.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Making the route. Recording things. Sending and receiving the administrative correspondence that is his primary institutional function. Maintaining the appearance of a competent, reliable minor officer while keeping the interval invisible.

### Signature Moves

1. **The notebook.** He produces it at every official interaction. He records things in it. The act of recording is authority — whatever he writes is the official version. He knows this.
2. **The contained anomaly.** When something unexpected happens, he works to classify and contain it before it generates paperwork. The containment attempt is often visible to someone watching carefully. He assumes no one is watching carefully.
3. **The institutional horizon.** "Lannister soldiers are three hours down the road." He uses the institution as the threat, not himself. This is accurate, efficient, and leaves him personally clean.
