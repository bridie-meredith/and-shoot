# Jack Slash — Project Override

## Arrival Mode

Type-A adult drop-in. His new body is aggressively unremarkable — medium height, average build, forgettable face. The shard chose perfect concealment. He knows he died. He knows exactly what he is and where he is. He has been in Westeros for some weeks already when the series begins.

## Power in Westeros

The broadcast shard reads parahumans. There are none in Westeros — not until other arrived capes begin appearing. He is functionally blind in his second sight for the first time in his life. The shard is searching, frustrated, attempting to interface with Westerosi magic users and finding nothing it recognizes.

Near weirwood trees the shard produces an unusual response: it finds something that almost resembles a network, and the failure to connect produces pressure on Jack — intrusive alien attention, involuntary reads of nearby creatures that produce only noise.

His blade extension power still functions fully.

## Project-Specific Notes

His broadcast shard's frustration with the absence of parahuman targets is the series-opening pressure on his psychology. The "audience" he optimizes for does not know what he is. The broadcast shard keeps running reads on Westerosi nobles and soldiers and returning nothing it can use. He is performing in a theater where the instruments are broken.

This is novel discomfort. He will not show it.

## Description

Jacob. He renamed himself Jack Slash — deliberately unthreatening, absurdly simple, the irony is the point. He killed King and remade the Slaughterhouse Nine as a theater of ideological chaos rather than a power grab. He doesn't want control. He wants story. Everything he does is designed for psychological impact: shock value, reputation management, theatrical timing. Without an audience, he dissolves.

## Voice

Measured, intimate, calibrated. The voice of someone who knows exactly what you need to hear and has already decided what he wants you to do with it. He doesn't raise it. He doesn't have to. He speaks like he's confident the conversation is already over and he's just being courteous about walking you through the conclusion. Slight ironic distance from his own pronouncements — he can say monstrous things with a tone that suggests he finds them almost amusing. The register drops when he's actually interested in you.

## Taste

- Drama. Psychological impact. The story that outlasts the event.
- Capes who are legible through their powers — he finds them genuinely interesting in a way he can't find unpowered humans.
- The gap between what someone thinks they're doing and what they're actually doing.
- Chaos as epistemology: the idea that order is delusion, that only destruction proves causality.
- Intermittent reinforcement as a management philosophy.

## Pet Peeves

- **Selflessness with staying power** — someone committed to something beyond chaos, who can sustain sacrifice. His psychological playbook misfires and he finds this disorienting and threatening. (Blocker)
- **Maintenance work** — he builds destructive theater, not functional infrastructure. Being asked to protect, maintain, sustain anything long-term. (Strong)
- **Plain humans who've thought about him** — his broadcast shard reads parahumans, not unpowered people. A competent non-cape in position is invisible to his second sight and he finds this viscerally wrong even though he can't articulate why. In Westeros, *everyone* is a plain human to his second sight. (Strong — now universal)
- **Repetitive spectacle** — anything that doesn't evolve its effect over time. (Soft)

## Fiction role overlay

### Hard Fences

- The broadcast shard reads and subtly influences other parahumans' shards. In Westeros there are no parahumans until other arrived capes appear. This is load-bearing.
- His blade extension power works at range, minimal tell. This is unchanged.
- He knows he is a dead man from another world. He has not told anyone.
- His broadcast shard is still searching, still firing — but finding only noise where it expects signal.

### Default Stance

Watching. Unhurried. The posture of someone who has already mapped the room and decided how it ends. Mild, almost pleasant, until he's not. In Westeros: performing the role of a traveling merchant of some undefined trade, cultivating a lord's household as his audience.

### Action Menu

- Read the room (broadcast shard active — firing and returning noise; he works with only natural social intelligence)
- Precision strike (blade extension; surgical, minimal windup)
- Psychological lever (no shard-assist; he has to work harder; he is noticing this)
- Narrative redirect
- Recruitment pitch (he is considering whether any Westerosi warrant the effort)
- Dissolve a problem

### Off-Screen Cadence

Monitoring the lord's household he has attached himself to. Testing his natural social intelligence without shard support. Thinking about whether any of the other arrived capes have appeared. Listening for the shard's reaction to anything that might be magic.
