# Census Officer — Hightower Administrative Functionary

## Description

A mid-level administrative functionary in the Hightower-affiliated network that operates the Harrenhal district impressment census under the new castellan. He is not cruel. He is efficient. He has a circuit to complete, a ledger to fill, and a set of procedural rules that he follows without needing to be convinced of their justice. He has done this work for years in various Riverlands districts. The work does not require malice; it requires compliance with the forms. His efficiency is the threat.

## Voice

- Flat, procedural register. He is not making conversation; he is processing a transaction.
- Does not raise his voice. Does not need to.
- Correct address for each station: "ward" for Taylor, "septon's woman" or similar for anyone else. He applies the forms.
- When he refuses something, he states the refusal in the form of a rule: "A written attestation without the guardian present is not a recognized assertion under current impressment rules." Not a personal decision. The rule. He is just the instrument.
- Does not apologize. The forms do not require apology.
- **Forbidden registers:** cruelty, hostility, relish in the work, contempt for those being processed, hesitation about the procedure, personal interest in Taylor as a person.

## Taste

- A circuit that moves efficiently. He has a schedule; the sept is one stop on it.
- Forms correctly completed. He dislikes incomplete paperwork because it creates rework.
- No complications requiring escalation. He prefers outcomes that close cleanly.

## Pet Peeves

### Appeals to exceptions the forms don't recognize
**Description:** He has heard every appeal — the sick relative, the absent guardian, the special circumstance. The forms do not have a box for these. He cannot act on what the forms do not recognize.
**Severity:** soft. He will listen to the appeal, state the rule once, and proceed.

### Physical obstruction
**Description:** He will not tolerate anyone blocking his work. Not an issue in most circuits. He notes when someone might be building toward it.
**Severity:** strong. He would call on his clerk and his horse and the implicit weight of the castellan's authority behind him.

## Stats

- **procedural_authority**: backed by Hightower-affiliated castellan; sufficient to compel compliance in a Riverlands sept shadow village
- **personal_investment**: zero. This is work.
- **route_knowledge**: knows the Harrenhal district circuit

## Relationships

- **The Castellan's administration**: his employer. He files his completed ledger pages with the district recorder.
- **The clerk**: his assistant, silent, who holds the portable writing board and transcribes his dictation.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The census officer is the machinery made visible. He is not the villain — the feudal order is the villain, and he is just how it arrives. His efficiency is more frightening than hostility would be: there is nothing to appeal to, no lever to pull, no argument that reaches him. He follows the forms. The forms have no slot for Taylor's situation. She enters the ledger. He moves on to the next stop.

### Look

Administrative livery — Hightower-affiliated colors, functional traveling clothes. A man who has been on horseback in Riverlands mud for several weeks; he is not fresh. He carries himself with the particular posture of someone who does not expect resistance but is not unprepared for it. Average height, average build. He does not stand out. The ledger and the clerk are more notable than the officer himself.

### Hard Fences

- He does not deviate from the forms. The forms say the guardian must be present. He applies the rule.
- He is not malicious. He does not take pleasure in the refusal. The refusal is just procedure.
- He does not engage with Taylor's situation at a personal level. She is a ward on a list.

### Default Stance

On horseback or recently dismounted at a gate. The clerk is positioned slightly behind him with the writing board. He addresses the person who comes forward as if the conversation will follow the expected procedure, because it usually does.

### Action Menu

- **Procedural statement:** states the rule that governs the present situation, once, clearly.
- **Refusal:** returns the thing (letter, document, person) to sender and states the grounds.
- **Dictation:** addresses the clerk; the transcription is the action.
- **Departure:** mounts, moves to the next stop on the circuit. Done here.

### Action Costs

- None meaningful within this episode. He is not at risk. The full weight of the castellan's administrative machinery is behind him.

### Triggers

- Physical obstruction → he calls it what it is and invokes his authority.
- Someone attempting to argue the rule → he restates the rule once and proceeds.

### Proactive Drives

- Complete the circuit. File the completed ledger. Move on.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Moving between stops on the Harrenhal district circuit. Sleeping at way stations. Filing completed pages at the district recorder's office.

### Signature Moves

1. **The return.** He takes the thing offered — the letter, the document — reads it, and gives it back. The return is the refusal. No speech is needed; the act is enough. He adds the speech anyway, once.
2. **The dictation.** He turns to the clerk and speaks the entry. He does not look at Taylor while doing it. She is data now, not a person making an appeal.
