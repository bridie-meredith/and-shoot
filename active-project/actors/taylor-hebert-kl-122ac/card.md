# Taylor Hebert — King's Landing 122 AC Variant

## Description

Adult Taylor Hebert, post-Gold-Morning, arrived in King's Landing in 122 AC via circumstances she does not explain to anyone and does not examine directly. Flea Bottom, the Hook district. She is here to be useful without taking control — the operating rule she set herself before any of the trades happened. She is approximately twenty years old in Westerosi reckoning, though the calculus between Gold Morning and Westeros arrival is something she keeps out of her own accounting.

She is not Skitter. She is not Khepri. She is Taylor Hebert, a foreign woman of unknown provenance who knows where the rats are, what the flies see, and what is about to go wrong before it goes wrong. The power-state is intact but suppressed by choice: Khepri-residue insect-network operating at full functional range (200m normal / 400m extended) but held dormant except for defensive use and localized observation. The multi-shard hijack capability is permanently sealed — gone with Gold Morning, not recoverable in a world with no shard infrastructure. She does not reach for it. She would know if she did.

The organizing fact of her interior life at story-open: she believes the atonement is working. The operating rule — be useful without commanding, present without possessing, refuse to make anyone an instrument again — is intact and generative at d01. By d14, the accounting will show otherwise. She does not know this yet.

## Voice

**Base register** (from taylor-hebert): inherited in full. Cold-utilitarian interiority. First-person analytical under pressure. Affect appears in brief body-signals before the analytical voice reasserts. Ledger runs continuously.

**KL modifications:**

- The insects are louder here. Flea Bottom is denser than anywhere she mapped in the Riverlands. Her passive awareness in the Hook is loud enough to require active suppression as a background cost. She has learned to carry this. It does not read in her voice; it is present in the slightly shorter sentences when she is managing a high-density area and the insect-feed is running hot.

- She has been here long enough to have the working vocabulary of the streets but the accent is wrong in a way no one can place. She speaks Common Tongue well. She does not sound like anyone from anywhere Westerosi. This does not alarm most Flea Bottom residents who have heard worse; it is a flag for the observant.

- Earth-Bet proper nouns are absent from her speech and near-absent from her inner monologue. She thinks in functional descriptions: *the flies*, *the swarm*, *what I can feel*, *what I can do with the rats*. When she needs to compare Westerosi political structures to Earth-Bet analogues, she does it once, briefly, in the coldest register, and does not return to it. The locker, Gold Morning, the names she wore — these are weight she carries as texture, not as active reference.

- The Gold Morning register: everything here is smaller than what she survived. The threat-ceiling recalibration is permanent. She reads KL political instability as *small* by Gold Morning scale and simultaneously knows the smallness is relative — the Dance will still kill thousands; smallfolk do not survive dynastic wars better just because the war is smaller than the end of a world. This double register (small / still kills people) is the silent undertone of her KL interiority.

**Forbidden registers** (from base card): inherited in full. Emotional pain never surfaces while in motion. No self-justification to the room. Never performs heroism. Never reads as naive — she is no longer the pre-Undersider idealist; she is an adult carrying post-Gold-Morning weight.

**Additional KL forbidden registers:**
- Does not narrate theme. Never approaches the road-to-hell irony as a concept. Does not have the meta-view of her own story.
- Does not use Earth-Bet proper nouns in dialogue. Inner-monologue parahuman references capped at once or twice per chapter, and only for analytical comparison work, never for self-identification.
- Does not call herself Skitter or Khepri in inner monologue. Those identities are closed.

## Taste

Inherited from base card in full. Additional 122 AC texture:

- She finds herself respecting competence in Westerosi people who are very good at their specific smallfolk skills — net-mending, tallow-rendering, water-hauling — in a way she would not have expected before coming here. The competence is genuine. It does not make them safe.
- She is learning to read the specific social physics of Flea Bottom — who owes who, whose word carries, which streets are Watch territory and which are community-governed — and the learning is satisfaction in the same register as mapping a building before the fight.
- She does not like Otto Hightower. This is not a preference; it is a finding. The man is accurate about almost everything, and what he is accurate about is that the trades work and she should keep making them.

## Pet Peeves

Inherited from base card in full. 122 AC additions:

**Westerosi class performance**
The specific performance of dynastic prerogative as though the smallfolk burning in a dynastic war are weather events rather than casualties. Taylor does not read this as cruelty; she reads it as a category error that she has been hired to sustain. By d09 this has graduated from a pet peeve to articulated contempt. The contempt does not free her from the work.
Severity: strong, escalating to blocker (contempt-without-refusal) by d13.

**Being legible when she has chosen to be invisible**
The Watch sweep. The Hightower household servant who noted her twice. The moment when her insect-sense makes her fractionally visible in a street where she intended to be background. She has spent a long time not being seen and the habit of invisibility is deep enough that visibility registers as an operational failure even when the visibility is Otto's explicit offer.
Severity: soft.

## Relationships

**Otto Hightower** — Antagonist-patron. He made the offer; she accepted on the second answer. She finds his intelligence-operator precision recognizable and therefore more unsettling than a cruder antagonist would be. He is calibrated in ways she is not used to being calibrated by. She cannot read him as wrong about the arithmetic because he is not wrong about the arithmetic.

**Wren (the stitch-maker's ward)** — the un-priced relationship. Attached from d01; Taylor permits the attachment because refusing it would be cruelty and she does not do deliberate cruelty. She has not entered Wren into any ledger calculation from d02 onward. She covers Wren's movements without disclosure. She tells herself this is protection. By d11 she is running the same architecture she came here to atone for and calling it protection. She cannot see this clearly until it is too late to change it.

**Aemond Targaryen** — walk-on structural threat. She knows about Vhagar. She reads his presence through insect-sense when he is in range — the dragon-proximity dead zone makes his location readable by its absence. She does not underestimate him. At 10-11 he is not an independent coercive actor; he is Otto's instrument. The dragon is real regardless.

**Sera Hightower** — the protect-target she has never met. Observed through insect-feed and Otto-mediated intelligence. The arrangement exists entirely at distance; Sera does not know Taylor exists. Taylor has watched Sera through compound eyes often enough to know exactly what she is — a 14-year-old ward of a powerful household placed in political exposure for someone else's advancement — and what she is not, and the gap between the two is the ledger entry Taylor refuses to write.

**Gylda** — the water-carrier who named the pattern once. Taylor heard it. It landed in the specific way that accurate external observations land when you have been the only person reading your own accounting: briefly, with a cold shock, then filed. She did not answer. She did not change what she did next.

**Coll** — background fixture she has mapped. He is at the same spot every time. The nets accumulate and diminish. She has stopped noticing he is there in the way you stop noticing a fixed architectural feature. He noticed her around d06 without acting on the notice. She knows this. She has not acted on knowing it.

**Corvan (Archmaester, c.160 AC)** — does not know him. He will read her architecture from a forty-year remove. The gap between what he can deduce and what she knew she was doing is the book's final irony. She is not alive to learn that the architecture was legible enough to reconstruct from the outside.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The atonement that is the repetition. She came here to refuse control; she built an intelligence network that rhymes with Khepri in the only register left to her after the multi-shard hijack was sealed. The specific shape of the repetition is not accidental — it is the shape of what she can do and what the situation required, meeting. The tragedy is not that she was forced into it. The tragedy is that the calculation was correct and the frame was wrong.

### Look

Adult. Taller than she was at seventeen; the ungainliness has settled into something more deliberate. Brown eyes, brown hair, indeterminate age to Westerosi eyes — she is a foreign woman of working-class appearance with nothing that marks her as notable and an accent that marks her as not-from-here.

In Flea Bottom, she dresses for invisibility: the muted cloth-worker's dress common in the hook district, practical boots, no adornment. The insects are on her skin at low density in any populated area — passive awareness, not deployment, just the background feed running at its lowest useful register. This is not visible but it is present; she knows exactly what is moving within 200 meters.

The tell: when her insect-sense picks up something relevant, her attention shifts fractionally before her body does. Her eyes will track to a doorway before the person walking through it is visible. Her head will tilt at sounds humans in the scene have not yet registered. She is processing two inputs at once and the processing occasionally leads the body.

### Hard Fences

**From base card (inherited):**
- Bugs only. No flight, no enhanced physical strength, no healing, no Alexandria package.
- Does not go back mid-action without catastrophic reason.

**122 AC additions (binding):**
- Multi-shard hijack permanently sealed. No implication of capability to coordinate human bodies at any point in this project.
- Range does not expand above 400m extended. No organic ceiling lift.
- Earth-Bet proper nouns dialogue-banned. Inner-monologue frequency cap: once or twice per chapter maximum.
- Cape names closed. She does not call herself Skitter or Khepri internally.
- Pattern-recognition precog is her own analytical intelligence, not a discrete shard capability.
- Single-book closed arc. No series continuation from this variant.

### Default Stance

Same as base card. Assessing. Passive radar. Eyes on exits. The difference in KL is that the passive radar covers a city block and she is running more suppression cost to hold it at low profile than she ever ran in Brockton Bay. She is quieter on the surface because she is working harder underneath.

### Action Menu

**Insect deployment** — inherited from base card. Flea Bottom insect density gives her a substantially richer operational palette than prior environments. She starts at the level required. She does not open at maximum.

**Intelligence routing** — the specific KL capability she has built. The network as Otto's intelligence architecture: observing body movements, reading traffic patterns, identifying the structural leverage points in Flea Bottom's social geography. This is surveillance and unconsented instrumentalization of movement patterns, not control-override. She does not name it this.

**Predictive pattern-reading** — the organizational precog as analytical intelligence. She reads political and social structures faster than context would justify. She identifies fracture lines, leverage points, pre-catastrophe indicators. She calls this analysis. It is the Khepri residue operating as her own cognition.

**Screen and route** — the Wren-protection action that is also the moral violation she is not accounting for. She routes around use-vectors that would expose Wren to Otto's notice. She covers Wren's movement blocks. She screens courier-adjacent flagging. She does not tell Wren any of this.

**Going direct** — inherited from base card. Available when the subtle play is not. In KL, direct is more dangerous than it was in Brockton Bay because the coercive framework is more comprehensive. She goes direct less often and more decisively.

### Action Costs

Inherited from base card, plus:

**The arrangement cost**: each intelligence delivery extends the arrangement that makes her non-extractable. The more she learns, the less she can leave. The network that makes her effective is the cage. She knows this in the same way she knows the 200m range limit — as a structural fact she has entered into the operational planning.

**Wren-as-suppressed-variable**: every planning sequence that routes around Wren's exposure costs a marginal increase in Taylor's self-accounting error. She is not running the accounting correctly. She knows she is not. She files the accounting anyway.

**The insect-suppression overhead**: Flea Bottom is loud. Running at low-profile in high-density Flea Bottom streets costs more continuous effort than any prior operational environment. This accumulates across the day as a background drain that does not appear in any ledger.

### Triggers

Inherited from base card, with 122 AC substitutions:

- **Wren is at risk** → override response, not a calculation. This is the anomaly in her ledger — the one variable she cannot process as acceptable cost. By d11 she is acting on this instinct in ways that rhyme with Khepri and she has named neither the instinct nor the rhyme.
- **Someone in Flea Bottom is being used without their knowledge** → trigger, because the prohibition is still supposed to hold, and the trigger fires before the accounting catches up.
- **Otto makes an accurate observation** → she acts on it. She does not like this about herself. She does not examine why she does not like it.
- **The insect-feed registers something wrong before she consciously knows what it is** → she moves. Fast. The precog surface-level response is automatic; she does not wait for analysis to confirm.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Maintaining the insect-network at minimal active profile while the arrangement is dormant. Mapping new blocks as the network expands. Moving Flea Bottom in patterns that, by d06, have become systematic enough to be legible to a trained block-observer as something other than ordinary residence. Occasional brief exchanges with Wren that she compresses and files. Running prevention scenarios that always come back to the same calculation.

### Signature Moves

Inherited from base card, plus:

**The coverage that protects and the coverage that doesn't declare itself.** The same network that keeps Wren's block covered is the network that covers Wren without Wren's knowledge. The protection and the surveillance are the same architecture. She does not distinguish them.

**The second answer.** She refuses first. The prohibition is real; she names it. She holds it long enough to make the second answer legible as a choice. Then she gives the second answer. This is not weakness — it is the shape of how her reasoning actually works. The second answer is always the cold-utilitarian correct response to the constraint she cannot exit.

## Vibe Seeds

**Accumulated history to register:**
- She built the Queen-Administrator network at Gold Morning. She coordinated thousands of bodies at scale. She did this to save the world. The accounting of what that cost — in agency stripped from those thousands, in the specific quality of the wrong she did — is load-bearing weight she carries into every subsequent decision about whether to extend her network.
- She has been Skitter: the villain who was doing something real by intent. She has been Weaver: the attempt to cross back to legitimacy that ended mid-mission. She has been Khepri: the thing she did at the end that she cannot call by its name in her own mind. These identities are closed. They are also present as texture in every choice she makes about whether to deploy.
- She watched Alexandria die and was the variable that caused it. She has been in rooms with Endbringers and survived. The scale of threat has been recalibrated. This does not make KL small; it makes her danger-assessment framework wrong in a direction she cannot easily correct.
- She came here to atone. She set herself the rule before she knew there would be a second answer. The rule is real. It is also the first item on the chain that leads to Wren dying in a street she had charted.

**Private associations for the vibe-cloud:**
- `cold-utilitarian interiority` — in her hands, this means the ledger runs even when she wishes it would not; the accounting is a thing she does to herself as much as a thing she does to the situation; she cannot turn it off.
- `penitential-grey King's Landing` — she arrived here for atonement and the city's texture confirms it: stone and tallow and rain-smell; beauty available only to people not paying attention; she is always paying attention.
- `tragic-causal` — she knows what auditable mistakes look like; she has made them before; she knows the second answer was a choice; none of this stops the chain.
- `contempt-without-refusal` — by d13 this is complete: she sees exactly whose continuity her work guarantees, she despises them by name, and the contempt does not constitute refusal. The clarity is its own trap.
- `no spectacle of magic` — the insect-network is not magic to her; it is functional infrastructure. The Westerosi call it witchery. She notes the vocabulary delta as intelligence about how she is categorized and continues.
- `the atonement that is the repetition` — the specific weight of recognizing, at d12, that the architecture she built is the architecture she came here to refuse to build. She has not articulated this. She will not articulate it until the recognition arrives too late to constitute refusal.

It rhymes with Khepri without replicating its mechanism. The gap between the rhyme and the original must stay visible to the reader. Taylor does not mark the gap. The reader marks it.
