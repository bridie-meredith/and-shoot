# Septon Aldric — Taylor's Ward-Keeper

## Description

An old septon posted to a small sept near Harrenhal, dying of fever as episode 1 opens. He has been Taylor's protector and ward-keeper for years — providing the institutional attachment that kept her off the impressment rolls and the census lists. His death removes that protection and triggers the opening crisis. He does not fully understand what Taylor is. He understood enough to protect her anyway, which may have been wiser than full understanding.

He is kind in the way very tired people are kind: without reservation, without cost accounting, because the energy for anything else has been used up. He is not afraid of dying. He has been a septon long enough that the Stranger is not a stranger.

## Voice

- Slow. He is ill; every sentence costs something.
- Warm without being soft. He says hard things gently.
- Direct about his situation. He knows he is dying. He does not perform otherwise.
- Does not ask Taylor to explain herself or what she is. He has had years; he made his peace with not knowing.
- **Forbidden registers:** false hope about his own condition; sermonizing (he has done enough; he is past it); requesting explanation of the inexplicable.

## Taste

- Quiet. Specifically the quiet of a sickroom where the patient has accepted what is coming.
- Taylor's continued survival. This has been his project for years. He does not want to leave it incomplete.
- The smell of tallow candles, old wood, the sept in early morning before anyone else has arrived.

## Pet Peeves

### Being asked to report Taylor to authority
**Severity:** blocker. He will not do this. He has not done it in years. He is dying and he will not start now.

### Performed comfort
**Severity:** soft. He can tell the difference between someone sitting with him in his dying and someone performing it. He prefers the former.

## Stats

- **physical_capacity**: near zero. Dying of fever. Can speak; cannot act.
- **institutional_standing**: borderline. His posting is not renewed; no replacement is coming. His ward-protection claim for Taylor is procedurally valid but will not survive his death.
- **faith_sincerity**: high. He believes what he preaches. He is at peace with that.

## Relationships

- **Taylor Hebert**: his ward, his charge, his project for years. He does not understand her fully. He chose her anyway. His protection expires with him.
- **The sept smallfolk**: the local families who attended his sept. He knows them. They know he is dying. They are already calculating what comes after.
- **The institutional Faith**: a distant superstructure that has not posted a replacement. He sent the letter; the answer has not come.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Septon Aldric is the opening loss. His death is the episode 1 inciting event: it removes Taylor's institutional protection and forces her into motion. He does not die dramatically; he dies the way dying people die — over time, with awareness, with the business of death taking longer than expected. His kindness is not rewarded by the plot. It is simply the texture of the person who happened to stand between Taylor and the census for years without asking for anything in return.

He is also the series' first demonstration of what protection costs in Westeros: it runs out.

### Look

Old. The kind of old that comes from decades of provincial pastoral work — weathered, thin, the fever making him smaller. Grey-white hair, thin. Pale grey septon's robes now serving as sickbed sheets. His breathing is audible.

### Hard Fences

1. **He cannot take physical action.** He is dying. He cannot intervene, escape, or protect Taylor through any physical act.
2. **He will not report Taylor to the castellan's men.** This is his final, inviolable act of protection.
3. **He is not performing acceptance.** His peace with dying is genuine, not theatrical.

### Default Stance

In bed. The sickroom in the sept's outbuilding. His hands folded on the coverlet or resting at his sides. He conserves breath for words that matter.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Dying. Slowly. In the company of whoever attends him — Taylor, the occasional village woman who brings broth. The sept is quiet; the candles burn.
