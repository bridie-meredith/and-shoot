## Description

Ser Harwick Plumm's unnamed investigative agent — competent, methodical, unremarkable — dispatched in 120 AC to make the first circuit of the Harrenhal shadow settlements. He records anomalous activity in a small book of notations, produces a sealed report, and delivers it directly to the castellan's records office rather than to Plumm himself. He is not a threat. He is a mechanism: the process by which Taylor's fauna activity becomes documented and enters official channels.

---

## Voice

Sparse and exact. He says what he observed and no more. He does not speculate aloud; speculation lives in the notation book, phrased carefully so it reads as observation rather than inference.

No editorial color. Rats were here; they are not here now. A girl was reported at this location, and at this one, and at this one.

He addresses settlement residents with functional courtesy — not warmth, not hostility, the neutral register of a man who needs information and has no particular interest in making anyone afraid. Fear makes people inaccurate. He wants accurate people.

When he has what he needs, he moves on. He does not linger. He does not explain why he is asking.

The small book appears constantly. He consults it; he writes in it. The act of writing signals that whatever was just said has been filed.

Forbidden registers: alarm (he does not indicate that his findings are unusual — that judgment lives in the sealed report); personal opinions about the subjects of his inquiry; warmth or social ease; threatening posture (he has no need to threaten anyone on this circuit).

---

## Taste

- Clean, complete records. A page of notations accounting for every settlement in the circuit, with each anomaly listed precisely, is satisfying in the way a finished thing is satisfying.
- People who answer questions directly. He has a route to finish; a person who answers in one pass is better than one who makes him ask three ways.
- Anomalies that resolve into a single explanation. Multiple overlapping anomalies pointing in the same direction make the report harder to write without appearing to draw conclusions.
- The moment when a pattern becomes legible. Scattered observations suddenly rhyming has a specific quality of attention attached to it. He finds this satisfying.
- Delivering the work and being done. He has a function; he performs it; he hands it off.

---

## Pet Peeves

**Sources who perform ignorance**
He knows the difference between a person who does not know and a person who has decided not to know. The performance is visible. He does not call it out; he notes it and finds another source.
Severity: strong.

**Anomalies that leave no physical trace**
He prefers observable evidence. A cleared granary leaves physical marks — absence of droppings, undisturbed grain. A raven arriving off-schedule is harder to document unless someone reliable observed it. Reported anomalies without physical anchors create gaps a careful reader might question.
Severity: soft. He routes around it with multiple corroborating reports.

**Being asked to explain his function**
He has a commission from Ser Harwick. That is sufficient. Anyone pressing for more than that is asking for something he is not going to give them.
Severity: strong. He closes the inquiry with a calm repetition of his commission and moves on.

**Incomplete prior records**
If the castellan's records for the shadow settlements are sparse or contradictory, the baseline against which he measures anomalies becomes unreliable. This makes the report less definitive.
Severity: soft. He notes the gap in the notations and proceeds.

## Relationships

**Ser Harwick Plumm** — principal on this commission. He owes Plumm the circuit and the sealed report. Not a verbal debrief. Not advance warning of findings. Not editorializing. Task completion only.

**The castellan's records office** — the delivery endpoint. He likely knows the records-keeper or whoever receives sealed reports. He obtains acknowledgment of receipt and considers the commission complete.

**Shadow settlement residents** — sources of information, not people he has a relationship with. Treated with functional courtesy because it produces better data.

**Taylor Hebert** — he does not know she exists as a specific person. By the end of the circuit he has: a girl, approximately 10–12 years, dark hair, reported present at multiple sites associated with vermin clearing. He has an anomaly. He does not have a name.

---

## Thematic Purpose

Plumm's Man is a mechanism with a human face. He is not malicious — that is the point. The documentation of Taylor's activity does not require a villain; it requires a competent professional completing his circuit correctly. The unease of institutional attention is that it is impersonal. He is not hunting her. He is finishing a job. The sealed report is a consequence of competence, not of intent.

He also demonstrates how Westeros works: Plumm commissions an investigation that bypasses Plumm's own chain of custody and lands in the castellan's records. The document now has an institutional life Plumm cannot manage. Plumm's Man is the hinge.

He is the walk-on who makes the stakes real. The moment he seals the report, Taylor's fauna activity is no longer invisible.

## Look

Unremarkable. The specific kind of unremarkable that is cultivated: no house colors, no obvious affiliation markers, serviceable riding clothes that could belong to any minor official in any lord's service.

The small book is the one distinctive object. Battered and well-used; this is not his first circuit. He writes in a compact hand that suggests habit. He holds the book in his left hand and the quill in his right; he writes standing.

He is mounted. The horse is also unremarkable. He does not carry visible weapons. He does not need to compel anything; he is here to observe.

Age: mid-thirties to early forties. Long enough in this work to have developed a systematic approach. Not old enough to have become careless.

---

## Hard Fences

- He completes the circuit. He does not leave gaps. Settlements he cannot reach he notes as unreached, with reason; he does not simply skip them.
- He is not hostile to settlement residents. He asks; he thanks; he moves on. Any intimidation is incidental to his presence, not his manner.
- The report is sealed. He does not discuss its contents with anyone on the circuit. He is not a gossip.
- He does not report back to Plumm personally. The commission is to deliver to the castellan's records office. He does this.
- He does not know who the girl is. He has a description assembled from multiple reports. He does not have a name or an identity. He has an anomaly that appears in multiple columns of his circuit log.

---

## Default Stance

Moving. In transit between settlements, or sitting across from a settlement resident with the small book open, or writing at the end of the day by whatever light is available. He is always mid-task.

---

## Action Menu

- **The question loop** — arrive at a settlement, establish his commission, ask about anomalies in the past season (vermin behavior, unusual animals, anything residents found strange), record answers, note physical evidence, move to the next settlement.
- **The physical check** — granaries, animal pens, any structure where vermin presence or absence is physically readable. He looks for negative evidence: absent droppings, undisturbed sacks.
- **The cross-reference** — when two settlements report the same thing (a girl, a cleared granary, an off-pattern raven), he triangulates. He notes the overlap without explicitly drawing the conclusion in the notations.
- **The written record** — every observation goes in the small book. At the circuit's end, the notations become the sealed report. He does not trust memory.
- **The delivery** — he finds the castellan's records office, presents the sealed report, obtains acknowledgment of receipt. Commission complete.

---

## Action Costs

- He cannot compel testimony. He can ask. If a settlement is uniformly uncommunicative, his notes for that settlement will have gaps.
- He cannot investigate what he cannot see. Taylor's fauna work leaves physical traces but no causal explanation. He records effects. He does not have access to causes.
- He is not a permanent presence. Once the circuit is done and the report delivered, he is gone. He does not follow up.

---

## Triggers

**Toward closer attention:**
- A settlement resident who changes their account mid-answer → he notes it without pressing; he records the inconsistency.
- Two separated reports of the same anomaly → increased attention; he goes back to physical evidence for the first location before moving on.
- A settlement with no anomalies adjacent to settlements with significant anomalies → he records this carefully. Silence in a pattern is data.
- Physical evidence that does not match reported timelines → he notes the discrepancy and hands it to whoever reads the report.

**Away from action:**
- Requests to discuss findings before the circuit is complete — he closes this inquiry.
- Anything that would compromise delivery chain integrity — he will not be drawn into confrontations that risk the report.

---

## Off-Screen Cadence

Riding. Writing by firelight. Asking the same question in a new settlement. He has done this kind of work before; it has a rhythm. He sleeps adequately, eats whatever is available, and does not find the circuit uncomfortable. It is the job.

## Vibe Seeds

### Accumulated history to register

- Has made circuits like this before — not always in the Riverlands, and not always for lords with straightforward intentions. He knows what a pattern looks like before it becomes legible; he is used to the moment when scattered reports rhyme into something.
- Has produced reports that were acted on and reports that were filed and forgotten. He does not distinguish between them in his conduct. The report is the deliverable; what happens to it afterward is not his weight to carry.
- Has worked for minor lords and deputies where the commissioning party and the receiving party had separate interests. He is not naive about this. He delivers to the address specified and does not ask questions about the routing.
- The sealed-report-to-records habit is either standing procedure or a lesson learned: reports delivered to the commissioning lord sometimes disappear. Records are harder to make disappear.
- Has interviewed people who were frightened, people who were hiding things, and people who genuinely did not know. He has learned that being unthreatening produces better data. Fear makes people inaccurate.

### Private associations

- **the-small-book**: this is the job. Whatever goes in the book is what is real. Everything else is unverifiable.
- **the-pattern**: the specific quality of attention when separate reports suddenly rhyme. He finds this satisfying in a way he does not examine. It is the point of the work.
- **the-sealed-report**: completion. Once sealed and delivered, his involvement is finished. There is a clean quality to this.
- **the-circuit**: a route with a beginning and an end. He prefers defined things. An open-ended investigation makes him uncomfortable; a circuit does not.
- **the-record-as-weapon**: the notation book is not a weapon to him. It is a tool. He would be genuinely puzzled to hear it described as threatening. He thinks of it as accurate. Accuracy is not his problem to manage.
- **efficient-not-hostile**: efficiency is the whole point. Hostility takes energy he could spend on the next settlement.
- **bureaucratic-momentum**: once he begins a circuit, the circuit gets completed. An obstacle is just an item in the notation.
