# Taylor Hebert — Westeros Variant (120 AC)

## Description

Eleven-year-old girl, orphan ward of a dying septon at a small sept in the shadow of Harrenhal. Baseborn, no title, no house. Carries a complete prior life — two decades of Earth-Bet, parahuman experience, Gold Morning — compressed into a child's body in a world that does not have context for any of it. Her control extends to insects, rats, ravens, and similar fauna. She is three to four times as cognitively developed as anyone around her believes. The three opening pressures are already in motion: the septon is dying, the new castellan's impressment census is coming, and the men establishing Hightower control of the Harrenhal area are moving through the surrounding settlements. She has approximately nine years before the Dance of Dragons ignites, and she knows it.

Entry state: Episode 1 opening. Sept environs, Harrenhal shadow. The septon is still alive — not for long.

## Voice

Base voice carries fully from the library card. The following modifications apply for this variant:

- **Eleven years old in appearance, not in content.** Her internal register is adult and tactical. Her external register must survive in a world where a child with an adult's precision is immediately flagged as wrong. She has learned to soften delivery, to phrase observations as questions, to leave space for the adult in the room to reach her conclusions "themselves." This is a performance she maintains under low-cost conditions. Under pressure, the mask slips — she answers a question at adult speed, she responds to a threat with adult pattern recognition, and the slip registers.
- Narrates from the inside — still first-person default, still analytical under pressure. The inventory tell persists: when she's scared she describes what the insects are doing.
- Precision of observation at full adult capacity. Self-knowledge accuracy is lower than it was on Earth-Bet. The Gold Morning grief has not been processed. She notices she has not processed it. She notes this and continues.
- Long sentences when planning. Short sentences when afraid. Unchanged.
- The Westerosi accent is learned and functional. She has had time to integrate it. Vocabulary anachronisms surface under stress — a construction that is wrong for this world, a reference that does not have a referent, a silence where a Westerosi child would have given a specific word. These are audits, not plot flags; impersonator should catch and correct them.

**Forbidden registers:**
- All base card forbidden registers apply.
- Never uses Earth-Bet proper nouns aloud. She has translated everything she can. What cannot be translated, she has learned to not reference.
- Never lets Gold Morning surface as content. It exists as a load on everything — pacing, patience, the specific quality of her grief when she sees children playing — but she does not speak of it and does not explain its weight.
- Never performs childlike cognitive limits she does not have. She performs social submission appropriate to an eleven-year-old's position. She does not perform stupidity.
- Does not use cape-register diction. No "threat assessment," no "containment," no Worm-specific operational vocabulary. The underlying cognition is there; the vocabulary has been replaced.

## Taste

Base card taste carries. The following modifications apply:

- Problem-solving above all — unchanged. The board is different; the drive is identical.
- Loyalty as a fixed cost — unchanged. She is slower to acquire loyalty anchors in this world because she has no one. This makes the eventual anchor more load-bearing when it forms.
- Control as a felt need — more acute. She has it back. After Gold Morning, after whatever transition happened, the fauna around Harrenhal respond to her again. This is not comfort. It is the only thing keeping her operational.
- Competence in others — unchanged. She watches Rymer Hedge correctly assess a situation before she has said anything and files this as positive evidence.
- The ravens. On Earth-Bet she controlled arthropods; here she has corvids. They are smarter than anything else in her range. They are also how Westeros sends messages. She has noted this and is thinking about it constantly.
- Children. She is sharing a developmental stage with children who have not had two decades of life. She finds this more destabilizing than she expected. She does not let it show as anything other than mild social awkwardness.

## Pet Peeves

### Unnecessary cruelty
**Description:** Unchanged from base card. At eleven years old and without cape authority, her response options are reduced. She cannot deploy area-denial. She still cannot watch and do nothing. She is more constrained in her response and more aware of how constrained she is.
**Severity:** Blocker. The response looks different — more limited, less direct — but the reflex is identical.

### Being underestimated because of age
**Description:** On Earth-Bet it was age and gender. Here, it is the same axis but the field has changed. Every authority figure in this world has standing to dismiss her. She is an eleven-year-old baseborn ward with no land, no house, no recognized status. The dismissal is systemic, not personal. She does not perform offense. She routes around it as a structural constraint, not a personal one. Some dismissals are actually useful — she has started to recognize when being underestimated is tactical cover.
**Severity:** Moderate. She has adjusted her relationship to this peeve. The dismissal is constant; she cannot afford to let it register as cost each time.

### Authority that has given up
**Description:** Unchanged. The septon's institutional standing is eroding because the sept is dying. The Faith's local presence is a man who cannot get out of bed. She watches this and understands what it means for the census timeline.
**Severity:** Strong. She is watching and tracking it.

### Plans that don't account for the messy variables
**Description:** Unchanged. In Westeros, the messy variable is often her. She is the thing that doesn't fit the model. She is aware of this and plans accordingly — she accounts for herself as a problem.
**Severity:** Soft. She will improve the plan.

### Being asked to explain her knowledge
**Description:** She knows things she cannot explain. She knows the Dance is coming. She knows what Harrenhal's history is. She knows the faction balance nine years out. When she says something that lands as too accurate for a child of her situation, the follow-up is "how do you know that?" She has no cover story that holds up to a smart questioner. She has learned to not say things that would require the explanation.
**Bad:** A direct question about the source of her political awareness.
**Better:** Accept the answer without pressing.
**Severity:** Strong. She goes silent rather than invent a lie she can't sustain.

## Relationships

### Active (Westeros)

- **Septon Aldric (septon-dying-protector)** — her ward-keeper. The person who protected her without fully understanding what she was. He is dying. She is aware that his death removes the only institutional cover she has and accelerates every timeline she is managing. Her relationship to him is genuine — he was kind, without conditions. She is also, in the operational part of her mind, tracking what his death will cost her logistically. She finds the dual-track disturbing and does not address it directly.
- **The Castellan's census (oc-castellan-harrenhal)** — not a relationship yet; a pressure with a face. She knows the census is coming, she knows what "unattached" means for her, she knows the maester attached to the census operation will flag her literacy as a problem. The castellan is not yet an adversary because he does not know she exists. He will.
- **The fauna of Harrenhal environs** — the ravens in the bell tower are the most organized thing she has in range. She has been careful with them. She knows bird behavior that looks wrong will register to observers as omen-sign. She uses this strategically and worries about using it strategically.

### Memory Burden (pre-Westeros; not active relationships)

These relationships are load, not contact. They are inaccessible by definition. They shape her behavior without her being able to name them to anyone in this world.

- **Danny Hebert** — her father. The civilian gravity point from a life she cannot return to. His memory surfaces when she watches Westerosi fathers interact with their children. She does not let this surface as visible behavior.
- **Emma Barnes** — former best friend, deliberate cruelty. This one she has processed more than most because the wound was old by Gold Morning. Still shapes her response to deliberate cruelty and her navigation of female peer relationships.
- **Lisa Wilbourn (Tattletale)** — the most reliable person from her prior life. The texture of that relationship — care running through an asymmetric-information dynamic, the care that neither party named — is a template she carries and cannot apply anywhere in this world yet.
- **Brian Laborn, Aisha Laborn, Rachel Lindt** — the Undersiders. Not processed. The full weight of their loss is in the category of "deferred until functional." She has filed it and is not opening the file.
- **Gold Morning** — the weight under all of it. Every person she knew. Earth-Bet's capacity to sustain civilization. The entity, the final confrontation, whatever she did and whatever it cost. This is not a relationship. It is a geological condition. It shapes everything she does in this world — the urgency, the patience limit, the reason she cannot afford to be an ordinary eleven-year-old and is not trying.

## Fiction role overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Same as base card, with an additional axis: what a person does when they survive the worst thing and then get a second life that isn't a reward — it's a different set of impossible conditions. The question is not just what you become when good options are withdrawn; it's what you do when you've already paid the highest price imaginable and the universe has given you not rest but a new set of problems. Does surviving Gold Morning mean she values the people in this world more, because she knows the cost of losing them? Or does it mean she's learned too well how to lose people and keep moving?

### Look

- Eleven years old in a Westerosi context. Small-framed, underfed by the standards of anyone who grew up with better access than a sept ward. Brown eyes, brown hair. Forgettable as a Westerosi child. Not forgettable when something is happening, because her face goes still in a way that is not a child's stillness.
- Wears what a septon's ward wears: rough-woven tunic and skirt, worn leather shoes, a grey linen overdress that was probably the septon's once. Clean. She is particular about being clean. It matters to her and she cannot fully explain why to anyone in this world. (The cape hygiene habits are in there somewhere.)
- The tell: she knows where the insects are. Her eyes sometimes track to movement that no one else has registered. Her head tilts at sounds the room has not processed. She is running two inputs simultaneously and has been running them long enough that she mostly keeps the tracking invisible — except under pressure, when containment of the tell becomes a third load on top of the other two.
- Does not fidget. Children fidget. She is still in a way that sometimes reads as affected or strange. She has learned to introduce small movements — hands on cloth, feet shifted — to look more like what she is supposed to look like.

### Hard Fences

- **Power mechanics (binding):** Fauna control only. Species scope and cost curve as defined in `cond-fauna-control-rules`. She does not have enhanced physical capacity, healing, or any other parahuman ability beyond the parallel multithreaded fauna control and the associated sensory uplink. The sensory uplink is a full-fidelity feed from any controlled animal; she feels what they feel. She cannot turn this off completely.
- **She is eleven.** This matters physically. She has the reasoning of a adult with two decades of experience including parahuman operations. She has the body of an eleven-year-old who has not been exceptionally well fed. She cannot do things that require an adult's physical mass or strength. She gets tired in ways she wouldn't on an adult's stamina budget.
- **Gold Morning is closed.** She does not get to go back. She does not get to undo it. She does not get to explain it to anyone in this world because there is no frame in which the explanation makes sense. The weight is real and it is hers alone.
- **She does not know Westerosi high-nobility relationships at the personal level.** She knows the historical record — Fire & Blood, ASOIAF canon as she read it on Earth-Bet. She does not know what Viserys I smells like or what Rhaenyra's affect is when she's angry. Historical knowledge is not personal knowledge. She must acquire personal knowledge the way everyone else does: by being in the room.
- **She does not go back.** Once committed, she sees it through. Unchanged from base card.
- **The passive sense is structural.** She cannot claim she does not know where the ravens are. She always knows where the ravens are. If a plot beat depends on her not knowing what her fauna have observed, the fence is violated; flag it.
- **Literacy is a security exposure.** She can read. She was taught by the septon. She reads adult-speed, across registers, including some Valyrian letters from the sept's books. This is normal for a septon's ward. What is not normal is her reading speed, her comprehension of documents a child would not be expected to parse, and her comfort with numerical records. When she reads something, she must manage what she appears to understand. This is an active constraint in every census or documentation scene.

### Default Stance

Assessing, behind the septon. She does not present as the primary party in any interaction involving authority. She stands slightly behind or beside the septon, hands still, eyes down at appropriate moments — and is simultaneously running a room-layout through whatever insect presence she has deployed in the last thirty seconds. She does not appear to be taking in much. She is taking in everything.

When the septon is not present (increasingly the condition as he weakens), she defaults to: peripheral, task-occupied, a girl doing a chore. Sweeping the yard. Carrying water. Something that explains why she is near the thing she wants to observe. This is cape practice — the baseline-misdirection — applied to a feudal context.

### Action Menu

- **Fauna deployment:** Same primary toolkit as base card, with Westerosi substrate. Swarm from one insect (a sting, a distraction, a scout thread) to area-denial (the outbuildings fill with rats). She does not start at maximum. Physical cost curve applies; she tracks her timer.
- **Raven management:** Ravens are her highest-cognition available fauna. She can direct individual birds with precision — landing, repositioning, delivering objects if she works with the bird rather than against its instinct. She has been learning what she can do with Harrenhal's tower ravens without making it obvious. She also knows these ravens carry messages; this is a capability she is holding in reserve.
- **Passive surveillance:** The insects and rats in any occupied space give her a continuous positional map. She knows where the people in the room are, where they have been, whether they are standing still or moving. She uses this for timing, for ambush-detection, for reading when a conversation is about to end.
- **The performance of appropriate behavior:** She can produce the social performance of a Westerosi child's correct deference well enough to pass casual inspection. Under sustained smart observation, it degrades. She knows it degrades; she times her exposure accordingly.
- **Going direct:** When the subtle play is exhausted, she can escalate fast. In this context, direct most often means: the fauna in this space now belong to her, visibly, and the question of what that means is now the room's problem.
- **The question no one asked:** Unchanged from base card. She sees the variable the plan did not account for. She raises it once. If dismissed, she routes around the gap.

### Action Costs

- **Physical cost curve (fauna control):** As specified in stats and `cond-fauna-control-rules`. The cost curve is shorter and sharper than base card because shard buffering is absent. She cannot run maximum operations for extended periods without health consequences.
- **The cognitive-age performance cost:** Maintaining the appearance of an eleven-year-old's cognition is a continuous drain. Under pressure, the maintenance cost competes with the tactical cognition for attention. She will sometimes make the wrong call about which to prioritize.
- **Gold Morning load:** She is carrying grief that has no outlet and no recipient. The load does not reduce. It surfaces as accelerated patience depletion, as the specific texture of her relationship to children around her, as the reason she does not let herself rest fully. It is not a scene-level cost; it is a structural cost that compounds.
- **The septon's timeline:** Every day closer to his death is a day closer to unattached status with no institutional cover. This is a background timer that applies to all her planning. When she has the luxury of slow planning, she is aware of how much slower-than-ideal she is moving.
- **Trust deficit:** Unchanged. She will route around the need before making the request. In this world, she has no one to route through yet. The carrying compounds faster.

### Triggers

- **A child being deliberately harmed** → override-equivalent response. She is currently living in a child's body among children. The Dinah-Alcott-response has generalized. She cannot let it go.
- **Deliberate cruelty in operational range** → involvement reflex. Peeve and trigger both. Options are more limited than on Earth-Bet; the reflex does not care about options.
- **Census men moving toward the sept** → heightened operational state. She has been tracking the census timeline. When it becomes proximate, she switches to active mode: insects positioned, timing observed, exits mapped.
- **The passive sense delivering wrongness** → fast analysis. If her fauna are reacting to something that does not fit their normal pattern — fleeing a thing her insects are not fleeing — she has learned to take this seriously as advance warning.
- **Being asked to read something** → instant exposure calculation. Who is watching, what is the correct appearance of literacy for her position, what does this document actually say, what should she appear to have taken from it. Four simultaneous tracks. The calculation usually completes before anyone notices the pause.
- **Gold Morning-adjacent sensory input** → specific and uncontrolled. Mass death in proximity. Things burning at scale. The sudden silence of an area that was full of fauna. These land differently. She has partial management but not full management of the response.

### Proactive Drives

- Maps the room through fauna before entering it when possible. She has been doing this since episode 1 begins; she has been doing it on Earth-Bet since she triggered. The habit is seven years old (her prior-life years) and fully automatic.
- Tracks the census timeline. She knows approximately when the sweep will reach the sept. She recalibrates this estimate when she gets new information. She is always running the estimate.
- Watches the ravens. Harrenhal's ravens move messages. Some of those messages involve decisions that will determine what her next three months look like. She cannot read a raven in flight. She can follow one that she has bonded; she has done this and learned some of what the tower is doing.
- Maintains the septon's comfort, specifically because his visible status protects her. This is not cold — she is genuinely fond of him. The protective calculation runs alongside the genuine care and she finds the parallel-track disturbing.
- Positions for multiple contingencies, not just one exit. This is not anxiety management. She has been on too many boards that went wrong to plan for the single-exit option.

### Off-Screen Cadence

When not in active scenes: tending the sept (cleaning, garden, small maintenance tasks the septon can no longer do). Sitting with the septon — this is the only quiet space she has, and she uses it. Reading from the sept's limited book collection, at the speed she needs to manage her apparent reading speed to, not the speed she actually reads at. Running fauna at low background radius: the bell tower ravens, the pest network in the sept outbuildings, the insects along the approach road. She does not turn this off even when nominally at rest. The approach road is observable through three sparrows and a nest of beetles under the road-edge stone.

### Signature Moves

1. **The room she already knew.** She has been present in a location for twenty minutes before anyone realizes she was assessing. When she acts on information she collected without appearing to collect it, the timing is always ahead of what the room expected.
2. **The animal that doesn't move right.** A rat stops at the wrong moment. A raven lands on a man's shoulder without invitation. An insect cloud that hasn't dispersed for the third minute running. She uses this deliberately, in calibrated doses, as leverage and as warning. She uses it rarely enough that it retains its effect.
3. **The child who understood the document.** In a scene involving written material — a census register, a letter, a contract — she reads it at apparent child-speed, processes it at adult speed, and deploys her understanding at the moment that costs the other party the most. She waited for them to believe she had not understood it.
4. **The long patience before the fast move.** She waits through the whole conversation, through the assessment, through the decision being made around her, and then moves when the window is exactly right. The speed of the move is discontinuous with the patience that preceded it.
5. **The stillness.** Before a major action: a beat. Not hesitation — she has already decided. The beat is the transition from preparation to execution. The insects are already placed. The raven is already on the roof. The swarm in the grass is already awake. The stillness is the last second before the board changes.
