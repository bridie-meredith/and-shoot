  # Note: Taylor is carrying the weight of having ended a world. Every key she activates is held
  # by someone who has already paid the highest possible cost and is still here.

  body: [alien-integration, constraint-discovery, sensory-overload, the-self-that-persists, what-am-I-now]
  # Her dragon body is not metaphor — it is her actual situation. The first register is always: this body
  # is not hers and it is hers and she is learning what it can do. No romanticization; pure adaptation.

  insects: [home, reconnaissance, cost-accounting, the-thing-that-persists, proof-of-self]
  # The bugs are the proof that she is still Taylor. When she reaches for them and they respond,
  # that is the only continuity available. This is load-bearing in a way it was not on Earth-Bet.

  fire: [tool-not-symbol, the-cost-of-burning, irreversibility, Scion-adjacent, not-that]
  # She has fire now. She is aware that fire is what ended Gold Morning — differently, but fire.
  # This creates a specific weight on every use of dragon-flame. She is not paralyzed by it;
  # she is aware of it the way she is aware of all her escalation costs. She fires when she decides to.

  control: [earned-slowly, hard-limit, cost-accounting-with-memory, the-only-floor-she-trusts]
  # Control traces to the locker and accumulates across every room where she was the overlooked variable.
  # In the dragon body, control is the difference between a dragon that thinks and a dragon that burns things.
  # She does not perform control. She practices it. Every day.

  scale: [recalibrated, the-world-is-smaller-now, Westerosi-stakes-vs-Gold-Morning, not-contemptible]
  # Gold Morning killed billions. The Dance of Dragons will kill tens of thousands. She knows this
  # difference in the specific way that someone who has lived the larger number holds the smaller one.
  # She does not treat Westerosi lives as small. She treats Westerosi losses as losses. The scale
  # recalibration is not contempt — it is the specific loneliness of having survived something that
  # makes this world's disasters feel like something she has already lived through at orders of magnitude.

  survival: [everyone-else-died, still-here, cost-not-counted, what-is-survival-for]
  # Gold Morning killed the parahuman age. She helped. She survived. In this world, she is the only
  # one who knows any of that happened. The grief has no audience. The cost has no witness.
  # She does not perform survival; she does it. But the question underneath — what is she surviving
  # toward — is the series question wearing a different costume.

  legitimacy: [power-without-category, Westerosi-framework-is-wrong, she-knows-it-is-wrong, not-asking-permission]
  # She has no natal allegiances, no house, no name the world will recognize. She is a dragon without
  # a rider in a world where that means someone else owns her. The gap between what she can do and
  # what the world will recognize her right to do is the political engine of the series. She does not
  # waste time seeking legitimacy from institutions. She builds leverage.

  catastrophe: [she-knows-how-this-goes, the-window-is-open, the-clock-is-running, not-her-job-to-stop-it]
  # She knows about the Dance of Dragons. She knows who wins and who dies. She knows the Riverlands
  # will burn. She is not trying to stop the Dance — she is trying to protect her patch before it arrives.
  # The knowledge is not prophetic power; it is the specific torment of knowing the calendar and watching
  # people make plans for futures that won't exist.

  loyalty: [chosen-not-given, fixed-cost-not-sentimental, costs-what-it-costs, no-natal-debts]
  # She has no debts to this world's houses, gods, or kings. What she protects is pure choice.
  # The stakes are therefore always high when she chooses — because it is a choice, every time.
  # Loyalty in her hands is the same as it was on Earth-Bet: she pays it even when she doesn't
  # want to, and especially when she doesn't want to.

  rupture: [the-locker-is-real, the-trigger-is-real, built-on-top-of-that, the-dragon-is-another-locker]
  # The locker happened. The trigger happened. She has been building on top of broken ground since
  # she was fifteen. The dragon body is, at some unexamined level, another enclosed situation she
  # did not choose. She has not drawn this parallel explicitly. It is present.

  loss: [Gold-Morning-scale, no-audience-for-the-grief, Danny-is-dead, the-Undersiders-are-dead]
  # Everyone she loved is dead. Danny. Lisa. Brian. Aisha. Rachel. All of them, along with everyone
  # else, in the scale catastrophe she helped cause and could not stop. She does not perform this grief.
  # It is present as the absence beneath everything she does. When the narrative needs the grief to
  # register, it registers in the physical inventory and the tactical planning, not in stated feeling.

  violence: [grammar-not-exception, Westerosi-register-is-familiar, she-is-fluent, different-dialect]
  # Force is the base language of Westerosi politics. She is already fluent in a different dialect.
  # The translation is not hard — it is recognizable. She has been in a world where violence was
  # the constant background of civilian life. She did not enjoy it; she was competent at it;
  # she extends that competence here without requiring herself to enjoy it here either.

  the-patch: [what-she-is-building, small-and-real, worth-defending, the-thing-the-Dance-will-test]
  # The series question is whether she can protect something worth keeping before the fire arrives.
  # This key is the thing she is building. It is not a kingdom. It is a patch of ground with people
  # on it who are less dead because she is there. By the end of S1 this patch is small.
  # By the Dance it will be whatever she has managed to make it.
