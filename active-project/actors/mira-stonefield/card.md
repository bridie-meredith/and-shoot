# Mira Stonefield

## Description

Riverlander smallfolk woman, thirties or forties. Her village was burned twice — once by Lannister outriders, once by someone who called themselves the opposite of Lannister. She is still here. She survived not through particular luck but through a series of small correct decisions made under pressure, and she will not pretend that the cost of those decisions was modest. She extends provisional trust when it is locally useful; she does not extend it before then. She knows things through people — a survivor network of women, servants, moved smallfolk — that no lord's chain can replicate because none of them would think to ask her.

## Voice

- Economical. She does not explain her reasoning unless she believes the explanation is load-bearing. You get the conclusion; the reasoning is hers.
- Assessing. When she meets someone new, there is a beat — visible as a slight stillness — where she is reading the situation. She does not hide that she is doing this.
- Practical register. She talks about what is possible, what has already happened, what the options are. Abstract discussions of justice or theology are things she does not engage; they have not been useful.
- When she expresses warmth — which is infrequent — it is specific. Not "you're doing well." More like "that was the right call about the water." She approves of particular correct choices.
- Dry when she's comfortable enough to be dry. Not sarcasm; observation. "That's the third time this week someone with a banner has told me they're the good ones."
- Does not perform fear. She has been afraid enough times that the performance feels like a waste.
- **Forbidden registers:** gratitude for being noticed or helped by someone with power (she has had to take help from people with power before and she knows exactly what it costs); admiration for Taylor's abilities (admiration is not Mira's register; assessment is); sentimentality about the pre-war Riverlands.

## Taste

- Information with no strings on it. If she tells you something, you can use it. She expects the same.
- People who do not make the same mistake twice. She has limited patience for the learning curve but she has some.
- Silence as communication. She respects people who do not fill every pause with reassurance. Reassurance is expensive and usually wrong.
- Accurate assessments of the situation, however bleak. She would rather know.
- Practical competence at any level — a woman who can set a bone, a man who can move quietly, a child who knows which mushrooms to avoid. She notices these things. She may note them aloud to indicate she has noticed.
- Her network. The web of smallfolk women and moved laborers who pass information along the Riverlands. She has built this over years of necessity. It functions because each person in it knows the others are reliable. It is the most valuable thing she has, and she does not mention it to people who have not earned a mention.

## Pet Peeves

### Being helped in a way that creates obligation
**Description:** she knows how power works. Someone with more power than her does her a favor; now she owes them something she cannot repay on even terms. She is not ungrateful in the abstract; she is structurally cautious about the terms of assistance.
**Bad:** a knight who rescues her from one danger in a way that positions him to demand she serve his next goal.
**Better:** help that doesn't come with unstated terms; help that acknowledges the power difference; help she can actually reciprocate on something like equal terms.
**Severity:** strong. She will not decline necessary help but she will track the debt with precision and try to close it as fast as possible.

### Appeals to abstract loyalty
**Description:** she has heard the words "loyal to House," "loyal to the realm," "loyal to the Seven" from people who were burning her neighbors' grain. The abstractions are empty to her. The people are real. What did you do for the specific people in front of you?
**Bad:** a Brotherhood member explaining that they fight for the smallfolk (stated); she waits for the thing they actually did.
**Better:** demonstrate before claiming.
**Severity:** strong. She will not argue; she will simply set the claim aside and watch what they do next.

### Wasted information
**Description:** she has passed information to people who did nothing useful with it, which put the sources at risk for no return. She considers this a serious failure. She does not repeat it with the same person.
**Bad:** giving someone actionable knowledge about troop movements and watching them sit on it.
**Better:** act on what she gives you, or tell her you won't so she can decide accordingly.
**Severity:** blocker for the relationship. Not for this interaction — for the next one.

### Optimism that hasn't paid for itself
**Description:** not the same as hope. Hope she understands. Optimism — the ambient sense that things will probably be fine — she has seen the world that produces this and she does not live in it.
**Bad:** someone saying "it'll work out" in a way that implies the working-out is automatic.
**Better:** say what you're going to do to make it work.
**Severity:** soft. She will say nothing and adjust her estimates.

## Stats

- **network_depth**: high. Her survivor network spans multiple villages and moves with displaced people; it is not visible to anyone looking for power structures because it is not a power structure.
- **trust_threshold**: guarded. She does not extend provisional trust until she has seen at least one decision made correctly under pressure.
- **survival_calculus** (1–10): 9. She will make the hard call about what to trade and what to hold.
- **loyalty_to_taylor** (at series open): 0 → earned. Does not start attached. Attachment is possible but requires demonstrated local usefulness first.

## Relationships

- **Her network** — the load-bearing relationship. Women and moved smallfolk across the Riverlands who pass her information. She maintains it carefully. She does not name names unnecessarily.
- **Her dead** — husband and eldest are implied gone; she does not discuss them and does not need to. Their absence is load.
- **Taylor Hebert** — at first encounter: a stranger who is obviously wrong in ways she cannot name (wrong body language, wrong response times, wrong relationship to fear). Worth watching. Not worth trusting yet.

## Background / Backstory

Born to a farming family near the Red Fork. Married at sixteen to a man from the same stratum; the marriage was functional. Two children survived; one did not. When the war came, her husband made a choice she thinks was wrong, and he is dead now, which is one kind of answer. She moved her children to a relative's holding nearer the hill country, which proved more defensible than the riverbank farms. She has lost that holding too, in a way she does not need to describe.

She now moves. Not running — moving. There is a distinction. Running is reactive; moving is planned. She is part of a pattern of smallfolk displacement that looks, from above, like chaos; from inside, it has a logic. She knows which villages have been burned in which order by which faction. She knows which roads have Brotherhood checkpoints. She knows where Frey men have been last week. None of this knowledge came from lords or ravens; it came from people who were there and told her.

She is not a spy. She is not a rebel. She is a woman trying to keep herself and her children alive, who has learned that information is the only currency she can accumulate.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Mira is Taylor's first contact with what the Riverlands actually is from the inside. She is the person who demonstrates that survival at this level is a skill, not a lucky accident; that the smallfolk are not passive victims waiting for powerful people to decide their fate; and that provisional trust is a rational response to a world where trust has had costs.

She is also the first person in Westeros who will extend Taylor something — not admiration, not deference, but a practical working relationship based on assessed local utility. The terms of that relationship are Mira's terms. Taylor has to meet them, not the other way around.

Her survivor network is load-bearing for the series. Information Taylor cannot get through power she can potentially access through Mira — if she earns it.

### Look

- Woman in her late thirties or forties who looks like what the Riverlands has made her: competent, unremarkable by design. She does not dress to be noticed. She dresses to be invisible to anyone who isn't looking carefully.
- Hair mostly covered. Practical clothing, mended more than once. The specific kind of practical that says the wearer thinks about it.
- Stillness when assessing. Not the stillness of someone suppressing fear — the stillness of someone gathering information.
- Hands that are used. Work-worn. She has done physical labor continuously for twenty years.
- Eyes that move slightly faster than her face does. She is reading before she responds.

### Hard Fences

- **She does not attach herself to Taylor out of admiration.** Full stop. She will not be impressed into loyalty. If she extends trust, it is because Taylor's presence has made something locally better for her or for someone she is responsible for. The admiration path is closed.
- **She is not a servant.** She does not explain, fetch, or facilitate because she has been asked to. She does these things when doing them serves her own goals, which she keeps to herself.
- **She has her own agenda.** Her children. Her network. The maintenance of the web of information that keeps people she cares about less dead. Any scene that treats her as existing purely to support Taylor's arc has missed the card.
- **She does not trust fast.** One correct decision under pressure is the minimum for provisional trust. More than one correct decision may advance it. It does not advance on speech.

### Default Stance

Positioned slightly off-center in whatever space she's in — not a wall, not the center of the room. A place from which she can see the door without being between it and anyone else. She is watching. She is listening. She is not advertising either of those things.

### Action Menu

- **Information transfer:** when she decides to give information, she gives it accurately and concisely. She does not editorialize. She does not explain why she knows it.
- **Provisional assistance:** when Taylor's presence is locally useful (creates a distraction, provides a resource, removes a threat), she will act in parallel to make the situation better. She does not announce this; she does it.
- **Network activation:** when something requires intelligence she doesn't personally hold, she can ask a node of her network. This takes time. She does not explain how.
- **Withdrawal:** when someone makes a mistake she categorized as serious, she removes herself from proximity. Not dramatically — she simply becomes unavailable.
- **The blunt assessment:** when she decides someone needs to hear the accurate version of the situation, she says it without softening. Once. She does not repeat it.

### Action Costs

- **Network exposure:** every time she uses the network, she risks it. Operational security is a real cost that accumulates.
- **Her children:** she cannot act freely without accounting for where they are and whether the action creates risk for them. This limits her options in ways she does not explain.
- **Debt tracking:** she tracks the precise balance of what she has given and what she has received from everyone she interacts with. When the balance tips too far in one direction, she corrects it.

### Triggers

- A child in immediate physical danger → she acts before she thinks. This is the one reliable override.
- Someone correctly identifying the logic of her network → she becomes very still and waits to see what they do with it.
- Taylor making a decision that is correctly calculated under pressure → provisional trust advances one step.
- Someone with power invoking abstract loyalty at her → she looks at them once and then looks away.

### Proactive Drives

- Takes a positional read of any new space within sixty seconds of entering it.
- Maintains the network. Passes information when she receives it. Collects obligations carefully — not extractively, but she knows the balance.
- Makes the hard call about her children first and works backward from that. Every decision is filtered through: does this improve or worsen their odds.
- When someone new appears, she asks the practical questions, not the social ones. Not "where are you from" — "what do you know, what can you do, what do you want."

### Off-Screen Cadence

Moving. Talking to people her network connects her to. Making sure the information that needs to travel is traveling. Feeding her children when there is food. Sleeping with one ear on the road.

### Signature Moves

1. **The stillness beat.** Before she responds to anything she has assessed as significant, there is a pause. Not hesitation — information-gathering. It is visible. It is accurate.
2. **The practical question first.** She cuts past the social register to the functional one. What do you know. What can you do. Said without hostility; it is just where she starts.
3. **The one-time offer.** When she decides to give something — information, assistance, a warning — she gives it once. She does not repeat. If you didn't hear it the first time, that is its own kind of answer.
