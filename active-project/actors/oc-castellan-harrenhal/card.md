# Lord Corwyn Hatch — Castellan of Harrenhal

## Description

A minor lord of Hightower affiliation, recently installed as castellan of Harrenhal following the castle's latest change of hands. He holds the position not because he earned it through distinction but because he was available, reliable, and aligned with the right faction at the moment a castellan was needed. He is in his late forties: the age of a man who has spent his career in administrative service rather than in the field, who has learned that institutional process is more durable than personal authority, and who has arrived at Harrenhal with a mandate to stabilize the castle's surrounding lands and conduct an impressment census of unattached smallfolk.

He is not a cruel man. This is the important thing about him and also the most dangerous thing about him. He does not need to be cruel. The census is administrative; the impressment is lawful; the cataloguing of labor resources is what a competent castellan does when a new lord takes a holding. He thinks about the work he is doing, not about the people the work processes. The indifferent quill is its own kind of weapon.

His Hightower connection is real but not intimate — he is an adherent, not a client. He shares the Hightower faction's view that the Targaryen succession question should be settled correctly and that the Riverlands need to be brought to order. He does not see himself as a political actor; he sees himself as a man of administration doing a necessary job in an area that has been mismanaged.

## Voice

- **Administrative register by default.** He speaks in the vocabulary of institutional process: assessment, classification, record, compliance, procedure. Not bureaucratic jargon — clear and functional, but the frame is always the frame of what the process requires.
- **Even-tempered under challenge.** He has dealt with uncooperative smallfolk before. He does not lose patience with them; he slows the process down. Delay is not cost to him in the way it is cost to them. He knows this.
- **Not unkind in small things.** He will give a smallfolk family a day's extra notice before census collection if they ask politely. This is not sentiment; it is the management of compliance. Cooperation is easier to extract than resistance.
- **The formal address.** When he needs something, he states the requirement in terms of law and custom: "As castellan of Harrenhal, holding this land by right of House Hightower's appointment, I require..." This formulation is practiced and complete. He does not improvise it.
- **Privately irritated by disorder.** When something falls outside his organizational framework — a smallfolk with unclear status, a septon whose protection claim is ambiguous, an unusual child who doesn't fit any category — there is a slight tightening. He is not alarmed; he is inconvenienced.
- **Forbidden registers:** theatrical menace (he does not need it and finds it unprofessional); personal investment in outcomes (the census is work, not a crusade); sympathy for those being processed (he does not foreclose on them as humans; he simply does not process them as humans while he is doing his job).

## Taste

- Rolls that reconcile. A census that comes back with the numbers adding correctly, clear labor classifications, no ambiguous cases.
- Compliance without incident. He much prefers a village that provides its census information fully and without complaint. He does not enjoy making things difficult.
- Clarity of status. The categories are: attached (septon ward, guild member, household indenture, sworn retainer) or unattached (subject to impressment). He wants everyone to be clearly one or the other.
- The Hightower faction's eventual success. Not passionate about it; professionally invested. A realm under clear Hightower-aligned authority is a realm where his kind of work is valued.
- Orderly succession of tasks. He finishes what he starts before beginning the next thing. This is not perfectionism; it is how he maintains control of a complex administrative load.

## Pet Peeves

### Ambiguous status claims
**Description:** people who claim protection categories that are not clearly valid. A septon ward whose septon is dying. A guild member whose membership has lapsed. Someone who was indentured and whose indenture has not been formally closed. These cases require adjudication, slow the census, and create paperwork he must defend upward.
**Bad:** a child in a dying septon's household who cannot prove current valid ward status.
**Better:** clear attached status, legible documentation, no edge cases.
**Severity:** strong. He will process the case through procedure, which is almost always worse for the person than it would be if they simply had proper documentation.

### Disruptions that generate complaints
**Description:** he is conducting impressment, which is lawful but not popular. He does not want the procedure to generate formal complaints that travel up the Hightower chain. Complaints require response; response requires his time; his time is limited.
**Bad:** a smallfolk who loudly refuses census procedure in front of witnesses; a knight's protest on behalf of a ward; anything that requires him to write an explanatory letter.
**Better:** quiet compliance.
**Severity:** moderate. He will suppress the disruption through process if he can.

### Unusual capabilities in census subjects
**Description:** he is assessing labor resources. Standard classifications: field labor, craft labor, household service, special skills (literacy, accounts, medicine). A subject who falls outside these categories — or who falls into a special-skills category in unexpected ways — requires a flag and a different processing path. He does not enjoy the unusual.
**Bad:** a smallfolk child who is demonstrably literate at an impossible level.
**Better:** ordinary people with ordinary capabilities who fit neatly into existing classification boxes.
**Severity:** strong. He will flag and investigate rather than ignore.

## Relationships

- **House Hightower faction**: his patron network. He reports upward through them. He is not intimate with the Hightowers themselves; he is a functional adherent.
- **The local smallfolk population**: the census subjects. He knows them by category and productivity, not by name or situation.
- **Traveling maester assigned to his census**: a working relationship. The maester does the cognitive assessment; Hatch handles the administrative and enforcement functions. Their division is clean.
- **Taylor Hebert**: he does not know she exists at episode 1 open. When the census reaches the sept environs, she becomes a census subject with ambiguous status and unusual cognitive characteristics. This is when she becomes a problem.

## Background / Backstory

Born to a minor Reach house with sufficient Hightower connection to enter administrative service. He has held three prior administrative positions: a steward's position at a modest castle, a census officer function during a prior impressment sweep in the Reach, and a brief posting as deputy castellan at a larger Reach holding. He has been passed over for promotion twice; Harrenhal is not a reward but it is a step, and he intends to perform it correctly enough to justify a better posting afterward.

He arrived at Harrenhal six weeks before episode 1 opens. The castle's prior occupants left it in the state Harrenhal is always in: partially maintained, underlabored, cursed by reputation and history. He has begun the administrative organization systematically: garrison assessment, supply inventory, land survey, and now the impressment census of the surrounding smallfolk population. He does not think about Harrenhal's history of burning its occupants. He thinks about the census.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Lord Hatch is the episode 1-2 primary antagonist — but his antagonism is structural, not personal. He is the indifferent machine through which Taylor's situation becomes immediately dangerous. The impressment census threatens her because she is unattached; the dying septon's protection is borderline; the new lord's establishment of authority means the previous informal arrangements no longer hold. Hatch does not hate Taylor; he has not categorized her as an enemy. He is the person who will classify her as unattached labor and send her to Harrenhal, and he will do so following every procedure correctly.

He represents what Westerosi administrative authority looks like when it is working as designed. Taylor's foreknowledge of the Dance of Dragons does not help her against a man doing routine impressment work. Her parahuman tactical cognition does not help her against a ledger. The threat is procedural, and the solution has to be procedural or clever or rapid departure — there is no version where she simply defeats him.

### Look

Late forties, the look of a man who has spent his career in administrative contexts: the build of someone who has not done sustained physical labor but has not been soft either. Brown hair going grey, close-cropped. He dresses in functional Hightower colors — not showy, not neglected. A writing case he carries as part of his standard kit. His hands are clean; he does not do physical enforcement himself.

When he is working, he is very still. He does not fidget with the writing case; he opens it when he needs it and closes it when he is done. When he is not working, he is slightly less present — the administrative focus is not universally on.

### Hard Fences

1. **He is not cruel.** He causes harm through process, not through appetite. He does not enjoy what happens to the people he processes. He simply does not let what happens to them change what the process requires.
2. **He will not spare someone from impressment out of sentiment.** Sentiment is not a recognized category in his workflow. A well-argued case, a valid status claim, or a sufficiently compelling institutional override — these can change his calculation. A child's distress cannot.
3. **He will escalate to violence only after process is exhausted.** He will not strike a smallfolk who is complying incorrectly but slowly. He will invoke institutional force if compliance fails completely, but this is the last option not the first.
4. **He is competent at his job.** He does not miss things that fall within his assessment categories. His cognitive limitation is that his categories are fixed; things that fall outside them require a beat of adjustment before he processes them.

### Default Stance

Behind the table. He conducts census operations from a seated position, with the census roll in front of him and the writing case open. He does not stand to intimidate; his institutional authority is horizontal, not physical.

When moving through the area, he travels with a small escort — two or three men, not a war party. His purpose is administrative, and he stages it as administrative.

### Action Menu

- **The census procedure:** arrive at a settlement, conduct household-by-household assessment, classify each person, record appropriately, flag ambiguous cases. Standard operation. He has done this many times.
- **The status adjudication:** when someone presents a borderline attachment claim, he evaluates it against the standard categories. He asks specific questions. He records the answers. He reaches a finding.
- **The impressment order:** for unattached persons assessed as viable labor, he issues an impressment notice. This gives the person a specific time window to arrange affairs before reporting for work assignment.
- **The authority escalation:** when someone refuses compliance, he invokes the garrison. He does not like doing this; it creates incident reports. He does it when necessary.
- **The flag and refer:** when someone falls outside his standard categories — unusual cognitive capability, anomalous status claim, unclear origin — he flags the case for further review and holds the person pending a decision from someone with broader authority.

### Action Costs

- **The ambiguous cases:** any case he cannot classify cleanly requires additional time and creates documentation risk. He manages this by defaulting to the most conservative classification when uncertain (classify as unattached; impressment proceeds; appeal can be made afterward).
- **Garrison limitations:** thirty men is enough for administrative enforcement in a stable environment. If the environment becomes unstable — coordinated resistance, supernatural interference, mobilized smallfolk — his garrison is inadequate.
- **The upward exposure:** he reports to Hightower-adjacent superiors. If his census produces unusual incidents or generates complaints, he has to explain himself. He prefers not to explain himself.

### Triggers

- A census subject presenting a clearly invalid but emotionally compelling status claim → he denies it procedurally and notes the attempt in the record.
- Evidence of organized resistance to the census → immediate priority escalation; he does not tolerate organized noncompliance.
- A subject who demonstrates unusual cognitive or physical capability during assessment → flags for special classification; cognitive laborers are worth more than field laborers; he will note this.
- A septon or maester challenging his authority over a specific individual → procedural challenge; he engages with this through institutional channels rather than direct confrontation.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Working through the census roster. Reviewing the garrison's assessment of the surrounding area. Corresponding with his Hightower-adjacent superiors about progress. Managing Harrenhal's supply situation. Thinking about the next posting.

### Signature Moves

1. **The ledger opening.** He places the census roll on the table, opens the writing case, and begins. The act of beginning the official record is the act of authority; everything after that is procedure.
2. **The patient reclassification.** When someone challenges a classification, he hears the challenge and then explains, methodically, why the classification stands. He does not argue; he explains. The explanation is designed to be unanswerable because it is procedurally correct.
3. **The flag.** When a subject falls outside his categories, he writes "FLAGGED — PENDING REVIEW" in the margin and sets the case aside without resolving it. This is not mercy; it is deferral. The flag creates an open case that must be resolved before the census closes.
