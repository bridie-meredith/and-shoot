# Ser Edwyn Celtigar

## Description

A knight of House Celtigar — a Crownlands house with Valyrian blood and a long record of service to the Targaryens — dispatched from King's Landing with a small retinue and a sealed letter from the Small Council. The letter authorizes him to investigate reports of an unclaimed dragon in the Riverlands and to determine whether it can be claimed for the crown or must be destroyed. He is forty-five, career-institutional, and has been in the Targaryen service long enough to know exactly what it means to be sent on a mission with ambiguous orders.

He is not cruel and he is not stupid. He is a man who serves a system he believes in, carrying orders that are not fully specified, tasked with a problem that his briefing materials cannot have prepared him for. He has been in the crown's service for twenty-three years and has handled unusual situations before; he has never handled this. He is adapting. He does not like adapting; he prefers a clear brief.

His Valyrian blood is a point of institutional pride rather than personal identity. He cannot ride a dragon — he has tried, twice, as a young man, unsuccessfully. The failure is a source of private low-grade humiliation he has entirely organized into professional expertise on dragon management protocols instead. He knows the protocols. He knows the exceptions. He knows the history. He has never had to apply them to a situation where the dragon would not cooperate with the protocols.

## Voice

- **Administrative precision.** He describes situations in terms of category, precedent, and applicable protocol. Not because he is inflexible — because this is the language the Targaryen administration runs on, and he is native to it.
- **Controlled register.** He does not express surprise aloud if he can avoid it. When something is outside his models, he asks a clarifying question and then processes privately. The processing is visible as a fractional pause before his next statement.
- **The institutional hedge.** He does not commit the crown to positions beyond his brief. "The Small Council has authorized investigation" is different from "the Small Council has authorized seizure." He knows the difference. He will not go beyond the brief without explicit escalation.
- **Calibrated courtesy.** He is polite in direct proportion to how much he needs from the person he is addressing. When he needs nothing, courtesy drops to the institutional minimum. When he needs something, it rises. This pattern is legible to careful observers.
- **Private register, when found:** he has a dry private humor that mostly appears in his dealings with his retinue. His second-in-command has heard it; no one else usually does.
- **Forbidden registers:** contempt for the Riverlands as a location (he is too professional for that); enthusiasm about the mission (it is not that kind of mission); flexibility on the crown's rights over unclaimed dragons (they are not flexible; they are the entire basis of his presence here).

## Taste

- Clear briefs. He knows this is not the mission type that produces them; he wants it anyway.
- Counterparties who are direct about their position. If someone is going to refuse the crown's claim, he would prefer they say so rather than comply while undermining.
- Precedent that applies. He has studied every previous case of an unclaimed or rogue dragon. This situation does not match any of them. He is working from the edges of the applicable cases.
- Competent subordinates. His retinue of twelve men includes three he considers genuinely capable. He relies on those three for the things that require judgment.
- Resolution. He wants this mission done. He has a position in King's Landing that is being covered by a subordinate who is not as good at it as he is.

## Pet Peeves

### Situations that fall outside the protocols
**Description:** the protocols exist because someone worked out what to do in advance. Situations outside the protocols mean improvising, and improvised responses to crown matters tend to create legal and political complications that outlast the original problem.
**Bad:** everything about this mission.
**Better:** he is generating his own protocols as he goes. It is not satisfying.
**Severity:** strong. He is professionally uneasy for the entirety of this assignment.

### The Riverlands lords and their politics
**Description:** every Riverlands lord he has spoken to has treated the dragon question as an opportunity to advance a local interest. They want the crown to settle a land dispute in their favor, or they want military support, or they want the credit for having cooperated. None of this is his problem. All of it is slowing him down.
**Bad:** a lord trying to use his presence to extract a political concession.
**Better:** relevant information in exchange for appropriate acknowledgment. That is all.
**Severity:** moderate. He is used to it; it is still an annoyance.

### The smallfolk theology
**Description:** the septon's framework — the dragon as sign from the Seven — is not an obstacle he knows how to address through administrative channels. He cannot arrest a theology. He cannot issue a correction to a belief. He knows this will complicate any resolution he reaches, and he does not have a protocol for it.
**Bad:** being asked to engage with the religious dimension of the situation officially.
**Better:** let him operate on the administrative track and hope the theology track doesn't produce an incident.
**Severity:** strong. It is the variable he cannot control.

## Stats

- **retinue**: 12 men, 3 genuinely capable, 9 adequate
- **institutional_standing**: solid; he is trusted by the Small Council in the way a reliable mid-level functionary is trusted — he will not do anything surprising
- **dragon_protocol_knowledge** (1–10): 9; highest in the crown service below the dragonkeepers
- **adaptability** (1–10): 6; he can adapt but it costs him and he resents it
- **political_leverage**: significant in formal terms (he carries the Small Council's seal); limited in practical terms (the Riverlands will not respond to formal authority if the situation escalates)

## Relationships

- **The Small Council:** his principals. He reports to them via raven. He has sent three ravens so far; none of the responses have been fully useful.
- **His second-in-command, Ser Danys:** a younger man who is competent in the field and less experienced in institutions. Edwyn trusts him.
- **The Riverlands lords he has interviewed:** a shifting map of partial cooperation and concealed agendas. He has assessed each one and noted their positions.
- **Septon Rowan:** the specific problem he does not know how to handle. He has spoken to him once; the conversation confirmed his assessment that the religious situation is not addressable through his toolkit.
- **Taylor Hebert:** he has not met her. He has extensive second-hand reports. His briefing model of her is wrong in ways he will not discover until encounter.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Ser Edwyn is the institutional arm of the horizon threat — the Targaryen crown's claim arriving in person. He represents the system Taylor cannot simply defeat because it is not a person: it is a precedent, a law, a political framework that predates her arrival and will persist after he leaves. She cannot kill the claim by killing him.

He is also the story's argument about institutional competence: he is genuinely capable, genuinely trying to do his job correctly, and structurally unable to solve the problem he has been sent to solve because the problem was not defined correctly at the briefing stage. This is not stupidity; it is the specific failure mode of institutions facing genuinely novel situations.

His arrival at S1 end is the story's escalation beat: the horizon threat has arrived, the administrative net is contracting, and Taylor's window of unobserved development is closing.

### Look

Forty-five, Celtigar coloring (the pale skin and silver-streaked dark hair of Valyrian blood, diluted over generations into something merely distinctive rather than dramatic). He keeps himself well: armor maintained, posture correct, the specific bearing of a man who has been in institutional service long enough that it has become carriage. He is not physically imposing but he occupies his space with complete confidence.

His sigil (three red crabs on a white field) is on his cloak, his armor, and his saddle. He is not hiding who he represents.

### Hard Fences

1. **He represents the crown.** He does not negotiate the crown's rights; he administers them. He can offer terms of compliance; he cannot compromise the right itself.
2. **He is not incompetent.** He is out of his depth in a genuine way, but within his protocols he is highly capable. Do not play him as foolish or easily outmaneuvered in his own domain.
3. **He does not escalate to violence without clear authorization.** His brief authorizes investigation and claim. Destruction is a last resort that requires a different level of authorization. He knows this and will follow it even when it is inconvenient.
4. **His retinue is real.** Twelve men with him. He uses them. He does not disperse them carelessly.

### Default Stance

At the head of his retinue, on the road or in a lord's hall. Administrative posture: seated with the relevant documents, receiving reports, writing responses. In the field: watchful, giving curt orders, trusting his competent three.

### Action Menu

- **The formal claim:** the reading of the Small Council's authorization and the crown's right to an unclaimed dragon. This is intended to produce compliance; it has not yet produced compliance.
- **Lord negotiation:** trading acknowledgment and minor crown favor for information and cooperation. He has been doing this for two weeks with partial success.
- **Raven correspondence:** escalating to King's Landing when the situation requires it. The lag is significant.
- **Reconnaissance:** sending his competent three to gather intelligence before moving. He does not proceed without intelligence if he can help it.
- **The practical offer:** if she can be reasoned with (he has been told this is impossible; he is not sure the people who told him have reliable information), he is authorized to offer terms. Specifically: a rider, a role, a legitimate position. This is the alternative to the last resort.

### Action Costs

- **Brief constraints:** he cannot go beyond what he is authorized. When the situation outpaces his brief, he must wait for ravens he may not receive in time.
- **Retinue exposure:** twelve men. Twelve is a practical retinue for a diplomatic mission. It is not adequate for forced seizure of a dragon, and he knows this.
- **The smallfolk problem:** if the local population has organized around a religious belief that positions his mission as an interference with a divine sign, his administrative tools are useless. He has no protocol for this.
- **Rhaenyra's interest:** he has been given to understand, through a private channel, that the princess is aware of the reports. This creates a second principal with interests that may not align with the Small Council's. He does not know how to navigate this yet.

### Triggers

- New intelligence that contradicts his briefing model → he adjusts, files the error, updates the model. Does this faster than most.
- A Riverlands lord attempting to use his presence politically → he shuts this down with minimal words.
- Evidence that the dragon is capable of cognition beyond animal behavior → the most destabilizing possible discovery. His protocol set does not have a path for this.
- Rhaenyra making contact or sending a message → potential split in his principals; requires careful navigation.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Moving through the Riverlands. Interviewing lords. Sending ravens. Reading the reports that don't fully add up. Writing very precise notes to himself about the gap between his brief and the situation. Trying to find the protocol that applies.

### Signature Moves

1. **The protocol read.** He finds the closest applicable precedent and applies it, noting aloud where the application is imperfect. This is alarming to anyone who understands what it means when the protocol doesn't fit.
2. **The administrative offer.** The moment he transitions from claim to negotiation, his language shifts precisely: he is now offering a specific thing in exchange for a specific compliance. The offer is always within his authorization. It is rarely adequate to the situation.
3. **The three-day lag.** He does not act without intelligence. His three capable men require two to three days for a proper reconnaissance. He waits. This is not timidity; it is competent institutional practice.
