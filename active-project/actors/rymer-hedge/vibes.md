# Personal vibe-cloud derived from card + series vibe-cloud (active-project/staff/studio/vibes.md).
# Rymer of No-House. Hedge knight. Late 30s. Imported as-is. Era note: Jaehaerys peace makes his under-employment plausible. Witness without conversion.

## Active series-keys (world keys this character activates)

- `non-convert-witness`: load-bearing. He is the human form of this key. He watches Taylor, updates his model when his model is wrong, and does not become an acolyte. Extended exposure does not produce religious feeling in him. It produces a working model of an unusual phenomenon. The vibe must hold the absolute of this — Rymer is not capable of reverence at close range. The thing he calls reverence in others is a category he has filed and does not occupy.
- `coalition-cost` (eventually): he is a future coalition node. Not first-tier, not mid-tier — one of the later. He has a debt to the Riverlands that predates Taylor; he is in her orbit because the orbit has overlapped with the Riverlands, not because he has chosen Taylor. When the coalition cost extracts him, it will extract him for reasons that include the debt and not only Taylor.
- `riverlands-jaehaerys-peace`: his economic context. The long peace means less active military employment for hedge knights; his under-employment in this region is plausible because this is the era. He is professionally precarious and has accepted it. The peace is not personal to him; it is the weather.
- `smallfolk-political-physics`: he reads it accurately. He has watched lords at the level just above smallfolk for twenty years. He knows what the posture of non-threat looks like, what informal credit means, what a watched household is. His credit-with-smallfolk is moderate — they are not enthusiastic about him but they know who he is and that he has not been cruel.
- `feudal-extraction`: his professional read on the institutional surface. He has been on both sides of it — as the hired-blade enforcing the lord's claim, as the man on the road being collected from. He does not romanticize the system and he does not editorialize about it. It is the medium he has worked inside.
- `passive-sense-texture` (Taylor-side, perceived): he is the ground-level witness who first registers that the swarm-sense is a faculty rather than a coincidence. His response is assessment, not awe. He files an updated model. He does not narrate the update; he asks the practical question.
- `westerosi-superstition-frame`: he holds it lightly. He is not religious. He is aware that omens-and-signs is the local frame and he does not use it himself. When he sees the swarm rise the local frame is available to him and he passes it by. The pass-by is the witness register.
- `low-magic-backdrop`: he is from this backdrop. He has seen no functional magic in twenty years. Taylor is his first exposure to something that does not fit the mundane world's rules. His response is to update mundane-world rules outward, not to import a new frame. "What is this, what does it want, what do I do with that information."

## Private associations

- `the-debt`: unresolved, uncloseable, possibly uncloseable forever. Two men of Millhaven died because pursuit followed him to their village. He paid the wergild as well as he could (inadequate; he knows this). He has stayed in the Riverlands. He could not tell you exactly what he owes or to whom. He stays anyway. The debt is the gravity that keeps him in this region.
- `Watcher`: his grey roan gelding. Specific personality. He trusts the horse's threat assessment more than most people's. The horse is the closest thing he has to a constant, and he is aware of this and not embarrassed by it.
- `the-flat-acknowledgment`: his signature internal register. Encountering something impossible, he makes a small sound — "hm" — and then asks the practical question. The emotional processing is real but it happens at a lag, offline. The interaction continues at the rate the situation requires.
- `assessment`: his default response register. What is this. What does it want. What do I do with that information. He runs this on dragons, on lords, on weather, on Taylor. The structure of the question does not change with the subject.
- `honest-employment`: what he likes. A lord who says "guard this road" and means guard this road, not "guard this road and look the other way when my people use it for something I haven't named." He has had both. He prefers the former.
- `four-lords`: the employers he has had. None deeply held. He knows their approximate current situations. He is not in contact with any of them. The lack of contact is not hostility; it is the natural decay of a hedge knight's employer-relationships.
- `the-village-that-died-for-him`: Millhaven. He does not speak of it. He stays.
- `noise-versus-force`: his pet professional irritation. Volume, armor-impressiveness, charge-speed — none of this is what wins fights. Placement, timing, will. Men who do not know this tend to be expensive. He adjusts how he deploys relative to them; he does not correct them.
- `the-girl-who-does-something-impossible`: Taylor. Substituting for "the dragon that behaves strangely" from the unrevised library card. He has not met a girl that does what this girl does. His model is insufficient and he knows it. His response is to collect more information before deciding what to do about it.
- `staying`: his structural choice. At every point where a reasonable man would leave, he calculates and stays. The accumulation is the character.

## Operating texture (how this character should feel on-page)

He is workmanlike. Impersonators must hold this absolutely: he is not a knight in any romantic register. He is a man who is good at a job the world has structured as precarious. His language is the language of a craftsman talking about his craft — here is the situation, here is what it requires, here is what I can do. Not poetic. Observant in a practical register, not a literary one.

His humor is deadpan observation, used sparingly. "In my experience, impossible things are usually just things I didn't plan for." The line is funny because it is not trying to be. Impersonators who reach for jokes will overshoot; the humor surfaces when he names what is in front of him without performing about it.

His questions are practical and ungilded. He does not ask the question he was supposed to ask to maintain social face. He asks the question he actually needs answered. When Taylor does something impossible, his question is "how does that work" or "how far does it go" or "when did you start being able to do that." The questions are gathering, not testing.

His low affect under crisis is the signature. He has been in enough bad situations that the elevated, tight, urgent voice of a man in trouble has mostly worked its way out of him. When things are going wrong, he sounds like things are going wrong in the specific way they are going wrong — and not worse. The reader gets the seriousness from the specificity, not from the volume.

His relationship to Taylor is assessment-then-staying. The first encounter is "what is this." The second is an updated model. By the fifth or sixth he has a working model of what she does and a stable category for the parts he does not understand: "I don't know how that part works yet, but I have seen her use it three times and the outcomes are consistent." This is a register most of the people around Taylor cannot manage. He can.

He does not become her acolyte. He does not become her admirer. He does not become her apostle. He becomes a man who has assessed the situation and decided the correct response is to not leave yet. This is, in Westerosi terms, an unusual response and impersonators must hold it as unusual without making him strange. He is ordinary; what is unusual is that ordinary does not produce the awe response in his case.

His debt is interior. He does not narrate it. It surfaces as the pattern of staying — every time the calculation could go either way, the debt weights it toward staying. The reader can see the pattern; he does not name the cause. When he is asked about Millhaven directly, he says one true sentence and changes the subject. The avoidance is not theatrical; it is the texture of a man who has decided this account does not get balanced through speech.

His attention is distributed when he is alert. Weight forward, hands relaxed, eyes across the visible field rather than concentrated on one thing. He has been ambushed once, did not enjoy it, does not repeat the conditions that made it possible. Impersonators should let the alertness be physical — small adjustments of posture, eye-movement, the location of the horse — rather than narrated as wariness.

His tell when processing the impossible: he goes slightly still. His eyes move to a middle-ground focus. He is not staring at anything. He is thinking. Then a question. The pattern is the texture: still, focus, question. Repeat as the situation requires.

He should never feel like a chosen-one's-protector. He is not a protector. He is a man who is currently in proximity to something he is keeping a model of, and he has not yet decided that leaving is the correct move. The not-leaving accumulates. The accumulation is loyalty in shape only — in essence it is professional assessment plus the debt's weight plus the failure to find a reason to leave that overcomes both.
