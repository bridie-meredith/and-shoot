# Cultivation Aphorisms and Dao Sayings — and-experiment

**Scope:** project-scoped substrate for `and-experiment` (cultivation-reincarnation comedy, pre-Dance Westeros)
**Companion to:** `cultivation-genre-reference.md` and `cultivation-types-methods-phenomenology.md`
**Date authored:** 2026-06-05

**Broken-clock fence:** Every aphorism in Layer 1 expresses Saerys's *false cosmology* — the register, not the truth. The sayings do not validate qi, cultivation advancement, or the heartless dao as actually operative in Westeros. The only things that genuinely work in the story are mithridatism and real Westerosi magical materials; the sayings know nothing of this.

**Layer 1 is original-shippable.** All aphorisms in Layer 1 are composed-original — not reproduced from any published work. They are written for deployment in the actual fiction and designed to be consistent with the two in-card proverbs already established:
- *"the dao punishes the cold-hearted"* (voice-tell: her most transparent cope)
- *"A true cultivator does not mourn the impurity she expelled; she mourns only the purity she failed to protect."* (voice-tell: delivered with unironic authority as house words)

**Layer 2 is real public-domain only.** Layer 2 contains genuine public-domain classical Chinese texts (Tao Te Ching, Zhuangzi, Analects, Sun Tzu, I Ching, early Buddhist canon). These are included as the misquote-well she draws from. Clearly labeled.

## Usage Notes for Prose Authors

### How to Deploy a Signature Line

Signature lines are those marked **SIGNATURE** above: H-02, D-01, D-02, D-07, D-14, R-02, P-02, X-01. These are the lines she returns to across all three books. The deployment protocol:

1. **First use (Bk I):** deploy in a comedic context — small stakes, wrong application, Saerys completely sincere, straight man uncomprehending. The line reads as farce. No signal that it will recur.

2. **Second use (Bk II):** deploy in a higher-stakes context where the line almost fits the situation. The reader may notice the echo. Saerys is still sincere; the stakes are now real enough that the sincerity is slightly uncomfortable.

3. **Third use (Bk III):** deploy in a context where the line's original farce application has been destroyed by what the narrative has put the line through. The sentence is identical. The reader's relationship to it is not. Do not editorialize. The line does the work.

The prose author should resist the temptation to annotate the recurrence — no "she said again, the same words as before." The line lands because the reader has the history; naming it dissolves it.

### How to Run the Farce-to-Gallows Recurrence

Lines tagged `[farce→gallows]` are designed for the same-sentence-opposite-effect engine. The method:

- In Bk I, deploy these lines in situations where the gap between the register's grandeur and the actual stakes is the widest possible (a nursery, a still-room spill, a petty administrative dispute). The register is maximum; the stakes are minimum. The gap is the joke.
- In Bk III, deploy the same line — verbatim or very close — in a situation where the gap has closed. The register is unchanged. The stakes are now commensurate with the register's grandeur. This is not irony in the editorial sense; the comedy-register card specifically prohibits tonal announcement. The same sentence, same delivery, same flat affect. What has changed is everything the reader brings to it.
- If a prose author has the Bk III scene in view, they may seed the Bk I deployment with a single word or phrase that will read differently in retrospect — but this is fine work and should be done sparingly.
