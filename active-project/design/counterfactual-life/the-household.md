# The Household — the people in Gael's day

*Counterfactual-life library, doc 1 of 3. The servants and handlers who would fill an ordinary
Targaryen princess's life if there were no plot. Read the INDEX naming/era note first: this is the
**Gael / Jaehaerys-as-father** layer.*

> **The governing principle (from `run-01/constraints.md` §2):** *the cage is the attendant, not the
> door.* A highborn girl is never alone. Freedom is **subtracting a watcher from the room**, not
> unlocking one. So the household is not background — it is the antagonist of every Book I caper,
> rendered as people. This doc is the roster of watchers: who they are, what they do *to* and *for*
> her, when they are present, and where the gaps between them open.

Names in **(placeholder)** are overridable (see INDEX OQ-CL3). They are era-plausible Westerosi
commoner/servant names chosen not to collide with existing project cards. The **functions** are the
durable content.

## How to read a household

A royal child's establishment has four concentric rings. The closer the ring, the more hours of the
day it occupies, and the more *intimate* — and therefore unwatched-feeling but never actually
unwatched — the contact.

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  Ring 4 — the court orbit (family, ladies, the King)   │
   │   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐  │
   │   │  Ring 3 — household officers (steward,          │  │
   │   │   housekeeper, wardrobe, the guard)             │  │
   │   │   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐  │  │
   │   │   │  Ring 2 — the handlers (septa, maester,   │  │  │
   │   │   │   masters of music/dancing)               │  │  │
   │   │   │   ┌──────────────────────────────────┐    │  │  │
   │   │   │   │  Ring 1 — the body & nursery      │    │  │  │
   │   │   │   │   (nurse, wet-nurse, maids,       │    │  │  │
   │   │   │   │   the rocker, the truckle-bed)    │    │  │  │
   │   │   │   └──────────────────────────────────┘    │  │  │
   │   │   └──────────────────────────────────────────┘  │  │
   │   └────────────────────────────────────────────────┘  │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

A scene's "who is in the room" is almost always **Ring 1 + one of Ring 2**. A heist is the math of
removing exactly those.

## Ring 1 — the body & the nursery (the closest watchers)

These are the people who touch her, feed her, dress her, and sleep in her room. In the infant and
toddler phases they ARE her whole world; they thin as she ages but never vanish.

### The wet-nurse — **(Mella)**
- **Function:** nurses the infant (Gael ~0–2). A healthy smallfolk woman, freshly delivered of her
  own child, brought into the Holdfast to feed the princess. Her milk is the infant's entire diet;
  she is present at every feeding, day and night, on a roughly two-to-three-hour cycle.
- **The political layer (canon-uncertain #2):** wet-nursing was used as a *bonding tool* — a
  princess and the nurse's own babe become **milk-siblings**, a tie meant to buy lifelong loyalty
  from a smallfolk family. So Mella has a child of her own in or near the nursery: a milk-brother or
  -sister to Gael, the first commoner Gael's body is bound to.
- **What she does TO the plot:** she is the relentless presence of I.1 — the feeding cycle is the
  clock the infant must cultivate *between*. "Monitored by worried nanny + Alicent" (I.1 step 6) is
  partly her. She is also the household's earliest alarm: a wet-nurse notices a baby that won't
  settle, that bleeds at the gums, that goes too still.
- **Loyalty/wage:** paid in coin + position + the milk-kin tie; her loyalty is to the family that
  fed her family. A natural early node of the below-stairs faction (loophole 5).

### The head nursemaid / "the nan" — **(Mistress Bryony)**
- **Function:** runs the nursery establishment. Senior servant, middle-aged, has raised other
  highborn children. She does the not-feeding care: swaddling, bathing, soothing, the day's small
  decisions; she directs the under-maids and the rocker; she answers to the septa and to Alicent.
- **The doting note:** she is fond. She is the one who gives the fretful infant the hard wooden toy
  it "expressed interest" in (I.1 step 4) — indulgence that becomes the egg-chipping tool. She reads
  Gael's stillness as a *good, easy baby* long after it has become something else.
- **What she does TO the plot:** the worried-nanny watch; the person who "would not leave a dead
  monster with an infant — or would she?" (I.1). She is the household's institutional memory of the
  nursery — what's normal, what's not — and therefore the first to be *wrong* about Gael in a way the
  comedy needs.

### Under-nursemaids / chamber-maids — **(Cissa, Nona)**
- **Function:** the hands. They carry water, change linen, tend the fire, fetch and bear, empty the
  chamber pot, lay out clothes, sleep in the room. There are usually two on rotating watch so the
  child is covered through the night. As Gael ages out of the nursery (≈6), one of these evolves into
  her personal **chamber-maid** — the body-servant who dresses her and sleeps on the truckle bed.
- **What they do TO the plot:** they are the literal answer to "who is in the room." Their rotation
  has overlap-gaps (the three-to-five-minute window in loc-maegors-holdfast). Their below-stairs
  errands are the supply line. **One of these is the slot the Book II maid most naturally fills** —
  see the seam below.

### The rocker / cradle-girl
- **Function:** a young girl (often a junior maid or a poor relation) whose job in the infant phase
  is literally to rock the cradle and watch the sleeping child. Lowest-status nursery role.
- **What she does TO the plot:** the night-watcher. The infant who wants to cultivate uninterrupted
  must wait out the rocker's attention. A real, boring, human obstacle — exactly the comedy register.

### The truckle-bed
- **Not a person but a fact:** Gael never sleeps alone. A body-servant sleeps on a low truckle bed in
  or beside her chamber, every night, her whole childhood. **Privacy is something she has to
  engineer** — this is why the night excursions (III.2) require the maid to *perform her absence*.
  The never-alone rule is the deepest, quietest cage.

## Ring 2 — the handlers (the people who shape her)

These teach, examine, and chaperone. They occupy the structured daytime hours. They are the ones who
*file reports*.

### The septa — **(unnamed; see `warehouse/saerys-septa.card.md`)**
- **Function (canon template: Septa Mordane):** the **central handler** — tutor, governess, and
  chaperone in one godsworn woman. From early childhood she owns Gael's days: lessons, deportment,
  devotions, and the watch between them. "Escaping handlers" = giving the septa the slip.
- **Already carded.** From Gael's locked-inside POV she is "the Septa." She is **right about
  everything except why**, files factually-correct reports that the King's "spirited-child filter"
  converts to nothing, and is ultimately **sent to a sept herself** when paper defeats her true
  accusation. (The card still says Saerys/Viserys — stale naming; the function is intact.)
- **What she does TO the plot:** the straight-man the antics escape; the surveillance routine that is
  the real Book I hazard (cross-referencing times, comparing windows — capable of catching Gael, has
  not yet). The lesson timetable she enforces (see `the-day`) is the alibi-frame for the missing
  hours.

### The maester — **(the Grand Maester / a household maester; canon-uncertain #1)**
- **Function:** physician, scholar, keeper of correspondence and records, tutor of letters/sums/
  history beyond what the septa covers. He tends the royal children's health, which means he *examines
  the odd one*. He keeps the cabinet of remedies and reagents, the library, the raven-correspondence,
  the harbor and trade records.
- **What he does TO the plot:** the I.2 **heist target** (his substances) and the recurring
  authority who is *alarmed/fascinated* by the poison-eater. In Book II he converts from target to
  resource — her tutor and the man whose signature legitimises letters (loophole: a front-man who
  signs). His correspondence and records are the raw material of the reagent-map and world-map (II.3).
  His room-inspections produce reports that never find what she's hidden (loc-maegors-holdfast).

### The masters of accomplishments
- **The dancing-master, the music-master, the master-at-letters** (and possibly a master of High
  Valyrian, though for Targaryens that is a *family tongue* learned at home, not a tutored subject).
  Each comes for a lesson-block on certain days. Lower-status than the septa, they are scheduled
  *through* her.
- **What they do TO the plot:** more scheduled hours, more watchers, more legitimate reasons to be in
  a particular room at a particular time — and more occasions to be "delicate" and excused.

### Companion girls / ladies — **(a Hightower cousin; a ward or two)**
- **Function (institution is canon; specific girls invent):** highborn girls of similar age placed in
  the princess's company — companions, playmates, and in time ladies-in-waiting. For a child this is
  *managed friendship*: they share lessons and leisure, they are also watchers, and they are being
  placement-trained themselves (cf. the ward/marriage custom). A Hightower kinswoman is plausible
  given Alicent's family at court (cf. Otto in the cast notes).
- **What they do TO the plot:** peers who must be managed, deceived, or kept at a distance; potential
  faction conduits (someone "gets to" a companion the way someone gets to the maid in II.5);
  contrast-figures who do girlhood *correctly* and find Gael strange.

## Ring 3 — the household officers & the guard

These run the establishment and the building. Gael interacts with them less intimately but they
control the *infrastructure* every operation rides on.

### The steward / comptroller of the household
- **Function:** runs the establishment's money and logistics — provisioning, wages, the household
  accounts, the supply deliveries. Senior, busy, not focused on one child.
- **What he does TO the plot:** the world of **paper** Gael learns to swim in. Stewardship is loophole
  6 — once she understands the household book better than anyone, no one can take it back (II/III).
  His weekly supply deliveries are the legitimate channel that brings her below-stairs contacts
  through the main gate (loc-maegors-holdfast exits).

### The head housekeeper / mistress of the household
- **Function:** runs the maids, the cleaning, the linen, the still-room *as a still-room* (preserves,
  cordials, simple remedies — the sanctioned domestic version of what Gael turns it into). Holds one
  of the four chamber master-keys (loc-maegors-holdfast fixed props).
- **What she does TO the plot:** she is who the sanctioned "collection" and still-room hobby are
  *cleared with*; her domain is the cover Gael's real still-room work hides inside (the household
  already does preserves and tinctures here, so reagent-work reads as more of the same).

### The mistress of the wardrobe / the tiring-women
- **Function:** clothes. Dressing a Valyrian princess is a production: the silver hair alone is a
  daily labour (see `the-day` §dress). They keep, mend, launder, and lay out; they handle the body.
- **What they do TO the plot:** the body is *handled, never private*. Excursions and the eventual
  tempering live in tension with a life in which other hands manage her flesh as a matter of routine.

### The guard — Kingsguard & the gate watch
- **Kingsguard:** the white cloaks guard the *family*, inside Maegor's. Their presence is the security
  weather; a Kingsguard posted in the courtyard at an odd hour is an interruption hook
  (loc-maegors-holdfast).
- **The gate / drawbridge watch:** two guards at the gatehouse at standard staffing; they log who
  crosses. **One is a beneficiary of below-stairs patronage** (loc-maegors-holdfast) — loophole 5
  made concrete. The Dragonkeepers (order of 77) guard the *dragons*, not the children — relevant
  only if Gael visits the Dragonpit.
- **What they do TO the plot:** the chokepoint accounting. Every official move is logged; every
  unofficial move rides the overlap-window or the patronaged guard.

## Ring 4 — the court orbit (the family)

Not servants, but they set the weather and the expectations. Detailed in `the-script`; named here for
the roster.

- **Jaehaerys I (King, father):** the *warm cage*. Indulges the miracle child; doting is
  transactional (dotes when she performs, withdraws when she doesn't — I.3). His verbal indulgence is
  the **King's-hand note**, the master key (loophole 1). Historically dies 103 AC — after Book I.
- **Alicent (Queen, mother):** saved the dying infant with the unsanctioned egg (I.1); love curdling
  toward horror as the child returns "wrong" (the Alicent interlude). The closest Ring-4 watcher —
  "almost never leaves her sight" in the infant phase; summons her to the solar for deportment review.
- **Helaena (younger sister):** the warm spot; the odd one who finds Gael *normal*. Spends the
  eccentric-royal budget on insects. The one Ring-4 person who is not a watcher.
- **The nephew Viserys; Daemon (the appraising relative, I.4–I.5); Aemond (martial foil, has a
  dragon); Otto (Hightower kin, ambient authority/intelligence):** the wider court — see
  `the-script` and the showrunner cast roster.

## The below-stairs economy (loophole 5, as people)

Servants have their own economy, parallel to the noble one. It runs on **patronage and protection**,
not coin. Favours, information, small mercies, looking the other way. A child who learns to *be owed*
— who protects a maid from dismissal, who gets a guard's brother a place, who keeps a secret kindly —
becomes a node in this economy. **She builds a faction, not a bribe list.**

Concrete strands of it, ready to draw on:
- The **wet-nurse's milk-kin family** — the first tie, bought by Alicent, inheritable by Gael.
- The **gate guard on patronage** — the man who doesn't note the inconsistency in his log.
- The **delivery-acceptance protocol** — the weekly supply run that legitimately brings her contacts
  through the main gate.
- The **maid's cousin** recruited into the startup (II.2) — the faction reproducing itself.
- The **overlap-window** between watch shifts — not a person, but the *seam between* people, which is
  where she actually lives.

The comedy and the pathos: this network of the powerless is the only place a caged princess has real
agency, and she runs it the same way she'll run a trade empire and steal a ship — with logistics,
ledgers, and accounting for who is watching.

## The seam: where the Book II maid comes from

II.1: *"A new maid is assigned to her household (reshuffling after the Daemon incident)."* The maid —
Gael's one channel to the outside, the co-protagonist of Books II–III — is **drawn out of this
roster.** This library does not name her (principal call; INDEX OQ-CL2) but it specifies the pool:

- **Most likely slot:** an **under-nursemaid / junior chamber-maid** (Ring 1) reassigned in the
  post-Daemon reshuffle — young, low-status, goes outside on errands, sleeps the truckle-bed watch.
  This makes the "bring me this herb / this sample / this book" opening (II.1) a natural extension of
  duties she already has, and the warmth a natural growth of proximity she already has.
- **Alternative slots:** a fresh outside hire (knows nothing of Gael's patterns — more friction,
  more to teach); or a **below-stairs faction recruit** (already owes Gael — starts loyal, less arc).
  See INDEX OQ-CL2.

Whoever she is, she begins as *furniture* (one of the watchers) and becomes the *one person* — which
is exactly the inversion the series turns on. The roster above is the furniture she is promoted out
of. The tragedy seed is already here: the household treats maids as interchangeable ("your maid can go
ahead to your new household — isn't that thoughtful?" — II.7), and the whole plot is Gael refusing
that interchangeability for exactly one person.
