# s01e01

I woke, and the three-hundred-metre sphere arrived before my eyes did. The count was already running on the flies in the thatch. I exhaled. I straightened my spine.

The exits had already been mapped. The father was the variable the morning bowl had been waiting on. He came in. The mother followed and set the bowl down between us.

He turned to me — to the body that had come back wrong. The watch-cost had just been priced. I reached for the salt. He pivoted toward the mother. I drew the salt back. He pivoted to me again, and his gaze rested on me at the angle Tya had used to hold the chin. I had not adjusted for that. The dogs came into the yard. I held the chin where Tya would have held it. The chin-hold was the body's argument, not mine. I let the body argue.

He stilled. His eyes held my hands and his face did not adjust. The stillness was the verdict, and the verdict was being held back from my face the way it would have been held back from any stranger he was still pricing.

The latch trembled, and the beetles took it a half-second before the door moved. Room-silence gave way to the door swinging open. I turned to it. He tilted his head. The mother faced the door. He went out into the yard.

I lowered the bowl. The breath left me before the bowl met the table. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it again. The log recorded the salt-reach and not what the salt-reach had cost the mother.

---

I crossed the yard. The flies fanned out along the perimeter at the count I had been holding since arrival. The beetles spread along the yard-wall. I walked the boundary. The wasps came back from the south wall and I walked it again. I exhaled. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

The mother came back into the room and set the afternoon bowl down. She opened her mouth and sang the first note. The yard-work ambient gave way to her singing — a song Tya had known. The spiders in the rafters did not know it. The second note. The third.

I held her eyes. The eye-hold was the only honest thing I had to offer her, honest by what it withheld.

The song stopped. Her hands stilled on the apron-front and her gaze held the girl's face. The mother-singing gave way to silence. The silence after the third note was the shape Tya should have filled.

She set the bowl down. She faced the wall. The wall was what she turned to when she had run out of daughter to look at. She left the room.

---

The father came over. His hands did not lift from the work as he spoke the task. He gave me the yard edge, routed the mother, routed the neighbour-boy. I went out to the yard edge and he crossed the yard. I lifted the feed bucket. He crossed the pit. I walked the perimeter. He routed the mother again, the neighbour-boy again.

I stopped. I had already counted the steps to the gate, the angle of the feed bucket, the cost of staying. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

The reeve came into the tanner-family yard. The exits re-ranked themselves; the back gate moved up the list. He spoke to the father. The father spoke to the reeve. I crossed the yard, and my shoulders set down and back. The watch-cost had just been priced to my name; the reeve's slowing had said it before his mouth did. The reeve slowed. He spoke to the father again, and the father spoke back. He left the yard.

---

The flies trailed the reeve out. The lord's man came into the village. His stride was parade-cadence; this was the village's category-event. The record book in his hand was a door already shut. He spoke to the reeve. The reeve spoke to him. The entry was permanent now; the parchment had just made me a line of ink in a record I would not be allowed to read. He opened the record book and wrote the entry. He closed it and left the village.

I opened my log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

The elder came in and spoke to the father. His tone was the tone he used for strangers he placed; the placement had begun. He spoke to me. The routing was the thing I had been waiting for and the thing I would have refused if refusal had been available. I turned to him. I spoke. I stopped — the stillness was the last second before the board changed.

He routed me. His eyes moved past me to the road before I lifted the pack. The gate was the last threshold that would cost nothing to cross; everything after was the city.

I lifted the travel pack. The mother faced the door. Her mouth parted on a word and the word did not come. Her face held the wood, and what she would not turn to watch had the shape she would not name. Her back was the registration; the door was what she gave her daughter who was not her daughter. The father held his feet where they were. He set his eyes on the work and did not lift them to the gate.

I crossed the yard gate. My gaze did not return to the door.

The elder walked the road. I walked the road.

---

I came into Flea Bottom. King's Landing arrived as a city I had already named, in a season I had not yet reached. The road-smell gave way to the Flea Bottom density-compound. The alley mouth ahead was the first navigable passage into the immediate block. The flies fanned out into it at the count I had expected from the tanner-village extrapolation. The count was higher. The beetles spread to the market-side junction. The wasps spread to the Fish Gate margin.

I walked into the alley, and the daylight gave way to canopy-dim. I came into the room — second-floor, the eastern-quarter base. Two exits, one window, a roofline I could reach the spiders to. I had cleared it before I set the pack down.

I set the pack down. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

I walked the perimeter. The beetles spread along the eastern-quarter approach. The approach gave me the upper room, and the upper room gave me a man who spoke to no one in particular. The flies relayed the doorframe. The spiders spread to the ceiling corners. I walked the perimeter again.

The maester spoke to the room. He was speaking to a room with no listener; I was the listener the room did not contain. The beetles relayed the sound. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

I walked the full perimeter. The flies relayed the Fish Gate margin. The beetles relayed the market-side junction. The wasps relayed the south-wall colony. The spiders relayed the room. The corners gave me the shelf, the corners gave me the page, the corners did not give me the words.

I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

The maester crossed the room. His hand reached the record-table before his gaze left the south wall. He spoke to the room — said a thing aloud that he should not have said aloud. The saying landed in a part of the log I would not annotate.

The beetles relayed the south-wall footfall. It was the warder's; the rhythm matched the patrol's third lap, and the patrol's third lap was not due. I straightened my spine.

The maester laughed. The laugh was not for the room; the laugh was for the thing the room contained. The beetles fell silent. The silence in the beetles was not the silence of the beetles; the silence was the shape of what the maester had just looked at. The network had dropped out, and the absence had the shape of an arrival I had felt come this way before.

I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

The Watch patrol crossed the Fish Gate margin. The cadence was the one that meant routine, and the cadence was two beats off. The flies relayed the Watch position. The relay confirmed what the cadence had already said: the patrol was moving on a count I had not seen before.

The dock-runner came into the margin. The flies relayed her. She pivoted — her eyes cut to the alley mouth before her feet moved. The pivot was not evasion of the Watch. The pivot was the runner pricing me, watching to see what I did with the price. She left the margin.

I held my feet. Moving would commit the wrong evidence, and the wrong evidence was anything. The flies relayed her again.

---

The elder spoke to the dock-runner. She came up to the market-side junction and spoke to him. He spoke to me — his shoulder turning from the exchange before my answer landed. He was placing me into the network the same way he had placed me into the labour web; the difference was the weight of the network.

I turned to face her. The Watch passage had cleared the margin thirty paces east. She spoke to me. I spoke back. My hand stayed still at the pack-strap. The word I gave her was the word I had given before to someone I could have burned and had not. The answer was the first irreversible thing I had said in this city. The city would route it before nightfall.

She left the junction.

I exhaled. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.
