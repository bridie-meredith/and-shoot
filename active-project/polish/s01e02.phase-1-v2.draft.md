*Through the first weeks in Flea Bottom I had walked my three hundred metres twice a day, mapped the market-side junction and the apothecary's upper room where the broken maester kept his hours, and made my first transactional pass with the dock-runner through the tanner-elder. The log was current. The tanner-village was a day's walk south and had stayed there.*

---

I faced the junction. The elder was already turned to the road, watching. My father crossed in from the market side, my mother a step behind him, and the elder greeted them both. I walked into the junction. The arrival entered. I pivoted toward it. My father went still. I faced him, shoulders square, weight even. He spoke to me. I answered. My father went still again. My mother went still. He raised the trade goods and addressed the elder; the elder answered him across the table. My mother turned to me. My father stepped back, his weight on his rear foot, gaze on the strap at his hip. My mother spoke to me. I answered her. She pivoted toward the road south, and my father with her. They walked out of the junction.

Back at the base room, I opened the log. Wrote it down, closed it.

After, I let the relays run. The flies had the tanner-elder. The wasps had the Watch patrol — tempo clean, a perimeter I hadn't walked. The wasps had the dock-runner too.

The daylight dropped as I stepped in. Empty room, alley-sound through the second-floor window. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

Out to the market-side junction. Wind off the road south, Fish Gate sound thin along the margin. The elder paused. He spoke to the carter. The wasps brought the pass: the interval held, the question closed. He turned and faced me square, working pitch, the placement-look set on his face. The carter rolled on out of the junction. I opened the log, wrote it down, closed it.

The flies spread the overnight network, the wasps the dock-side alleys, the beetles the south-wall colony, the spiders the eastern-quarter approach. I rolled the shoulders, the move clean and remembered. The flies relayed the junction return; the wasps held the Fish Gate — the southern dock-side gate of King's Landing, the city's busy fish-trade mouth — perimeter; the beetles relayed the south-wall return; the spiders the window. I exhaled. The flies spread the northern block, the wasps the eastern-quarter adjacent. I exhaled again. I walked the perimeter, wrote the entry, wrote it again, and exhaled.

I woke. I held the eyes still. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed the log.

The night alley was iron-latched and occupied when the lord's-man stepped into it, his stride loaded wrong for Eastern-Quarter ground. He spoke to the family at the door. His man set a shoulder to the latch — and the alley-murmur cracked. The bar gave. His man carried possessions out. The family exited the two-room dwelling. A neighbour pressed the doorway. The flies relayed the lord's-man's man. The flies were an ear pressed to a wall. The beetles relayed the lintel. The flies held the wall. The neighbour withdrew. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

The tanner and his wife came back into the junction. The ground between us was open. He turned to me and spoke. (What the Crownlands called the customary wage — the unwritten claim a family carried for the labour a returned child owed them, a tally without paper.) I turned to him and answered. He spoke again. I let the breath out. I opened the purse. I counted the coins once across my palm, then again, and held them out to him. He closed his hand on them without looking up at my face. He spoke to me once more. I turned to him. He left the junction; she left after him. I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed the log.

The flies spread out into the autumn-density network, the wasps along the dock-side relay, the spiders into the eastern quarter. I walked the perimeter. I wrote the entry. I wrote another. I flexed the hand. I woke. The stylus dropped. I held the eyes. I lowered the chin. I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed the log.

Afternoon held the side alley clear. The visitor entered the side alley, then the stairwell, then the upper room. He spoke to the maester. The maester spoke to him. The beetles brought the register through the wall. I held my feet still. He went out by the upper room, by the stairwell, by the side-alley door. I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed it on a name.

The beetles brought a rhythm. The beetles brought a phrase. The beetles brought a rhythm. I went still. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

Dusk cut the apothecary corner to a strip. The broken maester left the apothecary, took the side alley, crossed the upper-room threshold. The beetles brought the maester. I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed it.

My mother came in. The vigil candle — a Stranger-light kept burning for the dead until the family released them — was on the shelf where it had been. She faced me. She sat. I faced her. She spoke. I answered without raising my chin from the log. She spoke again. She looked down. She stood. Her thumb passed once, stained crease against the edge of the bench, and her weight came off it; the chair gave, the floor gave under her. She left the room.

I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed the log.
