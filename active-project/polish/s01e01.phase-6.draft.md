# s01e01 — Phase 6 draft (post Phases 1–6)

I woke. The sphere had already arrived before my eyes did, the count running on the flies in the thatch. I exhaled. I straightened my spine. My father entered the room — the exits already mapped. My mother followed. She set the morning bowl. My father faced me. I reached for the salt. He pivoted toward my mother. I drew the salt. He pivoted back toward me; the gaze rested on me at the angle Tya used to hold the chin, and I hadn't adjusted for that. The dogs entered the yard. I held my chin. My father stilled — his eyes on my hands, his face unmoved. The latch trembled, and the beetles caught it a half-second before the door swung open. I pivoted toward the door. My father tilted his head. My mother faced the door. He stepped out into the yard. I lowered the bowl; my breath left before it met the table. I opened the log. I wrote the entry — the log held the salt-reach, and what the salt-reach had cost my mother was what the log didn't hold. I closed the log.

---

I crossed the yard. The flies took the perimeter — the count I'd been holding since arrival. The beetles took the yard-wall. I walked the boundary. The wasps relayed the south-wall return. I walked the boundary again. I exhaled. Opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

The mother came in. She set down the afternoon bowl. She opened her mouth.

She sang the first note. A song Tya knew. The spiders in the rafters didn't.

She sang the second. She sang the third.

I held her eyes.

Her hands stilled on the apron-front and her gaze held my face — and the silence after the third note was the shape I should have filled.

I didn't fill it.

She set the bowl down again. She faced the wall. She left the room.

---

Father gave me the yard-edge task, hands not lifting from the work as he spoke. He routed the mother and the neighbour-boy. I stepped to the yard edge. He crossed the yard. I lifted the feed bucket. He crossed the pit. I crossed the perimeter. He routed the mother and the neighbour-boy again. I stilled. I had already counted: the steps to the gate, the angle of the bucket, the cost of staying. I opened the log and wrote it down.

---

The reeve came in through the yard gate. The exits re-ranked themselves; the back gate moved up the list. He said something low to the tanner-father, and the tanner-father said something low back. I crossed the yard with my shoulders set down and back. The reeve slowed. He said another low thing to the tanner-father, and the tanner-father answered. Then he went out the way he'd come.

---

The flies told me before he did. The lord's man walked in at a parade beat, the record book in his hand a door already shut. He stopped at the reeve. The reeve answered him, eyes down. He opened the book, wrote, closed it. I was a line of ink now, in a record I wouldn't be allowed to read. Then he was gone, back out the way he'd come.

I opened my own log and wrote the entry and closed it.

---

The elder's tone was the tone he used for strangers he placed. Then he turned it on me. I faced him. I asked where. He told me. I stilled.

His eyes went past me to the road before I lifted the pack. The gate was the last threshold that cost nothing to cross. Everything after was the city.

I lifted the pack.

The mother faced the door. Her mouth parted on a word and the word did not come; her face held the wood the way she had held other things she would not turn to watch. The father set his eyes on the work and did not lift them to the gate.

I crossed the yard gate. My gaze did not return to the door.

The elder walked the road. I walked the road.

---

King's Landing was a city I'd already named, in a season I hadn't yet reached. The alley mouth was the first navigable passage into the immediate block, and it smelled of leather, bone, ash, people. The flies filled the immediate block — higher than I'd extrapolated from the village. The beetles spread to the market-side junction. The wasps spread to the Fish Gate margin. I walked the alley; the canopy cut the light. The room had two exits, one window, and a roofline I could reach the spiders to — I'd cleared it before I set the pack down. I set the travel pack. I opened the log, wrote the entry, and closed it.

---

I walked the perimeter. The beetles spread along the eastern-quarter approach — and the approach gave me the upper room, and the upper room gave me a man talking to no one in particular. The flies relayed the doorframe. The spiders spread across the ceiling corners. I walked the perimeter. The maester spoke to the room. I was the listener the room didn't contain. The beetles relayed the sound. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

I walked the full perimeter. The flies took the Fish Gate margin, the beetles the market junction, the wasps the south wall. The spiders returned the room's ceiling-corners. The corners gave me the shelf. The corners gave me the page. The corners didn't give me the words. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

The maester crossed the room — his hand reaching the record-table before his gaze left the south wall. He said a thing aloud that he shouldn't have said aloud. The saying landed in a part of the log I wouldn't annotate.

The beetles relayed a footfall on the south wall. The warder's. The rhythm matched the patrol's third lap, and the third lap wasn't due.

I straightened my spine.

The maester laughed. The laugh wasn't for the room. It was for what the room contained.

The beetles dropped out. The silence wasn't theirs. It was the shape of what he'd just looked at. I'd felt an arrival come this way before.

I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

---

The Watch crossed the Fish Gate margin in routine cadence. The cadence was two beats off. The flies confirmed it. The patrol was on a count I hadn't seen before.

A runner came into the margin from the dock side. The flies marked her — quick, low, threading the stalls. Her eyes cut to the alley mouth before her feet moved, and she pivoted. The pivot wasn't for the Watch. She was reading me — watching what I did with it. She slid out past the gatepost on the dock line and the margin closed behind her.

I held my feet. Anything I did would be the wrong move. The flies stayed on her — across the cobbles, around the cart, down the alley she'd glanced at — until she was someone else's count.

---

The elder spoke to the runner. She came up to the market-side junction and answered him back. Then he turned to me. His shoulder turned from the exchange before my answer landed. He was placing me into the network the same way he'd placed me into the labour web. The weight was different.

I faced the runner. The Watch had cleared the margin thirty paces east.

She spoke.

I answered. My hand stayed on the pack-strap. The word I gave her was the word I'd given before, to someone I could have burned and hadn't. It was the first thing I'd said in this city that I couldn't take back. The city would route it before nightfall.

She left the junction.

I exhaled. I opened the log. I wrote it down. I closed it.
