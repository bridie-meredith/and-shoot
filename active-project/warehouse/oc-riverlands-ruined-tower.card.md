# Riverlands Ruined Tower

## Geography

A stone watchtower on a low hill in the middle Riverlands, between the Red Fork and the hills of the Bracken-Blackwood borderlands. Built perhaps a hundred and fifty years ago as a signal tower for a lord whose house no longer exists; abandoned when the lord's line died out in a minor war nobody now remembers the name of. Three stories still standing, the fourth collapsed inward. The hill itself is modest — perhaps sixty feet of elevation above the surrounding farmland — but enough to see a mile in every direction across flat ground and two miles toward the river.

Stone walls two feet thick at the base. A defensible structure by accident rather than design. The collapsed upper story creates a rubble pile inside that partially fills the ground level; it would take a week's work to clear it fully. The staircase, cut into the wall itself, is intact on three of four rotations to the second story landing.

No current claimant. The nearest lord (Bracken of Stone Hedge) considers it technically on his land but has not garrisoned it in thirty years. He would notice if someone else garrisoned it. He would probably send a man to ask about it before sending men to do something about it.

## Layout

- **Ground level:** Partially blocked by rubble from the collapsed fourth story. A fire ring from someone who camped here in the past several years. One narrow window, arrow-slit width. The original door is gone; the doorway faces east.
- **Second story landing:** A full floor still intact, accessible by wall-stairs. One full window opening (the shutter long since rotted away) facing west — broad enough for a small figure to pass through. The floor is stone; sound travels.
- **Third story remnant:** Half a floor. The half facing south is gone; the half facing north holds. There is open sky above and the edge of the ruined wall above it, useful as a perch but not a room.
- **The hill approach:** Open ground on the east and south sides of the hill; a stand of old oak on the north side that provides concealment from that direction.

## Sensory Vocabulary

- **Sound:** Wind at height — the tower amplifies it. The sound of the surrounding farmland (animals, carts on the track below, a river in the far distance) arrives clearly on still days. Rain on the stone produces a specific reverberant sound.
- **Smell:** Old stone. Damp. Woodsmoke if the fire ring is in use. The specific smell of aged mortar and the moss that has taken hold on the north face.
- **Light:** The west-facing window on the second story turns gold in the late afternoon. Otherwise: dim interior on ground level, brighter on the upper landing. From the top: full light, full exposure.
- **Temperature:** The stone holds cold. Warmer than outside in summer; colder than outside in winter. The second story has no wind protection once the window shutter is gone; it is an observation point, not a sleeping chamber, in cold weather.

## Fixed Props

None currently. The original fixtures (door, shutters, torch brackets) are long gone or rotted.

## Exits

- **East:** the primary approach; down the hill to the farm track below; ten minutes to the nearest village.
- **North:** through the oak stand, then west across open country toward Bracken Stone Hedge (half a day's ride).
- **South:** across open farmland; visible from the road; exposed.
- **West:** the slow slope down to the Red Fork tributaries; a mile and a half; the ground gets soft before the river proper.

## Hazards

- The rubble on the ground level is unstable in one section; a heavy weight in the wrong spot will shift it.
- The third-story remnant has one load-bearing stone that has been compromised by frost heave; a careful observer with construction knowledge might notice. Most people would not.
- Visibility is a hazard as well as an asset: anyone on the hill can see a long way; anyone in the farmland below can see the tower.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A trader's cart on the farm track below, audible before visible
- A hunting party from Stone Hedge, moving through the oak stand on the north
- Birds flushed from the oak stand (signal that something is moving in the trees)
- A fire in the distance (the Riverlands burn with some regularity; a plume of smoke on the horizon is not unusual and not necessarily immediate)
- The tower itself, on a specific type of structural stress: a deep vibration rather than a crack — the kind of thing that suggests the third-story remnant is about to have a problem
