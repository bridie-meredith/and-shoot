# King's Landing — Burning Street, First Days of the Dance (Closing-Image Site)

## Geography

A specific street in the middle tier of King's Landing, between the eastern quarter's lower boundary and the Fish Gate approach: the part of the city that is neither the poverty of Flea Bottom below nor the maintained order of the noble quarter above. The street runs east-west, a wide enough lane for cart traffic in normal times, flanked by the ground floors of merchant housing and service premises — the kind of street that had a normal function the day before, and no longer has it.

This card fixes the closing-image geography: this is where Lyra (the cost-bearer, the collateral cousin) dies, in the first days of the Dance's KL phase, in the ambient chaos of a city that has become a contested space. The chronicler's coda arrives first — a register-break that does not need a described physical location — and then the closing image grounds in this street, at this time of day, with this specific sky.

**Specific coordinates (project-binding):**
- **Which street:** The north-south lateral that connects the eastern quarter's through-road to the Fish Gate approach — the street Taylor has used on every transit between Flea Bottom and the Dragonpit approach. She knows this street at the level of habitual familiarity. Every irregularity in it reads against her baseline.
- **Which slope:** The slight downhill from the eastern quarter toward the Fish Gate — the direction is east-southeast, descending, so the sky visible above the rooftops to the northwest is the Dragonpit's hill and the sky above it.
- **Time of day:** Late afternoon, the light angled and red-tinted from the west. The street is in partial shadow from the buildings on its western face, the upper stories still in full afternoon light, the ground level already in the shadow of the buildings' own heights.

**The wrong dragon overhead:** Silverwing, with Ulf the White as rider (claimed after Vaegon's death left the bonding-window open and the worst available claimant moved into it). Silverwing is visible from this street as a silhouette against the late-afternoon sky, above the Dragonpit's hill to the northwest — not in the street, not overhead in the claustrophobic sense, but in the sky above the city in the direction of the hill. The silhouette is visible to anyone who looks up from this street toward the northwest. This is what Taylor has been trying to prevent: a powerful dragon with an unreliable rider, already airborne, already beginning the trajectory whose historical conclusion she does not need to know because the shape of it is Gold-Morning familiar.

The closing image is not Taylor looking up. Taylor is not the point-of-view of the closing image; the closing image belongs to Lyra's death, rendered cold at street level. Silverwing is overhead as a visible fact of the scene's geography, not as Taylor's narrative object.

## Layout

**The street in normal operation (reference baseline):** Merchant housing on both sides, ground floors in commercial use (food preparation, small goods, a tinsmith whose hammer is a daily street-sound). Midway down the street, a wider section where a well serves the block — the standard KL street-well that functions as a social node. Carts move through this street regularly; foot traffic is mixed-status (this is the transit route for anyone moving between the eastern quarter and the Fish Gate). The street's normal function is legible from its furniture.

**The street in the first days of the Dance (scene state):** The commercial ground floors are closed and shuttered — the shutters on the merchant housing are not the light daytime-ventilation position but the locked-closed security position. Several shutters have been broken and relocked from outside; at least one shutter is missing entirely, the window opening dark. The tinsmith's hammer is not audible. The well area is empty of the usual social cluster; the well's bucket is down (someone drew water and did not raise the bucket back).

Fire damage: one building on the western face, two doors down from the well, has a fire-mark on its upper story — the char and smoke staining of a fire that started, was contained at that level, and did not spread. The damage is recent: the char is not weathered. The street does not smell of active fire; it smells of old fire, the specific compound of burnt timber and stone that the previous day left behind.

**The chaos layer:** This is not a battlefield. It is a city street in the first days of internal conflict — which means it is mostly empty, occasionally not empty, and the non-emptiness is either authoritative (City Watch in tense patrol, or something worse: riders in factional livery moving fast) or vulnerable (people trying to get somewhere else, carrying what they could carry). The closing image does not need a crowd for Lyra's death; it needs the specific exposure of a person who should not be on this street alone, who is on this street because her protector is dead and there is no one left to tell her which streets are wrong today.

**The closing-image blocking:** Lyra is at the well area, in the wider section of the street. She is moving toward the Fish Gate — toward the exit, the harbor road, away from the city's center of conflict. She does not make it. The specific mechanism of her death is not architectural; it is the absence of Vaegon's protection in a city where that protection was the only thing standing between her and the ambient violence. The street provides the physical ground; it does not provide the meaning. The meaning arrives from the chronicler's coda, which lands before this image.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Old fire, the dominant note — absorbed into the street's ambient from the burned upper story, present everywhere in the air. Underneath this, an absence: the food-preparation smells from the commercial ground floors that are normally present in this street are gone with the shuttered buildings. The smell-environment of a street that has had its normal function suspended is a subtraction more than an addition: what is not there registers first. The well-water smell, faint, mineral.

**Sound:** The closing image's acoustic register is partial silence. Not complete silence — KL is a city, and even in the Dance's first days the city's physical plant produces sound: birds, the creak of stressed timber in the building with the fire-mark, the distant and indeterminate sound of conflict somewhere else (not in this street, not yet). What is absent is the street's normal operational sound: the tinsmith's hammer, the market-level voices, the cart-wheel rhythm. The absence is audible in the way that a stopped clock is audible.

Silverwing in flight overhead produces a shadow before the sound reaches the street level — the wing-beat is low-frequency, felt in the chest before it is identified as sound. This arrives approximately three seconds after the silhouette crosses above the rooftop line.

**Visual:** Late-afternoon red-tinted light on the upper stories of the western-face buildings; shadow at ground level. The shuttered street. The broken shutter with the dark window. The fire-mark char on the upper story. The empty well area. Lyra at the well. Silverwing as a silhouette against the late sky to the northwest — a shape that Taylor had a glimpse of, that the chronicle will name, that the reader knows as the wrong dragon in the wrong hands over the city they are watching begin to burn.

**Temperature:** Autumn afternoon, slightly cool. The shade in the street is meaningfully cooler than the sunlit upper stories. The thermal differential that Taylor has learned to map as Dragonpit-proximity marker is present in the sky-direction of Silverwing's approach, but Taylor is not in this image.

## Fixed Props

- Street well (architectural, permanent) — well-head, stone surround, bucket on rope, currently drawn-down
- Building with fire-mark (architectural damage, west face) — upper-story char, structurally intact

## Exits

- **Fish Gate (east-southeast, continuing direction):** The direction Lyra is moving toward. The Fish Gate is approximately twelve minutes' walk at the end of this street and two turns south. It is the harbor exit — the departure point. She does not reach it.
- **Eastern quarter (northwest, street's origin):** The way this street comes from. The direction of Vaegon's household, which Lyra has already left.
- **Cross-alleys (two, mid-street):** Side alleys that connect to parallel streets. Normal use is for residents' shortcuts. Both are currently closed at their far ends by a barricade of household goods — the specific kind of ad-hoc block that residents erected early in the first day of chaos and have not moved.

## Hazards

**Factional riders:** The Dance's first days in KL are characterized by mounted movement of factional forces through the city streets on errands that are not military in scale but are dangerous in outcome for anyone who is wrong-place at the wrong moment. The street's fish-gate approach function makes it a transit route; it will see factional movement before the fighting concentrates elsewhere.

**Fire spread risk:** The building with the fire-mark is structurally sound, but the adjacent buildings are timber-frame upper stories. If the fire-mark building's internal damage is more significant than the external staining suggests, or if a new fire event occurs on the street while the structures are already stressed by the first fire's heat damage, spread is possible. This hazard is available for escalation; it is not the mechanism of Lyra's death.

**Isolation:** The street is empty enough that a person in distress here has no one to call. The shuttered buildings are occupied — people are inside, not absent — but they have made the decision to not be present in this street, and that decision will not be reversed for one person at the well.

**The wrong dragon as ambient threat:** Silverwing overhead is not directly targeting this street. The closing-image function of the silhouette is confirmatory, not active: Taylor's intervention produced this configuration, and the configuration is now visible over the city. The dragon is the consequence made visible, not the agent of Lyra's death.
