# Harrenhal — Exterior and Approach

## Geography

Harrenhal is the largest castle in Westeros — a distinction that carries no comfort. It sits on the northern shore of the Gods Eye lake in the western Riverlands, approximately a half league north of the small sept. Its scale is not fortress-scale; it is wrong-scale. The towers are too tall. The walls are too thick. The gates are too wide. Everything about it is sized for a kingdom that does not exist anymore, and the result is a structure that cannot be fully garrisoned, cannot be fully maintained, and cannot be fully occupied by any living house.

The outer walls: thirty feet high and twelve feet thick, running perhaps half a mile in their full circuit. The walls have been repaired in places and allowed to crumble in others; the pattern of repair and neglect is the record of every house that has held the castle and then lost it. From the south — the direction of the sept — the walls read as a single dark mass, irregular at the top where crenellations have fallen and not been replaced.

The towers: five of them, named for elements by the Harren who built them. Three are fully standing; one is a stump, the upper stories collapsed inward during the burning; one is a hollow shell, its interior open to the sky, the outer stone somehow still upright. The central tower rises above the others by perhaps thirty feet. The fires that Aegon's dragons brought to Harrenhal were hot enough to run the stone like wax; you can see the streaks on the tower faces if you know to look for them. Most local smallfolk do not need to look. They know.

The Gods Eye lake is visible to the south and east from any elevation near the castle. At dawn and dusk, the lake's surface catches the light in a way that makes the castle appear to float above its reflection.

## Layout from Exterior

- **The main gate:** South-facing, sized for two wagons abreast. The gate itself is iron-banded oak; a postern gate cut into one door is in current use. The gatehouse is manned by two guards at minimum; more when the castellan expects arrivals.
- **The approach road:** The road from the south (from the sept direction) is straight and clear for half a league before the gates. There is no concealment between the road and the gate; arriving parties are visible to the wall-walk well before they arrive.
- **The outer ward:** Visible through the gate when it is open. Extensive — the outer ward alone is large enough to house a small army. Currently underlabored and partially used as a livestock pen by the garrison.
- **The wall-walk:** Visible from outside as silhouettes against the sky when guards are present. From the south, the wall-walk gives the garrison a clear view of the approach road and the fields to the south, including the sept's bell tower.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Scale and distance:** Harrenhal looks closer than it is. The scale is wrong in both directions — from a distance, the towers seem higher than they should be for their apparent distance; up close, the walls press down in a way that makes human scale feel provisional. Visitors who have not seen it before often stop on the road before they mean to.

**Sound:** Wind channeled through the gates produces a low-register moan audible for several hundred feet. The castle is not quiet; the garrison's activity (horses, men, the sounds of maintenance) carries over the walls in a general ambient noise without any individual sound being clear. Ravens — Harrenhal always has ravens; more than any castle its size should need.

**Smell:** From outside: wood smoke from the garrison's fires, the smell of horses, the specific stone-cold smell of very old masonry. When the wind is from the lake: water and reeds. The organic smell of the underlabored outer ward.

**Light:** The castle's stone is grey-black. It does not catch light the way limestone does; it absorbs it. At midday in summer, the walls look darker than they should. At dusk, the castle disappears into the shadow before the surrounding countryside does. The lights in the windows at night are few and look far away even when you know the distance.

**The melted stone:** The five towers show the dragon-fire's work. The stone above the old burning line has a different texture — less course, more continuous, the mortar and rock run together into something that looks almost organic. This is visible at the tower bases, closer to the ground, where the stone was hot enough to move but not hot enough to collapse entirely. Local smallfolk call it "the scar" without elaborating on which scar they mean.

## Fixed Props

- The iron-bound oak gates (the postern currently the active entrance)
- The gatehouse with its two-guard minimum
- The wall-walk silhouette
- The tower with the collapsed upper story (the stump, southeastern position)
- The hollow tower (northwestern, stands but interior open to sky)

## Exits (from exterior)

- **South:** the approach road to the sept environs and the surrounding farmland; half a league to the sept; the only developed road in this direction.
- **East:** a track along the northern shore of the Gods Eye; connects to the main Riverlands road network; longer route but avoids the direct Harrenhal road.
- **West:** open country toward the foothills; no road; used by people who do not want to be on a road.
- **Through the gates:** entry to the castle proper. Not a direction available without the garrison's consent.

## Hazards

- **The history.** Every house that has held Harrenhal has lost it, been destroyed by it, or been consumed by it. This is common knowledge among Riverlands smallfolk and is not superstition — the record is factual. The ambient dread this produces is not irrational.
- **Wall-walk sightlines.** The garrison can observe the approach road and surrounding fields from the wall-walk. Anyone moving through the area south of the castle is potentially visible during daylight.
- **The castellan's authority.** Lord Corwyn Hatch holds the castle. His impressment census extends to all unattached smallfolk within Harrenhal's traditional land claims. The castle is the administrative center of his authority.
- **Raven traffic.** Harrenhal sends and receives ravens. Any unusual information about the surrounding area — an unusual child, a septon's ward whose protection has lapsed — can travel by raven to the Hightower faction's network quickly.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A garrison patrol leaving through the postern gate, heading south — the census team, or enforcement of a census decision
- Ravens launching from the castle towers in numbers — this means ravens in the air over the fields; potentially visible as unusual patterns to a fauna-aware observer
- Smoke rising from the central tower when it had not been smoking before — the garrison is active in a way that suggests a new operation or arrival
- The sound of the gate mechanism (heavy, audible at distance on still days) indicating the main gates are opening — a large arrival or departure
- The castle at night showing more lights than usual in the upper windows — the castellan is working late or has visitors

## Interaction Notes

**As ambient antagonist:** Harrenhal functions as a constant presence rather than an active scene location in early episodes. It is visible from the sept. It is the source of authority that threatens Taylor. It is the direction the census men come from and return to. Characters do not go inside Harrenhal in episodes 1-2; the castle presses on the scene from outside.

**Scale as psychological pressure:** Any scene that references Harrenhal should register its scale. It is not a castle Taylor can navigate around; it is too large. It is not a threat she can locate in a specific person; it is too institutional. The scale is specifically designed to produce the feeling that individual competence is insufficient.

**The fire history as ambient load:** The melted stone, the scar, the record of burning are ambient knowledge for every Riverlands character in the scene. They do not need to be stated; they are present in the way people do not look at certain parts of the castle walls when they can help it.
