# KL Feudal Physics Mirror — King's Landing Capital-Specific Political Authority (Pre-Dance, ~125 AC)

## Description

The King's Landing variant of Westerosi feudal physics. Covers the City Watch's jurisdiction and its relationship to Red Keep authority, the Red Keep's legal authority radius over the city (who can override whom, what the chain looks like), noble-quarter access constraints for non-escorted smallfolk, and the specific way Flea Bottom operates as a semi-autonomous zone at the bottom of the city's enforcement attention.

**This card is the KL-specific layer on top of `cond-feudal-hierarchy-law` and `cond-smallfolk-political-physics`.** The base cards remain authoritative for the underlying legal structure and the internalized smallfolk state. This card governs the capital-specific institutional configuration that makes King's Landing different from a Riverlands village: more institutional layers, more jurisdictional overlap, the specific anomaly of Flea Bottom, and the specific access constraints that govern Taylor's movement through the city.

## Sensory Impact

KL feudal physics manifest differently from rural feudal physics. In a village, the lord's man-at-arms is a rare and significant presence. In King's Landing, institutional authority is ambient: the City Watch's gold cloaks are omnipresent in the noble quarter and the market districts, visible at every gate, stationed at Dragonpit approaches. The Watch is not a rarity; it is wallpaper. What is rare is when the Watch actually engages with you — and that rarity is precisely what Flea Bottom residents cultivate.

Taylor experiences King's Landing's feudal physics as layered noise — multiple institutional authorities competing for signal, the Red Keep's gravitational pull felt in every street-level power interaction, and a specific sensory cue: proximity to the Dragonpit registers as a zone where Watch presence is tighter and the authority gradient shifts.

## Duration

Persistent throughout the project. The institutional configuration described here is stable in the pre-Dance period and does not shift during the story's time scope.

## City Watch Jurisdiction

The City Watch (the Gold Cloaks) holds primary jurisdiction over order within King's Landing's walls. Its mandate:

**What the Watch can do:**
- Detain anyone acting in breach of the peace, on the Watch commander's authority
- Conduct sweeps of any city district (including Flea Bottom) when ordered by the commander or when royal authority directs
- Enforce market regulations (weights, measures, unlicensed trade)
- Remove unauthorized persons from noble-quarter approaches
- Transport persons of interest to the Watch's own holding facilities or to the Red Keep on higher authority's request

**What the Watch does not do, in practice:**
- Continuous surveillance of Flea Bottom — Watch presence there is periodic and targeted, not embedded
- Individual investigation of smallfolk disputes — below the Watch's institutional threshold unless violence reaches a level that threatens order outside the district
- Proactive intelligence-gathering in Flea Bottom on ordinary days — Watch attention to Flea Bottom is triggered by events (a body, a fire, a crowd, a complaint from a higher-status resident of an adjacent district) rather than scheduled patrol cadence

**The Watch's institutional relationship with the Red Keep:** The Watch commander holds appointment from the Iron Throne and serves at royal pleasure. In the pre-Dance factional period, the Watch commander's loyalty is politically inflected — which faction's candidate holds the command determines which political direction Watch intelligence flows. This is part of the political weather Taylor reads (Watch deployments as faction-activity signal).

## Red Keep Authority Radius

The Red Keep's authority over King's Landing is not mediated through the Watch alone. The Hand of the King maintains a separate administrative apparatus (the king's own men-at-arms for Red Keep security; the Hand's administrative staff for governance functions). The hierarchy in the city:

1. Iron Throne / King's direct word (absolute; rarely expressed directly in street-level matters)
2. Hand of the King (administrative authority over the city; can override Watch; can issue city-wide directives)
3. City Watch Commander (operational authority over Watch; jurisdiction over order and peace)
4. Watch officers (district-level enforcement; the Watch lieutenant whose territory includes a given street)

For Taylor's practical purposes: the Red Keep's authority radius means that anything that reaches the Hand's notice — or the notice of the king's court — shifts from Watch jurisdiction to a qualitatively different institutional category. A problem that stays in Watch hands is a policing problem. A problem that reaches the Hand's administrative staff becomes a political problem. This distinction is load-bearing for Taylor's situation: her patron's umbrella keeps her in the policing-problem category for as long as it holds.

**The Hightower network:** In the pre-Dance period, Otto Hightower (Hand or former Hand, depending on timing) and the Green faction maintain intelligence channels through the administrative apparatus. Watch deployments and city-level threat assessments are influenced by this network. Taylor's witch-label, as it travels up the social hierarchy, will eventually touch this network — which is what makes her visible as a court-level threat rather than a street-level curiosity.

## Noble Quarter Access Constraints

The noble quarter (the streets immediately below and approaching the Red Keep, the tourney grounds, the high market districts where noble households maintain townhouses) operates under a different Watch deployment density than Flea Bottom. Access constraints for non-escorted smallfolk:

**What requires escort or invitation:**
- Entry into the noble quarter during any period of heightened Watch presence (faction tension days, court days, the king's public appearances)
- Approach to any noble household's premises without a recognized factor or retainer as intermediary
- Access to the Dragonpit exterior approach — the Watch maintains a checkpoint at the Dragonpit road during ordinary operations; non-escorted persons approaching the Dragonpit are stopped and questioned

**What is not gated:**
- Movement through the city's commercial districts (the Street of Steel, the harbor approaches, the common market areas) is relatively unconstrained for persons who appear to have legitimate business
- Flea Bottom is not gated at its entry points — Watch will enter but does not maintain checkpoints at the district edge under ordinary conditions

**The escort mechanism:** Noble household factors (agents managing a house's practical affairs in the city) are the practical intermediary for smallfolk who need access to higher-status spaces. A factor vouching for a person is a sufficient credential for Watch checkpoint passage in most cases. The patron reaching down to Taylor does so through this mechanism — a retainer or factor arranges the access, not a formal invitation.

## Flea Bottom as Semi-Autonomous Zone

Flea Bottom is not ungoverned — it is under-governed relative to the rest of the city, and the under-governance is largely tolerated by the institutional apparatus because it is useful. The Watch's relationship to Flea Bottom:

**Why the Watch tolerates semi-autonomy:**
- Full surveillance of Flea Bottom would require resources the Watch does not have and would produce more arrests than the Watch's facilities can process
- The district's informal economy (rendering yards, fishguts processing, unlicensed small trade) produces goods that the city depends on; disrupting the district's functioning would harm the city's supply chains
- The Watch's intelligence about Flea Bottom comes primarily from informants within the district; embedded patrol would disrupt the informant network without providing better coverage

**What the Watch is looking for in Flea Bottom:**
- Major violence (bodies, fires, organized fighting between gangs or factions)
- Unlicensed weapons-carrying above the small-blade threshold
- Persons known to be wanted by higher authority (specific fugitives, not general sweeps)
- Organized resistance to city authority (this is rare but would trigger a response)

**What the Watch ignores in Flea Bottom:**
- Small-scale unlicensed trade and commerce
- Minor disputes and violence below the level that produces Watch-incident reports
- The district's informal social organizations (labor brokers, the whisper networks, small-scale mutual aid)
- Individual strangers who appear to be operating within the district's informal economy

**Taylor's position in this framework:** Taylor arrived weeks ago with nothing and is operating within Flea Bottom's informal economy. She is not on the Watch's active-interest list. Her witch-reputation is circulating within the district but has not yet produced an event that would trigger Watch attention. The semi-autonomous zone is her operating environment — she is visible to Flea Bottom's own social networks but functionally invisible to institutional surveillance as long as she avoids Watch triggers.

**The zone's limits:** Flea Bottom's semi-autonomy ends when the Hightower intelligence network or the Watch commander's political priorities direct attention there. If Taylor's patron's faction loses ground, if the witch-label generates a formal complaint, or if Taylor is present at a Watch-trigger event, the semi-autonomous zone provides no protection. It is tolerance, not immunity.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-feudal-hierarchy-law`:** That card governs the underlying legal structure (rights, obligations, the lord's authorities). This card governs the specific institutional configuration in King's Landing — the Watch as the operative enforcement layer, the Red Keep's authority gradient, and the way the capital's institutional complexity differs from rural feudal enforcement.

**With `cond-smallfolk-political-physics`:** That card describes the internalized state. This card describes the specific environmental constraints Taylor's internalized-state neighbors are navigating. Both must be loaded for scenes in Flea Bottom involving institutional contact.

**With `cond-kl-witch-label-formation`:** The label's danger-valence is partly a function of this card — how far up the authority hierarchy it travels determines what kind of threat it becomes. A label that stays in Flea Bottom is the Watch's noise floor. A label that reaches the Hightower network is a political asset or threat.

**With `cond-smallfolk-court-access-mirror`:** That card governs the specific access chain from Taylor's position to the spaces she needs to operate in. This card is the institutional physics that chain must navigate.

**For auditor use:** Any scene where Taylor moves through noble-quarter approaches without escort or the patron's umbrella is a violation. Any scene where the Watch maintains continuous Flea Bottom surveillance is a violation. Any scene where Taylor's witch-label generates an automatic Watch response (without escalation trigger) is a violation.
