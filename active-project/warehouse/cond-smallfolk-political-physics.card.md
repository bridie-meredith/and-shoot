# Smallfolk Political Physics — Subjugation as Internalized State

## Description

The internalized political physics of Westerosi smallfolk subjugation. This card describes how smallfolk understand and reproduce their own subordination — not as externally imposed oppression they resent and resist, but as the water they swim in. It is the pre-existing substrate Taylor's block-network work operates with and against.

**Note for dragon-gate-foreclosure:** The era-specific Fairstead and Riverlands references in the source card are not operative here. The underlying mechanics — learned invisibility, posture of non-threat, informal whisper networks, cost of visibility — transfer without modification to the KL Dragon Gate context. Dragon Gate block smallfolk are gate-district (mixed-class, traveler-transit, guild-adjacent) rather than Riverlands rural, but the political physics operate on the same substrate.

## Sensory Impact

The political physics are felt in the body and in the rhythm of daily existence: the specific quality of silence when Watch officers enter the market alley, the posture adjustment that happens without deciding to make it, the way a group at the well disperses when something official begins nearby.

## Duration

Persistent. The ambient substrate of King's Landing smallfolk life throughout the project.

## Learned Invisibility

The primary survival technology of Westerosi smallfolk is not resistance but invisibility. A smallfolk who is not noticed by power is a smallfolk who will not be extracted from by power.

**In Dragon Gate context:** The mixed-class gate district creates specific learned-invisibility dynamics. Gate-district smallfolk must be visible enough to do commercial business (visible to customers, visible to guild inspectors, visible enough to justify their presence in a transit zone) while remaining invisible to the Watch's operational attention. The balance point is: visible as function, invisible as problem.

**Taylor's problem:** She cannot be invisible. She is the witch-labeled foreign woman the captain lets alone. Her visibility is permanent, labeled, and institutionally managed. The question for the series is not whether she is visible but what kind of visible, and whether the visibility can be shaped.

## Posture of Non-Threat

The active performance of harmlessness. See source card for full content. In Dragon Gate context: Taylor performs foreignness and strangeness accurately (both are true) and relies on the Watch management note to keep the witch-labeled visibility from activating institutional action. The performance is sustainable as long as the management note holds. When it revokes, the performance's underlying truth (she is foreign, she is strange, she is persons-risk) is what the category acts on.

## Informal Credit and Whisper Networks

Source card content applies without modification to Dragon Gate block context. The whisper network is the mechanism through which the witch-label traveled from the septon's arrival shout to the block's ambient social knowledge overnight. The same network is the channel through which Taylor's patron-calibrated reports occasionally reach block residents before they reach the Watch.

## Cost of Being Visible to Power

Source card axiom applies: being seen by power is dangerous. Taylor is visible to power from day one. The cost is not theoretical; it is the permanent operational condition of her identity in the block.

## Taylor's Organizing Against This Substrate

Source card content applies: the substrate offers existing whisper networks, informal credit economy, community compliance habits. The substrate costs her: she must earn community trust on community terms, the community's primary commitment is its own survival, and the learned-invisibility habit makes the community resistant to the visibility that any effective organizing requires.

**Dragon Gate specific:** Taylor's block-network is not the Fairstead organizing model (building against a lord's administrative apparatus). It is the patron-dialect model: building information-exchange relationships with institutional nodes (Watch, guild, steward). The substrate the patron-dialect model operates on is the same smallfolk social physics, but the organizing target is different — not community self-advocacy but Taylor's own intelligence-gathering and block-safety work.

## Interaction Notes
