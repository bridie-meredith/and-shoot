# Impressment Census — 120 AC, Hightower-Affiliated Castellan at Harrenhal

## Description

The impressment census procedure as practiced by Lord Corwyn Hatch, the newly installed Hightower-affiliated castellan of Harrenhal, in the year 120 AC. This card defines the legal and procedural framework of the census, what counts as protected status, what triggers impressment, and what the process looks like on the ground.

## Sensory Impact

Census day in a smallfolk settlement: a small party of men arriving from the direction of Harrenhal — typically the castellan's steward or deputy, a maester (for cognitive assessment), two to three armed men for enforcement. The party is not hostile on approach; it is procedural. The table is set up in the common or the headman's house. The census scroll is opened. The process begins. The tone is administrative until it stops being administrative.

The visual marker of census activity: the scroll, the writing materials, the seated steward or deputy, the armed men standing at the periphery not touching anything but very visible. The tone is one of the authority that does not need to assert itself because the assertion already happened when the castle changed hands.

## Duration

Temporary in scope (the census is a finite operation), but its consequences are persistent. The census produces records; the impressment notices create obligations that continue beyond the census day. As an active condition on the board: high for episodes 1-2; declining but not absent through the early series.

## The "Unattached" Definition

Impressment applies to persons classified as unattached. Unattached means:

- **No sworn lord:** the person is not a member of a household sworn to a named lord. Being a smallfolk farmer on Harrenhal's traditional land counts as unsworn unless the farming is under formal indenture to a specific lord.
- **No active household indenture:** formal labor contracts to a specific household provide protection if current and documented. Expired indentures do not.
- **No recognized guild or craft membership:** guildsmen are attached to their guild. This requires active membership with verifiable record.
- **No recognized sept or religious house membership:** septon wards are attached. This is Taylor's current protection and its current vulnerability: the septon's dying status creates a question about whether his ward-protection remains valid.
- **No proven blood claim on productive land:** if a person can prove they hold land by blood claim (inheritance), they are treated as attached to the land and therefore to whatever lord holds the land's lordship. This is not available to Taylor.

**The "septon ward" edge case:** A dying septon is still a septon. His ward-protection is technically valid until he is dead and the ward-protection claim is formally challenged. However, a castellan's officer who notices the dying septon and the unverified ward will flag the case and seek a ruling. The borderline status is the specific threat.

## The Impressment Process

1. **Census visit:** The castellan's party arrives. Household-by-household or cluster-by-cluster assessment. The steward records names, ages, occupations, status claims. The maester (if traveling with the party) conducts cognitive assessment of candidates flagged as potentially useful for household service rather than field labor.

2. **Status determination:** At census close, each person is classified: attached (protected) or unattached (subject to impressment). Edge cases are flagged for further review; pending review, the person is held in place — they may not leave the area without authorization.

3. **Impressment notice:** Unattached persons assessed as viable labor receive an impressment notice. Standard terms: report to Harrenhal's outer ward within three days. Bring tools of your trade if any. Bring no more than can be carried in one pack. Labor assignment will be made at Harrenhal.

4. **Labor terms:** Impressment is not slavery. Legal framework: 2-5 years of compelled labor service. Assignment categories: castle maintenance (the most common; Harrenhal always needs repair labor), field labor (for the castle's home farms), garrison support (cooking, laundry, logistical work), household service (for persons assessed as literate or cognitively capable).

5. **Resistance:** Refusing an impressment notice is theft of labor under the legal framework Lord Hatch operates within. Consequences: the armed men are invoked. The person is taken directly rather than given the three-day window. In practice, this means arrest and immediate escort to the castle.

## What the Maester Specifically Does

The traveling maester accompanying the census has a secondary function beyond basic cognitive assessment. He is specifically looking for persons whose capabilities exceed what the field-labor or castle-maintenance classification can use. Literacy. Mathematical ability. Any demonstrated skill in accounts, medicine, or scholarly work. These persons are flagged for household service rather than physical labor — and their impressment notices go to a different administrative track.

A child who is demonstrably literate beyond what the sept education explains is a specific flag trigger. Such a child gets a separate assessment, a separate record, and a notation that goes directly to the castellan rather than the general census roll.

## Compliance and Resistance Options for Smallfolk

Direct refusal is not viable — it triggers the armed men immediately. Available options for smallfolk:

- **Documentation of protected status:** produce evidence of septon ward status, guild membership, or indenture. Must be current and verifiable on sight.
- **Appeal to a competing authority:** invoke a knight's protection, a septon's claim, or another lord's claim over the same land. This creates a procedural dispute that delays the process while the dispute is adjudicated. Delay is time. Time is the resource.
- **Disappearance before the census arrives:** this requires advance notice of the census team's arrival and a destination outside Harrenhal's traditional land claims. It converts the impressment threat into a fugitive-labor status, which is a different but potentially more manageable threat.
- **Claim illness:** a person assessed as too ill to work is not impressed — the labor value is not there. This is a temporary measure; the census records the person as ill and expects to re-assess.
- **The septon's last intervention:** a dying septon can, in principle, formally certify his ward's status and request a grace period for the ward to find new protected placement. This is procedurally valid; it creates an obligation on the castellan to honor it for a limited period. How long and under what conditions is at the castellan's discretion.

Given her demonstrated literacy and unusual cognition, she will not be classified as field labor — she will be flagged for household service, which means a direct line to the castellan's attention rather than the general labor pool. This is worse, not better.
