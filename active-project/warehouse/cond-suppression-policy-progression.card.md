# Suppression Policy Progression — Institutional Response to Taylor Hebert (84–101 AC)

## Description

The structural stages by which institutional response to Taylor's organizing escalates from incident-response (~86 AC, lord's steward makes first note) to active suppression-as-policy (the lord directs systematic monitoring, reporting, and eventual action against Taylor specifically). Defines what triggers each escalation step, institutional actors' incentives at each level, and what "suppression-as-policy must be reached no later than mid-S3" means in scene-level evidence.

This card governs the antagonist clock. Aldric Pryor (House Ryger's steward) is the primary institutional face across S1–S3.

## Sensory Impact

Felt as a change in the quality of official presence in Taylor's environment. At Stage 1, the steward's men ride through on the same schedule; one of them watches the Ashford workshop with a slightly different kind of attention. At Stage 4, there are men whose assigned task includes Taylor's monitoring.

## Duration

Evolves across the series arc. Does not resolve; escalates. Stage 1 begins at ~86 AC. Mid-S3 deadline is policy-level Stage 3. Council close (101 AC) is the terminal pressure point.

## Stage 1: Incident Response (~86 AC)

**Trigger:** The ignition event. Taylor's swarm rises involuntarily. The collector's men are stung; horses bolt; collection abandoned for the day. The retinue leader reports upward. Pryor makes a note: the Ashford dyer's daughter, approximately nine years old, insects behaved abnormally.

**Pryor's incentive:** A single incident is not worth formal escalation. A note costs nothing. If it turns out to be nothing, the note is filed. If it turns out to be something, the note establishes he was thorough.

**Scene-level evidence:** Tax collection abandoned. The collector's men avoid looking at Taylor. The word "strange" appears in gossip. Pryor or his deputy asks the reeve casually about the Ashford girl. The note exists.

---

## Stage 2: Patterned Response (~87–90 AC)

**Trigger:** A second anomalous event associated with Taylor, or Pryor's record reaching a weight where the pattern is undeniable as non-coincidental.

**What changes:** Pryor begins actively monitoring Taylor's immediate environment. He or a designated deputy observes her during every official visit to Fairstead. He begins asking questions more systematically — of the reeve, of Septon Rowan, of neighboring factors. He is building a file, not an accusation.

**Pryor's incentive:** Documentation, not action. He is a minor lord's steward; he does not have authority to take direct action against a smallfolk based on insect behavior. His lord would want to know about a genuine threat, not about a steward who overreacted.

**Scene-level evidence:** Steward's men visit slightly more frequently. Pryor asks Septon Rowan a direct question about Taylor (Rowan deflects with theological language). The reeve tells Taylor's parents that the steward has been asking about the family. The whisper chain carries: "someone official is curious about Edwyn Ashford's daughter."

---

## Stage 3: Policy (~91–97 AC, no later than mid-S3)

**Trigger:** Pryor concludes monitoring is insufficient; the lord must be informed and given a recommendation. Trigger: an event significant enough that Pryor cannot justify keeping it in his personal file, or the accumulated file reaching a weight where administrative duty requires escalation.

**What changes:** The lord of House Ryger is briefed. He reads Pryor's file. He makes a policy decision: Taylor is a named subject of institutional attention. A suppression directive is issued: systematic monitoring with regular reports; restriction on Taylor's movement (she may not leave Fairstead without the reeve's notification of Pryor); inquiry into the scope of her organizing activities.

**World-notes mandate:** "Suppression-as-policy (not incident response) must be reached no later than mid-S3." Hard constraint on the series' antagonist timeline.

**Lord's incentive:** Protect his territory from instability, protect his relationship with Tully, and not create a problem larger than the one he is solving. Administrative oversight first; violence is available but is not the first tool.

**Scene-level evidence:** An official summons delivered in writing with the lord's seal. The reeve is now under official instruction to report Taylor's activities. Pryor visits Fairstead personally to interview Taylor's parents. The Faith itinerant spends unusual time with Septon Rowan. The lord has been told.

---

## Stage 4: Action (~98–101 AC, Council horizon)

**Trigger:** Stage 3 suppression is not effective. Taylor's organizing continues or escalates despite official oversight.

**What changes:** Official violence potential enters the picture. The lord might choose economic strangulation of Taylor's coalition (call in debts, restrict market access, deny guild recognition to her allies) before physical violence. Or he might dispatch men-at-arms with explicit instructions. Tully-level reporting becomes possible.

**Tully involvement:** If the lord escalates to Tully, the problem moves from minor-lord-with-a-strange-girl to Great-House-institutional-response. Tully's tools include inter-lord diplomatic pressure, official investigation with Citadel backing, and the possibility of trial-level process.

**The Council horizon:** Stage 4 and the Great Council of 101 AC are designed to converge. The Council is when all of the suppression machinery's accumulated weight meets Taylor's accumulated organizing at maximum institutional pressure.

**Scene-level evidence:** Men-at-arms stationed in Fairstead on a permanent basis. A formal investigation opened under Tully authority. Economic pressure applied to known Taylor-associated people. The lord's seal on a warrant. The arrest of a coalition member as leverage.

---

## The Steward as Clock

Aldric Pryor is the series' institutional antagonist across S1–S3. His escalation is professional, not personal. He does not hate Taylor. He is doing his job correctly. The record does not care how she feels about it.

## Incentive Structure at Each Level

| Stage | Primary actor | Primary tool | Primary incentive |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pryor (steward) | Note-taking | Be thorough; create no record of overreaction |
| 2 | Pryor | Active monitoring, file-building | Demonstrate pattern before escalating |
| 3 | Minor lord (via Pryor) | Official directive, official summons | Manage territory without creating a larger problem |
| 4 | Minor lord + Tully (potential) | Direct intervention or warrant | Restore stability; protect institutional position |

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-riverlands-84ac-state`:** The long peace creates stable institutional machinery that can afford to move slowly and methodically. Stage 1 to Stage 3 takes 5–11 years precisely because the administrative apparatus is not under pressure.

**With `cond-feudal-hierarchy-law`:** That card defines what the lord is legally permitted to do at each stage. This card defines his incentives.

**For auditor use:** If institutional response escalates faster than the trigger criteria above support, that is a pacing violation. If Stage 3 is not reached by mid-S3, that is a structural violation of the series' antagonist clock. Track the stage-level on each Pryor appearance.
