# Roper's Court and Tributary Lanes — Rope-Making Ward, Third Coverage Expansion

## Geography

A craft-trade ward in Flea Bottom, adjacent to one of the other three coverage wards (the stitch-house lane ward or Pig Tallow Lane network). The ward takes its name from the dominant trade: rope-making, a specialist smallfolk industry in a port-adjacent city. King's Landing's proximity to the Blackwater Rush and Blackwater Bay creates sustained demand for cordage — rigging rope, mooring lines, cargo ties — and a ward that specializes in it occupies a stable if low-prestige economic niche.

Rope-making requires linear geometry: the process of laying hemp fibers into twisted rope requires a rope-walk — a long clear lane down which the spinner can walk backward while twisting. Roper's Court's tributary lanes are wider-spaced and longer than typical Flea Bottom alleys as a result, or the residents have cleared specific lanes for this purpose. The ward has a physical texture shaped by its trade: long sight lines in the tributary lanes, fiber and tar residue underfoot, hempen smell.

The court itself is a central open space — larger than the connecting-alley courts of Pig Tallow Lane, necessarily, since rope-makers use a central drying and coiling area. The tributary lanes radiate off the court at multiple angles, each a potential rope-walk in the making.

## Layout

**Roper's Court (central space):** The defining feature — an irregular open area where the tributary lanes converge. Used for rope coiling, drying finished line, and the social functions a Flea Bottom court serves: informal meeting, children's movement, weather shelter. The court is large relative to its neighborhood, visible from multiple tributary lane-mouths simultaneously.

**Tributary lanes:** Multiple lanes branch off the court, narrowing as they extend. The near tributaries (opening directly off the court) are wider — wide enough for a rope-walk where two people can pass the twisting spindle between them. The far tributaries, at the ends of the branching network, narrow to standard Flea Bottom single-file width. Taylor's observation (b01c04 s03) places the far tributary lane at the point where foot traffic in the early morning thins to single-file — this is the end of the accessible rope-walk geometry, where the lane narrows enough that the ward's function gives way to pure residential cramming.

**Fiber-work stations:** Positioned at intervals along the wider tributaries — anchor posts driven into the lane walls, fiber-bundles piled at one end, finished coil-points at the other. Consistent environmental features but not card-level fixed props.

**Court perimeter buildings:** The structures surrounding the court are a mix of rope-making workshops on the ground floor and residential tenements above. Workshop faces open during working hours — wide doorways through which finished rope is carried. Shuttered at night.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Hemp is the dominant note — raw fiber has a green-vegetable smell when fresh, a dry dusty smell when aged. The tar used for waterproofing certain rope grades (rigging rope particularly) adds a sharp resinous overlay that concentrates in the tributaries where waterproofing work is done. The hemp-and-tar combination is distinctive and specific to rope-making work; it does not appear in the Hook, Pig Tallow Lane, or the stitch-house ward at the same intensity. A character who has been in Roper's Court carries the smell on their clothing.

**Sound:** In working hours: the rhythmic creak of rope-twist — the spindle turning produces a characteristic sound as fibers resist, a low rhythmic creak-and-drag audible the length of a tributary lane. Conversation in the tributary lanes is possible but carries (the geometry of a long lane transmits sound well). In the early morning grey (Taylor's preferred time for coverage here, per b01c04 s03 chunk): near-silence. The rope-walk requires daylight to do accurately; work does not begin before first light. Before the ward wakes, the court is quiet enough that a person walking in it can hear footsteps from two tributary lanes over.

**Light:** The court's central open space is relatively well-lit during the day — the open geometry catches more sky than the Hook's cramped alleys. The near tributaries have adequate working light; the far tributaries, where the lanes narrow and the overhang of upper tenements increases, are dark by mid-afternoon even in summer. In the early morning grey: the court is in dull pre-dawn light; the far tributaries are dark enough that movement through them requires familiarity with the lane.

**Texture:** Hemp fiber residue is constant — short fibers shedding from the rope-walk land on the lane surfaces and are tracked through the court by foot traffic. The ground in the rope-walk tributaries is permanently fibrous, a distinct texture from the compacted-earth Flea Bottom standard. Tar spots on the cobbled sections of the wider tributaries.

## Fixed Props

None card-level. The rope-walk anchor posts and spindle equipment are ward property and runtime state. Finished rope coils staged for delivery are runtime state.

Taylor walks it "in the grey of early morning when foot traffic is thin and the feed's geometry is cleanest" (b01c04 s03 chunk). This four-ward footprint is "the largest operational footprint [Taylor] has run in King's Landing" per the b01c04 s03 chunk.

**Feed geometry rationale:** The tributary lane geometry (long, linear, relatively low-overhang compared to Hook alleys) produces the most precise single-ward insect-feed resolution in the four-ward map — the linear lanes allow a cleaner geometric saturation pattern than the Hook's contorted alleys. Taylor walks here at off-hours specifically to exploit this geometry with minimal body-saturation noise.

**Single-file far tributary:** The b01c04 s03 chunk specifies "the early-morning grey thins the foot traffic in Roper's Court to single-file at the far tributary lane." This is the ward's distinctive early-morning texture — single-file not because the lane is normally that narrow but because traffic is thin enough that it resolves to single figures in the lane.

**First intelligence report content:** The movement-pattern report Taylor hands to Jarvis in b01c04 s03 includes data from all four wards (including Roper's Court): "the junction-agitation patterns across the four wards for the past six days, the clustering that precedes crowd-trouble, the three passage-avoidance points that mark which routes the ward-residents read as unsafe."

## Exits

- **Tributary lane network (multiple exits):** Multiple exit points — each tributary lane-mouth opens to a different section of the Flea Bottom alley network.
- **Court mouth (primary approach):** The main entrance to the court from the adjacent ward network.
- **Far tributary lane exits:** The narrow far-end lanes open to the outer Flea Bottom network, connecting eventually to the lanes below the Street of Steel approaches.

## Hazards

- **Far tributary darkness:** The narrow far-end tributaries are dark in early morning and evening; navigation requires familiarity.
- **Sound transmission in rope-walk geometry:** The long tributary lanes transmit sound unusually well for Flea Bottom — conversation at ordinary speaking volume can be heard from the court entrance.
- **Tar hazard in waterproofing areas:** Fresh-applied tar is a skin and clothing contact hazard.
- **Single-file far tributary as containment:** The narrowing far-end lanes are chokepoints — walls on both sides, one direction only if blocked.
- **Ward-resident recognition:** The rope-making trade worker population is stable; a stranger appearing on multiple successive mornings may acquire notice faster than in the transient Hook.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A rope-walk starting before dawn — an urgent commission pulling workers to the spindle before first light, disrupting the early-morning quiet Taylor uses for clean-feed coverage
- A finished coil delivery staging in the court, blocking the central space with large-diameter rope rolls and the dray-cart maneuvering around them
- A tar-pot accident in a waterproofing tributary — fire risk, quick ward response, smell and sound change propagating through the lane network
- A child running a fiber-count errand through the court at the wrong hour — witness to a person in the ward before its workers have arrived
- The far tributary lane mouth blocked by a collapsed overhanging shelf — forcing a reroute through a less familiar lane
- A Ward Watch officer cutting through Roper's Court on a shortcut between patrol routes — using the court as a navigation point
