# The Hook Upper Provisioning Platform — Green-Faction Household Supply Node, Hook Ward

## Geography

A supply-receipt point at the upper end of the Hook, operating as the loading and account-settling station for a Green-faction noble household's Flea Bottom provisioning chain. Not a market stall and not a warehouse interior — a liminal supply threshold: an exterior platform under a lean-to overhang at the back-alley face of the household's ward-level logistics node, where smallfolk suppliers deliver and the household-agent conducts the account transaction. The platform is above street level by a single step, enough to position the household-agent above the suppliers without the formal elevation of a shop counter.

The upper Hook placement is deliberate: the ward's highest-density provisioner supply is concentrated at the lower end, near the fishmongery and the tallow chandler's row. A provisioning node at the upper end draws suppliers upward, away from their own clustering point, which puts them at the household-agent's approach geometry rather than their own.

In 122 AC: operating as a regular supply-receipt point for a mid-tier Green household, one tier below a named lord's household but above a simple merchant-factor arrangement. The agent conducting transactions here has the household livery but wears it as a functional garment, not a display.

## Layout

**Trestle-table:** The dominant feature — a heavy wooden table set perpendicular to the lane at the platform's edge, used for weighing, counting, and producing the tally-ledger. The table is wide enough for two people to stand opposite without crowding; the household-agent positions behind it, suppliers approach from the lane side. The table surface carries the permanent marks of crate-setting, ledger-use, and the occasional impression of a dried fish crate.

**Crate-ledge:** A lower wooden ledge running along the platform's lane-side, built to take delivery crates at waist height for the supplier. Crates can be set here for inspection before transfer to the platform proper. The ledge is old enough that its wood shows the compression from years of crate-weight.

**Lean-to overhang:** A shallow roof-extension covering the platform from direct weather. Functional, not aesthetic — the overhang is narrow enough that standing rain blows in from the lane side. It keeps the tally-ledger dry during light precipitation.

**Account-space behind the table:** The narrow lane-gap between the trestle-table and the wall of the logistics node. The household-agent works this space. Suppliers remain at or below the table edge; the lane-side is theirs until account-close.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Salt-fish is the primary register during delivery windows — the crates arriving from the lower Hook carry the preserved-fish smell of a morning's supply run: brine and oil and the slight ferment of fish compressed together. Secondary: the household's logistics node behind the platform carries the smell of stored dry goods (grain dust, pepper, dried herbs) when the back door is open. The trestle-table itself smells of the accumulated deliveries — salt-fish dominant, with tar-wood notes from the crate timber.

**Sound:** During delivery transactions — the creak of crates set on the crate-ledge; the scratch of the tally-stick or the quill on the ledger; the household-agent's voice at the register of someone conducting routine business but with slightly too much air of authority for the smallness of the transaction. The supplier's acknowledgment sounds are minimal and clipped. The supplier's son, if present, is silent.

**Light:** Mid-morning overhead light in the lane, partially blocked by the lean-to overhang. The platform itself sits in the lean-to's shade; the lane in front is in ambient morning light. A person standing at the crate-ledge is slightly better lit than the household-agent behind the table.

**Texture:** Platform step: worn stone or compressed earth, depending on original construction. Trestle-table surface: scored wood, slightly sticky from dried brine at the ledger corner. Crate-ledge: smooth from use. The lane approach has the compressed-earth and fish-scale underfoot of the upper Hook supply corridor.

## Fixed Props

- `oc-fish-account-ledger` (runtime; present during account transactions — carried by the household-agent, not fixed to the platform; treated as fixed during active scenes)

## Exits

- **Lane approach (south, toward lower Hook):** Primary arrival for suppliers coming up from the fishmongery and the lower ward chandler's row. The lane widens slightly before the platform approach.
- **Back door of logistics node (platform-rear, toward street-side):** The household's interior access. Not available to suppliers; used by the household-agent to retrieve goods post-transaction. Not an exit for on-stage use unless the scene involves the agent's interior.
- **Upper lane continuation (north):** The Hook's lane network continues upward and northeast toward the Rushwick approach. Not a supplier route; more typically foot-traffic continuation.

## Hazards

- **Account-authority asymmetry:** The elevated position, the ledger, and the household livery combine to produce a structural pressure: the supplier is operating on the household's tally against their own memory of the delivery. Disputes require the supplier to contest the ledger without access to it, which the household-agent will not allow. The copper-margin coercion operates at this gap: the tally can record a lower count or a quality deduction the supplier cannot verify in the moment.
- **Supplier's son as leverage-point:** A supplier who brings a child to assist delivery has introduced a bystander whose silence the household-agent can use as normative pressure. The son's learned silence (the product of prior deliveries where speaking caused problems) is itself a coercion instrument.
- **Observation geometry:** The crate-ledge is slightly lower than the trestle-table, putting the supplier at a persistent below-eye-level register relative to the agent. The platform step is structural, not incidental.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A second supplier arriving before the first transaction closes — queue management that the household-agent can use to pressure the current transaction to conclude faster on the agent's terms
- A delayed delivery requiring the household-agent to adjust the tally for a partial-crate count — an improvised coercion opportunity
- Noise from the logistics node interior breaking through the back door — evidence of the household-scale behind the transaction
- A Ward Watch officer passing the lane mouth during a transaction — neither party wants Watch attention, but for different reasons
- Weather shifting during a transaction — rain beginning under a lean-to that does not fully cover both parties equally
