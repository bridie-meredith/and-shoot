# Riverlands River Ford

## Geography

A ford on one of the Red Fork's tributary streams, in the middle Riverlands between Stoney Sept and Stone Hedge. Not the Trident itself — a smaller crossing, chest-deep on a man at its deepest channel, knee-deep on the banks. Wide enough that a wagon can cross if the driver knows the correct line. The stream is fast in spring runoff, manageable in summer, low in late autumn.

The ford is a strategic chokepoint: there are three crossings in a ten-mile stretch of this stream; this one is the only one usable by wagons, which makes it the one all trade traffic uses. Whoever controls the ford can tax or interdict most commercial movement between the two sides. Ser Aemon Bracken's holding derives a significant portion of its income from this ford's toll.

The banks on both sides are open — flat farmland, no concealment near the water. The best approach cover is a stand of willows on the eastern bank, about thirty feet upstream. A hedgerow runs along the western bank road.

## Layout

- **Eastern approach:** a dirt track following the east bank north-south; the ford turnoff is marked by a weathered post with no remaining inscription.
- **The crossing:** stone-bottom ford, roughly forty feet wide at the crossing point. The correct line angles slightly upstream. The incorrect line (the obvious straight shot) hits the deeper channel and the current.
- **Western approach:** the road continues into open farmland; a small toll-house here (stone, one room, occupied by a toll-keeper in Bracken employ when normal conditions hold; currently empty given recent disruptions).
- **The willows:** upstream on the east bank. Dense enough to conceal several horses. The stream-sound masks approach noise from below but not above.

## Sensory Vocabulary

- **Sound:** The ford — moving water over stone, constant. Louder in spring. The creak of a wagon on the crossing; the sound of horses in water. Frogs in the evening. A heron downstream that has been there for years.
- **Smell:** River water, mud, the specific cold-stone smell of fast water. Willowleaf. Smoke from the toll-house when occupied.
- **Light:** Open sky; the ford is exposed. Light off moving water in the afternoon. Dusk comes slowly here because of the open ground.
- **Texture:** The ford bottom is stone and it is slick. A man who rushes the crossing in armor is at real risk of a fall. Horses know this instinctively; men often don't.

## Fixed Props

- The toll-house (stone, one room, fireplace, currently empty)
- The weathered post marking the ford turnoff
- The willow stand on the east bank

## Exits

- **North (east bank):** road follows the stream north; next village three miles.
- **South (east bank):** road south toward Stoney Sept; four hours by horse.
- **West (after crossing):** farmland road toward Stone Hedge territory.
- **The ford itself:** a chokepoint; in conflict, crossing while under fire is extremely dangerous.

## Hazards

- The deep channel: a man who takes the wrong line in armor goes down and has a real chance of drowning before he gets the armor off.
- Ambush from the willows: anyone waiting in the willow stand controls the crossing approach with minimal exposure.
- Flood: spring runoff makes the ford impassable for several weeks; any strategic plan dependent on this crossing must account for the season.
- The toll-house as chokepoint: whoever holds the toll-house holds the narrowest point of the western approach; it is small but thick-walled.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A merchant's wagon arriving at the ford, driver calling out to the empty toll-house
- A kingfisher diving upstream — the heron flushing from below the ford means something large is moving downstream
- The sound of hooves on the stone ford in the night (unexpected; most traffic avoids night crossings)
- Water level visibly higher than yesterday — the rain upstream has arrived
- Ser Aemon's garrison patrol arriving for the daily toll count, finding the toll-house empty and the post disturbed
