# Fauna Control Rules — Taylor Hebert in Westeros

## Description

Governing constraint card for Taylor Hebert's parahuman fauna-control ability as it operates in Westeros, absent shard buffering. This card defines the exact mechanics, physical cost structure, species scope, and wrongness-perception rules that apply in this project. All impersonators, auditors, and constraint checks must load this card when Taylor uses her ability.

## Species Scope

**In scope (controllable):** insects and arachnids; rats and mice; corvids (ravens and crows specifically; also rooks, jackdaws); any bird without complex social cognition (sparrows, pigeons, starlings); any creature without higher-order social behavior. In the Riverlands specifically: flies, beetles, moths, spiders, rats, mice, ravens, crows, common sparrows, starlings.

**Explicitly excluded:** horses, dogs, direwolves, dragons, humans, any creature capable of complex social behavior or tight-bonded social structures. Excluded on the same grounds: herd animals that move as coordinated units (they have distributed cognition that resists individual control). Cats are borderline — can be influenced but not reliably commanded; Taylor treats them as unreliable and avoids relying on them.

**Practical note:** The Riverlands in autumn is rich in insect life, particularly flies and beetles around livestock and stored grain. Rats are ubiquitous in any structure. Ravens are common and culturally significant (they are the realm's communication network; their behavior is observed). This creates both resources and risks.

## Range

**Normal load (not concentrating):** approximately 200 meters radius. Within this range, Taylor has passive awareness of all controllable fauna, similar to her original swarm-sense. She can direct individual creatures or groups without significant effort.

**Extended range:** Concentration extends range to approximately 400 meters. This requires active effort that displaces other cognitive tasks. Taylor cannot hold extended range and hold a complex conversation simultaneously; she can hold extended range and perform simple physical tasks.

**Maximum range:** Under extreme focus, range may extend to 600+ meters, but this pushes into immediate physical cost territory. Not a sustainable operational mode.

**Note on ravens specifically:** Ravens can be dispatched from her range and continue on trajectory, but she loses granular control beyond range. She can send a raven in a direction; she cannot fine-steer it after it leaves her radius.

## Capacity

**Swarm-mode (insects):** Taylor can maintain simultaneous individual-level control of hundreds of insects without meaningful cognitive load. Thousands of insects can be managed as a coordinated mass with somewhat less individual precision. This is her most fluid operational mode.

**Mid-size fauna (rats, ravens, individual birds):** Taylor can manage up to approximately ten to fifteen large-ish creatures (rats, ravens) simultaneously with individual-level command. Beyond that, she's managing a group rather than individuals, and group management is less precise.

**Cross-class management:** Running insects AND rats AND ravens simultaneously is possible but costs more. The cognitive load of managing multiple species with different sensory and behavioral profiles is significantly higher than single-species operations. Taylor can do it; it drains her faster.

## Physical Cost Curve

**0–5 minutes of sustained use:** No meaningful cost. Normal operational tempo.

**5–15 minutes of sustained use:** Headache onset. Dull pressure at the temples and behind the eyes. Taylor can function at full capacity but is aware of the cost accumulating.

**15–30 minutes of sustained use:** Nosebleed onset. The headache intensifies. Physical performance begins to degrade — Taylor can still plan and move but is not operating at peak. Using this phase to make critical tactical decisions is possible but carries risk of errors she would not make rested.

**Beyond 30 minutes continuous OR multiple shorter sessions without adequate rest:** Blackout risk. Probability increases with continued use. A blackout in a hostile environment is a potential death event. Taylor knows this. She has approached this threshold once and pulled back.

**Recovery:** 4–6 hours of complete non-use. During recovery, Taylor retains passive awareness (she cannot fully turn off the sense) but does not direct or control. A partial rest (2–3 hours) provides partial recovery — the cost curve resets to a shorter fuse, not a full reset.

**The passive sense cannot be disabled:** Taylor is always receiving some level of fauna-awareness within her range. She can suppress attention to it but cannot stop the input. In environments with high fauna density (Harrenhal's rat-rich storage areas, for example), the passive feed is louder and more demanding of suppression energy.

## Wrongness-Perception

**What Westerosi observers register:** When Taylor directs fauna at scale or in coordination, observers register it as uncanny. Birds that move wrong — wheeling in non-natural patterns, landing without cause on unusual surfaces, moving against wind or toward people rather than away. Rats that gather and wait rather than fleeing. Insects that do not flee fire, or that mass on surfaces without the usual dispersal triggers. All of these read as wrong to Westerosi observers, whose relationship with the natural world is strongly normative.

**Cultural frame:** Westeros has no framework for fauna-control as a human capability. The available frames are: omen (Seven-sent or evil-sent), Old Gods working through animals, warging (the Northern capability, not well-understood in the Riverlands), or simple wrongness-without-category. All of these frames attach significance to the observer, not to Taylor. She becomes the person near whom wrong things happen with animals.

**Concealment:** Taylor can reduce wrongness-perception by:
- Using a single animal or small group rather than masses
- Operating at the edge of her range rather than close to observers
- Using passive reconnaissance (awareness without directing) rather than active control
- Ensuring directed animals maintain superficially natural behavior patterns (a raven flying in a straight line is less suspicious than a raven circling a specific person)

**She cannot fully conceal her ability in extended use.** A sustained operation involving multiple species in close quarters will eventually produce observable animal behavior that does not fit natural patterns. This is a hard constraint; the question is how long before someone with sufficient attention notices.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-impressment-census-120ac`:** The census process specifically flags unusual cognition and unusual capabilities. If Taylor uses fauna-control in the presence of the maester or castellan's men, it is almost certain to be flagged. Even indirect use (birds behaving oddly during the census visit) creates risk.

**With `cond-westerosi-customary-authority`:** A smallfolk child who can make animals do things is not categorized as dangerous by default — she is categorized as touched, cursed, or strange. This provides some protection (people avoid the subject of the strange child) and some exposure (the subject of the strange child is discussed; it travels through the smallfolk information network).

**With Septon Rowan:** The wrongness-perception is his raw material. When he witnesses fauna-control events, they become theologically significant. He is not wrong that something unusual is happening; his interpretation is simply not the one Taylor would choose.
