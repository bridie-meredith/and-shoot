# Fauna Control Rules — Taylor Hebert in Westeros (84 AC Config — Child-Body Edition)

## Project-Scope Note

This is the working copy of `cond-fauna-control-rules` for the taylor-hebert-jaehaerys project. The base mechanics (species scope, range, capacity, wrongness-perception) carry forward without modification. The **Physical Cost Curve** section is adjusted to reflect Taylor's starting age (7–8 at arrival, ~9 at series open) and the child-body ceiling that must be tracked across the series as she grows to ~17 at Council close.

The key project-specific addition: **the ambient-vs-directed distinction.** In a child body, the passive ambient sense (always-on, no directional control) is functionally free — it runs continuously at near-zero cost. Active directed control is where the child-body ceiling bites. This distinction governs her pre-ignition management problem (two years of concealing involuntary passive awareness before the ignition) and her post-ignition rationing problem.

## Description

Governing constraint card for Taylor Hebert's parahuman fauna-control ability as it operates in Westeros, absent shard buffering, in a child body that grows across the series. Defines the exact mechanics, physical cost structure, species scope, and wrongness-perception rules. All impersonators, auditors, and constraint checks must load this card when Taylor uses her ability.

## Species Scope

**In scope (controllable):** insects and arachnids; rats and mice; corvids (ravens and crows specifically; also rooks, jackdaws); any bird without complex social cognition (sparrows, pigeons, starlings); any creature without higher-order social behavior. In the Riverlands specifically: flies, beetles, moths, spiders, rats, mice, ravens, crows, common sparrows, starlings.

**Explicitly excluded:** horses, dogs, direwolves, dragons, humans, any creature capable of complex social behavior or tight-bonded social structures. Excluded on the same grounds: herd animals that move as coordinated units (they have distributed cognition that resists individual control). Cats are borderline — can be influenced but not reliably commanded; Taylor treats them as unreliable and avoids relying on them.

**Practical note:** The Riverlands in warm seasons is rich in insect life, particularly flies and beetles around livestock and stored grain. Rats are ubiquitous in any structure. Ravens are common and culturally significant (they are the realm's communication network; their behavior is observed). This creates both resources and risks.

## Range

**Normal load (not concentrating):** approximately 200 meters radius. Within this range, Taylor has passive awareness of all controllable fauna, similar to her original swarm-sense. She can direct individual creatures or groups without significant effort.

**Extended range:** Concentration extends range to approximately 400 meters. This requires active effort that displaces other cognitive tasks. Taylor cannot hold extended range and hold a complex conversation simultaneously; she can hold extended range and perform simple physical tasks.

**Maximum range:** Under extreme focus, range may extend to 600+ meters, but this pushes into immediate physical cost territory. Not a sustainable operational mode.

**Note on ravens specifically:** Ravens can be dispatched from her range and continue on trajectory, but she loses granular control beyond range. She can send a raven in a direction; she cannot fine-steer it after it leaves her radius.

## Capacity

**Swarm-mode (insects):** Taylor can maintain simultaneous individual-level control of hundreds of insects without meaningful cognitive load. Thousands of insects can be managed as a coordinated mass with somewhat less individual precision. This is her most fluid operational mode.

**Mid-size fauna (rats, ravens, individual birds):** Taylor can manage up to approximately ten to fifteen large-ish creatures simultaneously with individual-level command. Beyond that, she's managing a group rather than individuals, and group management is less precise.

**Cross-class management:** Running insects AND rats AND ravens simultaneously is possible but costs more. The cognitive load of managing multiple species with different sensory and behavioral profiles is significantly higher than single-species operations.

## Physical Cost Curve — Child-Body Edition

**The ambient-vs-directed distinction (critical for this project):**

The passive ambient sense — the always-on awareness of controllable fauna within range — runs at near-zero physical cost in the child body. Taylor cannot turn this off; it is structural. The cost is negligible. This is the passive sense that has been running since she arrived in the new body. She has been managing it for approximately two years before the series opens without triggering physical cost.

Active directed control — choosing where creatures go, what they do, running a coordinated swarm — is where the child body's shorter cost curve bites. The child body has less physical reserve than an adult body, and the cost curve below reflects this.

**Active-control cost curve (age ~9 at series open):**
- 0–3 minutes of sustained active control: No meaningful cost.
- 3–10 minutes of sustained active control: Headache onset. Dull pressure at the temples and behind the eyes. Taylor can function but is aware of the cost accumulating.
- 10–20 minutes of sustained active control: Nosebleed onset. Headache intensifies. Physical performance degrades — Taylor can still plan and move but is not operating at peak.
- Beyond 20 minutes continuous, or multiple shorter sessions without adequate rest: Blackout risk. Probability increases with continued use. A blackout in a hostile environment is a potential death event. Taylor knows this threshold exists. She has not approached it yet at series open.

**Recovery (age ~9):** 4–6 hours of complete non-active-use. Passive sense continues during recovery (cannot be disabled). Partial rest (2–3 hours) provides partial recovery — the cost curve resets to a shorter fuse, not a full reset.

**Growth across the series:**

The child-body ceiling rises with age. This is not a power upgrade; it is biological development. Writers should track Taylor's approximate age:

- Age 9–11 (S1): short fuse as above; rationing is essential; the ignition event's involuntary swarm is costly for this age body even though it lasted under two minutes.
- Age 11–14 (S1–S2 bridge): fuse extends toward 15 minutes; recovery becomes slightly faster.
- Age 14–17 (S2–S3): approaching the base-card cost curve (30-minute active window, 4–6 hour recovery) without fully reaching it.
- Age 17+ (Council horizon): effectively at or near the base-card cost curve. Full adult cost curve applies at series close.

**The ignition event (retroactive note):** The involuntary swarm at ~86 AC was brief (under two minutes of active-direction equivalent) but involuntary — meaning Taylor had less control over cost than she would in a deliberate use. Involuntary or distress-driven activation is slightly more costly than deliberate controlled use at the same duration. She recovers within hours. The event does not establish a new ceiling; it establishes her awareness that the ceiling exists and that it can be breached by involuntary activation.

## Wrongness-Perception

**What Westerosi observers register:** When Taylor directs fauna at scale or in coordination, observers register it as uncanny. Birds that move wrong — wheeling in non-natural patterns, landing without cause on unusual surfaces, moving against wind or toward people rather than away. Rats that gather and wait rather than fleeing. Insects that do not flee fire, or that mass on surfaces without the usual dispersal triggers. All of these read as wrong to Westerosi observers, whose relationship with the natural world is strongly normative.

**Cultural frame:** Westeros has no framework for fauna-control as a human capability. The available frames are: omen (Seven-sent or evil-sent), Old Gods working through animals, warging (the Northern capability, not well-understood in the Riverlands), or simple wrongness-without-category. All of these frames attach significance to the observer, not to Taylor. She becomes the person near whom wrong things happen with animals. See `cond-westerosi-superstition-frame` for the full superstition categorization.

**Concealment:** Taylor can reduce wrongness-perception by:
- Using a single animal or small group rather than masses
- Operating at the edge of her range rather than close to observers
- Using passive reconnaissance (awareness without directing) rather than active control
- Ensuring directed animals maintain superficially natural behavior patterns

**She cannot fully conceal her ability in extended use.** A sustained operation involving multiple species in close quarters will eventually produce observable animal behavior that does not fit natural patterns. This is a hard constraint.

**Pre-ignition management (84–86 AC):** In the two years between Taylor's arrival and the ignition event, she is managing the passive sense: allowing it to run without acting on it, suppressing the instinct to direct. This is not physically costly (passive sense is free) but is cognitively costly — a child's executive function managing an always-on awareness of the surrounding insect population is draining in a different register. She has been succeeding at this management for two years. The ignition breaks it.

## Interaction Notes

**With `condition-swarm-in-foreign-ecology`:** That card governs the ecology (what is available by season and terrain). This card governs the cost structure and mechanical limits. Both must be loaded together for any scene involving Taylor's power use.

**With `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`:** The cost curve is the consequence of no Shard buffering. The cost curve cannot be reduced by in-world intervention.

**With `cond-westerosi-customary-authority-jaehaerys`:** A smallfolk child who can make animals do things is categorized as touched, cursed, or strange — protective (not threatening) and exposing (discussed in the smallfolk network).

**With Septon Rowan:** The wrongness-perception events are Rowan's raw material. Each visible swarm event becomes theologically significant to him.

**For auditor use:** Track Taylor's age when evaluating the cost curve. A nine-year-old running a twenty-minute active swarm without nosebleed is a fence violation. An adult-equivalent Taylor at seventeen having a five-minute fuse is a fence violation. Growth is real and the ceiling rises. Apply the age-indexed curve above.
