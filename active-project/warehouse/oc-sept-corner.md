# The Sept Corner — Devotional Corner, the Hook, Flea Bottom

## Geography

A street corner in the Hook — the curved, waterfront-adjacent warren of Flea Bottom — where a small neighborhood sept abuts the side wall of a chandler's storehouse. The sept itself is a minor devotional structure of the kind common to dense working-poor wards: not a formal sept with a septon in residence, but a corner alcove with a carved Seven-faced facing, candle niches, and enough overhang to stand under in rain. The chandler's storehouse occupies the corner's dominant mass — the sept is built into or against its street-facing wall, so the two share the same stone and the same cold.

The chandler's storehouse is Septon Halvard's habitual station. He holds the corner on holy days and most other days — available to the Hook's residents for the pastoral functions a ward's only accessible septon provides: blessing requests, sick-child visits, informal counsel, dispute arbitration at the level below Watch involvement. The corner is not a destination for outsiders; it is an ordinary stop on the circuit of Hook life.

The sept corner sits within Taylor's ward-coverage circuit. The insect-feed's anchoring bodies here are the regular parishioners, the chandler's workers, and Halvard himself — recognizable by Halvard's clerical gait, which the feed places reliably at this corner's coordinates across most of the ward's morning-to-midday window.

## Layout

**The passage:** A lane passage narrows at the sept corner to near-single-handcart width. The chandler's storehouse wall juts slightly past the sept's line on the south side, and the opposing building's corner does the same on the north, creating a natural choke: a single handcart loaded with bundled rags can block passage entirely, forcing any through-traffic to stop or reverse. This geometry is not architectural — it is the compression artifact of buildings that grew against each other rather than being planned in relation. The blocking point is nonetheless reliable: anyone moving from the Hook's interior toward the outer alley network must pass through it.

**The chandler's storehouse wall (sept side):** Cold-holding stone. The storehouse is old enough that its masonry has settled into the deep cold-retention of thick construction — the wall face holds the previous night's cold well into morning, and on overcast days does not warm before mid-afternoon. The sept's carved facing and candle niches occupy a recessed bay in this wall, set back far enough to shelter a flame in moderate wind. The bay floor is worn smooth from long use: feet, knees, the press of hands.

**The corner ground:** Irregular paving where two lane-surfaces meet — the junction of the passage lane and the cross-lane running perpendicular. The surface holds cold as the wall does; on clear mornings the paving radiates cool upward even after the air temperature has lifted. Taylor's insect-feed registers this as a consistent ground-temperature differential between the sept corner and the broader lane network.

**Halvard's station:** He positions himself against the storehouse wall, not inside the sept bay. The bay is for supplicants. Halvard stands in front of it, at the near corner of the wall-jut, where he can see down the passage in both directions and be visible from the cross-lane. A body standing there has sight lines that include the approach from the Hook's interior, the passage choke-point, and the cross-lane mouth. It is not a concealed position.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Tallow and wax are the sept corner's dominant register — the chandler's storehouse breathes tallow-smell from its stock and working surfaces, and the sept's candle niches add spent-wax residue. On still mornings the two combine into a single warm-fat-and-cold-stone compound that is the corner's characteristic ambient. The tallow is the Hook's own tallow texture (the same trade that feeds Pig Tallow Lane's output); the sept candles are cheaper wax, and the smell of cheaper wax in an enclosed bay is flat and slightly acrid where better wax would be clean.

**Sound:** The passage and cross-lane junction produces a mild sound-collection effect at the corner. Voices at the choke-point carry in both directions down the passage; voices at the chandler's wall position carry toward the passage but not far down the cross-lane. Halvard's station places him where he can be heard without raising his voice by anyone stopped at the choke-point. Lane ambient: cart wheels on the cross-lane, the chandler's workers moving stock, the ordinary foot-traffic sound of the Hook in morning-to-midday hours.

**Light:** The sept bay is north-facing (the Seven-faced facing is oriented toward the lane, which runs east-west in this stretch of the Hook). The bay is in morning shadow; the candle niches are the primary light source during overcast days. The passage mouth catches early light if the sky is clear; the cold ground warms slightly by midday when sun can angle into the lane. The chandler's wall is always in shadow on its sept side.

**Texture:** Old stone — the chandler's storehouse wall is the oldest construction on the corner, its face rough where the mortar has cracked and been re-pointed multiple times. The sept bay floor is worn smooth, as noted. The passage lane surface is packed earth over old cobble, the cobble exposed where the earth has thinned near the choke-point. Lane cold rises from this surface in the morning and holds at the corner longer than it holds in the open Hook lanes.

## Fixed Props

None card-level. The sept's candles and votive objects are runtime state. The chandler's storehouse stock (tallow blocks, wax rounds, cord) is not accessible to scene actors without specific narrative warrant.

## Exits

- **Passage (Hook interior direction)** — through the choke-point and further into the Hook's interior alley network. The direction Taylor approaches from on her ward-coverage circuit.
- **Passage (outer alley direction)** — out of the choke-point and toward the Hook's outer lanes, eventually connecting to Pig Tallow Lane's adjacent ward network and the broader Flea Bottom alley system.
- **Cross-lane** — the perpendicular lane at the corner junction; connects to other Hook streets running parallel to the waterfront. This is the direction a departing body takes when leaving Halvard's station without re-entering the passage.
- **Chandler's storehouse entry** — service door on the storehouse's storefront face, not on the sept side. Accessible to the chandler's workers; not a public exit.

## Hazards

- **Passage choke:** A single loaded handcart blocks through-movement in the passage. Characters caught behind the blockage cannot move through the corner without the cart clearing; characters who need to clear quickly have no alternative route without backtracking to the cross-lane. The choke is unremarkable — this kind of blocking is routine in the Hook — but it is reliable as a constraint on exit timing.
- **Halvard's sight lines:** His station position gives him visibility over the passage approach and the cross-lane mouth simultaneously. A character who wishes to observe the sept corner without being observed by Halvard must approach from the storehouse's non-sept side or wait for the passage blockage to provide cover. The insect-feed has no such constraint; physical bodies do.
- **Cold-holding ground:** The sept corner stone retains cold longer than surrounding lanes. In scenes set at morning or in wet weather, the cold is a physical presence — feet on the sept-corner ground register the temperature differential. Extended standing at the corner in cold weather is a stamina cost; Halvard absorbs it; characters in less habitual relationships with outdoor cold notice it faster.
- **Devotional context:** The corner's function as a sept creates an implicit social register: behavior at the sept corner is legible to the Hook's residents as behavior in a devotional space. Aggression, disrespect toward the sept, or visible dismissal of Halvard's clerical function is witnessed and logged in the ward's social memory.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A Hook resident stopping at the sept bay mid-passage — genuine devotion or a pause in transit, indistinguishable from outside
- The handcart blockage arriving at a moment of heightened scene tension — the passage closing behind a character who has just entered the corner
- A sick child brought to Halvard at the storehouse wall — the ward's fever-and-poverty condition made visible at the devotional station
- The chandler's workers moving stock through the storehouse's service entrance, producing noise and movement that partially masks dialogue at the corner
- A second resident pausing at the choke-point behind the handcart, waiting — an unintended audience who cannot proceed and is in visual range of the corner
- Halvard's station empty on a day he is not at the corner — the absence as a ward-state signal
- The candle niches dark (no active candles) — indicators of ward resource state or of the sept's material condition in a given week
