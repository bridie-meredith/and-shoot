# Dragon Gate Arrival and Witch Label — Permanent Operational Infrastructure (126–129 AC)

## Description

Governing condition for Taylor's permanent operational-infrastructure identity in King's Landing: the "lunatic woman near Dragon Gate, the one the septon called a witch." The label originates at Taylor's arrival event (late summer 126 AC) and is the permanent social-physics condition of her position in the block for all three years of the arc. It is never lifted. It is never corrected. It is only layered.

This card defines: how the label functions as permanent infrastructure; how it is read by different actors; how it layers with the captain's tolerance; and the prose constraint that the label is never resolved.

## The Arrival Event

Late summer 126 AC. A market fire near Dragon Gate — cause unclear (a rendered-fat vessel overturned at a provisions stall, the fire catching quickly in the dry summer air). Taylor arrives in the open ground of the gate-approach during the chaos of the fire and the initial Watch riot-control response.

She arrives disoriented. The insects of the block respond to her distress without her directing them: flies and beetles lift visibly toward the heat in a coordinated motion that does not fit natural fire-response patterns. The coordination is involuntary on Taylor's part — her distress-response fires the ambient-sense in a way that manifests as visible insect behavior before she has orientation to suppress it.

Three gold cloaks are present on riot-control. They see the insects lift. One of them later files a report noting "the animals went strange near the foreign woman." The Watch report does not use the category "witch"; it uses the category "unusual."

The ward septon Aldyn is present at the sept's perimeter, managing his parishioners' distance from the fire. He sees the insects lift. He sees Taylor at the center of it. He shouts "witch" in a voice that carries across the smoke-filled market approach. The shout lands in the ambient crowd before anyone has processed what they saw.

Taylor is held overnight in the Dragon Gate guardhouse. She is questioned in Old Valyrian, which she does not speak. The Watch officers question her in basic Common Tongue with Westerosi accent; she understands but cannot respond in register that would clear the "foreign lunatic" classification. She says almost nothing. Nothing she says is coherent in context.

The next morning, the precinct captain reviews the case. The septon's "witch" shout, the Watch officers' "the animals went strange" report, the woman's foreign-affect non-response, and the minimal public-safety value of detaining a non-violent foreign lunatic produce the administrative calculus: "lunatic-not-worth-paperwork." He signs the release. The septon's "witch" shout has traveled through the block's smallfolk whisper network overnight. By the time Taylor leaves the guardhouse, the label is the ambient block description of her.

## How the Label Functions

**The label is permanent operational infrastructure.** It is not a temporary designation that will be resolved by demonstrated harmlessness. It is the first and lasting public identification of Taylor in King's Landing. Three years later, when the Watch processes her under persons-risk protocols, the intake form notes "witch-labeled foreign national, prior watch management note." The label is in the record from day one to the final day.

**Taylor cannot correct the label without disclosing what she actually is.** Correcting "witch" would require explaining why the insects moved. Explaining why the insects moved would require disclosing the power. Disclosing the power would mean the Watch processing her as a parahuman-equivalent capability rather than as a foreign lunatic — a significantly more urgent institutional response. The misfiling as lunatic is her protection. She cannot maintain the protection without maintaining the label.

**The label generates specific social physics:**
- **Watch indifference:** The management note is the captain's operational designation. Below the captain's level, Watch officers in the Dragon Gate precinct treat Taylor as a low-priority managed problem — the witch the captain lets alone. They do not actively investigate her; they do not extend her assistance; they note her presence in patrol reports. She registers on their awareness as "category: managed-unusual, no current action required."
- **Merchant wariness:** The block's merchants know the label. They extend commercial transactions (she buys things; they sell things) without extended social contact. They do not ask questions. They do not invite conversation beyond the transactional. The specific wariness of people who have decided that a person is strange-but-tolerated: they are comfortable as long as nothing unusual happens.
- **Septon hostility:** Continuous, low-temperature, pastoral. See `oc-ward-septon-dragon-gate`. The septon's position is: he named her witch; the Watch did not act on it; his assessment is on record; he maintains the assessment indefinitely.
- **Smallfolk ambient:** The block's smallfolk population knows the label through the whisper network. Their relationship to the label follows the standard Westerosi superstition-frame response (see `cond-westerosi-superstition-frame`): she is "touched," strange-but-tolerated, someone the world is slightly different around. Not actively threatening; not one of us; there.

## The Layering Mechanism

The label never resolves. It layers.

**Layer 1 (arrival):** The septon's "witch" — active Faith-institutional designation.

**Layer 2 (morning after arrival):** The captain's "lunatic-not-worth-paperwork" — Watch administrative designation. This does not replace Layer 1; it overlays it. The witch-label is now covered by a Watch management note that functionally suspends its enforcement, but the label underneath is not removed.

**Layer 3 (developing through Act 1):** The block's social physics absorb the compound identity. "The witch the captain lets alone" is the operational synthesis of Layers 1 and 2. This is a half-step above "witch they haven't moved on yet" in practical terms, but it is not clearance. The smallfolk read: she is unusual, the Watch tolerates her, the Faith doesn't trust her, the Watch's tolerance is not the same as the Watch's approval.

**Layer 4 (Act 2, as network builds):** Taylor's block-network activity adds a further overlay: "the foreign woman who knows things." This does not change the underlying label; it adds a functional description on top. Some block residents find her useful (she knows things, she has Watch tolerance); this utility registers in their interaction without changing the fundamental category.

**Layer 5 (Act 3, persons-risk category activated):** The persons-risk procedures activate the witch-label as an "unusual-capability indicator" for administrative purposes. The label that was protection (the witch-classification kept her in the innocuous-foreign-lunatic category) becomes a liability flag when the persons-risk category reads it differently. The same label, unchanged, reads differently in a different institutional context.

## The Label Is Never Lifted

**Prose constraint:** The label is never corrected in any scene. No character tells Taylor the label has been resolved, cleared, or superseded. No scene shows the block's ambient social reading of Taylor shifting from "witch" to something without that core. No character, regardless of relationship to Taylor, ever says anything equivalent to "you've earned your place here."

The captain's tolerance does not rescind the label. Mira's genuine attachment does not change the block's ambient reading. The three years of operational presence do not move Taylor out of the "touched/witch" social category. She dies in a Watch guardhouse on a persons-risk charge, and her intake form still has "witch-labeled foreign national" in it.

**Why this constraint matters:** The label is the book's structural irony at the identity level. Taylor cannot explain herself in this world. The misfiling that protects her is the label that eventually contributes to her processing. The label is both cover and cage — and she cannot correct it because the correction requires the disclosure that would remove the protection before the alternative protection was in place. There is no alternative protection. The label is permanent.

## Auditor Enforcement

**Primary violations to flag:**
1. A scene where the label is described as having been corrected or superseded. Label-permanence violation.
2. A scene where a character tells Taylor she has earned acceptance/legitimacy on the block. Soft register violation.
3. A scene where the septon abandons his assessment without formal institutional grounds for doing so. Character-fence violation.
4. A scene where Taylor's persons-risk processing omits the witch-label as an intake indicator. Continuity violation.
5. The captain's management note described as formal legal protection equivalent to cleared status. See `cond-kl-authority-dragon-gate-129ac`.
