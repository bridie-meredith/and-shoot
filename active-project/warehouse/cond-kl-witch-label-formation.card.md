# KL Witch-Label Formation — Without Visible Power-Display Trigger (Pre-Dance, ~125 AC)

## Description

How the witch-label forms in King's Landing when there is no visible power-display trigger — no swarm event, no fire that does not burn, no supernatural occurrence anyone can point to. In this project, the label forms from behavioral expression of Taylor's flicker: she tenses before things happen, she is not where she is expected when notable events occur, she has said things that turned out to be true. The label forms through whisper-chain mechanics specific to Flea Bottom and the lower city, and it transforms as it travels up KL's social hierarchy from street-level curiosity to court-level threat-category.

**This card is the KL-specific version of `cond-westerosi-superstition-frame` and `cond-crownlands-superstition-frame-125ac`.** The prior cards were authored for a project in which Taylor's swarm-control was the visible trigger. In this project, the flicker's involuntary behavioral expression is the trigger — different in character, different in the label it produces, different in the institutional threat it generates.

## Sensory Impact

The witch-label has no direct sensory expression — it is a social fact, not an environmental condition. Its presence is felt in the specific quality of attention Taylor receives: the slight rearrangement at the water-carrier's corner when she passes, the way conversations in the rendering-yard don't quite resume at normal volume until she is out of earshot, the older woman who presses a small bundle of warding herbs into Taylor's hand without explanation. These are not dramatic moments; they are the texture of being seen-as-strange in a community where strange has a specific category.

## Duration

The label is dynamic, not persistent. It forms, accumulates, and transforms over the story's arc. It cannot be dispelled — once the whisper-chain has it, it stays in the network. But its character changes: from harmless-eccentric to valuable-strange to patron-asset to court-level threat.

## How the Label Forms Without a Visible Trigger

The witch-label in ASOIAF Westeros does not require supernatural spectacle. The Westerosi superstition frame has a long-standing category for people who "know things" — the wise woman, the touched, the one the gods have marked. This category does not require visible power. It requires consistent behavior that falls outside the normal register.

**Taylor's flicker as involuntary behavioral expression:**
The flicker is invisible to observers as a power-act. What is visible is what it does to Taylor's body and speech:

- **The tension before**: Taylor's body registers something before anyone else's does — she goes still, her attention shifts, a micro-adjustment in posture that the practiced eye reads as pre-event bracing. Flea Bottom is full of practiced eyes.
- **The not-being-where-expected**: Taylor sometimes turns away from a location minutes before something happens there. She leaves the corner by the rendering yard; twenty minutes later, a Watch sweep comes through. She was not hiding; she was following a flicker. Observers do not know that.
- **The things she said that turned out true**: The flicker leaks in speech — a fragment spoken aloud, a question about a future event that is too specific to be a guess. She does not always realize she has said something notable. Others remember.
- **The absence of flinch**: Taylor does not flinch at things that should startle. Her nervous system has recalibrated from Gold Morning; small threats register below her threshold. Observers interpret this as foreknowledge.

**The distinction from the prior config**: In the swarm-control projects, the witch-label formed around a visible event that required explanation. In this project, the label forms around a pattern — accumulated over weeks — of behavior that does not fit the normal register. There is no single event to point to. The label is the community's attempt to categorize a pattern that defies ordinary explanation. This makes it slower to form and harder to pin on any single incident, but also more resistant to refutation (there is no "you saw wrong" moment — the pattern is real, even if the explanation is wrong).

## Whisper-Chain Mechanics: Flea Bottom

The specific mechanics of rumor travel in Flea Bottom (see also `cond-flea-bottom-social-physics`):

**Information velocity**: High. Flea Bottom's dense housing, shared water sources, and communal work spaces mean that observations are shared quickly within the district. A notable event at the rendering yard is known at the eel-seller's corner within hours.

**The chain's amplification tendency**: Each retelling adds interpretation. "She left before the Watch came" becomes "she knew the Watch was coming." "She said something about fire and it rained" becomes "she called the rain." The amplification is not malicious fabrication; it is the normal human process of making patterns coherent. The accumulation over weeks produces a consistent narrative that is more coherent than the individual observations warrant.

**The community's ambivalence**: Flea Bottom already has figures who are "touched" or "strange" — the old woman who talks to the crows, the former maester's apprentice who knows too much about poisons, the dockside water-carrier who seems to know everyone's business. Taylor is not the first strange figure in the district. What makes her different: she is foreign (not a familiar figure from a known local family), she is adult (not a child eccentric tolerated because children are eccentric), and her pattern-behavior has a precision to it that the other strange figures lack. The touched old woman's predictions are vague; Taylor's are specific.

**What "witch" means at street level in Flea Bottom**: Not supernatural power in the formal sense. "Witch" at Flea Bottom means: someone whose knowing exceeds what ordinary sense allows, who may or may not be dangerous, whose good will is worth cultivating and whose ill will is worth fearing. It is an asset-or-threat category, not a supernatural-creature category. The Flea Bottom whisper chain does not call Taylor a witch with a capital W; it says she "has something" and "knows things."

## The Threat-Valence Specific to KL

In a rural village, the witch-label generates local social management (keep her favorable, avoid her ill will, route the Faith septon to handle it if it gets bad). In King's Landing, the label has institutional reach that a rural village lacks.

**Institutional enforcement channels present in KL but not in a village:**
- The City Watch: if the witch-label generates a formal complaint or escalates to a Watch-trigger event, the Watch has both the authority and the incentive to act on it
- The Faith Militant (if applicable in this period): the Sept of Baelor's institutional reach in KL is greater than a rural septon's informal advocacy; a complaint to the Faith in KL has more institutional traction
- The Citadel's maester network: maesters attached to noble households report through the Citadel's channels; a notable street-level phenomenon in KL is more likely to reach a maester's attention than in a village
- The Hightower intelligence network: Otto Hightower's political infrastructure is specifically attentive to persons of unknown provenance and unusual characteristics in KL; the witch-label, once it reaches the right ears, is a trigger for Hightower intelligence attention

The label is more dangerous in KL than elsewhere not because the superstition is more intense but because the institutional apparatus capable of acting on it is far larger and more connected.

## Label-Transform Up the Social Hierarchy

**Street level (Flea Bottom and lower-city districts):**
"Strange woman who knows things." Asset-or-threat. Community ambivalence. The label circulates in the informal whisper network and is mostly managed through social behavior (keep her favorable, avoid provoking her). This is the label's starting state.

**Middle level (factors, minor merchants, the Watch's informant layer):**
"Prophetess of uncertain provenance." By the time the label reaches the factors and merchants who operate as information intermediaries between the lower city and the noble quarter, it has gained coherence. The specific incidents are sorted, the most striking ones are the ones that survive, and the label has acquired the vocabulary of the prophetic. She is not just "strange"; she "sees things."

**Patron level (when it reaches the Rhaenyra-adjacent patron's attention):**
"Prophetic asset: Flea Bottom witch with accurate near-future readings." The patron's reception is the key transformation. The patron does not hear "witch" as a threat category; the patron hears "witch" as a capability category. In the factional pre-Dance political environment, a person who can read near-future states with any reliability is a resource, not a problem. The patron reaches down to Taylor because the witch-label has been filtered through the patron's factional-asset calculus.

**Court level / Hightower network (eventual endpoint if the label propagates far enough):**
"Foreign witch of Flea Bottom — no documented origin, displays behaviors consistent with prophetic faculty, attached to [patron's faction]." The court-level threat reading is not about Taylor's power; it is about her as a factional asset. If she gives one faction an informational advantage, the other faction has a strong incentive to neutralize her. The Hightower network's interest in her is the label at its most dangerous stage.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-flea-bottom-social-physics`:** That card describes the information-velocity and whisper-chain mechanics in detail. This card focuses on what the witch-label specifically is and how it transforms; that card covers the broader information infrastructure the label travels through.

**With `cond-kl-feudal-physics-mirror`:** The label's danger is partly a function of KL's institutional architecture. This card describes the label; the feudal-physics card describes the institutions capable of acting on it.

**With `cond-patron-amplification-theory-mirror`:** The patron's reception of the label (as asset, not threat) is a specific instance of label-transform. The patron's wrong theory is partly an extension of the label's Flea Bottom character — "she knows things near dragons" is the label's translation of the near-dragon distress the patron observes.

**With Taylor's behavior**: The flicker's involuntary behavioral expression is the source material for the label. This card is the community's interpretation of that source material — the label is real (something is happening) and wrong (the mechanism is not what the label says it is).

**For auditor use:** Any scene where the witch-label requires a visible power-display as trigger is a violation (the label forms from behavioral pattern, not event). Any scene where the label generates an immediate institutional response without the escalation process described here is a violation. Any scene where the patron describes Taylor as a witch in a threat-frame (rather than asset-frame) during the alignment phase is a violation.
