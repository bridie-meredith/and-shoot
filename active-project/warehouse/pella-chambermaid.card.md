# Pella — chamber-maid (the clever one)

*Naming/era note: protagonist card slug `saerys-targaryen`; here she is **Gael**. Jaehaerys's peace,
Gael age ~6–9. **Strongest maid-candidate** for the Book II co-conspirator role (OQ-CL2) — built as a
candidate, NOT committed; the principal chooses.*

## Description

The sharp one: quick, bright, observant, ambitious, and well-liked below-stairs — the chamber-maid who
*does* go outside on errands and *does* notice the herb-smell, the odd hours, the jars she's told not
to open. A poor girl with more wit than her station has any use for, hungry for betterment and clever
enough to find it. Everything that makes her a slight risk in Book I (she pays attention; she reaches
the world; she wants more) is exactly the engine of the Book II maid: the face, the hands, the legs of
an operation a caged princess runs through one trusted pair of eyes. If the maid is anyone, she's
Pella.

## Voice

**Register:** quick, bright, town-clever smallfolk Common Tongue; reads a room fast; asks good
questions and knows when not to; a little forward for a maid, charming enough to be forgiven it. Warm,
but with a self-interested edge that the years (and Gael) could turn to fierce loyalty.

**Samples (synthesized):**
- *(forward, smiling)* "You'll want this from the apothecary, my lady — and there's a better one down
  by the river sells it cheaper and asks fewer questions. I could go. I'm good at fewer questions."
- *(noticing)* "The grey sleeves smelt of the still-room three days running, and you weren't at
  needlework those afternoons. …I'm not saying it to anyone, my lady. I'm saying it to *you*. So you
  know I noticed, and I didn't tell."
- *(ambition showing)* "I don't mean to carry water my whole life. A clever girl can rise, if she
  picks the right mistress to be clever *for*."

## Taste

- Being useful in a way that's *noticed* and *rewarded*; rising
- A mistress worth being clever for; a secret worth keeping
- The street, the market, the world outside the walls — she likes the air of it
- Outwitting people who underestimate a maid

## Pet Peeves

- **Being treated as furniture** — *the unsaid rule that a maid is interchangeable.* It galls her; she
  knows she's worth more. **Severity: strong.**
- **Wasting her wits on dull work** — *carrying and fetching when she could be running things.*
  **Severity: soft.**
- **Being lied to by her own mistress** — *she'd rather be trusted with a dangerous truth than fed a
  safe lie; the lie is what would lose her.* **Severity: strong.**

## Stats

- **age:** ~15–16 · **faith:** the Seven, lightly — she's too practical to be devout
- **origin:** smallfolk; poor, sharp, ambitious; well-connected below-stairs
- **status:** chamber-maid to Gael — **maid-candidate: HIGH** (the strongest Book II maid; OQ-CL2)

## Relationships

- **Gael** — the mistress she's chosen to be clever for; the relationship that begins transactional
  ("bring me this") and is built, over books, to become the one bond that matters. Pre-romantic warmth
  is *latent here*, per the outline — not stated, available.
- **Nona** — the dull chamber-maid; Pella is fond and a little impatient with her.
- **Nesta** — the tiring-girl; her gossip-ally below-stairs; her ear to the court.
- **A cousin** *(unnamed)* — the helper she could recruit into the operation (cf. II.2).

## Thematic Purpose

The maid the whole back half of the series turns on, in seed form. Built as the strongest candidate
for the Book II co-conspirator: her wit + reach + want-of-betterment are precisely the qualities a
caged operator needs in her one channel to the world, and her hatred of being treated as furniture is
the exact dignity Gael ends up refusing to spend (the tempering, III.5). In Book I she is a small,
charming risk (she notices); the arc converts the risk into the bond. **Until the principal commits the
maid, keep her a candidate, not a fact.**

## Look

Bright-eyed, quick-moving, neat and a little vain about it; an easy smile she uses on purpose; deft
hands; the alert, sizing-up look of someone always reading the room for an angle and a kindness at
once.

## Hard Fences

1. **Smallfolk, setting seen from below.** No notion of cultivation or what Gael truly is — but, unlike
   the others, she *notices the evidence* (smells, hours, jars) and keeps it to herself as a deposit of
   trust. She reads a *secret*, never the true *system*.
2. **Candidate, not committed.** She is the leading option for the Book II maid; the card does not
   declare her the maid. Do not write her as the confirmed co-protagonist until OQ-CL2 is ruled.
3. **Pre-romantic warmth is latent, never explicit (Book I).** The bond is built; in Gael's childhood
   it is loyalty and partnership, not romance. The reader infers the future; the text does not state it.

## Default Stance

Half a step ahead of her duties, having already noticed the thing you didn't mention, deciding whether
to mention that she noticed.

## Off-Screen Cadence

Running errands beyond the walls; trading gossip with Nesta; charming the gate guards and the
market-folk; quietly cataloguing her mistress's odd hours; thinking about how a clever girl rises.
