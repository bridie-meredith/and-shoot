# Series Tone Constraints — taylor-hebert-jaehaerys (84–101 AC Config)

## Description

Project-level tonal law. Defines the operational registers this series runs in, the registers it does not run in, and the specific escalation-as-structure rule that governs how scenes must be constructed. These constraints apply to all agents across all scenes. The auditor loads this card when reviewing scene-level register drift.

## Sensory Impact

Tonal constraints have no ambient sensory expression. Their expression is felt in scene velocity, in the ratio of action beats to reflection beats, in whether a scene's center of gravity is forward-moving or interior.

## Duration

Persistent across all episodes of this project.

## The Primary Register

**Fast. Pulpy. Dramatic.**

Scene pace is driven by physical stakes and immediate consequence. The sentence-level and beat-level writing style prioritizes forward velocity. A scene that lingers must be lingering on something that escalates. Pulpy register means the world's drama is felt in the body. Dramatic register means the series acknowledges that what is happening matters and treats it accordingly.

## The Prohibited Registers

**No chess-match scheming as the primary mode.** Planning happens in motion or in brief, and then the situation changes and the plan is worthless anyway.

**No extended introspection as the primary mode.** Interior moments happen in the gaps between action beats, not as extended set-pieces.

**No teaching-the-reader-the-world as a scene function.** Lore dumps are a register violation.

**No scheming-through-foreknowledge as the primary mode.** Taylor's ASOIAF foreknowledge is orientation, not script.

## The Escalation Rule

**Taylor's escalation-reflex is structural.** When threatened, she escalates. She does not de-escalate by default. Note: `cond-shard-behavioral-weight` makes this tendency harder to self-correct — the Shard's thumb on the scale amplifies the existing reflex without adding a new one.

Every escalation has consequences. The Westeros world does not absorb escalation neutrally. A child who responds to an armed man with unusual sharpness creates a record.

**The escalation ratchet is one-directional within a scene.** A scene that has escalated does not de-escalate. The new equilibrium is the floor of the next scene's starting tension.

## The Violence-as-Fluency Rule

Taylor speaks the language of organized violence more fluently than almost anyone in this world. This is both an asset and an isolating condition. This is allowed to cost her. It is not allowed to be corrected into a softer default.

## The Cost-Migration Rule

**Early escalations cost Taylor personally.** Personal cost phase: S1 primarily.

**Mid-arc escalations cost the coalition.** Taylor's internal failure modes are the mechanism: self-isolation, distributed control as substitute for trust. Coalition cost begins no later than S2 mid-arc.

**Late escalations cost the institution.** Suppression-as-policy (not incident response) must be reached no later than mid-S3.

The cost-migration rule is not optional.

## The Council-Close Rule

The Great Council of 101 AC is the series end-state. It is not a resolution. It is a stress-test.

**Costs become final at Council close.** Debts that have been accumulating across the series come due at the Council.

**Catharsis before the Dance is prohibited.** Taylor knows the Dance of Dragons follows the Great Council regardless of what the Council decides. Any scene that implies her work resolved something in a way that will survive the Dance is violating the structural prohibition on catharsis. The audience should feel that the work mattered and that it was not enough — simultaneously, not sequentially.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-shard-behavioral-weight`:** The escalation rule and the Shard rule are additive, not redundant. Load both for any scene involving Taylor's decision-making.

**For auditor use:** Flag any scene where the primary action is uninterrupted planning, strategy session, or introspective monologue with no forward physical movement. Flag any scene where Taylor de-escalates by default without external constraint forcing her to. Flag any scene at Council close that implies resolution rather than stress-test. Flag any catharsis that arrives before the Dance horizon.

**For impersonator use:** The fast/pulpy/dramatic constraint means Taylor's output in any scene should be short, targeted, and forward-moving. Her internal voice does not over-explain. If you find yourself writing extended internal monologue, you have drifted; trim and accelerate.
