I paid the building-keeper at the corner-room threshold — Flea Bottom, the bottommost ward of King's Landing, stone tenements packed around hook-channels; rent went in copper stars to whoever held the corner, no titles and no guild rolls, subsistence-class permanent — the anonymous copper-star transaction purchasing entry.

The drain channel put the yard on one side and the alley-mouth on the other; I had already counted what was between them. I crossed — wet stone gap at the yard crossing, footing cost a stride's width. Coll lifted his eyes. He worked the net — net-mender, same corner since before I arrived, the kind of presence a ward accumulates the way buildings accumulate moss, noticed without registering as notable; his range of interest ran one street and he did not ask what strangers were. I circled the block. I passed the tallow-stall — the smoke-funnel drawing within arm's reach of the circuit path, flea-bottom open air giving over to tallow-smoke.

I dropped the pack. Coll's needle moved, his attention not landing on my face. I set both feet even on the flagstones. The sense ran the walls and the walls gave it back unchanged, because nothing here had called for what I was not doing; the sense ran the block and the block was what it was, and the block was not requiring anything of the kind I was no longer doing.

I threaded the needle, working corner off the Hook, Coll's net-frame at the far edge: midday under overcast, the working position's spatial anchor settled. The needle crossed the mesh — thread-resistance and metal-smoothness, the hand-sensation taking its first count of the day. Every warm body in the block was legible at the work-level density I was not working against.

The walls cooled, releasing day-warmth into the overcast. I passed the well-step, the stone wet from mid-morning draws — sidefoot past the standing pool. The hands were on the needle and the needle was what they were for today, and this was what having hands was, here, in this place, at this work.

The boots struck the cobbles. The city-watch passed the Hook's bend: four-body patrol in gold cloaks, the working corner with unobstructed line-of-sight to the bend. I held the eyes.

Coll folded the net. The day held under the count I had been running, and the weight of what I had not done was in the count.

A girl stepped into the alley-mouth from the meat-stall direction — Wren, ward of the stitch-maker's household two buildings over, a Flea Bottom arrangement where the household took a child for light work in exchange for shelter and two meals; she stitched trim-ends, had been here long enough that she was not new to it, and watched what was happening before she acted on it — and the approach read as deliberate, not incidental, in the narrowness of the way.

"You walked the block three times this morning," she said. "I wasn't looking for you."

I had already mapped the observation-radius and run the circuit count before the held label registered that I had. I lifted my eyes.

"Nothing for you here," I said. "Go on."

She moved her eyes to my hands before she spoke. "There were flies on the meat-stall," she said. "There weren't any on your hand." What the girl saw was what the read should have returned — the girl had arrived at it without the insects.

I held the eyes. She crossed the street.

I lifted the needle.
