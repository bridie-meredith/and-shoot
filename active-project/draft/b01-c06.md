*Yesterday the coverage held; the intelligence kept flowing up the Jarvis line to Otto, and the arrangement that pays for the quiet on Sera's question ran the way it had run since I agreed to it. Movement patterns, not persons — that was the line I had been delivering on, who passed which junction, which passage went unused. This morning the count runs the Hook at its standard density. The harm I can prevent is still the only column the accounting closes. The form is the same. What Otto asks for next will not be.*

---

The handcart sat across the lane-mouth, spanning it wall to wall, and the crowd that should have moved through stacked up against it at the junction, every body in the count compressed into the same narrow stretch of paving with nowhere to go. I held my working position and watched them bunch.

Then one of them came out of the press toward me — crossed the whole width of it to reach me, a small body I had known by sight for as long as I had run this ward, filed since the first morning as a ward-child of the stitch-house, a familiar profile holding to its stitch-house edge. The feed had her exactly placed. What she did now came from a layer the feed does not measure, the layer between the bodies and not between the things I count, and I heard her before I had any category to put her in.

"There's a way past. Cut before the cart, by the tallow-boiler's wall — the south court. It's narrow, but it's there. I been through," she said.

I took it. I went where she had sent me, before the cart and along the tallow-boiler's wall into the south court, an angle-gap I had never used, the morning light falling away to a dimness against the close stone as the passage narrowed and the wall came up beside me.

I opened the coverage-notes and started the entry. I marked the contact-role field the way I mark every contact — ward-resident, Hook, routine — the same three strokes I had laid down a hundred times.

Then I came to the field that wanted her name, and I held my hand over it. I knew what the name would cost if I wrote it down — what it would make legible, in which substrate, to whom, once it was in the record where a record can be read and asked after and moved along. This was the one omission I ran the full accounting for and still left empty. I left the field blank. The blank was not a thing I forgot to do; it was the thing I did. Then I closed the entry, the name unwritten and the entry whole around the place where it was not.

The Jarvis channel opened in the late-morning window, the standard hour for the asks that are not urgent. I was still at my working position when it came, the lane gone still around me and the tallow-smoke off the stitch-house pooled at floor-level in the dead air, settled low against the stone where I stood in it.

I opened the message. I read it. Then I read it again, because the unit had changed under my hand and I needed to be sure of it. The channel did not want movement patterns this time. It wanted the names of the Black-faction ward elders — not who passes which junction, not which passage goes unused, but the persons themselves, set down by name. A node holds a frequency and a heading and the question of who it takes instruction from. A name holds a body that the heading can find. I had moved bodies along a record before, in a place I do not say, and the bodies had moved when the record named them; that was the whole of what a name does that a node does not. I had priced this conversion four months ago and deferred it, and the channel was asking for it now.

I pulled the coverage-memory. I did not need to deploy anything new — the elders were already in the long-pattern record, meeting-frequency and errand-direction and who they receive instruction from, nodes I had held for months. The ask only wanted me to convert the nodes to names.

I filled the form. Four names, pulled out of the pattern and written down as persons, the calm holding in my hands the way it holds when the compiling runs ahead of the deciding. Four names, and the form complete.

Then I lowered the form. I did not send it. I held it filled and unsent in the loaded quiet, the names compiled and the channel open and the send not made.

I opened the accounting ledger. The board came down heavy into my hands, its weight settling across my palms, and I ran the accounting the way I run it before I send anything — honestly, both arms at full weight.

I wrote the first arm: the four names against Sera's protection. The stylus dragged as I wrote, catching on the surface, each name coming out of me against a friction in the hand, the resistance of setting a person down where a person can be priced.

I wrote the second arm: the omission's own risk against Sera's exposure — what it would cost her if I held the names back, weighed against what it costs to send them.

Then I marked the Red Keep coverage, the record's far end, and the far end resolved to a body. Fourteen, placed, held at an arm's length that is not mine, kept exactly where she is by an arm that is not hers — a body I have watched through the feed and never met and never will. The four names travel so that body stays where the arm has set her. The gain has a face. I marked the coverage past the face and did not write the face down.

I closed the accounting. Both arms had run clean, at full weight, and the framework arrived where the framework arrives when the terms are stated true. I squared the form against the board.

I sealed it. The accounting had closed and this was the place it closed to; I sealed the four names into the channel and let the form go, the first persons the arrangement had ever asked of me by name, the breach made and not stumbled into, the framework operating correctly on the premises I had agreed to. The courier took it from my hands and moved off down the line, and the names were in the arrangement's downstream now, past my reach.

Then I opened the ward-coverage notes. My fingers settled on them and did not open them at once. Four names had gone down the channel, and here the contact-source field still read ward-resident, Hook, routine, and under it the blank where I had not written a name. The contrast sat in two substrates at the same time — the four sent, the one withheld — and I opened the notes to read the blank I had left where a name is not. The blank was where I had left it. I closed the notes.
