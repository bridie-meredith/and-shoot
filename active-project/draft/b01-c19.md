*The counting runs at full scale now, and the channel runs both ways. Otto's apparatus takes my readings through Jarvis and pays for them by keeping the question of Sera's parentage quiet, and the arrangement holds while the work holds. I have read the court at its own scale long enough that the contempt for it sits in me with nowhere to go — accurate, complete, attached to nothing I do next. And somewhere in the upper city a name has been put to the woman who maps Flea Bottom. The name has not reached me yet. It is traveling.*

---

Before dawn the trough released its bundle, and I opened it where I knelt. Not the fortnight-weight of the last drop — a single folded sheet, Jarvis's own notation, and I held it up to read it. The grey-dark that fills the room above the rendering works in the hour before first light is not full dark and not lamplight; it is the grey that asks you to tilt the page and hold it close, and I have read every dead drop of eleven months in it. The sheet named a corridor. A court-tier reading, not the lower-city surveillance I have run for a year: a chamberlain in the Red Keep's outer administrative ring — a rank above the wards I map — whose corridor patterns had run wrong for six weeks. Confirm the role. Map the contact interval. Name the faction. I set the sheet on the bare floor and opened the column.

The form was new. The content was not. The chamberlain's corridor sat inside the bottlefly nodes I already keep; the protection-line ratio held; there was no part of the request that fell outside the architecture I had agreed to, and so the accounting ran clean. While it ran, the standing nodes returned the ambient outer-ring feed — the court keeping itself for no audience the lower city would ever name, performing its function into the dark — and the feed carried the color the fortnight had taught me to read. The column stayed open. There was no entry-format that could take the color, and so it stayed in the column, and the naming of it did not neutralize it.

The accounting was still running when it caught the thing it had been recording. The column carried eleven months under it, and the weight of those months was the floor this entry stood on; the entry going forward was the eleven months agreeing with it. I ran the factional-reading column and it came up in the position it always comes up in, the same ratio, the same structure — the way a track wears into stone once enough feet have moved the same line. There is a name for a shape like that. The shape was one I had set into a column before, a different accounting in a different place, for reasons that did not hold, and the track is the same track whatever wears it. I reached the name. The stylus stopped above the column and my hand held it there, a beat past the mark. The column was named, and the entry it would take had not changed where it went. The naming was not the stop. The entry going forward unchanged was. The structure assembled exactly where it had always assembled. The feed returned a groom lifting a message-case on an interval that mismatched the morning rotation, and I filed it into the column whose shape I had just read — the reading having moved nothing about where the line landed.

Over four days I opened the three outer-ring corridors I had held for months, and the chamberlain's pattern returned inside four hours and resolved across two days: a hand-off at a pillar junction before dawn, the courier out through the service-gate, the interval repeating until it was too regular to be accident — a routing node, message-traffic running to a Black-faction contact in the harbor approaches. The Jarvis channel took the compiled reading in the form the arrangement always takes, and the drop closed the request. I closed the column. I lifted the stylus. I brought it to the ledger-edge and it came to rest there, beside the closed column, not down and away from it. The contempt did not enter the accounting. It sat alongside it, the way a person's name stays in a ledger after the body is dead — complete, unreferenced, adjacent to every line and not itself a line. The distance the stylus did not travel was the whole of it. Clarity forecloses nothing.

Three days after the drop I ran the reciprocity approach for Daven. He is a tallow-render hand I have worked seven months — a lower Flea Bottom node who passed me his lane's open windows twice a season for a small protection-reading I kept on it; he tells me when the lane is clear, I keep it covered, and that exchange is the whole of the channel. I took the corner position at the Tallow Croft mouth. The cold came up off the stone and the render-smell hung close, the same one that saturates my own room, met here in the open lane. Second-bell passed the corner and the corner read empty. The vat-house shutter was shut over the east window — shut, where it has stood open across forty-three approaches in seven months. I have the count of them. The open window was the constant, and the count is what told me the shutter was not weather. It is not a wall. It is a number arriving down through the service-layers, and the number had resolved before I reached the lane. I took the lane-position at third-bell. The corner stayed empty. Daven did not come to it.

I walked the lane. My stride did not break at the empty corner and my hands stayed loose at my sides, and the lane ran on under me, one node lighter. Someone in the upper city had put a name to the woman who maps Flea Bottom; the name had traveled down to the people who know my accessible face, and Daven had done the arithmetic anyone with a working accounting would do. I opened the running-architecture record. The coverage-map dropped his node — his lane runs now through the standing bottlefly-node at the Croft intersection, without the reciprocity-layer, a lane like any other lane — and I closed the record. The contempt is complete. The continuation is unchanged. The architecture is still running, one node lighter than it ran this morning, and the count that severed him sits in no record I have written today.
