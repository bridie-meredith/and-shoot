*The argument the septon left in my hand had not resolved, and I had not stopped carrying it. The circuit ran the way the circuit always ran.*

I came up into the lane-junction at the Rushwick margin where the hill's stone skirt cuts the north sightline, and the junction opened the way it opens every morning — broken on one side, lane-open on the other, the circuit-start coordinate the count uses to know where I am. The feed returned the chandler-corner without remark.

The watcher-boy was at the water-point, weight set on one foot the way a boy sets it when he has been told to stay where he is and means to. The basket-woman stood at the lane-mouth with her body angled outward, facing the open lane, the angle her weight made saying nothing she would have agreed to be saying. The apprentice — the third of them, by the chandler's door — lowered the eyes as I came on.

I followed the boy's line of attention, then the woman's, then the apprentice's, three reads taken in the feed without breaking my step; and the sightlines resolved as a coverage already in place; her own overlay slots in above it, geometric not contested.

I set my hand on the junction-stone and held it there. My weight stayed forward over my front foot. My feet did not move. The feed completed the figure and I stood through the completion, palm flat to the stone, the stone cold under my palm, my body refusing the next step until the overlay had settled where it was going to settle.

The feed delivered the gap-corridors.

Then my step landed at the close, and the circuit closes; nothing files; the geometry has updated without a corresponding entry in any column I keep.

My shoulder dropped. My breath went out long, an unmeasured exhale into the lane-air, and the held position released — not into anything I named, not into anything the accounting would have a line for, only out of the held shape and into the next one.

The Jarvis packet arrived at the feed-station, the standard channel's standard receipt at the standard hour, the Hook's enclosed quiet sitting around the intake surface the way it sits around any fixed-point inside the coverage radius. I broke the seal — my thumb under the wax, the small sharp crack of it in the quiet of the room — and the documents came open under my hand. The handler-rotation named the chain-of-responsibility down its column, the names and the dates in their possessive notation, scheduling adjustments running clean. Below the rotation, the feed-geometry met the coverage-gap where I had been expecting it to meet it, the morning's gap accounted, no surprise in the meeting.

I logged the Aemond-name where it took its place in the handler-rotation row, logistics object among logistics objects, the interior noting the weight and filing it flat. My hand set on the next entry. I set the stylus down. The hand stayed where it set, palm to the worked wood of the desk, the stylus released along the edge of the page.

Aemond Targaryen — twelve years old, Vhagar's rider since Driftmark, the prince Otto's apparatus uses where consequence needs a body; a name in a handler-rotation is the apparatus moving the largest dragon in the sky.

I read the bread-price report from the lower Blackwater approaches, the numbers tracking the parity I had been expecting them to track, my hand moving down the page entry by entry.

The perimeter had acquired an edge it did not have at the open of the read; I noted the new arc as geometric fact, the edge a coverage-line in the feed-map rather than anything else — and my eye held on the page a beat past the perception, the body pausing before the filing closed.

I came back into the Hook ward in the evening, the lane-light gone to dusk-fall, the water-point lit low and the chandler-corner-adjacent stones already past the long shadow. The feed returned Meryn Cobb where the lane-coordinates had been holding her. The feed placed Oswyn at the water-point. The feed placed Corwick beside him.

A figure stood at Oswyn's shoulder. Tall enough that the shoulder-line set above Oswyn's, mass solid through the chest, the orientation of attention turned not toward me but along the lane in the direction Oswyn was facing — a body in the position a body takes when it has stopped walking somewhere and not yet begun walking again.

Oswyn shifted his weight as I came near, and he spoke past me into the lane-air, not toward and not away:

"Corwick. Runs errands for someone above his station. Up the hill twice this month."

His weight stayed on the back foot past the last word.

The body-map advanced on the read; the courier-name files into the spot the body has been holding empty since the water-point pass — and my step came shorter than the last, the heel landing earlier than the rhythm called for, the rhythm broken for one beat and then resumed.

I completed the circuit, my line through the ward closing out at the standard nodes, the evening pass running to its standard time.

The watcher-boy-position was empty at the water-point. The boy was gone from the stone he had been posted on; the angle his weight had made was not in the geometry anymore.

The geometry completes itself; the coverage-matrix subsumes the watcher-positions it has been adjacent to, smooth, the water-point approach holding its feed-weight unchanged under the new cover.
