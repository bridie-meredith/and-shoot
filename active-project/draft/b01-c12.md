*By now the work balanced clean every night — every part of it but two. The cost my insects carried in the lanes where the wrong eyes named them, I never set down. The route the stitch-maker walked through the one stretch I could not reach, I never set down. Two things held open against everything else that came out even, and I had stopped pretending I did not know what they were for.*

---

The insects came back to me first off the overhang-joints, the close timber where the upper stories lean in over the lanes east of the water-gate. That was the old stretch of the lower city, where the overhangs sit near enough to pass a thing hand-to-hand across the gap — the one ground my insects could not work. I spread the rest of them wide along the lane-mouth and let them bring me the open throat of the street. Then I took the gate-tower shadow before I stepped into it. The shadow fell across the lane at second bell and made the west edge of the thing, and I held my placement low and back, under the height where dense insects stop being insects and become the word these streets keep for women like me. I knew the word. I let the placement stand. The gate-tower made one wall. The rendering-yard's east wall made the other. The gap sat between them now — not a vague stretch anymore but a named ground with two stone edges, the exact lanes I had walked at dawn.

Then the insects came back off the stitch-house route — the lane Wren took from the rendering-yard up to the upper stitch-house, the stitch-maker whose name I had kept out of every report since the first one. I let her route stay in my head and nowhere else. It ran the eastern edge of everything I covered and stopped there, the one line I knew by heart and would not write.

I lifted the stylus over the new ward-cluster to the north. My hand did not come down on the source-field; it came up off the surface with the field still bare. Then I set the cluster — the first of two, the insects laid into the upper lanes where the count could hold them — and the entry under it closed clean, and the eastern lanes were not inside it. The boundary held the whole map up now, and the thing that held the boundary had never cost a single line.

---

Jarvis set the packet at the edge of the station where the packets always land and was most of the way back to the channel before I touched it. I broke the wax seal with my thumb, the small sharp crack of it loud in the quiet, and the covering-sheet came open under my hand. It named the corridor by the gate-tower's old mark — the west wall, the exact edge. I turned the sheet over. The second mark came up, the rendering-yard wall, the east boundary I had walked at dawn. Both edges of my gap, written out in the standard flat hand, the request reading the ground back to me with more precision than it had any means to fill. The apparatus had seen a shape it could not resolve and wanted exactly the lanes that would not give it up. I set the packet down and set the stylus beside it. The morning over the bay had gone warm against me, the damp burned off the stone before the wind off the water came in to clear it.

---

I took the stylus up and wrote the answer in the same flat hand as every report before it: the east-water-gate lanes not reachable within the current coverage, a fixed limit, not a thing that would pass. I did not write why. My hand stopped short of the source-field and would not come down to it. The stylus lifted with the field still bare, and the answer would go out with that clause missing. I sealed it over the empty field — and my hand knew the shape of the gesture by now. Wren's route, never written. The burned message at the Hook's south end, never written. And this. The same withhold, the third time, and I knew it for what it was and did not stop.

Jarvis took the sealed packet. The apparatus would accept the limit the way it accepted any limit and route the courier's corridor some other way, which meant no one would ever see the withhold for what it was. I opened to the part of my record where I kept Wren's weight, and I wrote the entry, and I let the weight settle where I put it. The lane-refusal was the thing that made her free movement the eastern edge of my whole map — and that one move paid off the second half of what I had owed for a long time. One debt, one mechanism, the same act settling both, and no name set down to carry it. I closed it and lifted my hand off the page and held it there a moment before I let it go.

---

The second cluster ran in the late afternoon. I sent the insects past the water-gate approach northward into the Muddy Way's upper margin and filled it, and the partial weight that had sat under the count all day went to a full one. The last open gap in the Flea Bottom coverage closed under them. With it closed, I read all five wards at once — every ward and the lanes between them, held simultaneous and whole, the first time I had ever carried the whole city in one piece. I could not pull a single node of it without leaving a shape the rest would not hold. I read the Flea Bottom approaches last, the place I had first come to cover, every way into it mapped now and held at full scale.

Then I weighed it the way I always weighed it — the gain set against the cost, the harm I stopped against the harm I caused, the whole of what I had built against the thing it was starting to look like. And reaching past any name I had for the architecture, I landed on the older word. Not a word for the thing in front of me but the shape of the thing I did when the world was ending, the one this rhymed with. It was there for one count. I held it that long and no longer. I pushed it back down and ran the count on past it — the gain, the cost, the harm-prevention, the plain operational need of the thing — and the word was not in any of that, because none of it required the word, and the gap between what I did then and what I did now was real, and the word did not help the count run.

I closed the architecture entry without it, my palm flat on the page where the stylus had been, my breath staying flat and not lifting. Then I wrote the cost of it down — the threshold crossed, the scope past walking back — in the same flat hand as every line above it. The end-of-day light had gone off the surface to dimming. I left the word out of the entry. The entry held what happened: the city carried whole, the count finished, the harm-prevention intact, and the cost set down at its full weight. The insects ran on at full deployment, the way they would run for every circuit after this one — the coverage complete, the architecture load-bearing, the gap east of the water-gate the one place the map did not go.
