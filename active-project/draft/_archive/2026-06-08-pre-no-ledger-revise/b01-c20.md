*The counting runs at full scale now, and the channel runs both ways: Otto's apparatus takes my readings through Jarvis and pays for them by keeping the question of Sera's parentage quiet, and that holds while my work holds. I have read this court long enough that the contempt for it sits in me complete, accurate, and attached to nothing I do next. The name they put to the witch-woman who maps Flea Bottom has reached the service-layers — it took a seven-month contact from me already. And the old king is dying. The Dance the realm has dreaded is at its first hour, and the apparatus is positioned to move on the succession the instant he goes. The catalogue I built is about to become the war's map.*

The servant-passages emptied before any bell. I read it in the grey-dark of the room, the sheet tilted close to get the count off it: the usual late-night traffic in the Red Keep ceasing all at once, the wrong doors opening in the wrong sequence. I had the death before the city had it. Then the Holdfast routes went live — the access lines I mapped three months back and sent through the channel — the right men moving to the right rooms at the right speed, a rehearsed thing executing against a cue only the apparatus had been waiting on. I lifted the stylus and set it to the column the apparatus had already filled. Position entry. Political entry. Cost on the lines that took it. I marked the ledger, and the marking required nothing of me that the night had not already done.

The succession bell came through the stone toward morning — the formal toll, not a house-bell, carrying across the ward-junctions. The men filed into the junctions at the counts I had logged, each entry-point taken in the order my own passage-tallies would have ranked them; I held the feed wide and watched one intersection resolve into the next, the discipline being to hold the whole and not the part. The patron channel shifted to a sequence that did not need my hand. The gate-side routes filled. I opened the feed across the lower city, and the faction-movement ran down my passage-counts as if reading them off the page.

By midday the smell of the burn reached the outer wards — combustion at ward-distance, the leading edge of it, ahead of any heat or visible smoke. The fire traced the ward-junction catalogue, propagating down the exact architecture I had built and delivered. The decommission reached me off the Jarvis route, by a channel the arrangement had never used. The message named a function and decommissioned it; no person was addressed anywhere in the wording. Its coverage, it said, would be absorbed into an existing network. My eyes held on it a beat before my hand moved to the ledger. The line it severed and the line that severed it arrived in the same flat register, and what had set the trigger — found out, or finished with — did not come down through the wording, and I did not reach for it. The arrangement had bought one thing, and the thing it bought was the silence around a girl's parentage, held against a council that could rule on it. That council was filing into the junctions to take the crown. The question had nowhere left to be asked. The apparatus took the coverage into a network it already held. I opened the ledger and marked the social-tether entry, accurate, no redraft.

The feed ran in the lanes east of the water-gate — the one ground I never let the coverage reach, the gap I held open report by report for a single child: Wren, the stitch-maker's ward who noticed what I was and chose never to ask. She was the one cost I would not enter, because to price her would be to weigh her against the architecture, and that was the line I had not crossed. The gap was her free movement. The gap was the only thing in the catalogue not accounted for. The feed read normally there. Then the smoke filled the lanes. The heat came in behind it and dispersed the insects — fire-heat at lane-range, warm enough to scatter them. They scattered. The signal went out of the lanes I had kept the feed open across, the drop landing in the exact gap I was holding for it; the one position I never costed was the position the calculus walked into, and the feed returned nothing where the return had been the reason the lane stayed lit. The east-of-water-gate lanes went blank. The signal had been there; it was not there. I lifted the stylus and held it above the blank, and opened no line. The shape the gone-signal left was a shape I had stood inside before — the same routing, the same reach held open over bodies that did not know the reach was there, in a different place for reasons that did not hold. The track is the track whatever runs it. The lift was the act and the not-descending was the entry, the distance the point did not travel down to the page being the whole of what the column would ever hold of this.

I closed the feed before I left the room — the insects dispersing to ambient range, below any useful threshold, the architecture of eleven months returning to substrate. The insects dispersed. The coverage released the wards. What dispersed was what was mine; the network the apparatus had taken was theirs. I lifted the pack — the first weight of leaving, the room turned from control-point to vacated space. I ran the ledger one more time and ran it whole, every entry walked though there was no error left to find: what I did, what it cost, what I would not let the column price. The run was the only form the record took, made once more for no reader and against no return. The contempt came to rest in the only place the ledger keeps it — beside the line, never the line, the way the name they gave me stays in the count after the cover fails. The step did not change at the threshold. The gate gave onto the road at dusk, the open sky going flat past the stone, and I took it, the wards already open behind me and the lanes already silent, the not-stopping carrying the whole of it because there was no column the stopping would have filled.
