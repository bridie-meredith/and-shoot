I took the drain angle again in the afternoon. The light had moved since morning — cutting across the tenement wall at a lower angle than it had any business doing, falling slant down the brick on the far side of the gap. I noticed the slant before I noticed I had noticed it.

The insects came back from the lane-mouth sweep. The count moved into the head the way it had been moving all day, in the order I had taught it, neither faster nor slower than I had asked of it.

The shadow filled the drain angle. I'd been watching it come for a while without naming what I was watching — the wall-shadow crossing the gap by degrees, and then crossed, and the angle gone into shade so that the gap was the darkest point in the lane.

I ran the map.

The fever-cluster corner returned its three bodies above ambient, where they had been in the morning and where they were still. The dark-junction corner returned the silence-pattern, the same negative shape it had held for days. The map returned the bodies — all of them, in their slots, where they belonged.

The accounting closed the count.

The accounting reached the ward-junction entry.

The ward-junction corner returned the junction-lane void.

The count stalled.

I held the breath I had just taken.

The line was the same line I had drawn at the alley-mouth and it came up under the same hand. There is a wrong way to come to a thing you have already understood. The wrong way begins with looking at it. I did not look at it. The hand drew where it had drawn before — same hand, same motion, an hour and an angle of light away from the first drawing — and the shape of the line was what a watching street would find a word for, and I did not give it the word.

I closed against the drain angle. The body closed before the decision did — chest into shoulder, shoulder into the brick, the weight on the rear foot as if that were the foot that had been priced — and the angle held me in the shape of not-having-looked. The drain ran below the heel. The wall took what I gave it.

The accounting closed the fever-cluster entry, and the ledger closed the dark-junction entry, and the ledger closed the ward-junction contact. The last one cost more to close than the others. The cost did not appear in the closing.

I exhaled. The exhale was not a question closing. The accounting was complete and the weight was where the weight had been, and I went on holding the angle against the dusk.
