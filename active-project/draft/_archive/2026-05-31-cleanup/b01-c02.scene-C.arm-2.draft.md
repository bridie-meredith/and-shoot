I took the drain angle. The light had moved since morning, cutting across the tenement wall at a lower angle, and the angle-gap held the late-afternoon slant the way it had held the even morning earlier. The insects returned from the lane-mouth sweep — the readings already arriving as the feet found the angle, the feed not waiting for me to settle.

The wall-shadow had crossed the gap. The drain angle had gone into shade, and the shade was the darkest point in the lane now, which was the point of the angle in this hour — the watcher unseen, the lane lit, the gap not.

I ran the map. The feet carried me through it the way the hands carried the gauge yesterday, corner by corner, and the head was elsewhere — the head was on what the map was becoming, while the feet went corner to corner the way they had gone corner to corner the day before, and the day before that. The fever-cluster corner returned three bodies above ambient.

The dark-junction corner returned the silence-pattern. The map returned the bodies — the count even, the corners in order, the sweep following the sweep that had followed the one before, the feet not needing the head, the head somewhere ahead of the feet on the same circuit by a different route.

The accounting closed the count. The hands had not stopped to write what they were doing; the accounting was running itself under the running of the feet, totting entries the way it had totted them every dusk that week, and the head was finally catching up to where the feet had already arrived. The accounting reached the ward-junction entry.

The ward-junction corner returned the junction-lane void.

I stalled the count.

I held the breath.

I drew the line. The line was the same line I had drawn at the alley-mouth, drawn now in the same hand — and the shape of this line was what a watching street would find a word for.

I closed against the drain angle. The wall at my back, the gap in shade, the shoulder against the stone where the stone met the spine — the body wedging into the angle the way the angle was built to take it, and the body knew the wedge without being told.

The accounting closed the fever-cluster entry. The ledger closed the dark-junction entry. The ledger closed the ward-junction contact, and the closing on the ward-junction cost more to close than the other two, and the cost did not appear in the closing.

I exhaled.
