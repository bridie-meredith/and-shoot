I came into the lane south of the Hook a little before the morning traffic had thinned to nothing, the single-entrance corridor opening ahead of me toward the stitch-shop lanes, and the insect-feed threaded the stitch-shop lane the way it threads any ground it has held long enough to forget it is holding it. The stitch-shop door stood open at the lane-mouth. Wren walked the lane from the shop toward the water-sellers, and the route has waypoints now — the door, the corner, the lane that takes one entrance — and a thing with waypoints is no longer a body that happens to be in range. My gaze held on the bread-seller's corner a beat before she reached it, and she reached it. I had the geometry filed before the lane closed behind her; the map takes Wren's pattern the way it takes any pattern, clean, indexed, kept — the architecture takes one more set of days and does not distinguish the keeping of a route from the keeping of a person. It has indexed lives at a scale this lane could not picture, and the keeping never asked. The ward-coverage notes received the boundary geometry.

That evening, the second circuit ran the Dragonpit margin — the outer lanes south, toward the hill. I came into the Dragonpit-margin lane, and the late-morning warmth of the hook-ward stone had gone out of the air; the hill-lanes lose the day's heat first. The supply cart stood on the lower-gate road — the lower gate, the Dragonpit's court-margin side, under Rhaenys's Hill, where bodies on the heir's business pass and not the Queen's: not a Green-faction gate. The stone-post stood at the gate's side-exit. Then the lane was its empty evening distribution, and out of it a body resolved — the feed returned Corwick, a man standing where a man at this hour should not be standing. He faced the second man, and I went still at the feed-edge as he turned. He squared his shoulders. The wrong-hour and the squared shoulders and the gate that backs onto the wrong faction resolved to a direction, not a name — I read the gate's loyalty the way a person reads a thing already written, the split's ending held at a remove no one standing in this lane could hold, a direction I did not infer so much as recognize. I priced the posture-class, and the price was small. I closed the observation-entry. I completed the circuit.

I took the feed-station at the end of the day. I folded the packet. The wax came down under my hand at the weight I had already settled; the packet was closed, and what the packet held was the only thing the channel would ever see. The ward-coverage notes sat to the station-left of it. The internal map held Wren's route. The feed-record closed the courier entry. The wax, soft and warm under my hand when it went down, set firm, and the seal dried.
