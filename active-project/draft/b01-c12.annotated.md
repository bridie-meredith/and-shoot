*I had been Otto Hightower's eyes in the lower city for months now — the count of the streets traded up the hill through Jarvis, the coverage of the wards grown wide and clean. The accounting closed each night the way it always closed. Two things I kept out of it and off the channel: the cost my insects carried in the lanes where the wrong eyes named them, and the route the stitch-maker walked through the one stretch the map could not reach.*

---

The insects came back to me off the overhang-joints first, the close timber where the upper stories lean in over the lanes east of the water-gate — the old lower-city stretch where close overhangs let a thing pass hand-to-hand, the one ground my coverage cannot reach — and then off the lane-mouth, the few I had fanned wide along it returning the open throat of the street. I took the gate-tower shadow before I stepped into it. The shadow fell across the lane at second bell and made the west wall of the thing, and I set my placement low and back, under the height where dense insects stop being insects and become the word these streets keep for women like me. I know the word. I let the placement stand anyway. The map closed the gate-tower boundary on the west. It closed the rendering-yard wall on the east. Both walls down, and the gap bounded between them — not a vagueness now but a named stretch with two stone edges, the exact ground I had walked at dawn. The insects came back to me off the stitch-house route, the lane Wren takes from the rendering-yard through to the upper stitch-house, returned on the feed the way any lane returns. And the map marked the stitch-maker route, the same passage written into the internal record as a line — the route that runs the map's eastern edge and stops there, unindexed, in the record I keep and nowhere a column can reach. I lifted the stylus. The northern ward-cluster wanted the source-field and my hand did not bring it down; the hand came up off the surface with the field still bare. Then I set the cluster — the first tranche north, the insects laid into the upper lanes where the count could hold them — and the ledger column closed the water-gate entry under it. The entry closed and the eastern lanes were not inside it. The boundary was load-bearing now, and the thing that held it had never been priced.

Jarvis placed the packet at the edge of the station surface where the packets always land, the working face where the accounting runs, and was already most of the way back to the channel. I broke the wax seal — my thumb under it, the small sharp crack of it loud in the quiet of the channel — and the covering-sheet came open under my hand. The sheet named the corridor by the gate-tower designation: the west wall, the exact mark. I turned the covering-sheet. The second wall landed on the page, the rendering-yard mark, the east boundary I had walked at dawn — both edges of the gap on the standard request in the standard flat hand, the request reading the thing back to me with a resolution it lacked the means to fill. The apparatus had spotted a shape it could not fill and wanted precisely the lanes that could not give it. I set the packet down. I set the stylus beside it, and the morning over the bay had gone warm against me, the damp burned off the stone before the wind off the water came in to clear it.

I took the stylus up. The coverage-entry opened the gap-column, and I wrote the boundary into it in the same flat hand as every deliverable before it: the east-water-gate lanes not accessible within current coverage parameters, a structural limit, not a temporary absence. I did not write why. My hand stopped short of the source-field and did not come down. The stylus lifted before the explanation field, the hand not reaching it, and the entry would seal with the clause absent. I closed the response entry over the empty field, and the source field stayed empty, and the hand knew the shape of the gesture now — the route never written, the burned cloth-account never written, and this; the same withhold, the third time, recognized and not stopped. Jarvis took the sealed packet, and the apparatus accepted the coverage limit the way it accepted any limit, and would route around it, which made the withholding a thing no one would ever see. The response entry closed the gap-column in flat register. I opened the anchor-column. I wrote the entry into it, and I closed it, and the weight settled where I had put it: the lane-refusal was the mechanism that made Wren's free movement the map's eastern boundary, and that one move settled the second tranche of what had long been owed — one debt, one mechanism, the single axis-move settling both, no name set down to carry it. I lifted the hand off the column.

The second ward-cluster ran in the late afternoon. I extended it into the Muddy Way's upper margin, the coverage reaching past the water-gate approach northward, and the insects filled the upper margin until the partial weight that had sat under the count all day went to a full one, the circuit settled and whole, the last structural gap in the Flea Bottom coverage closed under them. That placement closed the fifth-ward circuit. And with it closed the feed returned all five wards — every ward and the lane between them at once, the count running the full circuit for the first time, the whole architecture returned simultaneous and complete and not walked back by pulling any single node. The count ran the full-circuit return. The feed returned the Flea Bottom approaches, the place I had come to cover returned now at the full scale, every approach into it mapped and held. The accounting ran as it always ran. It reached the aggregate-shape entry, the whole of it summed; it ran the harm-prevention column, the gain weighed against the cost the way the gain is always weighed; it reached the breach column. And the count was running and the record reached past any name for the architecture and landed on the older word — not a description of the thing in front of me but the shape of the thing I did when the world was ending, the one this rhymes with. The architecture was the older shape, built in a different city. It was there for one count. I did not let it settle. The accounting advanced the count past it, and I closed the architecture entry without it, my palm flat on the closed page where the stylus had been, my breath staying flat and not lifting. The ledger entry took the full-circuit count. The breach column took the threshold entry — the cost filed in the same flat column register as every entry above it, the end-of-day light gone off the surface to dimming, the word not filed with it.
