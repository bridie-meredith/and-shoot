*It is the Crone's stretch still, the bay-cold on the morning stone, not lifting before the first bell. The arrangement holds, and it has held without being named — each delivery still mine to weigh in sequence, the function never written down. The count runs the Hook and the wards past it; the coverage extends; the harm I can prevent is still the only column that closes. The courier has a name now, Corwick, and a body-map I have kept in the feed-record nine circuits without routing it to Jarvis: the errand-corridor, the posture, the lower-gate where his errands face the wrong way for the Queen's business. This morning the packet comes at the standard hour, in the standard channel.*

---
Jarvis set the packet down on the morning stone and the wax under his thumb was Otto's, and that was the whole of the meeting — his hands, the seal, the folded weight of it on the cold surface, no word passing that I would have answered. The text inside did not ask. It stated, and what it stated had the weight of a thing already closed: the arrangement named outright as an ongoing function, my work the lower city's intelligence delivered up the line, and the deniability I had been carrying through every prior errand was what the words spent. There was no version of the sentence I could fold back into a question. The packet sets it plainly: the protection over Sera continues as the consideration the function is owed against. The bay-cold had come overnight and held on the morning stone under my hand, and I was a person standing in a cold place reading a page. I folded the packet along its crease and let the gesture be what it was — a thing received, a thing kept. Then I let my breath out over the content of it without turning the content over, and the road outside ran the way it ran.

The second item opened under the first, and it was a description, not a name. Otto's apparatus had the silhouette already — the shape of a man who moved the wrong faction's logistics along the lower-gate road — but it did not have the body. I had the body. I had carried it for months in the feed-record that did not route to Jarvis: the errand-corridor and where it emptied, the posture-classes, the gate that backed the wrong side, the geometry of him laid down circuit by circuit until it was as fixed in me as the stone-post he squared himself against each pass, the way the record had held that posture nine times running. All of it came up out of the record now, into my hand. My breath shortened once over it. Then my hand turned the months into the channel's flat register — the conversion priced and paid in the one motion that could not be unmade, the stylus going down onto the channel surface with a small sound in the morning quiet — and that was the first act. The second was separate from it. I routed it up the line, the delivery its own motion, the giving distinct from the making. The wax dried over the channel and the channel was sealed on him, on Corwick, and not on any other name.

Two days later. The supply cart marked the lower-gate road the way the supply cart marked it every cold morning, its odor of animal and cargo and wood reaching the circuit-pass ahead of it, and then the man at the post: the circuit ran the road through its ninth pass the way it had run the eight before, his mark in the geometry as fixed as the stone-post, the rhythm steady enough that I had stopped counting it. Then the road came back a body short, and the short was a positive fact and not an absence — the errand-corridor mouth where his form ordinarily stood returned to me empty, the geometry holding all around the place his mark had gone, and the empty had the shape of an action that had already happened somewhere I could not see. The stone-post still squared the side-exit, and the side-exit kept the shape of how a machinery this old empties a corridor of one body and leaves the stone unmarked — the errand-corridor a channel the circuit does not count, a way out the road need not register. Within two passes the circuit returned a Gold Cloak pair — the City Watch, the crown's armed guard in King's Landing — posted at the junction where his corridor emptied, at rest, not passing through. The insect-feed swept the corridor and brought back the same: the apparatus had run, within two circuit-passes of my delivery, the war's logic moved through my own network without my consent or my design. The bay-cold pressed the lower road, its weight on the body making the pass, and that too was a fact I could hold.

At the end of the day I opened the ledger. The convention ran the way the convention ran: the gain set against the cost. Sera's protection continuing, the arrangement formalized, the channel operational — and against it Corwick, body-mapped across months, withheld through nine circuits, gone within two days of the line I sent. The feed-station stone firmed cold under my wrist as the accounting ran. He faced the lower-gate in the record, and he crossed the errand-corridor in the record — the crossing came back through the feed the way the posture had come back nine passes running, one more pass of a body I had held without his leave, and the crossing was one more entry in the months of him: the architecture that does this to bodies it never asked, doing it again in my hand, the corridor closing behind him the way the channel had closed over the entry. I wrote him into the ledger as a closed entry. Then I closed the ledger, the cover falling shut with a single sound into the day's-end quiet, the accounting shut on the function it confirmed. I pressed my hand flat to the feed-station. The posture squared itself again in the feed-record after the entry was closed — the gait-signature, the set of his shoulders, the lower-gate face holding in the register where the ledger kept no column for it. My gaze stayed on it and did not turn away when the ledger had closed. The ledger did not ask me to stay.
