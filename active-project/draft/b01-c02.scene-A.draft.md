The flies returned the heat-signatures: settling onto skin, taking the warmth of every body they had crossed. And under them the beetles returned the threshold-count, running the stones, the weight of every foot recorded against the count.

I left the drain angle. The angle-gap where the two tenement walls pinched was the farthest south point — from here every approach along the forty paces of lane was visible before it arrived.

The fever-cluster returned: three heat-signatures across two alleys, the day's unresolved load. The three would be priced before sundown or not at all.

The insects fanned the alleys and the ward delivered the heat-signatures, and I took the alley-mouth — the lane-mouth onto the Hook alley, line-of-sight both ways, into the lane behind and out to the traffic ahead — and the insects crossed the threshold-stones in front of me.

The feed held four hundred bodies.

Then the alley-back dropped from the feed. The whole rear of the lane went silent before the head turned — a receipt for the range I had not yet committed to take.

I extended the range.

The stitch-house-lane precinct gave way to the broader Hook alley-grid; the feed ran past the forty-pace lane and out into the ward beyond. The suppression-cost rose with the range, and the four-hundred ceiling had just been paid for in the back of the skull.

I drew the line. The prohibition was the same prohibition I had set myself; the line drawn here was the line that would be asked of me again. It held at the ceiling and not past it.

The insects filled the Hook — the alley-network inside the feed now, not only the precinct around the lane-mouth.

The foot-traffic knotted the ward-junction — a corner in the Hook where three or four alleys gave onto one another under a single precinct-ward's reach.
