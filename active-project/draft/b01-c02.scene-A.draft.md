The flies came back warm — landing on the skin at the throat and the inside of the wrist, returning the heat-signatures of every body they had crossed — and the beetles came in under them, threshold by threshold, returning the count of feet that had crossed each stone.

I left the drain angle. The angle-gap where the two tenement walls pinched was the farthest south point; from there every approach along the forty paces of lane was visible before it arrived, and I had been holding from there since before the grey-hour.

The fever-cluster came back the way it had come back yesterday — three heat-signatures across two alleys, the day's unresolved load. The three would be priced before sundown or not at all.

The insects fanned the alleys; the ward delivered the heat-signatures back. I took the alley-mouth — the lane-mouth onto the Hook alley, line-of-sight both ways, into the lane behind and out to the traffic ahead — and the insects crossed the threshold-stones in front of me.

The feed held four hundred bodies.

The alley-back dropped from the feed. The whole rear of the lane went out at once, a feed-zone going dark behind the eyes, the four-hundred ceiling paid for in the back of the skull before the head had turned.

I extended the range. The stitch-house-lane precinct gave way to the broader Hook alley-grid, the feed running past the forty-pace lane into the ward beyond, the suppression-cost rising with the range — the four-hundred ceiling had just been paid for in the back of my skull.

I drew the line. The prohibition was the same prohibition I had set myself, and the line drawn here was the line that would be asked of me again; it held at the ceiling and not past it.

The insects filled the Hook — the alley-network inside the feed now, not only the precinct around the lane-mouth.

The foot-traffic knotted the ward-junction — a corner in the Hook where three or four alleys gave onto one another under a single precinct-ward's reach.
