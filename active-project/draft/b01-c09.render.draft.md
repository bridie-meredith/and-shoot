*The arrangement holds. The count runs the Hook and four wards past it now, the coverage extends, and the harm I can prevent is still the only column that closes. I know the stitch-maker's days the way I know the lanes — Wren keeps the Hook's children fed and clothed, and the feed has been threading her street since the boundary moved, and I have not had to ask it to. The courier I had without a name has one now; I filed it last circuit and did not ask whose errands he runs. This morning the circuit is routine. The Jarvis line will run at its standard interval.*

---

I came into the lane south of the Hook with the morning foot traffic still thin, the single-entrance corridor opening ahead of me toward the stitch-shop lanes. The feed was already running it — threaded through the lane the way it threads anything I have left it long enough in, no act of mine required any longer to keep it there. The stitch-shop door stood open at the lane-mouth.

Wren came out of it and walked the lane that runs from her shop down toward the water-sellers. My gaze held on the bread-seller's corner a beat before she reached it; then she reached it. The route has waypoints now — the door, the corner, the lane that takes one entrance — and a thing with waypoints is no longer a body that happens to be in range. I filed it. I had the geometry filed before the lane closed behind her; the map takes Wren's pattern the way it takes any pattern, clean, indexed, kept. The ward-coverage notes took something narrower. They took the boundary geometry — the south extension, the lanes the feed now reaches — and nothing else of her.

That evening, the second circuit ran the Dragonpit margin — the outer lanes south, toward the hill. The day-warmth had gone out of the stone here; the hill-lane air came up evening-cool against the route I'd held warm all morning in the lower wards. A supply cart sat on the gate-road ahead, the road that runs up to the lower gate — the Dragonpit's court-margin side, under Rhaenys's Hill, where bodies on the heir's business pass and not the Queen's: not a Green-faction gate. A stone-post stood at the side-exit, marking where the gate let out.

Then the feed gave me a body. The lane had read empty along that stretch — cart, post, the ambient nothing of an evening corridor — and a man resolved out of it where a moment before there had been no one, and the man was Corwick. The evening feed runs the margin and the figure in it is wrong-houred and wrong-warded, a man standing where a man at this hour should not be standing.

I went still at the edge of the feed as his body turned. He faced a second man, one I could not see, set out of the feed's reach. He squared his shoulders — held the line of them, the closed stance of a thing handed over and done. The wrong-hour and the squared shoulders and the gate that backs onto the wrong faction resolve to a direction, not a name; she prices the posture-class and the price is small. I read the gate's loyalty the way a person reads a thing already written — the split's ending held at a remove no one standing in this lane could hold, a direction I did not infer so much as recognize. I closed the entry on it. The circuit ran out its length and finished, and the channel ran at its interval and carried what it carried.

I took the feed-station at the end of the day, the station surface clear in front of me — the ward-coverage notes, the unsealed packet, the workspace where the accounting closes. I folded the packet over what it held. The wax came down under my hand at the weight I had already settled; the packet was closed. The ward-coverage notes sat to the left of it. The internal map held Wren's route. The feed-record held the courier's entry, closed. The wax was soft and warm under my thumb where I'd pressed it, and then it began to set, going firm, going past the point where a thumb would mark it. It dried.
