*The crowd had broken open around the stitch-maker's ward, and the hands had gone up before mine did. After that I went back to the drain angle and did not feed anyone for three weeks. I held the count at its edge by the cost of holding it. What I did instead was start to listen — at first because subsistence-reading is what survives in the Hook, and then because the listening had a shape I had begun to extend on purpose. The rule was the same rule. Reading was not directing. I had not crossed the line.*

*What I had built I did not yet have a name for. The flies and beetles told me where heat clustered and where foot-traffic knotted. I held a count of bodies in my head — who was sick, who was hungry, which alley junction emptied before dark and which one never did. I called it a coverage map when I was being plain with myself; I called it an accounting when I was not. The ledger was where it closed at end of day. The rule was that I read and did not direct. That was the line. The instrument was harm-reduction. That was how it stayed permitted.*

---

The flies returned the heat-signatures: settling onto skin, taking the warmth of every body they had crossed. And under them the beetles returned the threshold-count, running the stones, the weight of every foot recorded against the count.

I left the drain angle. The angle-gap where the two tenement walls pinched was the farthest south point — from here every approach along the forty paces of lane was visible before it arrived.

The fever-cluster returned: three heat-signatures across two alleys, the day's unresolved load. The three would be priced before sundown or not at all.

The insects fanned the alleys and the ward delivered the heat-signatures, and I took the alley-mouth — the lane-mouth onto the Hook alley, line-of-sight both ways, into the lane behind and out to the traffic ahead — and the insects crossed the threshold-stones in front of me.

The feed held four hundred bodies.

Then the alley-back dropped from the feed. The whole rear of the lane went silent before the head turned — a receipt for the range I had not yet committed to take.

I extended the range.

The stitch-house-lane precinct gave way to the broader Hook alley-grid; the feed ran past the forty-pace lane and out into the ward beyond. The suppression-cost rose with the range, and the four-hundred ceiling had just been paid for in the back of the skull.

I drew the line. The prohibition was the same prohibition I had set myself; the line drawn here was the line that would be asked of me again. It held at the ceiling and not past it.

The insects filled the Hook — the alley-network inside the feed now, not only the precinct around the lane-mouth.

The foot-traffic knotted the ward-junction — a corner in the Hook where three or four alleys gave onto one another under a single precinct-ward's reach.

Days of it, and the pattern came.

The insects brought a body back across sweeps — the same body, the ward-junction body, returned as a junction-signature on the feed. The map had not yet carried a slot for that body-type, and now there was one to make.

Tallow smoke from the stitch-house east wall rolled at floor-level into the lane: the lamps were lit, the work-day was running. The coverage map anchored on the stitch-house threshold. The beetles came back across the stones sweep after sweep, the threshold-crossings repeating the weight-on-stone sequence.

I took the drain angle. Forty paces off, the lane-mouth and the alley beyond stood north of me.

The ward-junction body crossed the thresholds. The alley admitted her at the lane-mouth — the same lane-mouth I held — the threshold occupied from both sides at once, and I yielded it. My weight settled back onto the rear foot. The alley-mouth had been priced as exposure already; the yield was calculated before her body arrived. The insects came back with a function-signature, and the map assigned her connector-type before the yielding finished.

I turned from the alley-mouth. The discipline held; the alley-mouth was not the place I learned anything I did not already have.

The insects filed the ward-junction contact. The gesture moved in a hand that knew the motion — a filing without contact, function-label sufficient ledger entry, the ledger holding a new entry and no signature against it.

The feed attenuated past the junction-lane. The insects thinned as they pushed outward, the ward-traffic no longer readable. They reached the far margin of the junction-lane network — the coverage map had a hard outer edge here where the feed attenuated, and the edge came back as absence.

I took the drain angle. The light had moved since morning, cutting across the tenement wall at a lower angle; the afternoon slant had come down, and the angle-gap held it the way it had held the dawn.

The insects came back from the lane-mouth sweep.

The shadow filled the drain angle. The wall-shadow had crossed the gap, and the angle-gap was now the darkest point in the lane — dusk arriving along the stone.

I ran the map.

The fever-cluster corner came back first — three bodies above ambient, where two alleys folded into one. The dark-junction corner came back next, the silence-pattern intact under it. The map returned the bodies.

The accounting closed the count.

The accounting reached the ward-junction entry. The ward-junction corner returned the junction-lane void — the attenuation I had filed at the edge, the negative space inside the ledger.

I stalled the count. The count had been clean to the ward-junction; the count was no longer clean.

I held the breath. The breath held at the threshold; the recognition was not permitted across it.

I drew the line. The line was the same line I had drawn at the alley-mouth; I drew it now in the same hand. The shape of this line was what a watching street would find a word for.

I closed against the drain angle.

The accounting closed the fever-cluster entry — the shadow held through the closing, the drain angle dark as the ledger moved entry by entry. The ledger closed the dark-junction entry. And the ledger closed the ward-junction contact — a half-beat longer at the close, the hand pausing at the entry that cost more to close than the others, the cost not appearing in the closing.

I exhaled.
