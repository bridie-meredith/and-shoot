*The crowd had broken open around the stitch-maker's ward, and the hands had gone up before mine did. After that I went back to the drain angle and did not feed anyone for three weeks. I held the count at its edge by the cost of holding it. What I did instead was start to listen — at first because subsistence-reading is what survives in the Hook, and then because the listening had a shape I had begun to extend on purpose. The rule was the same rule. Reading was not directing. I had not crossed the line.*

*What I had built I did not yet have a name for. The flies and beetles told me where heat clustered and where foot-traffic knotted. I held a count of bodies in my head — who was sick, who was hungry, which alley junction emptied before dark and which one never did. I called it a coverage map when I was being plain with myself; I called it an accounting when I was not. The ledger was where it closed at end of day. The rule was that I read and did not direct. That was the line. The instrument was harm-reduction. That was how it stayed permitted.*

---

I left the drain angle in the grey hour. The flies and beetles fanned out ahead of me along the alleys, and the ward came back in heat and motion — bodies clustered against bodies, the warmer knots where people slept piled, the cooler runs where the lane stood empty. Forty paces north, the lane-mouth opened onto the Hook, and I took the threshold and held it: a single-file pinch I could see both ways from. Sound thinned at my back and thickened ahead, a low murmur where the foot-traffic moved. The insects crossed the threshold-stones outward. I pushed the range further than I had ever pushed it — past the working ceiling, into the fours, where the suppression cost would price out by noon — and before the range went I said the rule again, in that order, so the order would be the thing the morning had said: I was reading. I was not directing. The flies fanned out and filled the Hook. The foot-traffic knotted at the ward-junction — a corner in the Hook where three or four alleys gave onto one another under a single precinct-ward's reach — and I let them have it.

Days of it, and the pattern came.

The insects brought her back, that body — the one I had logged at the ward-junction knot, end of the first sweep. Tallow smoke pooled at lane-floor along the east wall's reach: the stitch-house lamp burning, the marker my feed was using for the threshold. She crossed the thresholds going in. She crossed them going out. The alley admitted her, the same alley-mouth where the foot-traffic had knotted on day one, and the insects returned a function-signature, and the map assigned her connector-type — a category the map had not needed to carry until this body crossed the threshold-stones.

I stepped out of the lane-mouth a half-beat before she reached it. The yielding was the cover and the cataloguing was what the cover was for.

The insects filed the ward-junction contact. My shoulders held where they were. The filing took less hesitation than it should have — categorization without contact, function-label sufficient ledger entry, not-looking-closer as the operational posture; the absence shaped to one body and the shape was the architecture I had come here to refuse to build, surfacing through the method I had not yet looked at.

The coverage map opened a gap. Negative-space cut to the contour of one body, and I had cut it.

It held the gap.

End of day. I ran the map.

I took the drain angle again — cobblestone underfoot at the pinch where the lane gave out south — and ran the map. The flies returned the bodies. The accounting closed the count down toward its edge: forty-three registered, the ward-junction body filed at the fold, the gap held in the shape I had cut it.

The count stalled.

What was on the other side of the stall was the recognition the architecture had been built to hold off — that I had built a surveillance over forty-three people who had not been asked. The word arrived. Surveillance. The moment it was visible to me as the thing it was.

I drew the line a second time. Harm-reduction. Reading, not directing. The second drawing did not retract the first crossing; it catalogued it under a name I could keep using, and the name closed the entry.

The accounting closed the fever-cluster. The ledger closed the dark-junction. The ledger closed the ward-junction contact.

I exhaled — longer than the morning's exhale, the suppression overhead priced through, the ledger closed without recording the cost it closed under.
