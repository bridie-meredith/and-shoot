*The crowd had broken open around the stitch-maker's ward, and the hands had gone up before mine did. After that I went back to the drain angle and did not feed anyone for three weeks. I held the count at its edge by the cost of holding it. What I did instead was start to listen — at first because subsistence-reading is what survives in the Hook, and then because the listening had a shape I had begun to extend on purpose. The rule was the same rule. Reading was not directing. I had not crossed the line.*

*What I had built I did not yet have a name for. The flies and beetles told me where heat clustered and where foot-traffic knotted. I held a count of bodies in my head — who was sick, who was hungry, which alley junction emptied before dark and which one never did. I called it a coverage map when I was being plain with myself; I called it an accounting when I was not. The ledger was where it closed at end of day. The rule was that I read and did not direct. That was the line. The instrument was harm-reduction. That was how it stayed permitted.*

---

The flies came back with the heat-signatures and the beetles came back with the threshold-count. I left the drain angle. The lane behind me was empty and the angle-gap was clear. The farthest south point, the pinch between the two tenement walls where every approach down the forty paces of lane was visible before it arrived.

The fever-cluster came back too. Three signatures across two alleys, the day's unresolved load. The three would be priced before sundown or not at all.

I fanned the insects out down the alleys and the ward delivered up the heat-signatures and I took the alley-mouth at the north end where the line-of-sight ran both ways, and the insects crossed the threshold-stones into the Hook. The feed held four hundred bodies.

The alley-back went silent before I turned my head. The dropout was the receipt for the range I had not yet committed to take.

I extended the range.

The stitch-house-lane precinct gave way and the feed ran past the forty paces into the Hook beyond. The four hundred had been the ceiling and it had just been paid for in the back of my skull. I drew the line. It held at the ceiling and not past it.

The insects filled the Hook. The whole alley-grid of it was inside the feed now, not only the precinct around the lane-mouth. Days of foot-traffic, not one morning's sweep. The foot-traffic knotted the ward-junction. A corner in the Hook where three or four alleys gave onto one another under a single precinct-ward's reach.

Days of it, and the pattern came.

The insects returned a body the map had not yet carried a slot for, a body that crossed the ward-junction at the same stones at the same hour, sweep after sweep. The tallow smoke marked the stitch-house lane that morning the way it had marked every morning since the precinct shift had started running before mine. The lamps lit on the east wall, the smoke rolling low along the lane floor, the day already moving by the time my feed was up. The coverage map anchored on the stitch-house threshold and held.

The beetles returned the threshold-crossings, and the threshold-crossings repeated the weight-on-stone sequence: the same gait, the same stones in the same order, pass after pass.

I took the drain angle.

The ward-junction body crossed the thresholds. She came down the alley from the precinct side toward the lane-mouth, and the alley admitted her: the same alley-mouth I held line-of-sight on from forty paces south, the threshold occupied from both sides at once, her body crossing into it and mine looking down it. I yielded the alley-mouth. The yield was already priced. The alley-mouth had been carried on the ledger as exposure since the second sweep, and the cost of standing in it when a ward-junction body crossed had been billed and paid before her body arrived in it. The insects returned the function-signature. The map assigned the junction-body the connector-type slot.

I turned from the alley-mouth. The alley-mouth was not the place I learned anything I did not already have.

The insects filed the ward-junction contact.

The ledger took the entry and there was no signature against it. The body had been carried into the map under a function and no name, without consent and without contact, and the gesture of filing moved in a hand that knew the motion: a hand that had moved this way before, in another accounting, under another architecture, and would move this way again because the motion was the architecture and the architecture was mine. I did not stop the filing. The entry stood.

The insects returned the junction-lane attenuation. The feed thinned past the corner, the body-count falling off where the alley-network frayed into the broader ward, and the insects reached the junction-lane edge. The map had a hard outer margin there, not a failure of the sweep but the second register of the same accounting. The place where the traffic across the ward was no longer readable was itself an entry, and the entry sat at the edge of the map, and held.

I took the drain angle in the late afternoon and the light had moved. The slant came down the tenement wall at a lower angle than it had carried at dawn, cutting a hard line across the brick where the angle-gap pinched. I stood where I had stood at first bell. The lane was the lane.

The insects returned from the lane-mouth sweep.

The shadow filled the drain angle. The wall-shadow had crossed the gap while the sweep was out, and the angle-gap was the darkest point in the lane now. I did not move from it.

I ran the map.

The fever-cluster corner returned three bodies above ambient — the same three signatures, the same two alleys, the heat-load steady through the day's coverage. The dark-junction corner returned silence — the corner empty in the same shape it had been empty in at the morning sweep.

The accounting closed the count.

The accounting reached the ward-junction entry.

The ward-junction corner returned the junction-lane void — the attenuation past the corner, the body-count falling off where the alley-network frayed, the count thinning to nothing and holding.

The count stalled.

The breath held. One beat.

I drew the line in the same hand. The line was the line at the alley-mouth at noon. The shape of this line was what a watching street would find a word for, in time. The word would not be mine to give or refuse.

I closed against the drain angle. The shoulders set against the wall-shadow and the wall-shadow held them. The shadow stayed across the gap, the angle dark as the ledger ran.

The accounting closed the fever-cluster entry. The ledger closed the dark-junction entry. The ledger closed the ward-junction contact. The cost of closing that entry was higher than the cost of closing the fever-cluster entry and the cost of closing the dark-junction entry, and the cost did not appear in the closing.

I exhaled.
