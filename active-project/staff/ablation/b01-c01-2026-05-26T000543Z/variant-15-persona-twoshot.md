*I had won my last world by becoming a hive of bugs, and I had not meant to wake. I woke anyway in another body — a foreign woman with no name in this city, in King's Landing, in the year before the year before the year the dragons would dance. The flies came with me. Two hundred metres of them, dense as the air. The first thing I decided was that nothing I had done at the end would be done here.*

*Three weeks in this ward and I have learned the specific weight of doing nothing. I sleep in the angle of a Hook alley where the drain runs and the air is full of small bodies I refuse to use. I have a count. I hold the count at its edge by the cost of holding it, every hour I am awake. The arrangement is that nobody here knows my name and I know nobody's name, and the arrangement is what I am paying for.*

*I came here to be useful without taking control. That is the rule I set before any of the rest of it. The rule is what I am holding the flies in for. The rule is older than anything in this city. The rule is what I have left.*

The drain-water threaded the angle-gap before light had fully arrived in the Hook — the lane-warren of Flea Bottom, King's Landing's slum-district where transient smallfolk go to be unremarkable. Beyond the angle-wall the stitch-house lamp still burned in what Flea Bottom called a ward (a trade household that took in a parentless child for shelter and light work, not warm and not cruel), and the tallow smoke crossed the stitch-house lane in the slow oily way of tallow in still air. I held the feet. The feet hold the only discipline I brought from before that still does what it was built for. The insects swelled by a quarter measure, and I pulled the suppression back before the count completed. The holding cost a quarter of me at the count's edge, and I paid it on principle every morning. The angle-wall narrowed the lane. I exhaled.

Later the fish-cart blocked the lane — a handcart of the kind the morning fish-trade ran through the Hook from the river-side gate — set crosswise where the lane could not absorb it, and the ground transmitted what the crowd had not caught: the small ragged breath of a child inside the press. The ground had carried the breath before the crowd registered the absence of one. The crowd compressed against the cart and the angle-wall both. I held the feet still. The lane-mouth pressed the dozen bodies together, and the pressure was already priced: the dozen I would need, and the second dozen I would not. The insects propagated. The propagation went where I had told it to go, and the telling was a thing I would not name. The nearest bodies yielded without knowing why, and the gap propagated along the lane in the precise shape the body inside it required. I faced the child. I raised my voice.

"Fever. Not the croup."

"She needs air. Stand back."

"Who knows her? Fetch them."

I lifted my hands.

After the gap closed, the crowd came apart the way Flea Bottom crowds did. The fish-cart man faced me, and what he meant by it I did not know. Two women faced the lane and did not look away. Then the Mudway man took the lane-mouth: a Hook ward-elder who had stood thirty years on these streets and named what he saw without explaining why he had noticed. He had taken the lane-mouth a week before he would have the word for it. The child cleared the lane in the arms of a woman who had a name for her. The gap closed behind. I faced the alley-mouth and set my body to it so the stitch-house lane would not need to be registered again. The tallow smoke layered the lane-floor. Oswyn lifted the chin the way the chin lifts in the country's older stories when a word has been waiting for a person and the person has finally arrived to fit it, and the chin-lift filed me in a category I recognized the shape of without needing the country's name for it. Then a face turned toward mine. The stitch-maker's ward: a Hook girl of eleven who watched cloth before she cut it, and watched people the same way. Her face had found mine. I filed the registration in the same fold where I kept the things that did not enter the ledger.
