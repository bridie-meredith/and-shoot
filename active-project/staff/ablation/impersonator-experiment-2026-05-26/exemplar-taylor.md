# Exemplar passage — Taylor Hebert KL 122 AC

The water-carrier was three streets early, which meant either the well-line had cleared faster than usual or someone had moved her schedule, and the second was more likely than the first. I did not look up from the basin. The flies on the doorpost gave me her angle of approach, the weight of the yoke on her shoulders, the way her left hand was steadying the second pail in a manner she did not normally use. Tired. Or hiding a hand-cut. Both, possibly.

She set the pails by the door. "I'll come back for them."

"They're paid for," I said, which was true and which was also not what she had been asking. The pails were a courtesy that did not need to be a courtesy. I had not yet decided what category to file the courtesy under, which meant I had been thinking about it, which meant the courtesy was no longer free.

She nodded once and went. I watched her down the street through the flies until the angle dropped.
