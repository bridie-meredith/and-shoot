*The crowd had broken open around the stitch-maker's ward, and the hands had gone up before mine did. After that I went back to the drain angle and did not feed anyone for three weeks. I held the count at its edge by the cost of holding it. What I did instead was start to listen — at first because subsistence-reading is what survives in the Hook, and then because the listening had a shape I had begun to extend on purpose. The rule was the same rule. Reading was not directing. I had not crossed the line.*

What I had built I did not yet have a name for. The flies and beetles told me where heat clustered and where foot-traffic knotted. I held a count of bodies in my head — who was sick, who was hungry, which alley junction emptied before dark and which one never did. I called it a coverage map when I was being plain with myself; I called it an accounting when I was not. The ledger was where it closed at end of day. The rule was that I read and did not direct. That was the line. The instrument was harm-reduction. That was how it stayed permitted.

The flies came back first, and the beetles came back behind them. I left the drain angle — the angle-gap where the two tenement walls pinch is the farthest south point, and from there every approach along the forty paces of lane arrives before it arrives — and I took the alley-mouth.

The fever-cluster was still out there. Three heat-signatures across two alleys, unresolved since the morning before. They would be priced before sundown or they would not. I fanned the insects out and the ward gave back its count. The beetles crossed the threshold-stones, and the feed came up. Four hundred bodies. That was the ceiling.

Then the alley-back went silent. I had not turned my head yet. The dropout had been the receipt for the range I had not committed to take. The cost had come in before the decision was fully mine.

I extended the range anyway. The range went past the forty paces of lane, past the lane-mouth, out into the broader Hook alley-grid. Four hundred bodies was where the billing sat. I had paid for it in the back of my skull, and the skull knew the price.

Then I drew the line. The prohibition was the same prohibition I had set myself — the ceiling held there and not past it. The line was where it had always been. I drew it now in the same hand that had drawn it before, and the hand did not hesitate, which was the only test I had for whether it still held.

The insects filled the Hook. The foot-traffic knotted at the ward-junction — a corner in the Hook where three or four alleys gave onto one another under a single precinct-ward's reach — and the map had its first name for that node.
