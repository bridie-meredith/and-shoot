The cart came in heavy and I went out to it before Ulrich did. He was in the back with the ledger and the ledger could wait. The cart could not.

The driver had loaded it wrong. The barrels were not the load you put on the rear axle — they wanted the middle of the bed, low, with the sacks behind them and the crates in front. He had done the opposite and the front wheels had ridden up at the gate so hard the boy on the bench had been thrown against the rail. The boy was fine. The driver was angry that he had been called for it.

I started with the barrels. The barrels are sixty pounds each and you lift them with your legs or you do not lift them. I lifted them with my legs. I moved them to the middle of the bed. I set them down and they were heavy enough to settle the cart back where it had been before the loading bent it.

The driver watched me work. He did not help. I had not asked him to help. I had moved three of the four barrels when Ulrich came out with the ledger and saw what I was doing and went back inside without saying anything and came out again with water.

I drank the water. I did the fourth barrel. The driver had not moved.

I went back in and let Ulrich settle with him.
