By the time the sun had cleared the rooflines and the morning-trade had moved through its first slack hour, the discard-air had begun to draw down toward the junction where the three alleys met, and what arrived in the nose was not the tallow-damp I had walked into at predawn but the heavier compound that came off middens at the hour when the carters had been working long enough for the load to begin to tell on itself.

I entered Pig Tallow Lane — the first Flea Bottom ward beyond the Hook, named for the rendering trade no other would carry, three alleys feeding a waste-middens junction at its mouth — and the lane took me in.

The range went out one ward, and the load that came back through the count was a load I had priced already on the walk down, so what arrived in me when the range came back full was not the strain of a new cost but the settling-in of a cost I had agreed to before agreeing to anything else, and the feed widened around me into the geometry of three alleys and a junction-mouth without the part of me that watched the cost saying anything about it.

I mapped the junction-agitation.

The penny-a-barrel carter parked the middens cart at the lane-mouth, and the feed registered the wheel-stop and the slacking of the harness as a single small event the count took in and held.

The insect-feed returned a body the routing had been carrying as a hollow before it returned as a name, and Oswyn of Mudway entered the feed at junction-range, and the thing that began to surface in me about what an elder at that junction at that hour meant for the shape of the coverage I had not yet finished pricing did not finish, because I would not let it.

I mapped the interval. He moved through the junction at the carter's pace.

Pig Tallow Lane returned ward-tier bodies only — no Hook-traffic carried over, no merchant-coats, no Watch.

The stitch-house frames marked the second ward at the lane's far end, the wooden uprights set against the wall in the manner of the trade.

The insect-feed returned Wren of the stitch-house at second-ward range, and the feed held her there, and the hand that had been keeping the ledger of who cost what to carry did not write her in, and the not-writing was the same hand and the same motion as the writing, and what passed through me at second-ward range was the steady recognition that she sat outside the ledger by the same discipline that had built the ledger, and the recognition arrived in the body before it arrived anywhere it could be named.

I held my feet. I did not take the stitch-house lane.

I mapped the second-ward junction.
