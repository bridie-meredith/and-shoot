*I had one day. The proposal sat on the night-side of the count the way an unpaid debt sits at the edge of a ledger. I did not decide twice. I decided in the hour the dark thinned and the flies came back inside the count, and what I decided was that the harm I could prevent was the only column the accounting would close. The terms I had named for myself I would name to the courier at first bell, in the yard, in the same place he had named the question. The rule I had not stated in those words now had a price tag and a destination both.*

---
The tallow-damp off the lane-caulking reached me before the yard did. The cooper's workers held the smallfolk-hours murmur at the near shed, the third bell already gone quiet, the predawn flat. I walked into the yard and took the shed-wall and put the timber at my back and the open ground between me and the lane-mouth across.

The feed had him a span before the lane-mouth did. The gait that came in through the count was the gait already mapped, settled in its weight, settled in its step-interval. Jarvis Coin entered the lane-mouth.

I said, "Yes. The terms hold with two changes. You will have patterns — what moves through the streets, what does not, where the seams sit — not raw report of who said what to whom. The interval is mine to set. The volume is mine to set."

My hand closed on the shed-wall at my back.

I held my feet.

He said, "The man I serve takes those terms. The volume yours. The interval yours. The substance pattern-reports from the wards you walk. Same place. First bell. Three days."

The feed held the hook-range around him.

Jarvis Coin exited the lane-mouth.

I exited the cooper's yard.

Late morning shifted the air at the junction-mouth. The tallow-damp off the lane-caulking thinned and went over to a heavier register, the middens-discard sitting on the throat.

I entered Pig Tallow Lane — the first Flea Bottom ward beyond the Hook, named for the rendering trade no other would carry, three alleys feeding a waste-middens junction at its mouth.

The work extended. The insect-range opened one ward outward, and the suppression-load that came with it settled at the weight I had already priced for it, and the held weight beneath my breath did not move.

I mapped the junction-agitation. The discard-air carried the body-count without the bodies; the count came in through the feed at the cadence of three alleys feeding one mouth.

A carter parked the middens cart at the near alley. Penny-a-barrel work; the wheel-creak settled and went quiet.

The feed returned the gait before the body did. The step-interval came in already mapped — Oswyn Mudway, the elder. I did not let the next thought finish.

The interval went into the count. Step-cadence, lane-position, range-from-junction; the count took him and held him at the reach the work had set.

Pig Tallow Lane returned ward-tier bodies only at the count I held. No prefect, no household-guard; only the foot-traffic the ward carried in its own hours.

The stitch-house frames marked the second ward. Frame-pitch and frame-spacing came back through the feed at the cadence of the stitch-house lane, and the second-ward boundary settled at the reach.

The feed returned Wren.

I held my feet.

The second-ward junction went into the count. Frame-pitch, lane-mouth-width, the reach the work had taken; the count carried the ward at the same hand that had carried the first, and I did not turn the count toward the stitch-house lane.

What the early-morning grey did to Roper's Court was empty it — not the way night emptied the lane behind the cooper's yard, held against lamp-fall and the carter-work that started before first-bell, but the emptying of a craft-court at the hour before its trade had begun to run, the central court sight-clear to the mouths of its tributary lanes, the carter-work of the surrounding wards arriving as a low ambient pressure from beyond the rooflines.

I entered Roper's Court — a Flea Bottom craft-trade ward turning on rope-making, a central court with tributary lanes radiating off long and linear enough to serve as rope-walks.

The range went out a fourth ward, and the feed came back full, and the load that arrived when the fourth ward opened pressed past the load I had carried before — shoulders set into it, breath shortened against it — and the part of me that watched the cost did not speak.

I ran the four-ward feed.

The cooper's yard at first-bell held the same low pressure the court had held at predawn, and the shed-wall took my back as it had taken it the day before, and a moment after I had taken the wall the lane-mouth gave Jarvis Coin over to the yard-air with a half-yard of open ground between the lane-mouth and the shed.

He took the note from inside his coat and held it where the yard-light could fall on it, the seal turned to face me, the seal-mark a small fixed point in the yard between his hand and my eye.

I delivered the report-sheet.

Folded twice along the same crease, the sheet went across the half-yard of yard-air between my hand and his coat, and the half-step that closed around the sheet's last contact closed before the part of me that watched the cost had registered the cost crossing.

Jarvis Coin pocketed the report-sheet.

What the sheet carried in its two folds was the four-ward map at the level the terms had set — which lanes ran heavy in the third hour and which sat slack, where the seams between the wards thinned to a single body's passage and where they widened to admit a cart, the count of bodies that took the junction at the carter's pace and the count that took it at the merchant's, and nothing about a name and nothing about a word said in any doorway in any of those wards.

What had filled the sheet had filled it at the source-tier the terms had set also — what a ward-walker at ward-level could see and price and carry out: in Flea Bottom, the rendering trade and the stitch-trade and the rope-trade and the middens and the foot-traffic that moved between them, and nothing from the Hook above and nothing from the merchant-coats that passed through the Hook on their way to anywhere else.

The arrangement that had set the terms held its shape across the handoff also — Jarvis the conduit from the wards up the line to whatever sat at the line's far end, the wards the source he drew from through me, the half-yard of yard-air between us the only span of the line I stood on.

Jarvis Coin exited the cooper's yard.

The sheet had crossed into a channel the feed could not reach into, and the operation that had been a held shape across the prior day and the predawn and first-bell had cycled now for the first time at scale, and what arrived in me was not relief and not cost but the recognition that the shape would hold the next cycle and the cycle after that.

I ran the ward-feed.

The morning had moved into the trade-hours by the time I reached the second ward at the lane's far end, and the tallow-lamps were still on against the stitch-house frames the way they had been the day before, and the insect-feed returned Wren of the stitch-house at second-ward range, and what passed through me when the feed returned her was the same reading that had passed through me at second-ward range the day before and the same not-writing that had followed the reading, the reading and the not-writing arriving now as a single motion in the same channel the four-ward feed ran through.

I exited the stitch-house lane.

My stride held the four-count past the stitch-house frame, and the north-end lane-mouth opened ahead into the alley that would carry me back into the Hook, and the lane-mouth took me through at the count's pace.
