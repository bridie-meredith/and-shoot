# Cold-read: b01-c04 (uninformed first reader)
Date: 2026-05-27

## 1. EVENTS

In rough order:
- Predawn. Narrator stands in a cooper's yard waiting for a man named Jarvis Coin. She accepts a deal she had apparently been considering: she'll supply "pattern-reports" from the wards she walks, on her interval and volume. Jarvis accepts on behalf of someone he serves. They part. Next contact: first bell, three days.
- She walks out and extends her "feed" / "count" (some kind of perceptual or insect-mediated awareness) one ward outward into Pig Tallow Lane. She registers an old man, Oswyn Mudway, passing through, and a carter. Nothing is done to or with these people.
- She extends to a second ward (a stitch-house lane) and registers "Wren." She does not act on this. Just notes her.
- She extends to a fourth ward (Roper's Court) and notes the load of carrying the four-ward feed presses harder than before.
- Three days later (implied — "the day before" references suggest a time skip), she returns to the cooper's yard at first bell, hands Jarvis a folded sheet — the four-ward map at "ward-walker" tier — and he pockets it and leaves.
- She runs the feed again, passes the stitch-house lane, registers Wren again, does not write Wren down, exits north toward the Hook.

A lot of the chapter is the same beat repeated: extend the feed, register a person or a junction, do not act. There is no event between the deal and the handoff except the act of perceiving.

## 2. JEOPARDY

Almost none I can name from the text. There are gestures: an "unpaid debt," "the harm I could prevent," "suppression-load," "the part of me that watched the cost did not speak." The narrator is paying some cost to do this work, and she's deliberately not reporting individuals (Wren, Oswyn) by name — which implies someone could be hurt if she did. But who Jarvis serves, what they'd do with names, what happens if she's caught, what happens if she refuses — none of this is on the page. I'm asked to take "stakes" on faith from the prose's weight, not from anything shown.

## 3. CAUSALITY

The deal → the walk → the handoff is a clean causal spine. Where I'm lost: WHY she took the deal. The opening italics say she'd decided, and that "the harm I could prevent was the only column the accounting would close" — but I don't know what harm, whose, or why this deal prevents it. The deal seems to *create* exposure rather than prevent harm. I assume there's a prior chapter that explains it. Standing alone, her decision is asserted, not motivated.

## 4. PAYOFF

The ending is the second iteration of the handoff plus the second non-writing of Wren. The text frames this as "the shape would hold the next cycle and the cycle after that" — i.e., the payoff is *that an arrangement is now stable*. As a reader, that's thin. Nothing turns. No one is surprised. No one resists. The Wren beat is the closest thing to a moral hinge — she withholds a name — but I don't know who Wren is or what withholding costs her, so the beat lands as texture, not consequence.

## 5. CONTINUE?

Tentatively yes — but on the strength of the *premise* (girl with weird perception sells intelligence to a shadowy patron, withholds names) rather than on this chapter. If chapter 5 is more of the same, no.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY

A girl with some kind of insect-mediated sixth sense agrees to sell map-level intelligence about four slum wards to a courier named Jarvis Coin, walks the wards twice, and hands over the first report — carefully omitting the people she recognized.

## Confusions, flagged

- "The feed" / "the count" / "insect-range" / "hook-range" — I inferred insect-mediated awareness from "the flies came back inside the count" and "insect-range." Not confirmed.
- "The Hook" — a place above Flea Bottom. Never described.
- Why she accepted the deal. Asserted, not shown.
- Who Wren is and why not-writing her matters. Inferred, not shown.
- The setting reads vaguely Westeros-adjacent (Flea Bottom, prefects, household-guard, smallfolk) — possibly fanfic, possibly homage. The prose style is so mannered (every noun hyphenated, every verb passive-of-sensing) that I'd describe the reading experience as "wading." Atmosphere thick; event thin.
