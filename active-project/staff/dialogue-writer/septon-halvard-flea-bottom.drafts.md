## Anchor 1 — @b01c13s04n03 (halvard:1) — PRIMARY

**Intent / state:** board-move = name what he believes was done wrong to Aldric (taken up on a charge that does not match the man Halvard knew) as WITNESS and LAMENT, working it through slowly, NOT aimed at Taylor, NOT an accusation. register-state = pastoral-counsel, slow, pausal. distance = near (they are both here, in the Hook, on his ground). framing = private. Held axis: moral_framework (Taylor is NOT confronted with new information — Halvard does not know she caused it, so nothing he says forces a framework breach; he laments, she is not moved by data she didn't already have).

**Draft A (chosen):**
> "They took up Aldric. You'll have heard. I knew him — knew the lanes he walked and who he talked to on them, and it was not what they put to him. He carried cord. He carried word between two old women who can't walk the hill themselves. That was the worst of him, and they made it the worst of something else. Somebody decided what he was from a long way off, from a street they've never stood in, and they got it wrong. A man who's never set foot in the Hook can't tell you who's a danger in it. He can write a name down. The name is enough for them."

**Draft B (rejected):**
> "You'll have heard they took Aldric. I knew the man. Whatever they wrote against him, it wasn't true — he ran cord and carried word between two women too old for the hill, and that's all he ever was. Someone far off looked at him and saw a threat, and they were wrong, and now he's taken."
> *Rejection: too smooth, too summarized. Collapses the connections into one breath. The persona's signature is pausing AT the connections — "He carried cord. He carried word..." — the parallel coordination doing the slow work. Draft B reads as report; Halvard testifies.*

**Draft C (rejected):**
> "A good man's been taken on a false charge, and the Father knows it false even if the Watch does not. Aldric served the old and the weak. The Seven see what he was, and what he was is not what they've made him."
> *Rejection: violates the persona leaf's "no theological jargon" override. Reaches for the Father / the Seven, which the class card permits but the LEAF forbids for this man — he speaks of specific people, not doctrine. Also "false charge" is a near-accusation-of-an-institution that edges toward the alternative-offering Halvard refuses. Cut.*

**chosen:** Draft A
**card-signatures:**

## Anchor 2 — @b01c13s04n06 (halvard:2) — FORECLOSURE

**Intent / state:** board-move = CONTINUE the counter-argument into the space Taylor has already left — name plainly what it costs to read a man from a distance, the argument going on existing, unanswered, after she has moved on / is departing. register-state = pastoral, plainer still, lower — the speech she does not wait for. distance = widening (she is in motion / leaving). framing = spoken to her back, but really to the air; the argument that does not need her to stay to remain true. **Draft A (chosen):**
> "You can know a street five hundred times over and never know what it weighs to be the name on the page. I've buried men a wiser hand wrote off. I'll bury Aldric too, like as not. And I'll not have the comfort of telling myself it had to be him."

**Draft B (rejected):**
> "Go on, then. It's always the same — they decide from far off and we bury the ones they decide on. You'll not stay to hear it. You never do. That's the cost no one writes down."
> *Rejection: "You never do" is an accusation aimed at Taylor — breaks Hard-Fence: he does not accuse her, and he is NOT supposed to register her departure as a personal slight. He is not scoring a point. The foreclosure works because he keeps speaking the ARGUMENT, not because he indicts her leaving. Cut.*

**Draft C (rejected):**
> "The Stranger comes for the man on the page same as any other, and the hand that wrote him there will answer for it in time. I'll bury him. I always do."
> *Rejection: theological-jargon override violation again (the Stranger, "answer for it") + edges into a threat/judgment ("will answer for it") that this man does not make — he names wrong, he does not promise divine reckoning. Cut.*

**chosen:** Draft A
