### Entry 3 — known-adult routing

**Objective:** Route the child to a known adult so Taylor can disengage. The chunk specifies "someone whose name she knows" as the third piece of information; this entry asks the crowd to produce that adult. Asking a real question (not rhetorical) — base-card §Syntax "No rhetorical questions in dialogue. She asks a question only when she wants the answer." She wants the answer because the answer is the disengagement-vector.

**Drafts:**

- (A) "Who knows her? Fetch them." [CHOSEN]
- (B) "Who is she? Get her people."
- (C) "Name her. Fetch them."

**Rejection notes:**
- (B): "Who is she?" asks identity, not adjacency. Taylor does not need the child's name; she needs the child's known-adult. "Get her people" is correct-Westerosi but "her people" is a household/kinship phrase that overshoots — many Flea Bottom children's "people" are simply other ward-network adults, not blood kin. The phrase would imply blood-search where adjacency-search is what's wanted.
- (C): "Name her" is a base-card-crisp imperative-question but assumes the crowd knows the child by name AND asks for the name; the chunk's "someone whose name she knows" implies Taylor wants the named-adult, not the child-name. (C) also has a closing-clause-twist potential ("Name her. Fetch them.") that lands a beat too aggressive for the moment — Taylor is still in mouth-shut public-frame and is not running adversarial-instruction-register here.

**Chosen rationale (Q1 + Q2):**
- Q1:
  - taylor-hebert.card.md §Syntax "No rhetorical questions in dialogue. She asks a question only when she wants the answer." — "Who knows her?" is a real question; Taylor needs the named-adult to step forward.
  - taylor-hebert.card.md §Syntax "Parallel structure for stacked observations." — "Who knows her? Fetch them." is parallel: question-then-imperative, both three-word, both subject-explicit-but-minimal.

  - taylor-hebert-westeros.card.md §Vocabulary Adds "period-plausible idioms: come morning, by nightfall, on the morrow, I have not seen, I am told" — "Fetch" is period-plausible Westerosi-smallfolk imperative (cards/dialects/westeros-smallfolk vocabulary-register).
