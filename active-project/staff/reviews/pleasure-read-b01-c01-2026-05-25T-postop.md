# Pleasure read — b01c01 — naive cold-read

Fork B of /and-postop b01c01. First-time reader. Read once at pace.

## 1. Did you enjoy reading it?

**Mixed, leaning no.** The italic opening hooked me — bugs, body-swap, Westeros, a rule — but the body of the chapter is so clipped and noun-stacked that I stopped *reading* and started *parsing* by paragraph four.

## 2. Where did attention drift?

Paragraph 9 lost me first: *"The drain water threaded the angle-gap of the stitch-house lane in the Hook — the lane-warren of Flea Bottom, King's Landing's slum-district where transient smallfolk go to be unremarkable — the trickle audible at the pinch-point, cobblestone-underfoot uneven at the angle-wall side (what Flea Bottom called a ward — a trade household that took in a parentless child for shelter and light work, not warm and not cruel)."* That's one sentence doing four glossary jobs in stacked em-dashes and parentheticals. I skimmed past "stitch-house," "Hook," "ward," "Flea Bottom" — I knew I'd be told again. Lines 11–19 are one-line beats stacked like stage directions ("The angle-wall narrowed the lane." "I exhaled." "The fish-cart blocked the lane.") — I skimmed three at a time. Oswyn's chin-lift in 59 is the second drift point — the simile spends fifteen words to do what an image could do in five.

## 3. Where did the prose grab?

Line 1: *"I had won my last world by becoming a hive of bugs, and I had not meant to wake."* That sentence I read twice. "Hive of bugs" + "had not meant to wake" is the whole pitch. Line 3 also landed — *"the specific weight of doing nothing"* is a real phrase, and the count "at its edge by the cost of holding it" gave me a body. Line 41 — *"Fever. Not the croup. She needs air."* — felt like an actual person speaking after a page of noun-cluster.

## 4. Voice — person or machinery?

**Machinery, mostly.** The italic frame reads like a person (rueful, specific, weight on the rule). The body reads like a stage diagram — "Lane-ambient — then tallow-smoke-onset" is a stage cue, not a thought. Compound-hyphen nouns ("angle-gap," "pinch-point," "lane-mouth," "chin-lift," "tallow-smoke-onset") accumulate until the narrator stops sounding like a woman holding flies and starts sounding like a labeling pass.

## 5. Did the ending pull me forward?

**Mildly.** Oswyn filing her into a category-without-a-name, and the eleven-year-old's eyes finding hers — that's a real hook, two witnesses she didn't want. But the final sentence's "fold where I kept the things that did not enter the ledger" is the kind of line that would land if I trusted the prose; after sixty lines of compound nouns I half-closed the book.

## 6. Genre / tone

Literary-leaning Westeros fanfic with a SF body-swap conceit. Restraint-and-rule first-person, slum-procedural setting. Reads like *The Goblin Emperor* crossed with a *Worm* interlude — interior, controlled, dread-adjacent, but the prose is straining for distinctiveness in a way that costs immersion.
