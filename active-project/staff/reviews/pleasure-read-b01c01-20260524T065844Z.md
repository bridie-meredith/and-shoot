# Pleasure-Read: b01-c01

**Mode:** first-time reader, single pass at reading pace.
**Length:** ~330 words.

---

**1. Did you enjoy reading it?**
Mixed — there is a real eerie undertone here (the flies, the count, "what I had not done"), but the prose works so hard to be technical that the pleasure is mostly retrospective, not in-the-moment.

**2. Where did your attention drift?**
Paragraph 1 — the building-keeper sentence. I skimmed past "subsistence-class permanent — the anonymous copper-star transaction purchasing entry." That clause-stack reads like a system note, not a sentence; I had to re-enter the chapter at paragraph 2. Paragraph 7 also lost me — "midday under overcast, the working position's spatial anchor established" is staging language pretending to be prose.

**3. Where did the prose grab you?**
Paragraph 23. "There were flies on the meat-stall. There weren't any on your hand." That's the whole chapter earning itself — a child speaking plainly cuts through the narrator's apparatus, and the implication (something is wrong with this person in a way insects can tell) is genuinely creepy. The follow-up "the girl arrived at it without the insects" is also a great line.

**4. Voice — person or prose-machinery?**
Prose-machinery for most of it — clauses like "the sense ran the walls and the walls gave it back unchanged" and "the working corner with unobstructed line-of-sight to the bend" read like an after-action report; the narrator surfaces as a person only when Wren forces a human exchange.

**5. Did the ending make you want more?**
Mixed-yes — "I lifted the needle" is too quiet to be a hook on its own, but the flies-revelation is sticky enough that I'd turn the page to find out what the narrator is.

**6. Genre / tone read.**
Westeros-set low-fantasy with a horror or uncanny-protagonist twist — Flea Bottom dressing, but the POV reads like a predator or revenant doing fieldwork. Quiet, cold, observational; closer to literary horror than to swords-and-intrigue fantasy.

---

## Awkward sentences quoted

- P1: "subsistence-class permanent — the anonymous copper-star transaction purchasing entry." (Two appositives chained; neither lands.)
- P7: "midday under overcast, the working position's spatial anchor established." (Stage direction, not prose.)
- P9: "The hands were on the needle and the needle was what they were for today, and this was what having hands was, here, in this place, at this work." (Reads as deliberate but tips into mannered.)
