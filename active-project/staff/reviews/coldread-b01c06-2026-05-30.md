# Cold-read: b01-c06 (2026-05-30)

First-time reader, text only.

## 1. EVENTS
1. A handcart blocks a lane; a crowd bunches at the junction. The narrator watches from a "working position."
2. A child the narrator knows by sight (a stitch-house ward-child) crosses to the narrator and says there's a way past, by the tallow-boiler's wall into the south court. The narrator takes that route.
3. The narrator logs the contact in coverage-notes but deliberately leaves the child's *name* field blank.
4. A message arrives on the "Jarvis channel." It asks not for movement patterns (as before) but for the *names* of the Black-faction ward elders.
5. The narrator pulls the four elders from existing records, fills the form with four names, but does not send it.
6. The narrator runs an "accounting" weighing the four names against "Sera's" protection, and notes a 14-year-old girl held captive at the Red Keep whose continued captivity depends on this exchange.
7. The narrator seals and sends the four names via courier.
8. The narrator reopens the ward-notes, looks at the still-blank name field of the child, and closes them.

Stretch with no nameable event: lines 1, 17-19 are interior/setup, not events. The accounting (27-35) is deliberation, not action — the only physical acts are filling, sealing, sending.

## 2. JEOPARDY
Yes. Three layers, all inferred from text: (a) four named ward elders are now exposed to a faction ("the heading can find" a name) — they're at risk of being hunted. (b) "Sera" is being protected by the narrator's compliance; non-compliance exposes her. (c) A 14-year-old girl is held captive at the Red Keep, kept there *by* this arrangement. I know all three only by the narrator's coded phrasing — none is dramatized; all are stated.

## 3. CAUSALITY
The chain is legible: blockage → child's tip → name withheld → escalated ask → accounting → send. The connective tissue holds. Where I was confused: *who the narrator is* and *what they are* (a person? a surveillance instrument? "the feed," "nodes," "the count" suggest the latter). Why the narrator obeys at all — the leverage ("Sera," the captive girl) is named but never explained, so the central decision rests on stakes I can't actually see, only trust.

## 4. PAYOFF
Partially. The ending — sends the names, then stares at the one name withheld — is a real turn: the contrast between four persons betrayed and one person protected is the point, and it's earned by the parallel structure (blank field at start, blank field at end). But it lands as a *figure* more than a *gut-punch*, because the four elders and the girl are abstractions I never met. The decision is the consequence; I just can't feel its weight.

## 5. CONTINUE?
Weak yes — the moral machine is intriguing and the withheld name is a genuine hook, but another chapter this airless would lose me.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
A surveillance operative who logs a ward by "movement patterns" is pushed to hand over four people by name, does it to keep a hidden girl alive, and quietly protects the one child whose name they refuse to write down.

## 7. READABILITY / ALIVENESS
This is the core problem. There is *almost* no one home. The narrator is written as an instrument reporting itself — "the count," "the feed," "nodes," "coverage-notes," "the accounting ran clean at full weight." It breathes in exactly two places: the child's spoken line ("I been through") and the physical friction of writing ("the stylus dragged... against a friction in the hand"). Everywhere else it goes airless — the "accounting" section (27-35) is the worst: abstract bookkeeping metaphor stacked on abstract metaphor ("both arms at full weight," "the framework arrives where the framework arrives") until I'm reading apparatus, not a person. I can follow the logic; I cannot inhabit anyone. The withheld name wants to be devastating and instead reads as a tidy diagram of a feeling.
