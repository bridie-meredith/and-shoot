# Naive cold-read — b01-c02

**Fork B / postop / 2026-05-26**

## 1. Enjoyed it?

No. This was work, and not the productive kind. The opening italics gave me a hook — a person who did a thing, paid for it, and is now doing something different. I leaned in. Then the prose hit the "insects returned the heat-signatures" register and stayed there for sixty lines, and I was decoding instead of reading. The only break from work was the brief arrival of the "ward-junction body" (line 37), where a person walks into the scene and something almost happens.

## 2. Where did attention drift?

Lines 7-15: heavy. Same nouns recycling (insects, beetles, heat-signatures, threshold-count, feed) before I knew what any of them did. Lines 25-34: same problem, worse — "insects filled the Hook," "foot-traffic knotted the ward-junction," "the beetles came back across the stones sweep after sweep." I skimmed. Lines 51-67: the final ledger-closing sequence reads like the writer ran out of verbs and started repeating the same beat ("closed the X entry, closed the Y entry, closed the Z entry") hoping cadence would carry meaning.

## 3. Where did prose grab?

The italic opener, line 1, the cost of holding the count at its edge — that's a person. Line 17: "the whole rear of the lane went silent before the head turned — a receipt for the range I had not yet committed to take." That landed. Line 33: tallow smoke at floor-level, lamps lit, work-day running — I saw it. Line 37, the yielded threshold, weight on the rear foot, body priced as exposure before it arrived — that was a paragraph of actual sensation. Line 49, dusk arriving along the stone, the angle-gap going darkest. Those five moments were the chapter.

## 4. Voice — person or machinery?

Machinery. The narrator has tics — "I held the line," "I drew the line," "the count," "the ledger," "the rule" — and they're deployed with such metronome regularity I stopped hearing a mind and started hearing a sentence-generator. Compound-hyphenated nouns ("ward-junction," "fever-cluster," "threshold-count," "junction-signature") at five per paragraph numb the ear. When the voice cracks open — italics, line 37 — there's a person there. Most of the chapter she's a system humming.

## 5. Want chapter+1?

Marginal yes. Because the italics promised stakes (she went three weeks without feeding anyone, she's holding a line) and I want to see those stakes touched. Not because this chapter gave me anything to want.

## 6. Genre/tone?

Literary surveillance-procedural with magic-system bones — Jeff VanderMeer with a notebook, or a much drier early-Miéville. Grimdark-adjacent secondary world. Goes on the "I respect this but don't reach for it twice" shelf.
