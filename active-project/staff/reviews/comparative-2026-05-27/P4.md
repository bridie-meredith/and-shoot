*I had one day. The proposal sat on the night-side of the count the way an unpaid debt sits at the edge of a ledger. I did not decide twice. I decided in the hour the dark thinned and the flies came back inside the count, and what I decided was that the harm I could prevent was the only column the accounting would close. The terms I had named for myself I would name to the courier at first bell, in the yard, in the same place he had named the question. The rule I had not stated in those words now had a price tag and a destination both.*

---
The tallow-damp off the lane-caulking reached me before the yard did. The cooper's workers held the smallfolk-hours murmur at the near shed, the third bell already gone quiet, the predawn flat. I walked into the yard and took the shed-wall and put the timber at my back and the open ground between me and the lane-mouth across, because three things at once is what the work asks, and the shed-wall asked for two of them, and the lane-mouth asked for the third.

The feed had him a span before the lane-mouth did. The gait that came in through the count was the gait already mapped, settled in its weight, settled in its step-interval, and the feed returned him to me a moment before the lane-mouth gave him to my eye. Jarvis Coin entered the lane-mouth.

I said, "Yes. The terms hold with two changes. You will have patterns — what moves through the streets, what does not, where the seams sit — not raw report of who said what to whom. The interval is mine to set. The volume is mine to set."

The yes went across the yard-air in the cadence of a settled count, parameter carried, prohibition still held. My hand closed on the shed-wall at my back.

I held my feet.

He said, "The man I serve takes those terms. The volume yours. The interval yours. The substance pattern-reports from the wards you walk. Same place. First bell. Three days."

The cadence carried receipt without the conditional that would have left the yes open. The receipt-form settled before my body finished holding.

The feed held the hook-range around him.

Jarvis Coin exited the lane-mouth.

I exited the cooper's yard.

Late morning shifted the air at the junction-mouth before the lane gave itself a name. The tallow-damp off the lane-caulking thinned and went over to a heavier register, the middens-discard sitting on the throat, the three alleys feeding the junction at the lane's near end and the discard-air sitting heaviest where they met.

I entered Pig Tallow Lane — the first Flea Bottom ward beyond the Hook, named for the rendering trade no other would carry, three alleys feeding a waste-middens junction at its mouth.

The work extended. The insect-range opened one ward outward, and the suppression-load that came with it settled at the weight I had already priced for it, and the feed widened, and the held weight beneath my breath did not move.

I mapped the junction-agitation. The discard-air carried the body-count without the bodies; the count came in through the feed at the cadence of three alleys feeding one mouth.

A carter parked the middens cart at the near alley. Penny-a-barrel work; the wheel-creak settled and went quiet.

The feed returned the gait before the body did. The step-interval came in already mapped, settled in its weight, the routing accounting for him before the eye took him in — Oswyn Mudway, the elder. I did not let the next thought finish.

I mapped the interval. Step-cadence, lane-position, range-from-junction; the count took him and held him at the reach the work had set.

Pig Tallow Lane returned ward-tier bodies only at the count I held. No prefect-weight, no household-guard register; only the foot-traffic the ward carried in its own hours.

The stitch-house frames marked the second ward. Frame-pitch and frame-spacing came back through the feed at the cadence of a lane that lived by needle-work, and the second-ward boundary settled at the reach.

The feed returned Wren.

I held my feet.

I mapped the second-ward junction. Frame-pitch, lane-mouth-width, the reach the work had taken; the count carried the ward at the same hand that had carried the first, and I did not turn the count toward the stitch-house lane.

The early-morning grey emptied Roper's Court. I entered Roper's Court — a Flea Bottom craft-trade ward turning on rope-making, a central court with tributary lanes radiating off long and linear enough to serve as rope-walks — and the carter-work-ambient that had carried the near hours thinned out and went over to near-silence, court-empty, the far tributary mouths still dark, the sight-lines from the court center running clear down each rope-walk to where the dark sat.

I extended the insect-range.

The fourth ward opened to the feed. The load came in at a register past the load I had carried before, the saturation-cost settling at a weight that had not been priced, and the holding stayed where the holding had been, and the count took the fourth ward and held it.

The four-ward feed ran. Roper's Court at center; three wards already routed; the fourth settling into the routing at the cadence the work asked, and the count carried four where it had carried three.

The cooper's yard took the next interval. Jarvis Coin entered the cooper's yard, half a yard of open air between his step and the shed-wall at my back, the lane-mouth giving him to the eye a moment after the feed had returned him.

He drew the note from his coat and held it up at the cadence of confirmation, the parchment turning once at the wrist so the seal-face came to the air and went back, and the note went back into the coat.

I delivered the report-sheet. The sheet was folded twice and crossed the yard-air at the reach the work had set, and the half-step of yard-air closed around the sheet's last contact with my hand, and the cost crossed before the hand had registered the crossing.

Jarvis Coin pocketed the report-sheet. The fold went into the inside coat-pocket at the cadence of received work; the coat closed.

The report-sheet carried ward-pattern. Lane-mouth counts at three intervals; foot-traffic registers by hour at the rendering ward and the stitch-house ward; the cart-routes that fed the middens-junction and the cart-routes that did not; cadence-of-shift at the rope-walks. No name on the sheet.

The report-sheet carried Flea Bottom-tier source-content. The hours were ward-hours; the bodies were ward-bodies; the carter and the rendering-hand and the stitch-house frame-worker and the smallfolk at the predawn shed. No prefect-weight; no household-guard register; no court-tier register at any line on the sheet.

The courier-arrangement held the conduit-rank. Jarvis Coin at the lane-mouth, the man he served two steps above him, the substance ward-pattern and the volume mine and the interval mine — the rank settled at the reach the terms had set, and the rank did not climb.

Jarvis Coin exited the cooper's yard. The sheet went with him into a channel the feed could not reach into, and the operation cycled at scale for the first time, and the count held.

I ran the ward-feed walking back through the Hook. Four wards in the routing; the cadence of the work tracking the lane-mouths and the cart-routes and the cadence-of-shift as the morning extended past the grey.

The insect-feed returned Wren. The frame at the stitch-house lane came in through the feed at the cadence already mapped, the second return now, the four-ward read continuing as the read continued, the one observation that did not enter any report continuing with it; reading and not-reporting ran the same operation at the same hand.

I exited the stitch-house lane. The stride held the four-count past the stitch-house frame, the tallow-lamps on at the lane, the north-end lane-mouth taking me through the last threshold before the Hook alley opened out.
