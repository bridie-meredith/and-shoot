## 1. Did you finish the chapter?

**Mixed.** I read every sentence, but I skimmed twice — once across the "the walls cooled, releasing day-warmth" paragraph and once across the threaded-needle re-description. Twenty-seven lines and I had to re-read four of them to figure out what was being asserted. I finished because it's short. If it were a chapter-length cape-fic post I'd have bailed at the third "I held the eyes."

## 2. Where did your Threshold Discipline fire?

Three fires, mapped to my card:

- **fatigue — "Skims lore sections when tactics stall."** Line 1: "I paid the building-keeper at the corner-room threshold — Flea Bottom, the bottommost ward of King's Landing, stone tenements packed around hook-channels; rent went in copper stars to whoever held the corner, no titles and no guild rolls, subsistence-class permanent — the anonymous copper-star transaction purchasing entry." That's a setting-codex dump welded onto a single action. I skimmed.

- **hot_buttons — "Character knowing something they haven't been told → boo."** Line 23: *"There weren't any on your hand."* Wren clocks a power-tell (flies-don't-land effect) on first contact, names the absence cleanly, and the POV confirms it (*"the girl arrived at it without the insects"*). I get that this is the asymmetry beat — but Wren is a stitch-maker's ward who has been on-page for one paragraph. She's earning a perception trust I haven't seen her pay for. **Boo, conditional** — if c02 shows her watch-history I'll forgive it; if not, this is the new-character-trust-without-paying tell.

- **fatigue — "Eye-glaze on unmotivated knowledge."** Line 19: *"I had already mapped the observation-radius and run the circuit count before the held label registered that I had."* I can feel the protag's competence-engine running but I cannot feel the cost. The "held label" phrasing is doing the work the prose should do. Glazed.

No walkout fire. The "established limits bypassed without cost" threshold did NOT fire — because I don't yet know what the limits are. That's the chapter's gamble.

## 3. What did you like?

**Line 23**, the same one I'm wary of: *"There were flies on the meat-stall. There weren't any on your hand."*

That is exactly the asymmetry-that-reshapes-the-board beat I read cape-fic for. The protag has been running a not-doing operation for a full page — running a count, holding hands flat, the sense running the walls and "the walls gave it back unchanged, because nothing here had called for what I was not doing" — and a thirteen-year-old ward names the leak in two sentences of meat-stall observation. The board flips. Setup paid. **Okay, that works.**

I'd also flag line 13 — *"the day held under the count I had been running, and the weight of what I had not done was in the count"* — as the thesis sentence of the chapter. Suppression-as-labor. I'll read on that.

## 4. What did you dislike or distrust?

**Line 7**: *"Every warm body in the block was legible at the work-level density I was not working against."*

This is where I distrust the prose. "Work-level density I was not working against" is doing four jobs in one clause — declaring the protag has a sensorium, declaring it has a throttle, declaring the throttle is currently engaged, declaring there's a baseline against which engagement reads. I had to read it three times. Cape-fic prose can carry compound power-ontology load but it has to *land* — this skids.

Also distrust the comma-em-dash-semicolon density. Line 3 has nine punctuation marks across two sentences. The voice is committed to it, fine, but it costs me forward velocity at exactly the moment the chapter needs me leaning in.

## 5. Would you read chapter 2?

**Yes.** Wren's flies-observation is a real hook — somebody non-powered just clocked the protag's power-tell cold, and the POV registered that she got there without the power doing what it usually does. I want to see what Wren does with it and what the protag does about Wren. That's a chapter-2 promise the prose actually wrote.

"Westeros POV-piece, suppression-engine protag working a not-doing operation in Flea Bottom, gets clocked by a thirteen-year-old ward on the flies-tell at the bottom of the chapter — prose is dense as a brick but the asymmetry beat lands, worth a chapter two."
