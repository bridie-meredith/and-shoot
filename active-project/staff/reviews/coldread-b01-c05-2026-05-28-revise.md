# Cold Read — b01-c05 (revise) — 2026-05-28

First-time reader. Read the chapter once, at pace, with no other context.

## 1. EVENTS

- An italicized prologue recaps (offstage) that the narrator gave a "four-ward sheet" to a courier; the arrangement protects a girl named Sera, ward of a Hightower branch in Alicent's household; the narrator routes intelligence to Otto in exchange.
- Narrator enters a new lane-area called "the Rushwick," near the Red Keep's servant passages, and sets up a surveillance "feed" using insect-bodies.
- A provisioner-train (four carts) passes through.
- A lone message-runner crosses.
- The courier (third sighting in three mornings) enters the lane.
- Three figures follow him into a side-alley; two block the mouth, one pins him to the stone. Something happens against the wall — described obliquely as sound the alley returns "below the register I would have called human."
- The courier stands back up. The three figures leave eastward.
- Narrator files the incident as an "enforcement-incident" and adds the courier to a body-map.
- Evening: narrator sits in a lodging room and re-runs the day's "feeds." The Hook feed closes clean. The "rushwick-feed" does not — a "color" persists on the architecture/route.
- Narrator stops the pass; their body recoils before they can name why. Closes the day.
- Tries a "rushwick flat-read" twice. Both times it fails to settle — first the provisioner-train holds it, then the courier-walk holds it. End.

Long passages have no event — just feed/count/categorization prose.

## 2. JEOPARDY

The courier is physically assaulted (or worse — the "below the register I would have called human" line implies sexual assault, though it's so veiled I'm not sure). That's the only concrete risk shown. The narrator says Sera is at risk if "Otto's rivals" surface a question about her parentage, but this is told in the italic prologue, not dramatized. The narrator themselves seems to be at psychological risk — their instrument is rejecting the work — but no physical danger to them is on the page.

## 3. CAUSALITY

Mostly opaque. I could not tell:
- Why the three figures attack the courier. Is this connected to the narrator's intelligence-routing? Are they Otto's people? Rivals? Random? The text refuses to say.
- Why the narrator does nothing to intervene, or whether they could have. The "report had a destination" line gestures at complicity but doesn't commit.
- What the "color" on the rushwick-feed actually is. Guilt? Recognition that the route they built is the route used to hurt the courier? I'm guessing.
- Why the feed "is no longer willing" — is this a literal property of the bug-swarm or a metaphor for the narrator's conscience? Unclear.

The scene-to-scene connection (morning incident → evening review failure) is causal in spirit but the prose hides the causal verb under jargon.

## 4. PAYOFF

The ending is the feed refusing to settle, twice. Earned in the sense that the morning event is what poisoned the read. Not earned in the sense that I, the reader, am asked to feel the weight of a thing the prose worked hard to keep me from seeing clearly. The turn lands intellectually — "the apparatus is no longer willing to support the discipline of a neutral read" — but emotionally it's muffled. I'm told the narrator's body stopped before their mind did; I don't feel the stopping.

## 5. CONTINUE?

Probably no. The voice is doing the same trick on every page — abstracting concrete events into "feed" and "count" and "architecture" — and after one chapter of it I'm tired and uncertain what actually happened. I'd put the book down.

## 6. ONE-LINE SUMMARY

A surveillance-bug-using narrator watches a courier get assaulted in an alley near the Red Keep, files the report up the chain they're complicit with, and that evening their instrument refuses to re-run the route cleanly.

## Confusions, flagged

- Is this Westeros? The "Hightower / Alicent / Otto / Red Keep" names say yes, but the "insect-bodies / feed / count" surveillance reads like a different genre grafted on. I could not place the narrator in the world.
- "Hook" appeared with no introduction — is it a place I should already know?
- What happens to the courier in the alley is deliberately unsayable in the prose, and I don't know if that's restraint or evasion.
