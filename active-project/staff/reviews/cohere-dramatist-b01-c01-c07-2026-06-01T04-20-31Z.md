## Axis 1 — Arc Legibility

**Verdict: ACCEPT**

The sub-section has a legible rise-peak-fall shape across the seven-chapter span.

Beat-by-beat shape:

- **C01 — Stasis with embedded tension.** Protagonist has arrived post-catastrophe with a self-imposed prohibition against control. The flies are held at the edge. The rule is named. Ward-elder Oswyn clocks her; the stitch-maker's ward clocks her. Two observers registered. Stakes: the rule itself is the dramatic pressure — can she hold it?
- **C02 — Observation escalates toward contact.** Coverage map expands. The stitch-maker's ward is placed as a recurring body. The accounting resists closing — "the last one cost more than the others" — signaling that neutral observation is already warping. Shape is rising.
- **C03 — Commitment threshold.** Jarvis presents the Otto offer. The protagonist takes the deferral ("a day") rather than refusing. The tether closes at the word "confirmed." Stakes cross from self-imposed discipline to external obligation.
- **C04 — The deal.** Terms agreed. First intelligence sheet handed over. Protagonist walks the four-ward feed and passes Wren's lane without entering. The suppression costs named explicitly: the count does not break for the stitch-house threshold. This is the point of no return — the line that "came up under the same hand" is crossed publicly, not privately.
- **C05 — Consequence arrives uninvited.** The enforcement-incident at the Rushwick: the courier is beaten. Protagonist files it as a report-entry and delivers it to the Otto channel. The evening re-run of the feed refuses to be neutral — "the instrument was recognizing the route it carried as the route." Internal apparatus begins to reject the deal.
- **C06 — The demand escalates.** Otto asks for names, not patterns. Protagonist fills the form, does not send — but then seals it. The four names leave her hands. The pivot is structural: the prohibition against directing persons has been crossed. Wren's name is kept out of the column; the blank entry is the only holdout. Peak is here.
- **C07 — Fall with argument, no resolution.** Septon Halvard, read as a fixture for two months, names the logic of compounding corruption without knowing what he is naming. Protagonist names Wenna Cobb — the founding cost the counting was supposed to prevent. Neither position is paid. She plants her feet, stays, refuses departure, then leaves before a resolution arrives. End state ≠ start state: the suppression has been broken, the names dispatched, and the argument has been left explicitly open rather than closed.

The trajectory moves. The shape is rise-peak-fall with a deliberate pyrrhic resolution at the fall.

## Axis 3 — Antagonist Pressure Curve

**Verdict: CAUTION**

Otto Hightower is the primary antagonist-force, but he is not a continuous pressure — he is an episodic demand-escalator who operates entirely off-page. His agency is:

- C03: the offer (mediated through Jarvis)
- C04: the confirmation (mediated through a parchment)
- C06: the escalation (mediated through a form)

Between these points, the antagonist pressure is structural — the arrangement bears down — but it is not agentive. Otto makes no move. The arrangement simply exists and the protagonist moves within it. This means C02, C04's long walk through the four wards, and C05's evening review all have rising internal pressure but no external antagonist move to sharpen against.

The enforcement-incident in C05 functions as indirect antagonist pressure (the protagonist's reporting of the courier's beating is morally implicating), but the three figures are anonymous and Otto's hand is not named. The connection is inferable, not declared.

The result is that antagonist pressure is correctly spiking at the transaction points (C03 → C04 → C06) but fragmenting in the intervals. The sub-section passes because the pressure is coherent in direction and escalating in demand. But the antagonist is absent as an active force across approximately half the sub-section's pages. Halvard in C07 functions as a proxy antagonist for the argument, but he is not adversarial — he is a structural critic inside the protagonist's same moral frame.

This does not fail the sub-section. It flags that the b01 stretch is an interior-pressure narrative rather than an adversary-pressure narrative, which places heavier weight on the try-fail mechanics and substance of the protagonist's internal arc — which carries.

## Axis 4 — Scene-Shape Distribution

**Verdict: CAUTION**

The sub-section is heavily weighted toward two shapes: **interior accounting** and **exchange/transaction**.

- C01: action / observation (the crowd-clearing) + interior filing. One extended interior.
- C02: interior observation. No argument, no external confrontation.
- C03: two exchanges (Jarvis at the market, Jarvis at the cooper's yard). Both are transactional — neither is an argument.
- C04: interior + transaction. No argument.
- C05: observation + interior review (the evening bedframe scene). The enforcement-incident is witnessed but not engaged.
- C06: interior + transaction (the seal-and-send). One charged internal moment.
- C07: **argument** — the first genuine argument in seven chapters, and it is the only scene where the protagonist's position is contested by a person who can hold the other side.

Distribution: ~1 action beat (C01), ~5 interior/accounting scenes, ~4 transaction scenes, ~1 argument (C07). The argument is structurally crucial and correctly placed at the fall — but its isolation means the stretch carries no contested dialogue for six chapters before it arrives. The Jarvis exchanges in C03-C04 are negotiations without debate; the protagonist's interiority has no external voice to push against until Halvard.

This does not block the cohere verdict — the interior-dominance is consistent with the POV architecture (first-person, observation-as-control, suppression-as-discipline) and the C07 argument lands with more force precisely because it has had no predecessor. But it is a structural note: the stretch would sharpen if one of C03-C05 carried a scene that put an external voice on the other side of the protagonist's accounting before C07. As built, Halvard bears the full weight of being the first opposition voice in the stretch.
