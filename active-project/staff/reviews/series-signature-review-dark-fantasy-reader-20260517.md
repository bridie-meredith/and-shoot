## Rationale

**On Otto's offer + Hand-in-fact apparatus as political mechanism:**
This reads as load-bearing F&B-aligned political physics, not window dressing. Otto's pressure curve — "calibrated asks; never threatens, proposes; the pressure is being right about the arithmetic" — is the correct texture for a Hightower operating in 122 AC. He is not the cartoonish schemer; he is the one who prices things accurately. The fact that the antagonist-pressure entry identifies the mechanism as *cumulative threshold-lowering* rather than escalating threat is right for Westerosi court politics: the danger is the accumulation of individually defensible trades, not a single extortion. The trajectory delivers this through d03→d07→d12 — each delta names a specific ratchet, not a genre-beat. That structure earns it.

**On Flea Bottom burning as Westerosi tragedy vs. generic grimdark:**
The cost-bearer mechanics are what separate this from grimdark gesture. The smallfolk death is not a consequence that arrives from outside the plot — it arrives through the infrastructure Taylor built to prevent it: the streets her network mapped, inside Otto's architecture. The trajectory anchors this at d14 explicitly: "moves through exactly the streets Taylor's network mapped." That is consequence-that-propagates (the persona's core pull condition). The ironic reversal — the protection apparatus is the cartography of the harm — is Westerosi in its physics: the smallfolk are not priced into anyone's survival calculus, and the one person who tried to price them was corrupted by the attempt. This passes.

**On Green-faction consolidation and dynastic-succession physics:**
Antagonist position 6→8 on position-axis, social-tether 7→9, agency 7→9. Those are real consolidation numbers and the cost-ledger entry cl-protection-buys-consolidation names the mechanism: every trade extending [protect-target]'s protection entrenches the Green faction. The trades work *because* they are accurate. This is not "evil people win" — it is a structural demonstration that accurate intelligence delivery is inherently faction-empowering regardless of the deliverer's intentions. The succession physics feel Planetos-specific: small-council capture, Maegor's Holdfast control, dynastic succession angle — these are F&B-recognized levers, not generic palace intrigue.

**On the world ending at rank 1 — whether the tragic weight is earned:**
World perspective: moral-framework 3→1, social-tether 4→1, relational-anchor 3→1, dynastic position 4→1. Four world-axes hitting 1 simultaneously at the Dance ignition is potentially over-stacked, but the trajectory earns it by making each a consequence of the same mechanism rather than independent disasters. Community substrate collapse, dynastic rupture, moral-order collapse, and smallfolk-bond severance are all the same event named from different angles. The persona's fatigue trigger is stakes that feel fake — these stakes are structurally interdependent, which means the collapse reads as systemic rather than cumulative-punishment. Passes, but barely: if the bone-gate does not differentiate the register of each collapse, they will read as the same disaster repeated.

**On the magic-fence vs. capability +4:**
This is the one tension worth flagging explicitly. Capability 3→7 (+4) is a large upward move on a protagonist-axis for a project with a hard magic-dormant-on-KL-court-layer fence. The condition card `cond-westerosi-magic-dormant-122ac` permits rumor, prohibits narrator-confirmation. The persona card flags "magic or power treated as reliable tool rather than uncertain force" as a hot-button flag. The capability gain is not magic in the Westerosi sense — it is the insect-network as intelligence apparatus — but the signature does not foreground the *uncertainty* of the capability's deployment. The trajectory implies systematic and reliable operation (d04 "builds the insect network outward," d12 "push the network to full coverage"). The fence says the court layer cannot confirm magic; it does not say the protagonist cannot use it. However: if the bones treat Taylor's capability-gain as a reliable intelligence tool with predictable outputs, the persona will flag it. The uncertainty that makes the capability Westerosi-compatible must appear in the grain of the operation, not only in the disclaimer layer.
