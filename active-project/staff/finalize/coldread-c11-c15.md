# Naive cold-read drag report — Chapters 11–15

Reader stance: reading for pleasure, flagging where attention slips. The cold ledger register is the book's signature and is NOT flagged for being cold/spare. Flagged only where density tips into genuine fatigue: abstraction with no concrete anchor, restatement, or passages where nothing advances.

Headline: this stretch runs ONE structural beat over and over — Taylor reads a thing through her insects, her hand lifts off the stylus / comes off the surface, a source-field or name-field stays blank, and she closes a ledger entry without writing the cost. Chapters 11, 12, 14, and 15 are all variations of this exact gesture. Individually most are well-built; in sequence the repetition is the dominant drag, and the "hand comes off the surface" / "source-field stayed bare" image is worn through. Chapter 13 is the standout of the range because it has an actual scene with two other characters and forward motion. Chapter 12 is the draggiest — it is almost pure ledger-mechanics abstraction with no human in it.

## CHAPTER 11

- CHAPTER: 11
- ANCHOR: "owed against the quiet over Sera — and naming it had changed nothing"
- SEVERITY: TIGHTEN
- WHY: The epigraph states the chapter's thesis ("naming it changed nothing"), then paragraph 4 ("a named function written into the channel and owed against the quiet over Sera ... though it changed nothing about how the morning had run") restates it almost verbatim — the reader is told the same thing twice within two screens.
- TRIM: Cut the restatement clause in para 4 ("a named function written into the channel and owed against the quiet over Sera, and ... though it changed nothing about how the morning had run") down to a bare beat; the epigraph already carries it.

- CHAPTER: 11
- ANCHOR: "the hand came off the surface with the field still bare"
- SEVERITY: TIGHTEN
- WHY: The signature gesture (hand off the surface, field stays bare) is delivered three times in this one chapter (the source-field omission in para 5, plus the closing "two omissions ... closed the entry over them") and it recurs in 12/14/15 — within Ch11 alone the omission-as-physical-gesture is explained more times than the beat needs.
- TRIM: Keep the first full statement (para 5, "That was the whole of the omission — a hand off a board"). Compress the para-9 closing recapitulation of the two omissions; it re-explains the mechanism the reader already watched.

- CHAPTER: 11
- ANCHOR: "The sourcing wrote itself in three motions"
- SEVERITY: CUT
- WHY: The soap-lane / precinct-pattern paragraph (para 8) is a third near-identical "I sourced it, the older question didn't intrude, I sealed it" cycle stacked on top of two already shown; it advances nothing and exists only to count to three. The "cycle closed on three and did not feel short" is filler about filler.
- TRIM: Cut the bulk of this paragraph or fold its single new fact (a contact reporting a nighttime visitor) into the end-of-day arm-closing summary; drop the "wrote itself in three motions / no fourth motion / closed on three" mechanics.

- CHAPTER: 11
- ANCHOR: "I ran it in motion and closed the arms one at a time"
- SEVERITY: TIGHTEN
- WHY: The closing paragraph re-walks all four "arms" (Jarvis / Oswyn / contacts / arrangement) the reader already saw open earlier in the chapter — it is a recap, not new ground, and reaching it after the omission has already been belabored makes the chapter feel like it ends twice.
- TRIM: Compress the four-arm roll-call to two arms at most; the Jarvis and Oswyn arms add nothing not already shown in paras 2–3.

## CHAPTER 12

- CHAPTER: 12
- ANCHOR: "every column but two ... never written ... never written"
- SEVERITY: FLAG
- WHY: The whole chapter is the same omission beat as Ch11 with a new map-coverage skin and no other human present — it is the draggiest chapter in the range because the abstraction (coverage parameters, ward-clusters, gap-columns, anchor-columns) has drifted almost entirely off concrete scene. Load-bearing (it establishes the eastern-boundary withhold that pays off in 14/15), so noted not cut.
- TRIM: Do not cut wholesale — but this chapter should lose the most words in the range. See the entries below.

- CHAPTER: 12
- ANCHOR: "The insects came back to me off the overhang-joints first"
- SEVERITY: TIGHTEN
- WHY: The opening paragraph is one ~250-word block of coverage geometry (overhang-joints, lane-mouth, gate-tower shadow, west wall, east wall, stitch-house route, ward-cluster, water-gate entry) with no human and no event — the reader's eye glazes because nothing happens, it is all map-state being recited.
- TRIM: Collapse the wall-by-wall boundary inventory ("The map closed the gate-tower boundary on the west. It closed the rendering-yard wall on the east. Both walls down ...") into a single sentence; the reader does not need each wall placed individually.

- CHAPTER: 12
- ANCHOR: "the same withhold, the third time, recognized and not stopped"
- SEVERITY: CUT
- WHY: This is the narrator explicitly counting her own repetitions for the reader ("the route never written, the burned message ... never written, and this; the same withhold, the third time") — it both restates the epigraph AND flags that we are on the third instance of an already-overused beat, which doubles the fatigue rather than relieving it.
- TRIM: Cut the enumerated tally of prior withholds; let the gesture stand once without the running count.

- CHAPTER: 12
- ANCHOR: "every ward and the lane between them at once ... the full circuit for the first time"
- SEVERITY: TIGHTEN
- WHY: The fifth-ward / full-circuit paragraph says "the circuit closed / the full circuit ran / the whole architecture returned simultaneous and complete" in four or five different phrasings back to back — pure restatement of "the coverage is now complete."
- TRIM: Keep one statement of full-circuit completion; cut "The count ran the full-circuit return. The feed returned the Flea Bottom approaches ... at the full scale, every approach into it mapped and held" which re-says it.

- CHAPTER: 12
- ANCHOR: "the older word — not a description of the thing in front of me"
- SEVERITY: FLAG
- WHY: The "older shape / built in a different city / the one this rhymes with" gesture toward Taylor's past appears here, and again in 14 ("a war whose dates I carry"), and again in 15 ("a war whose dates I carry and do not say aloud") — coy past-allusion recurs across the range and is starting to read as a tic. Load-bearing here, but the trio should not all use the same withholding move.
- TRIM: Note only — vary or thin the past-allusions across 12/13/14/15 so they don't all land as the same "the older one did not acquire a name" move.

## REPETITION ACROSS 11–15

The dominant problem in this range is one beat repeated with different surface skins. Counts below.

1. **The "hand comes off the surface / source-field stays bare / lifted the stylus past the name-field" omission-gesture.** This is the signature move and it is the structural spine of 11, 12, 14, and 15 — but as a *physical gesture* it is rendered at least 8–10 times across the five chapters:
   - Ch11: "the hand came off the surface with the field still bare" (para 5); "closed the entry over them without giving either a name" (para 9).
   - Ch12: "the hand came up off the surface with the field still bare"; "My hand stopped short of the source-field and did not come down"; "The stylus lifted before the explanation field."
   - Ch14: "the stylus stopped above the closed entry"; "lifted the stylus above the breach-entry and added no line."
   - Ch15: "My hand crossed the field where a name would go ... set no name down."
   Recommendation: this gesture should appear in full perhaps twice across the range and be implied the rest of the time. Right now every withhold gets the same choreographed hand-and-stylus close.

2. **"The route never written" / Wren-and-Sera as the two permanent open columns.** Stated in the Ch12 epigraph, restated in Ch12 body, re-established in the Ch14 epigraph, and re-established AGAIN in the Ch15 epigraph and body. ~5 statements of "two columns I never write." The epigraphs are doing redundant set-up work — by Ch15 the reader has been told three times that Wren's route is the unpriced open entry.

3. **The coy past-war allusion ("the older shape, built in a different city" / "a war whose dates I carry and do not say aloud" / "a different kind of count ... at another scale").** Appears in 12, 14, and 15 (and gestured at in 11's "every quiet machine of mine"). ~4 instances of the same withheld-backstory move. It reads as a tic by the third use; vary or thin.

4. **"I walked on / the route did not bend / unchanged before and after" closer.** Ch11 ends on the four-arm close; Ch13 uses it twice (lane-stop para 590 AND trough para 606–608); the "nothing bent the route" idea recurs. ~4 "I continue unchanged" endings.

5. **"The arrangement continued" / "the arrangement still ran" / "that arrangement held only while her deliverables held."** The Sera-arrangement status is re-narrated at the head or foot of nearly every chapter (11 body, 14 epigraph, 15 epigraph, 15 final line "The arrangement continued"). Load-bearing as continuity glue, but it's stated more often than the slow-moving plot requires.

6. **Self-counting of repetitions.** Twice the narrator explicitly tallies that this is the Nth instance of a beat ("the same withhold, the third time" — Ch12; "the same counter I had run at this trough before" — Ch13). This is the prose admitting its own repetition; it amplifies rather than excuses the fatigue and should be cut both times.
