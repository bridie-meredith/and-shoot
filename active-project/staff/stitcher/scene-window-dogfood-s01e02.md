## Rendered episode

*Through the first weeks in Flea Bottom I had walked my three hundred metres twice a day, mapped the market-side junction and the apothecary's upper room where the broken maester kept his hours, and made my first transactional pass with the dock-runner through the tanner-elder. The log was current. The tanner-village was a day's walk south.*

---

I faced the junction. The elder was already turned to the road, watching for them. My father came in from the market side with my mother a step behind, and the elder greeted them both. I walked into the junction and pivoted toward them. My father stopped. I faced him with the shoulders squared and the weight settled. "We come down with the hides," he said. "Your mother would see you." I answered, "Goodman. I have not forgotten." He held, and my mother held with him. Then he raised the trade goods and turned to the elder. "Old Tom. A word, when you've a moment. The Crownlands lot, same as before." The elder kept his gaze on the goods through the speaking. "Aye. Same slate, same cut. She's earning her place, last I heard. We'll keep it that way." My mother turned to me. My father's weight came back onto the rear foot, his gaze on the strap-end at his hip. "You've grown thin, child," my mother said. "Are you eating?" I answered, "Goodwife. The road is yours." She turned toward the road south. My father went with her, and they walked out of the junction.

Back at the base room, I opened the log, wrote it down, closed it.

After, I let the relays run. The flies had the tanner-elder. The wasps had the Watch patrol — a perimeter I had not had to walk — and the wasps had the dock-runner.

The daylight dropped when I came in: the room empty, the second-floor window carrying alley sound from below. The flies still had the carter and the wind. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

Out to the market-side junction. The wind was off the road south and the sound from the Fish Gate margin came thin along the edge of it. The elder paused. "Off with you, then. Tell the dock-man Tom's word is good for the load. Before the bell," he said. The wasps brought the pass through: the interval had held, the question closed. He turned and faced me square at his working pitch, the placement set on his face. The carter rolled out of the junction. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

The flies spread the overnight network; the wasps the dock-side alleys; the beetles the south-wall colony; the spiders the eastern-quarter approach. My shoulders dropped back and down, the move clean and remembered. The flies brought the junction return; the wasps were on the Fish Gate — the southern dock-side gate of King's Landing, the city's busy fish-trade mouth — holding the perimeter; the beetles brought the south-wall return; the spiders the window. I exhaled. The flies spread the northern block; the wasps the eastern-quarter adjacent. I exhaled a second time. I walked the perimeter, wrote the entry, wrote it again, and exhaled once more.

I woke and held the eyes still, then opened the log, wrote the entry, and closed it.

The night alley was iron-latched and occupied when the lord's-man stepped into it, his stride loaded wrong for Eastern-Quarter ground. He spoke to the family at the door. His man broke the latch — and the ambient murmur cracked. The bar gave. His man carried the family's possessions out. The family came out of the two-room dwelling. A neighbour pressed at the doorway. The flies relayed the lord's-man's man — an ear pressed to a wall. The beetles relayed the lintel. The flies held the wall. The neighbour withdrew. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

My father and mother came back into the junction. He faced me across the open ground. "There's the wage-claim, girl. Customary. Two coppers a moon till the harvest turn, and we're square," he said — what the Crownlands called the customary wage, the unwritten claim a family carried for the labour a returned child owed them, a tally without paper. I faced him. "Name the sum. I will see it paid," I answered. "Two coppers. That's the way of it. Same as your mother's people done for hers," he said. I let the breath out. I opened the purse. I counted the coins once across the palm and again, then held them out. He closed his hand on them without lifting his gaze to my face. "We'll come again at the next moon's turn. Mind yourself," he said. I faced him. He went out of the junction; she went after him. I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed the log.

The flies spread the autumn-density network, the wasps the dock-side relay, the spiders the eastern-quarter relay. I walked the perimeter and wrote the entry, wrote it again, flexed the hand. I woke. The stylus dropped — the rhythm against wax cut to a clatter. I held the eyes still. I lowered the chin. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

The afternoon held the side alley clear. The visitor crossed it, then the stairwell, then the upper room. The visitor spoke; I did not hear what. The beetles brought the register through the wall, and the maester's voice came down from the upper room: "Yes, yes. Come, sit. Let us see what you have brought." I held my feet where they were. He went out by the upper room, by the stairwell, by the side-alley door. I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed it on a name.

The beetles brought a rhythm. The beetles brought a phrase. The beetles brought a rhythm. I went still. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

Dusk cut the apothecary corner to a strip. The broken maester came out of the apothecary, took the side alley, crossed the upper-room threshold. The beetles named him through. I opened the log, wrote the entry, closed it.

My mother came in. The vigil candle — a Stranger-light kept burning for the dead until the family released them — was on the shelf where it had been. She faced me and sat. I faced her. "The candle's gone short. I'll let it burn what's left and trouble you no more," she said. "She is not here, goodwife," I answered, without raising the chin from the log. "I'll stop coming, after this one. Seven keep you. Whatever you are, you are warm — that's what I came to see," she said. She lowered her gaze. Her thumb passed once across the edge of the bench, the stained crease at the rim, and then her weight came off it; the chair gave, and the floor gave under her. She went out of the room.

I opened the log. I wrote the entry. I closed the log.

**Scene-window is materially better than per-anchor for s01e02 — but the magnitude is moderate, not transformative.** The two prior dogfoods (breath-log, organic-render-p4) concluded "modest improvement, leverage is upstream." Scene-window agrees with that conclusion at the macro level.

Where scene-window leverages best:
- Multi-bone percussion-clusters (stilling-trios, exhale-pairs, sentence-opener chains).
- Seams between adjacent scenes (the relay-register carry, the close-on-name → rhythm-open).
- Lens-fold positioning across a cluster (sensory spike at a known peak, NI fold at a transitional verb).

Where scene-window does NOT change the picture:
- Protected patterns (cardinal-quartet, three-note buildup, log-trio): bone-locked, no variance license.
- Hard scene-jumps (H→I, M→N): the seam IS the jump; awareness doesn't change render.

- Wallpaper percussion (log-trio cadence repeated 12+ times): the count is bone-determined; scene-window can vary form, not count.

**The leverage is upstream**, as the prior dogfoods concluded:
- Bone-author Q9 discipline (don't put "autumn-density" / "alley-murmur" / "placement-look" in bones).
- Tens-3 placement (the scene-window made the @125 and @173 peaks visible; if more bones were tens-2, scene-window would have more variance license to spend).
- Log-trio count control at the dramatist/proto-line level (12 log-trios in this episode is series-law-load-bearing 3-4 + 8 wallpaper; the bones author chose to render every log-write).

Scene-window can polish the surface but cannot fix the underlying density distribution. Recommendation: scene-window for production renders (the percussion-break is real and worth the cost), AND upstream Q9 discipline pass on bones authoring, AND tens-rebalance toward fewer-but-richer beats.

### 6. Cost

Input width: per scene-fork's input ≈ scene bone-set (avg 11 bones) + adjacent scene preview (5 bones) + facet citations on those 16 bones (avg 8 facet entries per scene) + persona + tensometer slice. Rough estimate: 2-3x wider than per-anchor fork's input (which reads 1 bone + previous-2-lines + facets-at-anchor). For a 14-scene episode that's 14 forks of ~3x width = ~42 anchor-equivalents of context; per-anchor would be 134 forks of 1x = 134 anchor-equivalents. Net token cost is LOWER per episode for scene-window (~30% of per-anchor), but each fork's reasoning is more complex.

**Worth it**: yes for production. Token cost reduces; quality improves at the multi-bone seams; the failure modes (bone-folding-into-summary) are catchable at log-time discipline.

**Not worth it**: not for projects where the bones are already tens-balanced with low percussion (e.g. lots of distinct events, no log-trio-density). On such an episode the per-anchor unit's outputs and scene-window outputs would converge fully and the architecture change earns nothing.
