SCENE-OPEN PROMPT PLAN — s01e02 bullet 1 — for taylor-hebert-westeros impersonator via coach:

Taylor is in the sept side-room. Eyes open in the dark. Pre-dawn. She has been awake for some duration or has woken into this — the distinction may not be clear to her. What is clear is the room around her and the thing her passive sense is not finding.

Sensory priorities in order of narrative weight:

1. THE DARK. The shutter is closed. The sept has no fire. There is no source of light in this room — not a rush-dip, not an ember, not the grey seep of approaching dawn through the gaps at the shutter's edge, or barely that, barely anything. The dark is the first fact of the room. It has texture: cold stone walls, low ceiling that she knows by the way sound stops, the specific enclosed quality of a room that is small enough to feel its own boundaries without seeing them. She knows this room by now. She does not need light to locate the door, the mat, the shutter. She knows where she is. That is not the same as being comfortable in the dark.

2. THE COLD. The sept stones hold the night. This hour is the coldest — the coldest before the cold breaks, the hour when the building has shed whatever warmth the previous day put into it. The mat is the only insulation between her and the floor. Whatever covering she has gathered around her is the only warmth in the room. Outside that radius: still, cold air. The cold is not dramatic. It is persistent and specific — the cold of stone that has been cold for eighty years and will be cold after she is gone.

3. THE ABSENCE IN THE WARRENS. This is the thing. She is not reaching for it — her passive sense is simply running, as it does when she is awake, a constant low-register awareness of what is within range. And the granary, a short walk from the sept, has warrens under its floor that should have rats in them. Pre-dawn is when the warrens are most active — scatter-patterns, the specific low-level churn of rodents before first light. She knows this pattern. She has been here long enough to have catalogued it without trying to, the way you catalogue a clock's tick without meaning to. The pattern is not there. The warrens are registering as empty. Not quiet — empty. The absence is the wrong shape for this hour. It found her, she did not find it. She may not have named it yet. It is the reason she is lying with her eyes open in the dark.

4. THE SEPTON'S BREATHING. Audible through the wall if she attends to it — and she attends to it. The wall between the side-room and the sickroom is thin enough to carry sound. She has memorized the specific rhythm of his breathing by now, without choosing to. The slow register of someone for whom breathing has become its own work. It is there. She can locate it. This is information: he is breathing. This is also its own kind of pressure: the sound is what it is, not what she wants it to be, and she knows the difference.

5. THE SETTLEMENT QUIET. The settlement smallfolk are in their dwellings. Nothing is moving outside the sept walls. No foot traffic, no livestock sound from the farms, no voices. The bell tower ravens are still — weight-shifts on timber, the occasional small adjustment, but no departure, no call. Nothing at the settlement perimeter is registering through her passive sense that is inconsistent with pre-dawn — nothing except the granary. The quiet is not unusual for this hour. The specific gap where the warrens should be is what makes the quiet strange.

6. THE SEPT SMELL. Tallow — the permanent smell, the decades of candles that have made it into the stone. Cold ash. Old stone. Sleeping-human warmth from her own body, which is the only warm thing in the room. She has been breathing this smell for however long she has been here. It is the smell of whatever passes for home at this point in her life. She does not have to be sentimental about it for it to be the background of every breath she takes in this room.

7. THE NOT-YET-DAWN. The shutter is closed. She cannot see the sky. She can infer it: the dark outside is not the same dark it was at midnight — there may be a quality of lightening at the very edge of perception, through the gaps in the shutter's fit, or there may not yet. The sun has not risen. The world is in its night configuration. This is the hour before it changes.
