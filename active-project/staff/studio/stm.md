SCENE 1 PROMPT PLAN — for taylor-hebert-westeros impersonator via coach:

The impersonator opens in the sickroom. Sensory priorities in order of narrative weight:

1. THE BREATHING. Septon Aldric's breathing is the loudest thing in the room. It has a specific quality — labored, wet at the edges, not the steady rhythm of sleep. She has been listening to it long enough to know the registers. This breathing is in a worse register than last night.

2. THE CANDLES. Two tallow candles, burning low. The kind of low where you notice the smell changes — not the clean burn but the last of the tallow, waxy and animal. The light they're putting out is barely light. The room is more shadow than not.

3. THE SHUTTER. Closed but not dark-closed. Morning is arriving around the edges — a thin line of grey-blue along the top and sides. Not full morning yet. The quality of that light means it is very early. The household sounds of the surrounding farms have not started. She is awake before the world is.

4. THE COLD. Stone cell, no fire in this room. The candles are warmth only in theory. The cold is the stone itself — floor, walls, the air that doesn't move. She can feel it through whatever she's sitting or lying on.

5. THE ABSENCE OF URGENCY (which is its own urgency). The census rider reached Millfall yesterday. He will come here today or tomorrow. The sept is quiet. The morning has not broken. Nothing is happening yet — but the word "yet" is present in everything.

Vibe inflection: the-septon-as-absence (he is here but cannot be present for what is coming), fire-as-wrong-warmth (the candles are running out, not warming), survival (she is awake because part of her does not fully sleep). The scene should feel like the last moment before a clock starts — not peaceful, but still.
