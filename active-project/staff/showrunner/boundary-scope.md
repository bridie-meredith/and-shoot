Brief (verbatim): "taylor hebert, post gold morning, in westeros king's landing before dance of dragons. theme should be 'road to hell is paved with good intentions' where taylor does bad things for a good reason which later turns out to be bad. one book long series"

## Structural end-requirement framing

The brief fixes the **theme** as the road-to-hell pattern: protagonist does bad-for-good-reason, which later turns out to be bad. This locks the cause structure — protagonist agency causes the bad outcome via her own well-intended choices. It does NOT explicitly fix whether she ends in a bad-place, ambiguous-place, or even survives. The dominant reading is **bad-place or ambiguous-place** (the theme only resolves if the consequences land), with the **cause structure** = protagonist's own good-intent choices compounding into harm. End-state-valence remains OPEN; end-state-causation is FIXED.

### STORY TYPE (that fits the boundaries)

- **Tragedy of agency** — protagonist's competence and good intent are the engine of catastrophe; the structure is hubris-without-vanity, the recognition scene is the climax. Best fit for explicit "later turns out to be bad."
- **Historical-irony tragedy** — protagonist's intervention is what makes the known-bad-history happen (or happen worse); reader-foreknowledge of the Dance is load-bearing dramatic irony.
- **Compounding-error noir** — chain of locally-defensible choices each foreclosing exits, ending in a trap of the protagonist's own building; smaller in scale than full tragedy, more procedural.
