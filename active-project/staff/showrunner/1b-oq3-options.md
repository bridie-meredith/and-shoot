# OQ-3 Options — Arcane Access Vector

**Question:** How does Taylor get her hands on Valyrian / blood / glass-candle / dragon-binding magic to experiment on? What is the primary access vector(s) that gates the arcane experimentation arc?

**Constraints in force:**
- Glass-candle work MUST be in scope and load-bearing (late-series relay infrastructure).
- Arcane experimentation is structural, not flavor. Grimdark contemplative-procedural-horror register.
- Taylor opens story village-embedded near King's Landing, ~300m insect range, no Khepri-mantle.
- Westerosi magic is fading, hoarded, taboo. She takes/builds access; she is not given it.
- Spurns nobility — sorcerer-apprenticeship to a noble figure is structurally on-tension and must be handled carefully.

---

## Option A — Extraction-by-Observation: The Insect Archive

**Primary access vector:** Taylor's bug-network is a surveillance instrument no Westerosi institution can detect or block. She does not steal texts or coerce practitioners — she reads over their shoulders, through walls, for years. Bugs in Citadel corridors sense ink on pages, register vocal recitation, map hand-positions over candle-flame. Bugs in the Red Keep throne-room witness blood-rite preparations no one performs publicly. She is the most patient natural philosopher in the history of the Seven Kingdoms because she never sleeps on a single research thread.

**Secondary vectors:** She acquires one glass-candle through a smallfolk intermediary — a dockworker who handles cargo from a drowned Citadel ship, salvage never formally catalogued. A second is taken from a dead maester after a fever-outbreak she deliberately did not prevent in time; the maester was actively suppressing outbreak data to protect a lord's harvest schedule. She frames this to herself as justice. The bugs were already in his room when he died.

**Chronology:**

- Seasons 1–2: Pure observation. She builds a theory of Valyrian sorcery from fragments — incomplete recitations, partial texts, witnessed blood-price rituals. The working theory is explicit: glass-candle relay requires living signal density, not bloodline. Her log tracks hypothesis variants, notes the conditions under which each falsifies, and replaces them. The research is slow. The register is clinical. What the log does not record is what she stops recording: the third month she notes that a maester she is watching has a family; by the sixth month that notation has disappeared from her entries. She does not decide to remove it. It stops being relevant data.

- Season 3: First active extraction. She begins using bugs as tactile sensors on active practitioners — mapping physical sensations of blood-price work through borrowed insect proprioception. This is the turn. She is not observing; she is riding someone else's experiment without their consent. The subjects do not know. Her log entry for the first session reads: *Proprioceptive bleed confirmed at 40-meter radius. Practitioner performed rite without deviation. No observed harm. Methodology: passive co-presence.* She tests the density-saturation hypothesis: above a threshold insect count, does candle response scale? Her working model says yes, linearly. She designs a first full-scale experiment for season 4.

- **Season 4 — The Foreclosure Event:** The density-saturation experiment runs in a Flea Bottom workshop over three days. Taylor's working hypothesis is that a relay pulse can be sustained without harm if the living resonance-bodies are healthy adults held at sub-threshold individual exposure. She has four smallfolk subjects — two who have worked with her before and trust her instrumentation, two recruited through the dockworker. She believes the load distributes. It does not. The saturation failure is not gradual: on the third day, the two newer subjects suffer acute collapse — one dies before she can break the relay; the other survives but cannot form new memories, retains nothing after an interval of approximately four hours, will never regain this. Taylor has three hours between the collapse and any risk of outside discovery. She dismantles the workshop, moves the body, disperses the surviving subject to a healer's house with a fabricated explanation, and burns the experiment log for that three-day window. She has, for the first time, destroyed research. Not to protect herself from Citadel detection — her method has always been invisible to them. She destroys it because the log records that she continued the experiment into day three after the first subject showed early distress signs at the end of day two, and she had logged that distress as *within acceptable variance.* She cannot record her way out of this. The deniability the rest of the arc rests on — that the research log is what it appears to be — is gone. She knows what she is. She keeps working. The density-saturation hypothesis is revised: load does not distribute linearly; threshold is individual, not aggregate. She knows this now. What she closes off by continuing is the possibility that she would have stopped if she had understood the risk. She understood it. She did not stop. **Foreclosed option:** withdrawal with a coherent self-narrative of scientific caution. She has suppressed the falsification, not revised the protocol in public. If the relay infrastructure is ever traced, the gap in the log is the evidence. If she ever tells anyone what the relay cost, it is this, not the watched fever.

- Season 4–5: Glass-candle acquisition and active experimentation continue under revised protocols. The relay breakthrough holds. She does not run another multi-body saturation event. She does not need to; the theory is confirmed. The surviving subject from the foreclosure event is alive in Flea Bottom, four hours of new memory at a time, and Taylor's bugs monitor him at all times. She tells herself this is to ensure he is cared for. The log does not mention him.

- Season 5–6: Relay infrastructure operational at limited range. Glass-candle research is now relay-mechanism only; scope discipline holds because she cannot source more candles without escalating into direct confrontation with the Citadel.

**Texture — dread through record-keeping (s1–3):** The pre-foreclosure log is not presented as sinister. It is exact, organized, and honest by its own terms. The dread is architectural: what is absent accumulates. She stops recording the human context of her subjects because it is not useful data. She stops recording her own emotional responses because she does not observe any. The reader watches a clinical instrument calibrate itself more finely over time, and the finest calibration is: *no notation for what does not affect the experiment.* By season 3, the people she is riding are experimental apparatus. The log does not say this. It does not need to. The register has done it already.

**Costs:** No direct confrontation until late series. After the foreclosure event, the cost is no longer moral and slow — it is structural and locked. She carries a gap in the archive. When the Citadel eventually identifies the pattern of knowledge flowing to an unknown actor, they look for a rogue maester. They do not look for a tanner. But the gap in the log is not about the Citadel. It is about what she would find if she looked at herself clearly, which she cannot do because she destroyed the instrument.

**Stakeholders in conflict:** Citadel (knowledge-hoard threatened without knowing by whom), individual maesters (surveillance victims), the smallfolk she uses as unwitting research subjects, the four subjects of the foreclosure experiment (two of whom are past harm; one dead; one alive in four-hour increments), eventually Targaryen bloodmages when she begins testing dragon-binding theory.

## Option B — Infrastructure-as-Weapon: The Counter-Citadel

**Primary access vector:** Taylor identifies that Valyrian knowledge is suppressed through institutional hoarding — the Citadel actively destroys or quarantines texts on blood magic, glass-candle theory, and dragon-binding because the dragonlord monopoly on these requires that no one else knows them. She does not try to enter the Citadel. She builds a parallel institution below it: a network of smallfolk scribes, hedge-maesters who failed their chains, and literate guildworkers who have seen pieces of the suppressed canon and been told it is heresy. She organizes them. Her bugs are the couriers, the security, and the editorial function: she reads everything that passes through the network and holds the synthesis in her own head because no written archive can be trusted.

**Secondary vectors:** A broken maester — expelled from the Citadel for pursuing blood-magic theory — becomes a willing collaborator. This is the structurally difficult move given her nobility-spurning constraint. She handles it by maintaining strict asymmetry: he is a resource, not an ally; she pays him in protection (her bugs prevent three assassination attempts against him in season 2); he knows she is using him and accepts this because she is the only one willing to let him work. No loyalty is performed. He dies in season 4 having given her the glass-candle activation theory she needed; she does not mourn him publicly.

**Chronology:**
- Season 1: Network seeding. She identifies broken-maester Veron while her bug-range still covers only the village and nearby road traffic. He is traveling under a false name. She knows this before she speaks to him. She makes an offer he cannot refuse framed entirely as self-interest.
- Season 2: Counter-Citadel grows. Bugs courier fragments. She synthesizes. She begins blood-price experiments on insects first — using her own power as the blood-price substrate — and discovers that the shard can serve as partial payment, which changes her theory of what the shard is.
- Season 3: First glass-candle test. She has sourced one through the network (a hedge-lord sold it to pay debts; a factor in her network bought it as curiosity). The test produces partial relay at ~600m, significantly above her unaided range. She records everything.
- Seasons 3–4: Active experimentation expands. Veron is still alive; his theory and her shard-substrate interact in ways neither anticipated. The relay mechanism is confirmed but unstable without a living resonance-body in the circuit. She begins using consenting smallfolk network members as resonance-anchors, fully disclosed, with payment. The consent is real. The information asymmetry is also real: they cannot assess the risk because she cannot fully explain what the risk is.
- Season 5: Veron dies. She dismantles the human-facing elements of the network — too much risk of Citadel identification now that she has what she needed. The relay infrastructure is operational. The counter-Citadel exists in her head alone.
- Season 5–6: Glass-candle relay at scale. Second candle sourced through network remnant. Late-series capability cap engaged.

**Costs:** The broken maester is a sustained moral compromise with someone who is not nobility but is performing the same institutional knowledge-hoarding on a smaller scale. She uses him. She is honest about using him. He uses her back. The smallfolk resonance-anchors are a harder cost: the consent is genuine but the information provided is incomplete. She has crossed the vivisection-logic line while telling herself it is natural philosophy because the subjects agreed.

**Stakeholders in conflict:** Citadel (institutional survival threatened by knowledge decentralization), surviving Citadel-loyalist maesters, Targaryen bloodmage tradition (she is accessing dragon-binding theory they treat as dynastic property), smallfolk network members who are now targets if the network is traced.

## Option C — Ruins-as-Laboratory: Old Valyrian Dead Drops

**Primary access vector:** Taylor identifies that the most complete surviving corpus of Valyrian sorcery theory is not in the Citadel — it is in the physical ruins of Valyrian-adjacent sites: dragonstone carvings, submerged Valyrian-era structures in the Blackwater, the burned-out dragon-pit below King's Landing, and one location she reaches only in season 4 (a Valyrian-construction vault beneath a Crownlands monastery, sealed after the Doom). These sites are not guarded because no one knows they contain recoverable content. She can read carved inscriptions through insect-tactile mapping — bugs tracing letter-forms in darkness, proprioception translating to text she processes through her shard's multi-body coordination.

**Secondary vectors:** Dragon-pit access is the major secondary vector. The old Targaryen dragon-pit beneath King's Landing is nominally sealed and considered haunted. It contains physical residue: bone-scored floors that record blood-price ritual geometry, soot-patterns that encode fire-breath direction data, and two glass-candles still in their housing from pre-Conquest practice. She accesses it via a Flea Bottom tunnel system her bug-network maps over six months in season 1. She tells no one.

**Chronology:**
- Season 1: Dragon-pit discovery. She does not immediately understand what she has found. The glass-candles are dormant and she cannot yet activate them — she lacks theory. She begins mapping the ritual-geometry of the bone-scored floor as a pure observation project.
- Season 2: Inscription-reading via tactile mapping expands to the Blackwater-adjacent ruins. She is building theory from primary sources with no interpreter, working in a dead technical language through insect-touch. Progress is slow and the errors are significant. She does not know which errors are errors yet.
- Season 3: First activation attempt, failed. The glass-candles respond but incorrectly — she is missing the blood-price component. She reconstructs this from the ritual-geometry and runs a self-experiment: shard-as-blood-price substrate, herself as the living cost. It works. The relay fires at ~900m. She is bedridden for four days afterward and tells the tanner family she had a fever.
- Season 4: Monastery vault. Her bug-network has expanded toward ~1.5km ceiling by now; she first detects the vault through insect-awareness of a sealed-air pocket. What is inside reshapes her theory of dragon-binding entirely — the Valyrians were not binding dragons to bloodlines; they were binding bloodlines to dragons. This is the turn that puts her on a direct collision course with the Targaryens.
- Season 5–6: Relay infrastructure at scale, glass-candles operational. The ruins are exhausted as a primary source. She has what she came for. The knowledge is in her alone.

**Costs:** The primary cost in Option C is self-cost: shard-as-blood-price means the experiments draw on her directly. She does not know what the ceiling is. She discovers it gradually through symptom-accumulation — bugs begin to die more frequently in her range (overflow? resonance bleed?), her sleep architecture degrades, she occasionally loses a few seconds of continuous insect-awareness without explanation. She keeps these records in her research log with clinical language and does not alter her schedule. The ruins-as-lab framing makes the moral ledger cleaner early (no unwitting subjects, no collaborators to use and discard) and then complicates it at the monastery vault: the knowledge she finds there was hidden for a reason she does not initially read as warning. She reads it as suppression. It is both.

**Stakeholders in conflict:** Targaryen dynasty (the dragon-pit is technically Targaryen property; the monastery vault knowledge threatens the bloodline-binding theory they use to legitimate their rule), Citadel (ruins-as-primary-source bypasses their gatekeeping; they cannot suppress what she already has), religious institutions (the monastery that sealed the vault sealed it on Seven-faith grounds; she has desecrated a consecrated space and would do it again).

## Recommendation

**Option A.**

Option A is the correct shape for this story, and the s4 foreclosure event gives it the peak the prior version lacked.

The insect-archive method places Taylor in a long rising phase where the moral degradation is real but self-concealing. She is a meticulous record-keeper running a research program that has always already been running on people who did not agree. The slow-burn version of "research ethics in a world with no research ethics" is not that she eventually starts using subjects; it is that she realizes she always was — and then, at the moment of forced clarity, chooses the research anyway and destroys the evidence of the choice. The s4 foreclosure event converts the rising phase into an actual peak: she is not tracking a cost and absorbing it. She is suppressing a falsification to protect a research program she will not abandon, and the suppression closes off every version of the arc where she could credibly claim ignorance. From s4 forward, she knows what the log omits, and the log is still running, and she is still writing it.

This is what the dramatist's shape requires: a cost that forecloses an option. The option foreclosed is the self-narrative of scientific caution — the one in which, had she known the risk, she would have stopped. She knew. She did not stop. She burned the page that says so. That moment is the point of no return for the deniability the rest of the arc rests on, and it arrives before the late-series institutional reckoning that the arc terminates in.

The density-saturation failure is also the correct candidate because it meshes with the cape-fic audience requirement: the hypothesis is named, the falsification is specific, and her response is not "she tested it and it worked" — it is "she tested it, it failed on the terms she set, and she revised the protocol after destroying the record of the failure." The hypothesis-falsification framing is maintained; the dread is that she is rigorous about everything except the question of whether to continue.

The s1–3 dread-via-record-keeping texture (dark-fantasy requirement) is architectural: the log is exact and honest by its own terms, and what accumulates is absence. She stops noting the human context of subjects because it is not useful data. By season 3 the people she monitors are apparatus. The register says this without saying it, which is the form the dread takes before s4 makes it explicit.

Option B front-loads the collaborator and externalizes her ethical accounting onto a named figure who can absorb the cost. Option C makes self-cost primary, which risks reading as sacrifice-for-the-cause — and the story's pressure is corruption, not sacrifice. Option A with the foreclosure event is the structure that delivers rise, a real peak with a real closing of options, and a late-series reckoning that the protagonist cannot narrate her way out of because she already tried.
