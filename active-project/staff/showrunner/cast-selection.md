## SLOT 5 — COST-BEARER

**Selected:** `wren-stitch-maker-flea-bottom-ward` (Candidate 5-B: Wren Stitch-Maker, age 11)

Wren is the strongest structural fit across four axes:

First, the rescue vector. The deterrence-rescue at d01 — where Wren traces the intervention back to Taylor through close observation — activates the asymmetric trust at the character's own instigation. This is correct: the cast-brief requires that [cost-bearer] attaches to Taylor, not that Taylor pursued the relationship. A child who figures out there is a pattern and chooses not to ask about it is more dramatically specific than a child who was simply at the wrong place.

Second, the un-priced quality. Wren's seamstress-trained close-reading habit (the "trained to observe before acting" quality) means she carries the structural irony cleanly: she notices Taylor's patterns, she sees the repetition, she could understand it if Taylor told her — and Taylor never tells her, because telling would make Wren a risk and a collaborator both. The surveillance that protects Wren is enacted specifically through silence. That silence is the micro-version of the Khepri-repetition named in the series chunk.

Third, the d08 activation. Wren's trade-walk routes provide read-through on neighborhood traffic — margit's profile articulates precisely why Otto's network would flag her as a courier-adjacent use-vector. Taylor routes around this without disclosure. The non-consent is not incidental; it is the exact shape of the moral-framework decline in the personal register.

Fourth, age and register. At 11, Wren is old enough to observe but young enough that her death at d14 reads as structural-innocent — she was not priced, she was not warned, and the protection Taylor ran for her was a silence that could not stop the Dance's Flea Bottom violence. The closing-image lands cold because Wren made the choice to trust Taylor and never received the knowledge that would have named what she trusted.

Candidates rejected: Pell (Candidate 5-A) is structurally clean but the dyer-family rescue is more passive — Pell does not actively trace the intervention. The irony is shallower. Alis (5-D) is the oldest candidate and her choosing-not-to-ask quality overlaps with Wren's but without the observer-training hook that makes the un-priced surveillance specific. Jonn (5-E) is the most isolated candidate — the parentless register is compelling for the atonement-working reading but the isolation weakens the community-substrate axis since Wren's stitch-maker household has block-level visibility that carries the social-tether world axis more directly. Bram (5-C) at age 8 is the coldest closing-image but the rescue is too passive; Sabel (5-F) risks functional-partnership overlap that would require additional constraint.

## SLOT 6 — PROTECT-TARGET

**Selected:** `sera-hightower-kl-122ac` (Candidate 6-A1: Sera Hightower, age 14)

**Alicent activation: NO** (see Slot 4 above).

Sera is selected over the Option B candidates (Orla, Hyla) for structural reasons, not incidental ones. The Option B candidates require Alicent activation, which is rejected above. More importantly, both Option B candidates embed the protect-target inside a household-records-exposure vulnerability — the protection ask is "suppress documentation." Sera's vulnerability is a legitimacy question about her parentage, which is more generative: it is not one document but a structural fact that cannot be erased, which means each Green–Black faction contest reopens the exposure risk on a fresh structural basis. The 14-delta non-resolution requirement is better served by a structural vulnerability than by a document-management problem.

Sera's specific profile fits the series' contempt-particularity requirement. Taylor observes a court-tier figure whose survival she has traded everything to secure, and Sera — a 14-year-old cadet-branch Hightower ward sent to court for political advancement — is sufficiently specific to anchor contempt. Taylor can see exactly what Sera is and what she is not, and the gap between "what Sera is worth to Otto" and "what Sera costs to protect" is the ledger entry Taylor refuses to write. The protect-target does not know Taylor exists; the distance is maintained through Otto as intermediary.

Canon collision: confirmed clean. Sera is an invented character within the Hightower cadet branch. No F&B or HOTD figure of this description is named for this era.

Alicent activation reconsideration: Option 6-A1 nominally supports Alicent scenes without requiring them. Alicent remains non-activated — Sera is in the Red Keep through the household apparatus that Alicent heads, which is sufficient structural grounding. Alicent's existence is the backdrop; she does not require scenes.

## SLOT 7 — WITNESS-MIRROR

**Selected:** `gylda-saltwater-flea-bottom` (Candidate 7-B: Gylda Saltwater, age 40s)

Gylda's naming-scene shape is the sharpest fit for the function. The witness-mirror requirement is: one figure who sees what Taylor is becoming and names it, once, from outside the attachment. Gylda's naming comes from block-observation rather than personal contact — she and Taylor have never had a confidant relationship, which is the non-overlap requirement. The naming emerges from pattern-accumulation ("too many places that are not the places a Flea Bottom woman with Taylor's apparent resources would have reason to be"), which means it names the surveillance architecture specifically, not Taylor's motivations or morality. That is the correct register: the naming is not a moral lecture, it is an accurate read of the pattern, stated once, then walked away from.

This contrasts productively with Taylor's interior. Taylor's register is cold-utilitarian and explicit-ledger; Gylda's observation is block-knowledge — empirical without framework, accurate without systematization. The naming lands as external precisely because it comes from a register that does not share Taylor's vocabulary. Gylda has no model of why Taylor is where she is; she has only the where-she-is, named once.

Timing: the d09–d13 contempt-articulation window is the target. Gylda's naming fits naturally in the d09–d10 range — after the network is load-bearing and Taylor's movement patterns have taken on the quality of systematic routing, but before the no-exit confirmation that locks the contempt-without-refusal axis.

Candidates rejected: Halvard (7-A) is compelling but the direct-encounter shape has a "passing judgment" quality that risks registering as moral-register rather than empirical-register. His characterization as someone who recognizes a type is less specific to Taylor's particular repetition than Gylda's pattern-observation. Osric (7-C) is well-constructed but the stripped-maester register is already occupied by the maester-chronicler function in Slot 9 — two above-apparatus deductive registers in a 9-character roster creates echo without differentiation. Brea (7-D) is the elder-register variant; functional but the Flea Bottom widow archetype runs close to the FLEA-BOTTOM-FIXTURE archetype's ambient-observational mode, risking blur between Slots 7 and 8.

## SLOT 8 — FLEA-BOTTOM-FIXTURE

**Selected:** `coll-net-mender-flea-bottom` (Candidate 8-A: Coll the Net-Mender, age 50s)

Coll is the cleanest fixture profile: he has a fixed outdoor position (the same spot for fifteen years), his work produces block-visibility through stationary presence rather than movement, and his observation accumulates without interpreting. The "net-mender's rhythm" register is the most non-interpretive of the four candidates — he counts in repairs; his conversation is about nets and what he sees. He does not analyze. He describes.

The fixture function requires ambient, subsistence-focused, non-interpretive. Coll satisfies all three. His arrival at recognizing Taylor as a pattern ("by d06, too regular to be accidental") is correctly passive — an accumulation in his block-level awareness, not a read he builds into a model.

Slot 7 / Slot 8 non-overlap confirmation: Gylda (water-carrier, movement-based observation) and Coll (net-mender, stationary observation) are distinct presence types, distinct registers, and their observation modes do not overlap. Coll never names anything. Gylda names once. The functional boundary is maintained.

Candidates rejected: Pott (8-B) is mobile and ambient-complaint, but the middenman's routine overlap with Taylor's block movement patterns creates too much proximity — the fixture should be present but not continuously intersecting. Helwy (8-C) is compelling (the sept-doorkeeper watching the woman who does not enter) but the Faith adjacency creates a subtext that risks loading into the magic-dormant / witch-label environment without structural purpose at this frequency. Rivin (8-D) is commercially observational and viable but the secondhand-goods stall has more transaction-adjacency than this role needs — the fixture should be a street-presence, not a commercial-node.

## SLOT 9 — MAESTER-CHRONICLER

**Selected:** `corvan-archmaester-retrospective-coda` (Candidate 9-A: Archmaester Corvan, writing c.160 AC)

The furthest temporal remove produces the most authoritative cold register. Corvan writes forty years after the Dance from consolidated documentary access — Red Keep household records, Small Council minutes, casualty records from the Dance's first week. This gives the coda the specific quality the closing-image requires: the ledger Taylor ran privately is echoed in the archival record that runs impersonally. The counterfactual naming arrives as a deduction from documented chain, labeled as inference, with no affect. This is the only register that can name the full shape of what happened without the narrative knowing more than Taylor did during it.

The temporal distance also earns the irony its full weight. Halvyn (9-B) is contemporaneous — he is recording absences in a ledger while the Dance is beginning. That is a different function: real-time gap-notation, which is too close to Taylor's own accounting mode to provide the structural echo. Perwyn (9-C) at c.145 AC is intermediate and personal — the "I have reviewed" first-person breaks the cold register. Orlith (9-D) is Faith-archival; the Faith's casualty vocabulary ("the departed") imports a register foreign to the series' secular-tragic frame.

Corvan's coda must land as the book's final institutional voice — the archival record naming what the insect-network built, what it cost, and what it could not protect. Forty years of Citadel polish produces the precision required. His access is the documentary record only; he does not know who Taylor was; he deduces from the intelligence-architecture's shape. The gap between his deduction and the reader's knowledge of Taylor is the book's final dramatic note.

## Dramatic-Range Review

**Do the selected carriers cover the signature's tension axes?**

Yes, across all nine axes:

- `moral-framework` (3→1 protagonist): carried by Taylor-Wren relationship and Otto trades throughout. The un-priced-but-surveilled dynamic is the primary moral-framework arc.
- `capability` (3→7): Taylor alone as network architect; Aemond-122ac's Vhagar presence provides the external capability-pressure that forces network expansion.
- `position` (1→1 rise-fall): Taylor and Otto together carry both phases; Sera's vulnerability is the mechanism that pins Taylor to the visibility-phase and prevents early extraction.
- `social-tether` (2→1): Taylor + Wren carry the relational layer; Coll the community-substrate ambient layer. The Flea Bottom embeddedness-as-trap is grounded in both.
- `relational-anchor-status` (3→1): Wren as the un-priced relationship; the two-entry cost ledger (cl-unpriced-cost-bearer + cl-protection-buys-consolidation) operates through Wren and Sera together.
- `moral-legibility-to-self` (7→4): Taylor carries this alone (protagonist axis); Gylda's one-time naming from outside is the external validation event that the axis requires — the moment the world's collective read and Taylor's interior accounting briefly align.
- `political-register-toward-elite` (5→9): Taylor's contempt arc builds from observation of Otto, Aemond, and Sera. Three court-tier figures provide the specific observable material without diffusing across too many faces.
- `knowledge` (3→8): Taylor's insect-network expansion; Otto's intelligence holdings (antagonist) grow in parallel as the cl-intelligence-arrangement trade-chain extends.
- `agency` (5→1): Taylor and Otto carry opposing ends of the axis; Sera's persistent non-resolving vulnerability is the structural mechanism that locks Taylor's agency decline.

**Are there axis-orphans?**

None. Every axis has at least one named on-stage carrier for the protagonist perspective. World-perspective axes (social-tether world 4→1, agency world 3→1) are carried by Coll (community-substrate ambient) and Wren (the most particular smallfolk community instance). The antagonist-perspective axes are fully Otto-carried with Aemond providing the capability-axis embodiment.

**Does any axis have too many carriers?**

The `capability` axis is the closest risk: Taylor's insect-network growth, Aemond's Vhagar presence, and Otto's intelligence apparatus all touch it. However, these are three different perspectives (protagonist/antagonist-secondary/antagonist-primary) rather than three characters all moving the same axis in the same direction — the capability axis requires exactly this multi-perspective pressure shape. No diffusion.

The `political-register-toward-elite` axis is also three-figure (Otto, Aemond, Sera) but these three provide different registers of the same observable material — institutional, coercive, and court-tier-decorative — which are each necessary for contempt-particularity at the d05/d09/d13 window.

**Does the roster have appropriate POV-friction?**

Yes. The antagonist pair (Otto primary + Aemond walk-on) produces two distinct pressure modes: Otto's calibrated-proposal pressure and Aemond's embodied-consequence presence. These are not redundant. Gylda provides the one external naming event from outside Taylor's attachment system — she is the friction point that tests Taylor's self-accounting without becoming a confidant. Sera produces friction by being exactly the kind of court-tier figure whose survival Taylor's trades guarantee — observable enough for contempt, distant enough to never become personal. Coll and Wren both provide low-frequency pressure by existing as stakes whose welfare the reader tracks.

**Does the roster honor the hard fences?**

- POV first-person Taylor-only: no character in this roster has a POV function. Gylda names once and exits. Coll observes but does not narrate. Corvan appears in coda only. All POV material belongs to Taylor.
- F&B canonicity: all named canon characters (Otto, Aemond) are used at correct 122 AC era with appropriate age constraints. Alicent is not activated and does not appear. No canon turning-point figures are at risk.
- Smallfolk/court class line: Wren and Coll are firmly smallfolk. Sera is court-tier. The class line is maintained — they do not intersect directly; Taylor is the bridge.
- Earth-Bet proper nouns in dialogue fence: no character in this roster creates a context that would require Wormverse jargon. The fence is structural (all pressure is delivered through Westerosi frames) and the cast does not crack it.

**Is the cost-bearer + protect-target pair structurally complementary?**

Yes, and specifically so. Wren's vulnerability is social-tether/smallfolk: she is exposed through the gaps in the network that Taylor deliberately created to keep her outside it. Sera's vulnerability is court-tier/structural: she is exposed through a legitimacy question that no intelligence delivery can permanently resolve. These two forms of vulnerability are non-overlapping and non-resolving by design. The escalating asks across 14 deltas operate through two independent compounding mechanisms: each Sera-extension delays Wren's entry into the ledger (cl-protection-buys-consolidation), and each Wren-non-pricing corrodes Taylor's moral self-accounting (cl-unpriced-cost-bearer). The two are not causes of each other but they compound in the same direction, producing the sum that the relational-anchor-status axis requires (3→1, two -1 entries). The structural interlock holds.
