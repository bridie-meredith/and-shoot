# Series Plan — Dead Capes in Westeros

## Premise

Six capes whose identities were conclusively ended between the Slaughterhouse Nine arc and Taylor Hebert's death arrive in Westeros, thirty years before the Dance of Dragons. Each arrived in a form their shard selected. None of them know the others exist at series start. None of Westeros knows what they are.

The shards are present, active, and reacting to Westerosi magic in ways that produce pressure on their hosts. The hosts, stripped of Earth-Bet context, must decide who they are without the infrastructure that made them legible.

---

## Series Theme

The problem of self-authorship when the author is an alien intelligence. Each cape's identity was shaped (or colonized, or built, or broken) by their shard in life. Westeros removes the context that made those shards' agendas legible — no other parahumans, no PRT, no Brockton Bay, no Endbringers. In the absence of the familiar stage, each cape confronts whether what they are is them or the shard.

---

## Series Question

Can you choose who you become when the thing that made you has its own agenda?

---

## Series Drama

The succession crisis building toward the Dance of Dragons is the background engine. Thirty years out, it is a political rumor. Over the series it becomes a visible threat. By the final season, it is the war the capes cannot avoid being inside.

The shards react increasingly strongly to dragon magic as the crisis escalates (more dragons in proximity, more wildfire, more greensight activity). The capes who are closest to their shards' agendas are most affected.

The central dramatic shape: isolated arrivals who find each other, are drawn into Westerosi political structures, and ultimately face the Dance as something they cannot remain outside of — and who, by that point, have had to decide what they are.

## Laws (summary — detail in world-notes.md and constraint cards in warehouse/)

1. Arrival is one of three modes (Type-A/B/C); shard selects.
2. Shard-host lock is absolute. No new bonding. No transfer.
3. Shards react to Westerosi magic as anomalous data — pressure on host, attempted interface, failure.
4. Powers function with environmental friction on Earth-Bet-specific expressions.
5. Capes know they died (Type-A full; Type-B via shard-dreams; Type-C do not).
6. Westerosi characters process cape powers through existing cultural/religious frameworks.
7. Dragon fire works on capes. No pre-adaptation.
8. Eligible arrivals include canonical deaths and identity-death (Bonesaw amendment).

## Lore

- Setting: ~120 AC, ~30 years before Dance of Dragons.
- Capes have no knowledge of each other at series start.
- No Westerosi organization knows about the capes.
- Season 1 focal region: Reach and Stormlands, converging toward King's Landing.

---

## Behaviors (series-scope constraints)

- Westerosi characters use their own frameworks (sorcery, gods, curses, demon-bargains) for cape powers.
- Jack does not reveal what he is without extreme dramatic justification.
- Riley does not know what she is. She has dreams.
- Crawler does not speak human language at series open.
- Clockblocker's humor is always running; the silence is the signal.
- Noelle's suppression cost appears in every scene she occupies.
- Shatterbird's grandiosity is survival architecture, not simple villainy.

## Season Chunk Statements

### Season 1: The Arrival Season
Six isolated capes discover they are not alone, negotiate survival in Westerosi society, and begin converging toward each other and King's Landing — while shards' reactions to Westerosi magic escalate from background pressure to active disruption.

### Season 2: The Convergence Season
The capes find each other; the meeting is not clean. Jack's shard fires on a real parahuman signal for the first time. Noelle's suppression is tested. The Targaryens notice something strange in the Stormlands. Each cape must decide whether the others are allies, threats, or both.

### Season 3: The Entanglement Season
Fifteen years before the Dance; factions forming; capes have Westerosi positions now, some chosen. The shard-agenda versus chosen-identity conflict peaks for each protagonist. One of them must yield.

### Season 4: The Dance Season
War begins. Maximum shard-load from concentrated dragon magic and wildfire. Each protagonist makes the defining arc choice. The series question answered, differently, for each of the six. Not all answers include survival.

---

## Plot Shape

- **Start:** Six dead capes, isolated in a medieval world with no framework for them, constructing provisional identities.
- **End:** The Dance forces a reckoning — each cape declares what they are, permanently.
- **Protagonist arc:** From fractured identity-in-hiding to chosen identity under maximum pressure.
- **Series question:** Can you choose who you become when the thing that made you has its own agenda?
