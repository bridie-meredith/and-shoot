# Series Plan

## Series spine

A protagonist trained to prevent extinction-level catastrophe arrives in a world facing dynastic war and applies her threat-model to it. The competence that ended Gold Morning is the competence that produces the Dance. Prevention, executed by the wrong instrument, produces the catastrophe it was trying to forestall.

## Seasons

- **s01** — Taylor's witch-margin existence at Flea Bottom pulls Rhaenys-adjacent patron attention; the amplification-theory forms and the patron's wrong reading of Taylor's dragon-adjacent distress becomes operational, authorizing Taylor's access into Black-faction proximity. Vaegon's patient bond-work with Silverwing and his quiet protection of Lyra run in the same household space Taylor is being inserted into, invisible to her as prevention.

- **s02** — The flicker misfire pattern compounds under patron-authorized access; Taylor's threat-reads tighten toward the Dragonpit as the access-chain shortens. Vaegon's prevention-work becomes legible at the household level — his bond nearly complete, his buffer around Lyra visible — while Taylor's doppelganger logic locks in: the rider she sees in the flicker and the rider Vaegon is keeping from Silverwing converge in her map as the same configuration.

- **s03** — Taylor's kill executes the intervention; Vaegon dies; the unbonded dragon falls to the worst available claimant; Lyra's protection ends. The chronicler coda names Vaegon's counterfactual; the cousin dies cold in the closing image with the wrong dragon above.

## Bridging notes

At s01's end the amplification-theory is installed and Taylor has faction access she did not earn — every subsequent move is authorized by a wrong idea that neither party can see is wrong. At s02's end the doppelganger logic is complete: Taylor has mapped Vaegon as the threat-configuration, the access to act is in place, and the only remaining variable is her nerve — which the next flicker misfire will supply. S03's kill is not a decision but a detonation: the road was finished, the stone already laid; the series question closes on whether any instrument could have read the difference in time, and the chronicler's answer is no.
