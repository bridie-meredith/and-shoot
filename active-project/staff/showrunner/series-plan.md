# Series Plan — taylor-in-westeros

## Series Identity

**Title:** Taylor in Westeros (working title)
**Theme:** Can competence protect anything against civilizational fire?
**Series question:** Will what Taylor builds in nineteen years of preparation be enough when the Dance of Dragons arrives?
**Genre register:** Fast, action, pulpy, dramatic, tension, escalation — locked.

---

## World Laws (binding — full detail)

### Law: Soul persistence
Taylor Hebert's consciousness persists through reincarnation. She remembers Earth-Bet, Gold Morning, and her entire prior life. The memories are complete. She is not confused about who she is; she is confused about what she now is.

### Law: Dragon form
Taylor has reincarnated as a dragon. She hatched from an egg in the Riverlands of Westeros approximately 110 AC. She has the full capabilities of a young dragon (fire, flight, physical force, apex-predator senses) AND her Worm shard power (insect control, see below). She is a hatchling at series open; she will grow across the series.

### Law: Shard persistence (critical)
Taylor's insect-control power persists through reincarnation because it is shard-linked to her soul-pattern, not her specific biology. In the dragon body, the power functions with modifications: sensory integration is more disorienting than in a human body; there is a bug-free dead zone of approximately ten feet around her own body; fire deployment kills bugs in its path (same sensory strike as always); cold seasons reduce bug availability. Full mechanics: condition-shard-in-dragon-body.card.md.
The shard may develop toward broader creature-influence over the series as it adapts; this is long-arc development not available at series open. No power extensions without explicit showrunner authorization.

### Law: Two toolkits
Taylor has two distinct action registers: the insect layer (covert, precise, reconnaissance-first, the Taylor-proof) and the dragon layer (visible, high-force, cannot be hidden). She chooses which to show at any given moment. This choice is always load-bearing.

### Law: Genre register
Fast, action, pulpy, dramatic, tension, escalation. This is not a slow-burn introspective piece. Things happen, they escalate, there are consequences. Every episode must move.

### Law: Escalation default
Taylor escalates to match the perceived trajectory of a situation. She does not wait for things to become fatal before acting. This is a character constant.

### Law: Patch not world
Taylor is not trying to stop the Dance of Dragons. She is trying to protect her patch. She may influence macro events but her goal is always local-scale survivability, not heroic intervention.

## World Lore

- Setting: Westeros, specifically the contested Riverlands
- Era: Opens ~110 AC, Viserys I Targaryen reign; Dance of Dragons (~129-131 AC) is the series horizon
- The Targaryen claim: any unclaimed dragon is the crown's property. Persistent, politically significant across all five seasons.
- The Riverlands: perpetually contested; multiple lords; Bracken-Blackwood feud; always in the middle of every war.
- The Dance of Dragons: succession war (Black vs. Green factions). Taylor knows this history. Nobody in Westeros does yet.
- Westerosi arthropod ecology: see condition-swarm-in-foreign-ecology.card.md
- Seasonal constraint: insect availability drops significantly in winter.

## Series Drama

A post-apocalyptic consciousness in a dragon body watches the prologue of another civilization's fracture while building a defensible patch in contested territory. The drama is the gap between what she knows is coming and what she can actually protect. Each season narrows the gap; S5 answers whether the narrowing was enough.

---

## Season Chunks

**S1 — The Unclaimed Thing** (~110-113 AC)
A hatchling dragon with no rider appears in the contested Riverlands; every lord, institution, and faction attempts to resolve this anomaly through existing categories; none of the categories work. The collision is between the Targaryen legal order (every dragon is crown property) and the dragon that will not be claimed; what cannot survive it is the assumption that the anomaly can be contained by protocol alone.

**S2 — The Territory War** (~113-116 AC)
Taylor has a patch and Ser Aemon has an alliance; the Riverlands lords who were watching now have to decide whether the dragon-in-the-hills is a resource or a threat; the Small Council's agent has failed and a more aggressive response is in preparation. The collision is between the established power network of the Riverlands lords and a new force that respects none of their arrangements; what cannot survive is the fiction that the Riverlands' existing order can wait her out.

**S3 — The Faction Bid** (~116-122 AC)
The Dance of Dragons is forming; both the Green and Black factions want Taylor as a military asset. The collision is between Taylor's refusal to be anyone's asset and both factions' certainty that she can be persuaded, traded, or coerced; what cannot survive is the mutual pretense that Taylor is available for claiming.

**S4 — The Consolidation** (~122-127 AC)
The Dance is clearly imminent; the Riverlands lords are picking sides; Taylor's patch is large enough to matter in the coming war's calculus. The collision is between the alliances she has built and the factional war that will demand those allies choose a side; what cannot survive is the neutrality her patch requires.

**S5 — The Dance** (~127-131 AC)
The war begins. The Riverlands burns. The collision is between everything she built and the scale of catastrophe she knew was coming; what cannot survive is the pretense that competence in sufficient quantity is adequate protection against civilizational fire.

## Series Plot Shape

- **Start:** Taylor hatches in the Riverlands with full memory of who she was, in a world that will try to categorize, claim, or kill her.
- **End:** The Dance of Dragons arrives; everything built is tested; the series question is answered.
- **Protagonist arc:** From "what am I now" to "I know what I am and what I am protecting and whether that was enough."
- **Series question:** Can she protect something worth keeping before the fire arrives?
