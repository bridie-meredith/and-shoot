# Chapter 17 — outline (scene-by-scene)

## Scene 1
Jarvis returns his weekly cipher-bundle in the usual way — a folded count-sheet tucked under the stone at the Fishmonger Gate water-trough, the agreed mark on the third column figure. Taylor retrieves it at dusk, reads it standing, and finds the query third from the bottom: a woman moving east of the water gate on a regular interval, footfall-pattern consistent across six observation points over eleven days, observed twice in proximity to ward contacts Jarvis's handler has already noted in Taylor's coverage zone. The apparatus wants to know who she is. The query is pattern-only — no name, no description beyond height-and-gait, no indication that the apparatus knows what it is looking at. It is a gap in their picture and they have noticed the figure using it. Taylor folds the sheet and stands at the trough and does not yet move. She knows the answer before she has decided anything. The gait-pattern is Wren's — the particular hesitation at the second step that comes from the scar tissue across her left ankle, the interval that matches the stitch-house rotation. Taylor has her own coverage data; she has been reading the east-of-water-gate gap in Jarvis's feed for months, knowing the gap-figure is Wren, never routing that knowledge anywhere it would surface. The apparatus has now looked at the same gap from the other side and asked the same question Taylor has been refusing to answer.

## Scene 2
Taylor does not go back to the stitch-house. She walks the Hook's southern edge in the dark, reading the ward's density through her feet, and lets the accounting run. The apparatus question has a cost: if she routes a description of Wren to Jarvis, Wren becomes a named person in Otto's picture — a smallfolk woman who moves through a coverage gap in a pattern that has already attracted scrutiny. Named means findable. Findable means usable. Taylor runs the price out to its terminus and the terminus is exposure. The counter-option also has a cost: she can route a different figure through the gap documentation — attribute the gap-movements to a different ward-elder, someone whose presence in that corridor has a plausible read that closes the question. This option protects Wren. Taylor prices it honestly: it inserts a false record into Otto's intelligence picture. It attributes movements to a person who did not make them. It places a ward-elder in a pattern-profile that will now accumulate future observations. The ward-elder is not Wren, which is the gain. The ward-elder did not choose this, which is the cost. Taylor runs both entries and the accounting does not resolve. She holds the question open and keeps walking. Wren's second-step hesitation surfaces in her memory without being summoned — not an argument, just the specific weight of a specific gait — and the recognition she has been not-opening begins to form at the edge of her accounting: the option that protects Wren is structurally indistinguishable from the architecture she built her coming-here to be the refusal of.

## Scene 3
Before the Hook turns north Taylor has already decided, because the accounting has been running long enough and there is no version of the ledger where letting Wren become a named figure in Otto's picture costs less than the alternative. She does not record the decision. She goes back to her room — the space above the tallow-render works that she rents for three coppers a week — and pulls the coverage log for the east-of-water-gate corridor. The log is in her own hand: a running tally of movement signatures, timestamped by light-quality, no names because she never writes names. She selects the entry-cluster that matches Wren's interval — eleven days, six observation points — and attributes the signature to Elder Norren, a tanner's-ward contact whose legitimate passage through that corridor twice a week gives the attribution a plausible read. In the morning she will add three supplementary observations to Norren's entry-cluster, dated backward, calibrated to the gait-interval the apparatus has already flagged. The addition will be indistinguishable from a coverage gap that closed on its own. When she routes the updated log segment to Jarvis's dead drop, the apparatus's gap-figure will resolve as a tanner's-ward elder whose movements have a mundane explanation, and the question will close. Taylor writes the supplementary entries. Three lines in her own hand, attributing to Elder Norren a presence in the east-of-water-gate corridor that belongs to Wren. The pen is the instrument. The log is the record. This is what protection looks like at single-person scale — routing a body through a coverage map without their knowledge or consent, substituting one person's pattern for another's, adjusting the record so the apparatus reads what Taylor needs it to read. The shape of the act is the shape of the thing she built her prohibition against. She does not close the log entry. She leaves the pen beside it.

## Scene 4
Three days later the dead drop returns nothing. The apparatus's question has resolved: Elder Norren's east-of-water-gate movements are in the picture now, attributed and closed. No follow-up query. No flag. Wren moves through the corridor as she has always moved, and the apparatus does not know her name. The second-step hesitation crosses the morning ward-read the way it always does — the scar-tissue interval that Taylor has been reading for months — and now there is a false record in Otto's intelligence picture that the interval belongs to Norren. Taylor does not enter this into the cost-ledger. She knows what the ledger entry would say: protection delivered, Wren screened, one ward-elder inserted into a pattern-profile without his consent or knowledge. Elder Norren will accumulate future observations. Future queries may arrive. The false attribution is not a single event; it is a structural feature of the record now. Taylor runs the accounting in her head and does not write it down. The column that would hold this entry stays blank. The protection worked. The architecture that worked is the architecture she came here to retire: routing a body through a record without their knowledge, adjusting what the apparatus reads, moving a person through an intelligence picture to produce a desired outcome at single-person scale. She protected Wren the same way she built the thing she came here to atone for — not the scale, not the number of persons, but the shape of the act. She keeps the accounting in her head where it will not exist on paper. Wren crosses the morning ward-read and Taylor does not route her name anywhere it will surface, and this is called protection, and the ledger is blank, and both facts are true.
