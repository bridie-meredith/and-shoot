## 3. Antagonist load

Otto carries seven of the nine antagonist-perspective axes as the sole named active antagonist. Aemond carries capability-antagonist as the embodied-consequence instrument. Alicent is not activated.

**Is one calibrated-asks figure sufficient?**

Yes, for a single-POV claustrophobic tragedy where the antagonist pressure operates through compounding ledger mechanics rather than through multiplying antagonist faces. The cast-brief's design is sound: Otto's pressure escalates not because he changes mode but because each accepted trade lowers Taylor's refusal threshold, and the cost-ledger entries compound in the same direction. Adding Alicent as a second proposing-voice would have introduced a second antagonist register that the 18-22 chapter budget cannot absorb without diffusing the Otto-Taylor relationship.

**FLAG F2 — antagonist register monotony risk in the middle arc (d04-d09).**
With Otto as the sole active proposer and Aemond as a walk-on with near-zero dialogue, the antagonist register across the visibility-phase (d04-d09) risks delivering six sequential "Otto proposes / Taylor accepts" beats without qualitative mode-shift. The trajectory's delta entries for this window are structurally sound, but the cost-ledger compounding must manifest as distinct consequence-shapes per delta — not as iterative delivery sequences. At chapter-level substance work: each Otto-scene in d04-d09 must land a consequence that differs in kind from the previous one (a different axis moving, a different cost paid, a different ledger-entry closing). If the delta sequence in this window produces six visibly distinct consequences, the monotony risk dissolves. If it produces six structurally similar delivery scenes, this is a flat-constant stretch inside a rise arc — which is a shape failure. Flag for screen-writer at /and-substance book b01.

**Is Aemond at 10-11 credible at structural crisis points (d10-d14)?**

Partially. The variant card's design is correct: at 10-11, Aemond cannot function as an independent coercive enforcement arm, but a child who has claimed Vhagar — the largest dragon alive — is a genuine coercive instrument when Otto-directed. The crisis-point walk-ons are structurally credible as Vhagar-as-consequence rather than Aemond-as-actor.

**FLAG F3 — Aemond variant card must deliver consequence-weight, not atmosphere.**
The hard fence in the selection notes ("walk-on frequency, not independent enforcement") is correct, but "walk-on" risks producing scene appearances that are atmospherically present without being dramatically load-bearing. At each structural crisis point (d10-d14) where Aemond appears, the variant card must specify that his presence produces a concrete change in Taylor's calculation or the protect-target's exposure — not just a visual register of coercive potential. An Aemond walk-on that does not shift any axis is scenery. The card authoring at Phase 4 must hard-fence this: each appearance produces a named consequence on at least one plot axis, or the appearance is cut. Flag for Aemond variant card author at Phase 4.

## 4. Cost-bearer / protect-target structural interlock

The cl-unpriced-cost-bearer + cl-protection-buys-consolidation compound mechanism (two -1 entries summing to -2 net on relational-anchor-status, 3→1) is structurally sound.

**Wren's profile (observer-training, figures-out-the-pattern, doesn't-ask, mutual silence as the relationship) interlocks with Sera's profile (structural legitimacy-question, no resolution) in the required way:**

- cl-unpriced-cost-bearer operates through Wren: Taylor's sustained refusal to price Wren corrodes her self-accounting (moral-legibility-to-self -1) and slides the relational-anchor-status -1. This mechanism is active from d02 onward, compounding at d06, d08, and d11.
- cl-protection-buys-consolidation operates through Sera: each Sera-extension (a Green faction maneuver that re-opens Sera's exposure and requires a new trade) defers Wren's entry into the ledger one additional notch — each trade that keeps Sera alive is a trade that keeps Taylor in the arrangement that makes Wren invisible inside the network. The relational-anchor-status slides -1 here.

The two mechanisms are non-overlapping and non-resolving by design. They compound in the same direction without being causes of each other. The -2 net is correctly allocated.

**Does Wren's d14 death carry the closing-image weight a single-POV tragedy demands?**

Yes. The four conditions for a structurally adequate closing-image death are all present:

1. The death is in a location Taylor mapped and covered — the coverage architecture is the thing that could not protect her.
2. The protection Taylor ran for Wren was enacted through silence — Wren trusted without the knowledge that would have named what she trusted.
3. Wren's trust was her own choice, made with information Taylor deliberately withheld.
4. The death arrives as the consequence of the trades that were supposed to prevent it — not as an unrelated accident, but as the Dance's Flea Bottom violence moving through streets Taylor's network enabled.

All four conditions require Wren to be structurally specific, not symbolic. The seamstress-household detail, the observer-training quality, the d08 courier-adjacent risk-intercept, and the mutual-silence shape of the relationship all produce a character who reads as genuinely attached rather than as a structural symbol. The closing-image will land cold because it is earned by specificity.

## 5. Witness-mirror placement

**Is empirical-not-moral the right register for this function?**

Yes. The moral-legibility-to-self (protagonist) axis requires an external event that briefly aligns the world's collective read with Taylor's interior accounting. A moral-register witness (accusation, judgment, shame-naming) would produce a confrontation between registers that are too far apart — Taylor's cold-utilitarian frame would reject a moral-register naming as category error, and the axis would not move. Gylda's empirical naming ("too many places you have no reason to be") arrives in the same observational-accuracy register that Taylor's own accounting uses. It lands as external precisely because it comes from a different structural position (Flea Bottom block-knowledge, not court-layer intelligence) while sharing the vocabulary of observation and pattern. The moral-legibility-to-self axis needs this shared-vocabulary external validation, not a moral lecture.

No concern here. The register selection is the strongest structural element in the witness-mirror slot.

## 6. Maester-chronicler temporal register

**Corvan at c.160 AC is the correct selection. The temporal remove produces coda authority rather than severing the witness function.**

The specific structural function of the coda is: the ledger Taylor ran privately is echoed in the archival record that runs impersonally. This echo requires the impersonal register — which requires that the coda's voice is not carrying grief, urgency, or personal proximity to the events. Forty years of Citadel polish produces this. The contemporaneous alternatives (Halvyn, Perwyn) are both disqualified for the same reason: their proximity to the events imports an affect-register that competes with the cold ledger-echo function.

Halvyn (contemporaneous) is too close to Taylor's own accounting mode. The parallel between Halvyn's gap-notation and Taylor's explicit ledger would produce an echo that reads as structural redundancy rather than institutional irony.

Perwyn (c.145 AC, personal-archival hybrid, first-person "I have reviewed") breaks the cold register. The "I" imports the personal, and the personal is what the coda must refuse.

The 40-year distance earns the impersonal echo. Corvan does not know who Taylor was. He deduces from the architecture's shape. The gap between what he deduces and what the reader knows is the book's final dramatic note — and that gap requires temporal distance to function. If the chronicler knows Taylor's name, the gap closes and the irony flattens.

**FLAG F4 — Corvan's inference-chain must be legible to the reader without explicit connection.**
The closing irony depends on the reader connecting Corvan's institutional deduction (the intelligence-architecture's shape, the casualty records, the gap between what the network protected and what it could not) to Taylor's experience of building it. This connection must be implicit — Corvan cannot make it explicit, because he does not know who Taylor was. The authoring constraint: the coda's inference-chain must use documentary details that the reader has encountered in Taylor's POV across the 14 deltas (specific wards mapped, specific coverage gaps, specific Flea Bottom blocks) so that the reader can close the gap themselves. If the coda uses details the reader has not seen in Taylor's POV, the connection cannot be made. Flag for screen-writer and stitcher at /and-facets and /and-stitch.

## 7. POV / fence honoring

**Hard fence: Taylor first-person only; non-Taylor chapters = marked interludes.**

Named interludes required by the roster: one — Corvan's coda. No other cast member requires an interlude. Otto, Aemond, Gylda, Wren, Sera, and Coll are all observable through Taylor's first-person POV without an interlude break. One interlude (Corvan coda) in an 18-22 chapter book is a fraction of the budget. The fence is honored.

At chapter-level substance work: when the agency-axis antagonist pressure moves (d07 Otto's-unofficial-instrument, d10 no-exit), the Otto scene carrying that delta must make the apparatus's dependency on Taylor's network legible through dialogue or Taylor's intelligence-feed observations, without requiring an Alicent scene. ## 8. Range and POV-friction

The roster has the correct pressure architecture for a single-POV claustrophobic tragedy:

- Otto provides calibrated-proposal pressure with two registers of contact: direct (scenes where he proposes) and indirect (Taylor's intelligence-feed observations of the court machinery his trades consolidate).
- Aemond provides embodied-consequence presence — the physicality of the coercive instrument without dialogue weight.
- Gylda provides the one-time external naming event that is outside Taylor's attachment system — the friction point that tests her self-accounting without becoming a confidant relationship.
- Sera provides the court-tier observable surface that earns contempt-particularity — specific enough for contempt, distant enough to never become personal.
- Wren and Coll provide the stakes-layer whose welfare the reader tracks across the book: one particular (the un-priced relationship) and one ambient (the community substrate).
- Corvan provides the closing institutional voice that makes the final dramatic note possible.

The cast does not overload. There is no confidant-pressure on Taylor from any cast member — Gylda names once and exits; Wren is attached but the relationship is asymmetric-silent; Coll is non-interpretive; Sera does not know Taylor exists; Corvan appears only at close. Taylor is structurally alone inside her own accounting for the duration.

The roster is lean and correctly proportioned. The range is sufficient to put pressure on Taylor's accounting from three distinct angles (antagonist-proposing, supporting-witness-naming, world-fixture-grounding) without multiplying faces or diffusing the primary Otto-Taylor relationship.
