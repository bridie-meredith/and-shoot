## OQ-1 — Taylor's power-state at story-open

### Options proposed
1. Khepri-residue range-limited: insect control intact but capped at roughly the density available in a pre-industrial city; the multi-shard hijack capability is gone; she retains a faint organizational precog that surfaces as pattern-recognition, not prophecy.
2. Queen-Administrator sealed with precog-thin: no insect control; weak organizational precog only — reads crowd-states and faction alignments, cannot intervene directly.
3. Shard malfunctioning and intermittent: full Khepri range but unreliable; power spikes at narrative-inconvenient moments, creating danger as often as advantage.

### Resolution
Khepri-residue range-limited: insect control intact but capped at roughly the density available in a pre-industrial city; multi-shard hijack capability is gone; organizational precog surfaces as pattern-recognition, not prophecy.

### Justification
This is the only state that satisfies the tragedy constraint without cheating it. Insect control in a pre-modern city is practically uncountable — King's Landing is a warm, stone-dense, refuse-rich environment — so Taylor is genuinely dangerous, giving her enough influence to bend events. The absent hijack capability closes the "just win" exit: she cannot puppet a Room-Full-of-Lords to produce a peace. Pattern-recognition-grade precog keeps the worm-canon-pedant satisfied (the Queen-Administrator sub-shard's administrative function survives as a residue) while preventing clean prophetic wins that would break the slow-prevention shape.

### Constraint carried forward
OQ-2 must name an arrival mechanism consistent with a partial shard state — full Entity-fragment displacement is inconsistent; shard-residue-portal or authorial-fiat cold-open are admissible.

## OQ-2 — Mechanism of arrival in Westeros

### Options proposed
1. Authorial-fiat cold-open: Taylor wakes in an alley off the Hook, King's Landing. First chapter opens in medias res; the how is never answered, held as inner-monologue gap that Taylor herself cannot close.
2. Shard-residue portal: the Queen-Administrator residue, on Gold Morning's final moment, rerouted the displacement that would have killed her — she arrives as a shard side-effect, not an intentional transit.
3. Entity-fragment displacement: a dying Entity-shard (Zion's remnant) pushed her through as a last data-collection move.

### Resolution
Authorial-fiat cold-open: Taylor wakes in an alley off the Hook, no transit memory, the how permanently unresolved in her inner monologue.

### Justification
The brief frames Westeros as the setting, not the puzzle — narrative budget spent on transit mechanics is budget stolen from the road-to-hell chain. Waking-in-an-alley is the most efficient possible entry: it is AU-tolerant (world-notes confirm this), it is Worm-canon-pedant acceptable because the departure from canon is named by the premise rather than smuggled in, and it immediately places Taylor in Flea Bottom proximity which OQ-4 needs. The cold-open also establishes Taylor's epistemological condition from scene one: she does not know what happened to her any more than she knows what is happening here, which is the correct register for a cold-utilitarian operating on incomplete information.

### Constraint carried forward
No transit backstory threads can be opened — inner monologue may reference the gap but the text never resolves it; OQ-4 entry-point must be consistent with waking in the Hook district.

## OQ-3 — Year within "before Dance of Dragons"

### Options proposed
1. Late Viserys-I (120–129 AC): the Alicent/Rhaenyra cold war has hardened; Lucerys is alive; the succession fault line is drawn; the Dance is close enough to feel.
2. Viserys-I middle reign (112–120 AC): factions forming but not yet locked; more prevention runway, less urgency; Dance feels distant.
3. Immediate pre-129 AC ignition window (126–129 AC): Viserys I failing; everything is about to move; Taylor has almost no rope.

### Resolution
Late Viserys-I, specifically 122 AC: Viserys I is on the throne but declining; the Alicent faction has consolidated; Rhaenyra is at Dragonstone; Lucerys and Jace are children; Otto Hightower has recently been dismissed (120 AC) and is operating in an advisory capacity from outside the Small Council.

### Justification
122 AC gives Taylor approximately seven years of story-time before the 129 AC ignition — enough rope for the road-to-hell chain to accrue three or four auditable mistake beats, which the single-book format requires. The cold war is already cold, so prevention logic is live from scene one (cape-fic-reader needs an active board), but the ignition is not imminent, so the slow-prevention shape is credible. Otto off the Small Council but still in King's Landing is structurally ideal: he is available as antagonist-as-person without holding a formal position Taylor can attack — the intelligence-architect with no official target is harder to counter than one with a defined role.

### Constraint carried forward
F&B-canonical cast availability fixed at 122 AC: Viserys I alive and declining; Alicent queen; Rhaenyra at Dragonstone; Daemon in Pentos; Aegon II and Aemond are teenagers; Helaena is a child-to-adolescent; Lucerys and Jace are children. Any character used must be consistent with this moment.

## OQ-4 — Taylor's social entry-point in King's Landing

### Options proposed
1. Flea Bottom anonymous, with smallfolk-attached relationship forming in the first quarter via the cost-bearer ward child.
2. Faith-attached: taken in by a septon of the Street of Sisters congregation, which gives her institutional cover and proximity to the Great Sept without royal entanglement.
3. Household-attached: servant position in a minor lordly household, providing upward access at the cost of the anonymous cover Taylor needs for insect-network operations.

### Resolution
Flea Bottom anonymous: Taylor surfaces in the Hook, acquires no institutional cover, and builds the true-ally relationship (and meets the cost-bearer child) organically inside the first quarter of the book.

### Justification
The patron-with-agenda (Rhaenys/Rhaenyra register) must be reached by the end of the first quarter — but Flea Bottom anonymous provides the longest runway for the true-ally relationship, which the lyra-cost requires to be a plausible weekly presence before the closing image lands. Flea Bottom also places Taylor inside the smallfolk-as-substrate that dark-fantasy-reader needs to feel as ground rather than set dressing. The surfacing mechanism (what brings her to Rhaenys-register attention) is provided by her insect-network capability, which in a pre-modern city is genuinely legible as witchery — she does something visibly useful and terrifying, and it reaches upward through the patron-false-ally channel naturally. No institutional cover is also consistent with the cold-open: she does not have cover because she has nothing to build it from.

### Constraint carried forward
Taylor starts with no social position, no money, no Westerosi language facility (inner monologue can address the acquisition arc), and no institutional affiliation. The cost-bearer child must be resident in or near Flea Bottom at story-open.

## OQ-5 — The specific "good intention" driving Taylor

### Options proposed
1. Prevent the Dance of Dragons entirely by disrupting the succession fault line before it locks — keep Rhaenyra and Alicent's factions from reaching open war.
2. Protect Lucerys Velaryon specifically — she knows Lucerys dies early in the Dance and she intends to prevent that death as the first domino.
3. Protect the smallfolk of King's Landing from the sack and siege she knows is coming — mass-preservation as the primary intent, dynastic politics as means.

### Resolution
Prevent Lucerys Velaryon's death: Taylor identifies Lucerys as the ignition event (his death at Aemond's hands triggers the war) and makes his survival her explicit, declarative objective.

### Justification
This is reducible to the single declarative sentence the cold-utilitarian needs: "If Lucerys lives, the war does not start." It gives the slow-prevention-tragedy a named target whose non-death is legible as success — which means the retroactive revelation (her prevention caused the war anyway, differently) is structurally clean and auditable. Worm-canon-pedant is served because Taylor's pattern-recognition-grade precog reading "Lucerys = ignition point" is the exact kind of cold causal diagnosis the Queen-Administrator sub-shard's administrative function would produce. Cape-fic-reader gets a tactical objective with a named stake.

### Constraint carried forward
Lucerys Velaryon must be accessible to Taylor's world within the story — he is at Dragonstone with Rhaenyra, reachable via the patron-with-agenda channel. Every bad act Taylor commits is downstream of "this is what it costs to keep Lucerys alive."

## OQ-6 — The specific "bad thing for a good reason"

### Options proposed
1. Taylor feeds calibrated intelligence to Otto Hightower to let him manage the succession fault line toward a negotiated settlement — making herself his asset.
2. Taylor assassinates a specific Hightower-faction operative whose death removes the faction's covert pressure on Viserys I's council, buying Rhaenyra's claim another two years.
3. Taylor engineers the removal of Lucerys from Dragonstone and installs him in a Flea-Bottom-adjacent safe location — effectively kidnapping a royal ward — breaking the one bond (Rhaenyra's trust) that would have made the patron-false-ally channel protective.

### Resolution
Taylor becomes an intelligence asset for Otto Hightower: she feeds him calibrated, accurate information about Rhaenyra's faction movements in exchange for his protection of Lucerys in the succession calculus — reasoning that Otto, as intelligence-architect, can steer the succession without open war if he has better information.

### Justification
This is auditable at a specific page: the scene where Taylor makes the first intelligence trade. It aligns with cold-utilitarian register (she is doing the math: Otto's institutional stability > Rhaenyra's good faith). It opens the patron-false-ally asymmetry cleanly: Taylor believes she is managing Otto; Otto has already priced in using her and discarding her when Lucerys stops being useful as a lever. Cape-fic-reader gets the information-asymmetry reshape the board beat. Dark-fantasy-reader gets the enemy who does not pause — Otto keeps moving after every exchange. The retroactive bad is structurally elegant: by making Otto more effective, Taylor accelerates the Hightower consolidation that makes open war inevitable when Viserys dies.

### Constraint carried forward
The betrayal of the patron-false-ally (Rhaenyra register) is downstream of the intelligence-feed being discovered or its consequence being made legible. The lyra-cost must land as a direct consequence of this feed — either Otto uses the intelligence against the cost-bearer, or Taylor's protection of Lucerys is revoked the moment she is exposed.

## OQ-7 — Cost-bearer (lyra-register ward) identity

### Options proposed
1. A Flea Bottom child Taylor meets in week one — a girl of eight or nine, unnamed in the canon, who becomes Taylor's functional ward through proximity and a specific act of material protection.
2. A minor Targaryen-adjacent ward child already in King's Landing — a hostage-child of a lesser lord, accessible via the patron channel, whom Taylor gains custody of as a side condition of the Otto arrangement.
3. Helaena Targaryen herself, encountered through the patron channel — F&B-canonical, tragic, already a cost-bearer in canon.

### Resolution
A Flea Bottom girl, original (non-canonical), approximately eight years old at story-open — named Nessa. She is resident in the Hook when Taylor wakes there; the relationship forms organically as Taylor's first act of protection in Westeros.

### Justification
Nessa must be a person Taylor has seen weekly by closing-image time — a Flea Bottom resident satisfies this from scene one. Using a canonical figure (Helaena) risks worm-canon-pedant flags for divergence from her canonical fate and dark-fantasy-reader flags for "world as set dressing for protagonist." An original smallfolk child is cost-specifically correct: she cannot be abstracted into dynastic calculus, which is exactly what makes the closing-image land — Taylor's accounting handled everything except this one particular irreversible loss. Naming her Nessa gives the closing image a one-word anchor.

### Constraint carried forward
Nessa is Flea Bottom-resident, non-canonical, approximately eight at 122 AC (approximately fifteen at potential closing-image time if the book spans to 129 AC). She must appear in at least one scene per act. Her death or irreversible harm is the closing-image cost.

## OQ-8 — End-place locus (Taylor / realm / both / third-party)

### Options proposed
1. Both: Taylor is exposed, expelled or dead; the Dance begins anyway (or begins because of her), and Nessa dies in the Flea Bottom violence of the Dance's opening.
2. Realm-only: Taylor survives, probably expelled; the Dance starts; Nessa and the smallfolk pay; Taylor lives to understand what she caused.
3. Taylor-solo: Taylor pays personally (death or exile or capture by the Hightower faction); the realm escapes the worst version of the Dance.

### Resolution
Both: Taylor is either dead or permanently expelled from King's Landing by the closing image; the Dance begins on the canon timeline or earlier; Nessa dies in the Flea Bottom violence that the Dance ignites.

### Justification
"Both" is the only locus that satisfies retroactive-revelation as proof rather than surprise. Taylor-solo lets the realm off too easily — dark-fantasy-reader reads it as hope-that-costs-nothing. Realm-only lets Taylor off too easily — cold-utilitarian protagonist must pay her own ledger. The "both" configuration means the closing image can show Taylor's consequence (her death or expulsion) and Nessa's consequence (the cost she could not absorb into her accounting) simultaneously, making the chain reconstructible backward from two points at once. Worm-canon-pedant is satisfied because post-Gold-Morning Taylor paying a terminal cost is consistent with the source material's register.

### Constraint carried forward
The closing image is Nessa in the Flea Bottom violence of the Dance's opening — either Taylor witnesses it (if she survives that long) or it is confirmed by the maester-chronicler coda. Taylor's end-state is dead or expelled before or at the same moment as the Dance ignites.

## OQ-9 — Book structural ranges

### Notes
OQ-5/6 together establish a road-to-hell chain with at minimum four auditable beats: (1) the first intelligence trade with Otto, (2) Rhaenyra's trust established and then broken when the feed is discovered, (3) Lucerys's situation worsened despite Taylor's intervention, (4) closing image. A single-book format spanning 122–129 AC (seven story-years) with this chain length requires:

### Tentative ranges
- Chapters: 18–24 (to be confirmed at /and-series)
- Scenes per chapter: 3–5
- Bones per scene: 8–14

### Justification
18–24 chapters gives enough runway for the four auditable beats to breathe across three acts without requiring each beat to be a single chapter. 3–5 scenes per chapter keeps the Taylor-first-person single-book format from collapsing into vignette. 8–14 bones per scene is consistent with the substance framework's requirement that each bone carry a delta — below 8 is too thin for axis movement, above 14 risks bone-level over-decomposition in a first-person close POV. These are tentative; /and-series owns the canonical commit.

### Constraint carried forward
Minimum chapter count is 18; minimum scenes-per-chapter is 3. /and-series may expand but not compress below these floors without re-running this OQ.
