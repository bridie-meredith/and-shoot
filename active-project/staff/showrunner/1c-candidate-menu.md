### Role: Taylor Hebert (Protagonist)

**Library status.** The persona index lists multiple Taylor variants:
- `taylor-hebert` — base card, Earth-Bet, full quality. Path: `cards/personas/taylor-hebert.card.md`
- `taylor-hebert-flea-bottom` — 125 AC Crownlands/Flea Bottom, 17yo, Khepri sealed, 300m range. Path: `cards/personas/taylor-hebert-flea-bottom.card.md`

**Assessment.** `taylor-hebert-flea-bottom` is the nearest existing card. Its variant-project is `flea-bottom-dance`, a multi-season arc. The current project is a single closed novel-scope story set in the same time-window (pre-Dance KL, Flea Bottom margins, patron-proximity, witch-reputation). However, the `flea-bottom-dance` config carries season-arc scaffolding (s1–s4, glass-candle relay, Khepri-unlock threshold, demonstration rule) that does not apply to a closed single-book story. The power state also diverges: in this project Taylor has no swarm-control at all — she has involuntary precognitive flicker in place of fauna-control. This is a different physical configuration.

**Recommended approach:** Author a new variant: `taylor-hebert-flea-bottom-mirror`. This variant composes from `taylor-hebert` and notes `taylor-hebert-flea-bottom` as a sibling variant. Key divergences to document in the new card:
- Power = involuntary precognitive flicker, not fauna-control. Flicker is unreliable, unverifiable, cannot be turned off, and costs interpretively each time it fires. No swarm. No Khepri.
- Arrival mechanism = Shard-mediated involuntary deposit (dying Shard fragment, byproduct of its own death-process). Not a body-occupation of a corpse — Taylor herself arrives. She is not Tya; she is Taylor with no local identity.
- Weeks-at-most duration in Westeros at story-open. Still calibrating.
- No multi-season arc. Novel scope. The flicker discipline and patron-contact arc are the load-bearing mechanics.
- Known as "suspected witch" in Flea Bottom margins — community has begun assigning the witch-label but it is not yet hardened.
- Has NO canon ASOIAF knowledge beyond pattern-recognition from Gold Morning analogy.

### Role: The Black-Faction Patron (False-Ally, Rhaenyra-Adjacent)

**Constraint reminders from world-notes:**
- Named canon figure (one selected from list).
- Rhaenyra-adjacent but NOT Rhaenyra herself.
- Interests align with Taylor's through mid-structure (both want to prevent war).
- Holds WRONG theory: dragon-proximity amplifies Taylor's flicker.
- Misreads Taylor's distress near dragons as confirmation.
- Must have plausible path to reach down to a Flea Bottom witch as a prophetic asset.
- Divergence lands mid-structure when prevention-conclusions become mutually exclusive.
- The patron's wrong-theory-of-Taylor is the structural seam of the collision.

**Library status.** `rhaenyra-targaryen` exists at `cards/personas/rhaenyra-targaryen.card.md` (full, targaryen-era, witness-figure). No other Rhaenyra-adjacent Black faction figures are carded. All candidates below except Rhaenyra herself are [NEW] authoring required.

---

**Candidate A: Rhaenys Targaryen ("The Queen Who Never Was")**

- slug: `rhaenys-targaryen` [NEW]
- Description: Baratheon-Targaryen, wife of Corlys Velaryon, denied the throne by the Great Council of 101 AC. Oldest living claimant of the senior Targaryen line by matrilineal descent. Bonded to Meleys (the Red Queen). Political sophistication is deep and battle-tested by decades of being the alternative — she understands precisely what faction politics costs, which makes her a genuinely compelling false-ally: she believes preventing the Dance is achievable and has personal reason to.
- False-ally mechanics: Rhaenys genuinely wants to prevent succession catastrophe. She does not want Rhaenyra to fight a war she might lose. She would use a prophetic asset — even a Flea Bottom witch — if the asset's predictions seemed to track. Her amplification-theory (dragon-proximity boosts the flicker) fits her worldview: she has lived with Meleys for decades, she knows what dragon-contact does to her own body. She would extrapolate wrongly but plausibly.
- Divergence trigger: When Taylor's analysis points away from preventing Rhaenyra's succession and toward an action that destabilizes the Black faction's internal structure, Rhaenys cannot follow. Her loyalty is to Rhaenyra-survives, not to Taylor's flicker-reading.
- Casting note: Rhaenys has access to Dragonstone and the Velaryon network. Her reach into Flea Bottom requires an intermediary step (a factor or retainer who surfaces the witch-reports to her). She is not a woman who walks into Flea Bottom personally; she would dispatch someone first.
- Proximity to Rhaenyra: Extremely high. Rhaenys functions as senior advisement for the Black faction and as the emotional voice of people who remember what the Great Council cost.
- Canon status: canon
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**Candidate B: Corlys Velaryon ("The Sea Snake")**

- slug: `corlys-velaryon` [NEW]
- Description: Admiral, master of Driftmark, the richest man in Westeros, husband of Rhaenys. His interest in the Black faction is partly dynastic (his grandchildren stand to inherit the Iron Throne if Rhaenyra succeeds) and partly strategic (Velaryon naval power is the Black faction's primary military advantage; he is protecting the investment). Older, pragmatic, capable of treating a Flea Bottom witch as a resource if the resource appears to work.
- False-ally mechanics: Corlys is mission-oriented. He aligns with Taylor while Taylor's flicker appears to generate actionable intelligence. His amplification-theory (dragon-proximity boosts it) fits his strategic worldview: he needs the asset maximally deployed. When Taylor's reading conflicts with his dynastic interests, the conflict is not emotional — it is a strategic recalculation. He withdraws support rather than betraying her; the collision is cold.
- Divergence trigger: When the opposite-number's death creates a vacuum that threatens Corlys's naval-political position (if the unbonded dragon falls to a Green claimant, Corlys is directly disadvantaged), and he understands Taylor caused this, the alignment ends.
- Casting note: Corlys has infrastructure — ships, retainers, a commercial network that reaches Flea Bottom's docks. His path to Taylor is more organic than Rhaenys's; he might identify her through dock-adjacent factor networks before anyone at Dragonstone knows she exists. This is plausible for the story's early contact arc.
- Proximity to Rhaenyra: High. Rhaenyra's position depends on Velaryon military and naval support.
- Canon status: canon
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**Candidate C: Baela Targaryen**

- slug: `baela-targaryen` [NEW]
- Description: Daughter of Daemon and Laena Velaryon. Bonded to Moondancer. Young (approximately 13–15 in the Viserys-declining era), ambitious, trained by Daemon's pedagogy of aggression. She is the Black faction's forward edge — the rider who acts while others deliberate.
- False-ally mechanics: Baela would recruit Taylor directly and impulsively. She is closer to Taylor's operational register — willing to take direct action, believes prevention is achievable through decisive intervention. Alignment is genuine but thin: Baela's loyalty is to Rhaenyra and to her own understanding of what the Black faction needs. When Taylor's action kills the opposite-number and it becomes clear what happened, Baela's response is simpler and faster than Rhaenys or Corlys — she is angry, not strategically calculating.
- Divergence trigger: Earlier and less complex than A or B. Baela cannot absorb the collision; she does not have the political sophistication to make it a mid-structure turn rather than an immediate break. This makes her a less useful false-ally for the structural requirement that the patron-collision lands mid-structure, not at the climax.
- Casting note: Baela in KL proper is plausible but requires justification. Dragonstone is her primary residence at this period.
- Proximity to Rhaenyra: Very high by blood (half-sister through Daemon). Less powerful as an independent actor than A or B.
- Canon status: canon
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**Candidate D: Mysaria ("Lady Misery" / Master of Whisperers, Black-faction adjacent)**

- slug: `mysaria` [NEW]
- Description: Daemon Targaryen's former paramour. Information broker. By the Viserys declining era she is positioned in King's Landing with her own intelligence network operating below the level of the formal court. Her allegiances are complex but she has a history of cooperating with Rhaenyra's faction when interests align.
- False-ally mechanics: Mysaria's path to Taylor is the most organic — she runs an information network in KL, and a Flea Bottom witch who appears to know things she should not is exactly the kind of asset her network would surface. Her amplification-theory (dragon-proximity boosts the flicker) is a working hypothesis an intelligence operator would form from observing Taylor's behavior near the Dragonpit. She is not emotionally invested in any faction; her alignment is contractual.
- Divergence trigger: When Taylor's action costs Mysaria's position — if the wrong-rider claiming the dragon creates political fallout that exposes Mysaria's knowledge of the Flea Bottom witch — the alignment ends because Mysaria's self-preservation calculation supersedes all else.
- Casting note: Mysaria's presence in KL during the Viserys era is canon-supported. Her intelligence access makes the patron's reach into Flea Bottom require zero intermediary — she finds Taylor directly.
- Proximity to Rhaenyra: Indirect. She is Daemon-adjacent rather than Rhaenyra-adjacent. She can be positioned as conveying information to the Black faction without being Rhaenyra's direct instrument.
- Patron's wrong amplification-theory risk: Slightly harder to motivate — Mysaria is a rationalist by temperament. She would be skeptical of a magical-amplification theory. If she holds it, it must be shown as a working hypothesis she has not yet falsified, not a belief.
- Canon status: canon
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**Margit recommendation:** Rhaenys Targaryen (Candidate A) best serves the structural requirement. She has independent standing (not subordinate to Rhaenyra or Daemon), a genuine prevention-aligned motivation, the political sophistication for a mid-structure divergence rather than an immediate break, and a plausible dragon-proximity theory derived from her decades with Meleys. Corlys (Candidate B) is a strong second, particularly if the story's early contact phase benefits from dock-network organics. Both can be carded; showrunner selects the active patron.

### Role: The Opposite-Number (Destined Bonder, Young Targaryen, Invented OC)

**Constraint reminders from world-notes:**
- Young Targaryen. Invented OC (to free the mirror-figure from canon constraint).
- True-believer who acts badly for good reasons and would have been proven right.
- Dragon-claim is their destined bond — the dragon Taylor reads as the Dance's first strike.
- Visible on-page enough that the chronicler's counterfactual naming lands.
- Must have proximity to the cost-bearer cousin (the OC they are specifically protecting).
- Taylor encounters them via the patron (the patron brings Taylor into faction-adjacency that makes the contact possible).

---

**OC Concept 1: Aeryn Targaryen**

- slug: `oc-aeryn-targaryen` [NEW]
- Household: House Targaryen, collateral branch — granddaughter of one of Viserys I's lesser uncles. Documented in the household registers but not in the succession chronicles. Young enough to be plausible as an early-Dance-era claimant without having established chronicle presence.
- Age: approximately 16–18. Old enough to be taken seriously as a rider candidate; young enough to read as someone whose conviction has not yet been seasoned by failure.
- Dragon-claim: Tessarion (the Blue Queen — a younger, smaller dragon also claimed in canon by Daeron Targaryen in the Dance proper). In the pre-Dance window, Tessarion is unclaimed; Aeryn has been making claim-attempts — approaching the dragonpit, enduring the closeness, surviving, building toward the bond. The destined bond is not yet formally recognized; it is in process.
- True-believer mechanics: Aeryn believes that the Black faction's best defense against the Dance is preventing their own aggressive riders from claiming the most combat-ready dragons — that peace requires the Black faction to foreclose its own military escalation. She is politically committed to a prevention strategy that requires her own faction to accept constraint. This is the true-believer register: she acts badly for good reasons (she has blocked at least one Black rider from claiming Tessarion, creating internal faction conflict) and would have been proven right. If she bonds Tessarion and keeps her, the Dance's first aerial strike cannot happen; without Tessarion in a competent rider's hands, a Green-adjacent worst rider eventually claims it.
- Cost-bearer relationship: Aeryn is specifically protecting a younger cousin (the cost-bearer OC) who lives on the margins of the Black faction's household — a child who is not a claimant but whom Aeryn has taken personal responsibility for, as the one adult in the child's life who is paying attention.
- Characterization: Rigorous, self-denying, correct in her prevention-analysis but unable to prevent Taylor from misreading her dragon-claim as the threat Taylor is trying to forestall. The dramatic irony is that the structure Taylor identifies as the problem (a young Targaryen building a dragon-bond) is the solution, not the threat.
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**OC Concept 2: Vaegon Targaryen**

- slug: `oc-vaegon-targaryen` [NEW]
- Household: House Targaryen, distant collateral — grandson of a legitimized bastard from the reign of Aenys I. The Targaryen blood is thin enough to be non-threatening to the succession but thick enough to permit dragon-bonding attempts. He is in the household registers as a ward rather than a claimant.
- Age: approximately 14–17. Younger than Aeryn, which makes his conviction read as more innocent and therefore more devastating when Taylor misidentifies it.
- Dragon-claim: Silverwing (bonded in canon to Ulf the White and briefly to Alysanne Targaryen; in the pre-Dance window she is riderless). Vaegon has been sitting with Silverwing in the Dragonpit, patient, unhurried, in the belief that the bond comes to those who do not grasp. This is characterologically specific: he does not want to claim Silverwing for factional advantage; he wants to bond with her because he believes the bond is the only way to protect her from being claimed by someone who will use her badly.
- True-believer mechanics: Vaegon believes the Dance is coming and that the only way to prevent it is for the dragons to be in the hands of riders who will refuse to deploy them. His bad acts: he has actively interfered with other riders' claiming attempts (on Silverwing specifically), in the conviction that any rider who wants Silverwing for war is the wrong rider. He is not wrong. Taylor kills him reading his interference as aggression, not as the prevention it is.
- Cost-bearer relationship: A younger half-sibling — another ward of the Targaryen household — whom Vaegon is specifically shielding from the faction's attention. The child has no dragon-blood potency, which makes their presence in the household politically marginal; Vaegon has made himself the buffer between the child and the faction's indifference.
- Characterization: Gentle, certain, operating on a long patience that Taylor's flicker-misfire cuts short. The counterfactual (Vaegon bonds Silverwing, refuses every deployment request, and by holding Silverwing out of the war makes the war slightly less catastrophic) is believable precisely because his method is so clearly not aggressive.
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**OC Concept 3: Maekar Targaryen**

- slug: `oc-maekar-targaryen` [NEW]
- Household: Son of a younger son of King Jaehaerys I's brother — third-degree Targaryen, entirely off the succession horizon, present at court as a matter of family custom rather than political weight. He is a recognized Targaryen in the household rolls but no one is tracking him as a factor.
- Age: approximately 15–19. Older end of the range — old enough that his prevention-strategy is articulated and reasoned, not merely instinctive.
- Dragon-claim: Dreamfyre (bonded in canon to Helaena Targaryen in the Dance era; in the pre-Dance window she is claimed by Helaena but the bond is contested and Helaena is young). The dragon-claim in this slot is contested rather than vacant — Maekar has been blocked from approaching Dreamfyre by Green-faction actors who are managing Helaena's claim. His bad acts include covert interference with the Green faction's management of Dreamfyre's access, driven by the belief that a Black rider on Dreamfyre forecloses a Green military option.
- True-believer mechanics: Maekar believes, specifically and articulately, that the Dance can only be prevented by eliminating the Green faction's air superiority before the war starts — not through combat but through preemptive bonding. He is willing to be politically radical about this; his bad acts are in service of a strategic vision that is not wrong. He would have bonded Dreamfyre, denied the Green faction the asset, and created a negotiated standoff. Taylor kills him reading the contested-bonding interference as an aggressive act.
- Cost-bearer relationship: A bastard-acknowledged child of the Targaryen household — Maekar's younger illegitimate cousin — whom Maekar has been quietly integrating into household routines to give the child cover. The child exists in the household records in a marginal status that Maekar's advocacy has been quietly shoring up.
- Characterization: Political, articulate, capable of explaining exactly why he is right. This makes the mirror-function more explicit — his reasoning is visible enough that the reader can compare it to Taylor's and see where they diverge. The divergence is small, which is the horror.
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**Margit recommendation:** OC Concept 1 (Aeryn Targaryen) or OC Concept 2 (Vaegon Targaryen). Aeryn's gender creates an additional resonance with Taylor's gender that the prose can use without making it explicit. Vaegon's patience-register is particularly useful if the story wants the opposite-number to be sympathetic rather than politically sophisticated. Maekar's articulateness is useful if the story wants the mirror-function explicit enough that the counterfactual naming in the coda is already legible to the reader from his prior on-page presence.

**OC Concept A: Lyra (no Targaryen surname, ward-status)**

- slug: `oc-lyra-targaryen-ward` [NEW]
- Age: 8–10. Young enough that their presence in the Targaryen household is unremarked; old enough to be a distinct person rather than an infant prop.
- Household: Kept in the household as a ward — a distant Targaryen collateral whose family is gone, maintained by custom rather than by any political interest in the child's future. The opposite-number is the only person in the household who is actively looking out for her.
- Relationship to opposite-number: The opposite-number has been quietly advocating for Lyra's position in the household — making sure she is fed, that her ward-status is not revoked, that the household machinery continues to include her. The advocacy is private and methodical, the kind that Lyra may not fully understand as advocacy.
- Taylor's path to the child: The patron brings Taylor into the household for a consultation (the amplification-theory drives this — the patron wants Taylor near a dragon). Taylor meets Lyra peripherally, in the way of someone who is always slightly peripheral to household activities. She notices the protection the opposite-number provides, registers it as a data point about the opposite-number's character. The informal protection Taylor undertakes is not a commitment; it is the reflex of someone who registers a child with inadequate cover.
- Closing image function: Lyra's death after the Dance begins is the consequence-confirmation. The opposite-number's protection is gone. The patron's attention is elsewhere. Taylor's informal protection is not operative. The child is in the closing image because the closing image requires us to see what the counterfactual would have saved — specifically, individually, by name.
- Characterization: Quiet. Watching. The child who understands more about her situation than anyone assumes. Does not perform her vulnerability; simply occupies it.
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**OC Concept B: Daeron (no distinguished surname, bastard-acknowledged)**

- slug: `oc-daeron-targaryen-ward` [NEW]
- Age: 6–9. Younger — makes the death colder because the child's moral weight is undeniable.
- Household: Bastard-acknowledged, given the Targaryen name by courtesy rather than formal bastard-naming. Politically invisible. The household tolerates his presence; no one champions it except the opposite-number.
- Relationship to opposite-number: Explicit adoptive-sibling dynamic. The opposite-number has been Daeron's functional protector since a specific moment (perhaps when a household crisis left Daeron exposed and the opposite-number stepped in). The protection is publicly visible within the household even if not politically remarked.
- Taylor's path to the child: Similar to Concept A. Patron-mediated introduction, peripheral encounter, reflex protection. The difference: Daeron is young enough that he is not capable of understanding Taylor's nature; he simply responds to her as someone who is present and not indifferent.
- Closing image function: Daeron's death reads even colder at this age. The cost-bearer function requires that the death land as consequence, not sentiment — at this age, the narrative register of "cold" is harder to maintain. The prose must earn the cold register by ensuring the chronicler's coda has already done the moral work before the death image arrives.
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**OC Concept C: Aemma (distant collateral, acknowledged ward)**

- slug: `oc-aemma-targaryen-ward` [NEW]
- Age: 11–13. Older than A or B — old enough to have a relationship with the opposite-number that is based on shared understanding rather than pure protection. They have worked together, in small ways, on the opposite-number's prevention project.
- Household: Distant collateral ward. The Targaryen name is her only connection to the household. She is documented but unremarkable.
- Relationship to opposite-number: More peer-adjacent than A or B. The opposite-number has been protecting her AND including her — teaching her the faction landscape, explaining who is safe to trust. Aemma has a sense of the stakes. She knows something is happening with the opposite-number's dragon-claim.
- Taylor's path: The patron-mediated introduction works here, but Aemma is also capable of noticing Taylor's presence on her own and responding to it. She is old enough to register Taylor as unusual without fully understanding why.
- Closing image function: Aemma's age means her death can be rendered with a specific detail that makes it concrete without being sentimental — she is old enough to have been capable of understanding the protection-absence when it arrived, and the prose can render the recognition without editorializing.
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**Margit recommendation:** OC Concept A (Lyra) or Concept C (Aemma). Lyra's young age keeps the cost-bearer from competing with the opposite-number for narrative weight; she is cost-bearer, not a second protagonist. Aemma's slightly older age creates a more specific final image but risks pulling focus toward the cost-bearer's experience, which the world-notes' two-register architecture prohibits in the body (the cost-bearer death is coda-register, not body-register).

### Role: The Wrong Rider (Faction's Worst Available Bonder)

**Constraint reminders from world-notes:**
- The rider the dead opposite-number was actively preventing from rising.
- Claims the unbonded dragon after the opposite-number's death.
- Taylor's prevention produces the exact configuration she saw in the flicker, with a different rider.
- Must hold to ASOIAF dragon-claiming lore — unbonded dragon post-bonder-death is unmoored; claiming-windows exist in the Dance period.

---

**Candidate 1: Aemond Targaryen (canon, Green faction)**

- slug: `aemond-targaryen` — EXISTS: `cards/personas/aemond-targaryen.card.md` (scant, targaryen-antagonist)
- Faction: Green. Vhagar's rider. The most militarily capable dragon-rider of the Dance era.
- Why worst available: Aemond on a second dragon (in addition to Vhagar, or as a placeholder before the story-open configures Vhagar's claim differently) would represent the maximum concentration of military dragon-capability in the Green faction. The opposite-number was specifically keeping the unbonded dragon from being approachable by Green-faction riders.
- Card status: EXISTS (scant). Would need expansion to full for this role. The existing scant card confirms Aemond's targaryen-antagonist function.
- Constraint note: If the dragon in question is one not bonded to Aemond in canon, the claim requires careful lore-handling. Aemond already has Vhagar; a second claim-window would be irregular. This is achievable under Dance-era claiming rules but needs the condition card to authorize it.

**Candidate 2: Criston Cole (canon, Green faction)**

- slug: `criston-cole` [NEW]
- Faction: Green. Lord Commander of the Kingsguard. Rhaenyra's former lover turned committed Green partisan. Cole does not have a dragon, which makes him a plausible worst-available rider in a different register: he is the Green faction's most politically dangerous committed soldier, and a dragon in his control (even if he does not ride it himself but controls who claims it) is worse than a dragon in Green-adjacent hands.
- Why worst available: If the wrong-rider is interpreted as "worst available" in the political sense rather than the military sense, Cole as the facilitator of the dragon's claiming is more threatening than any individual rider, because Cole's institutional position in the Kingsguard gives him access to the claiming process. He is not a rider himself; he is the mechanism by which the wrong rider gets to the dragon.
- Card status: not-yet-authored

**Candidate 3: Ulf the White (canon, later history)**

- slug: `ulf-the-white` [NEW]
- Faction: nominally Black faction in the Dance, but known as a dragonseeds claimant who betrayed the Blacks during the Dance. A Targaryen bastard of uncertain origin who claims Silverwing.
- Why worst available: If the dragon in question is Silverwing, Ulf's claiming of Silverwing is documented in the canon Dance history. Taylor's kill of the opposite-number who was specifically preventing Silverwing from falling into the wrong hands would produce exactly the historical outcome — Ulf claims Silverwing, betrays the Blacks, and the Dance is worse for it. The historical irony is legible to ASOIAF-literate readers without being stated.
- Card status: not-yet-authored
- Constraint note: Ulf claiming Silverwing requires that Silverwing is the unbonded dragon in question. If the dragon is Tessarion or Dreamfyre (per the opposite-number candidates above), this candidate does not apply.

**Candidate 4: Aegon II Targaryen (canon, Green faction)**

- slug: `aegon-ii-targaryen` — EXISTS: `cards/personas/aegon-ii-targaryen.card.md` (scant, targaryen-antagonist)
- Faction: Green. The other claimant to the Iron Throne.
- Why worst available: Aegon II claiming a second dragon (or facilitation of his dragon's claiming) represents direct Green-succession consolidation. The opposite-number was preventing this; Taylor ends the prevention. However, Aegon II already has Sunfyre in canon, which complicates a second-claiming scenario in the pre-Dance window.
- Card status: EXISTS (scant).

**Margit recommendation:** The choice of wrong rider is determined by which dragon serves as the story's load-bearing dragon and which opposite-number OC is selected. If Vaegon / Silverwing: Ulf the White is the historically appropriate worst rider and the irony is most legible to ASOIAF-literate readers (Candidate 3). If Aeryn / Tessarion: a new OC wrong-rider is likely better than a canon character, since Tessarion's canon claiming by Daeron Targaryen (a relatively sympathetic figure) makes him a difficult "worst" rider. If Maekar / Dreamfyre: Aemond or a new OC Green-faction rider.

**OC option: `oc-wrong-rider` [NEW]** — An invented Green-faction Targaryen bastard, documented in the household records but not in the chronicles, whose capabilities and temperament make him the faction's worst option. Constructed to be factionally correct (Targaryen blood for claiming) and characterologically wrong (the opposite of everything the opposite-number stood for). This option frees the story from the canon claiming-history constraints.

### Role: The Maester-Chronicler (Witness, Coda-Only)

- Must name the opposite-number's counterfactual explicitly.
- Voice: cold, institutional, historical verdict.
- Placement: in KL, Oldtown Citadel, or attached to a household.

**Candidate X: Maester Edwyn (OC, attached to a Black-faction household)**

- slug: `oc-maester-edwyn` [NEW]
- Placement: Household maester, attached to a minor Black-faction lord or to the Targaryen household proper (not to the Red Keep's maester, who is Viserys's and politically controlled).
- Why useful: A household maester has access to the household records — the opposite-number's ward-assignments, the cost-bearer cousin's documented status, the chain of events that follows the opposite-number's death. He can write the coda as a household chronicle entry, which gives the cold register an institutional texture: this is the record of a household that was altered by a specific death.
- Voice register: Citadel-trained. "Yes, yes" verbal tic, polite-brief, citation-as-interruption. The coda should feel like a chronicle entry being dictated — flat, precise, retrospective. See `cards/dialects/westeros-maester.card.md` for the behavior card.

### Condition C10: The Patron's Wrong Amplification-Theory (Binding Behavior Card)

**Library status:** No existing card applicable.

**Required card:** `cond-patron-amplification-theory-mirror` [NEW]

This card must cover:
- The theory: the patron believes dragon-proximity amplifies Taylor's flicker. This theory is wrong.
- The evidence the patron has: Taylor's behavior near dragons is significantly more disrupted than in ordinary settings. She goes still, her distress is visible, she sometimes speaks in fragments that turn out to be accurate descriptions of nearby events. The patron interprets this as the flicker firing more reliably and more powerfully near dragons.
- Why the theory is wrong: The flicker is involuntary and unreliable regardless of proximity. Near dragons, it fires with greater violence (more cost, more fragmentation, more interpretive noise) because of the Gold Morning trauma trigger — dragons-at-proximity are Scion-scale threat-pattern activators in Taylor's nervous system. The increased output is increased noise, not increased signal. The patron cannot distinguish this.
- How the patron uses the wrong theory: They arrange for Taylor to be near dragons when they want the most actionable intelligence. They misread her increased distress as confirmation of quality. They build the bad act on this foundation: if Taylor's flicker near the Dragonpit shows her the dragon above a burning city, the patron accepts this as a reliable sighting.
- The structural seam: the patron's wrong-theory-of-Taylor is where the collision lives. When Taylor's prevention action (the kill) produces the opposite of prevention, and the patron realizes that their amplification-theory was the framework through which they authorized the bad act, the collision is not betrayal — it is the mutual recognition that their shared framework was wrong. The patron cannot accept the cost of this recognition easily; Taylor's prevention-logic was enabled by the patron's wrong-theory-of-Taylor.
- Binding constraint: No agent should write a scene where the patron explicitly acknowledges the wrong-theory during the alignment phase. The wrong-theory is invisible to the patron while it is operating. It becomes visible (and becomes the collision point) only when the bad act's consequences make it impossible to ignore.
