# World Notes — Decided Constraints
# Internal. Written in step 1a. One constraint per line. Law/Lore/Behavior prefix where applicable.

## From brief (hard)
Law: Taylor Hebert is the protagonist — not a variant, not an OC shaped like her.
Law: She arrives in Westeros via reincarnation — soul persists, prior body is gone; she did not time-travel, she was reborn.
Law: She is post-Gold Morning — she has survived civilizational collapse, mass death at Endbringer scale, and the end of the parahuman age. The grief and the scale-blindness-reversal are load-bearing, not backstory flavor.
Lore: The setting is Westeros. Not Essos as primary theater. Not another continent. Westeros.
Lore: The era is Dance of Dragons lead-up (Targaryen civil war, ~120-130 AC) or earlier — not the War of Five Kings (which is ~280-300 AC, roughly 150 years later). This project is in the Targaryen era, when dragons are still alive and the dynasty has not yet fractured.
Law: Genre register is locked: fast, action, pulpy, dramatic, tension, escalation. This is not a slow-burn introspective piece. Things happen, they escalate, there are consequences. Every episode should move.

## From brief-expansion (derived — high confidence)
Behavior: Taylor's escalation-default carries forward — she does not wait for situations to become fatal before acting. This is a character constant, not a mood.
Behavior: Loyalty without origin — she has no natal allegiances in Westeros; what she protects is pure choice and therefore load-bearing every time she makes that choice.
Behavior: Post-trauma pragmatism — grief is a luxury, people are variables, she has already outlived the worst thing she can imagine. This does not mean she is emotionally absent; it means she does not perform feelings while moving.
Lore: The Dance of Dragons (or the lead-up to it) is the macro event. The story lives one level below it — the local lord who will back the wrong side, the garrison that will be abandoned, the thing that the big war makes possible or makes irrelevant.
Law: Survivor-knowledge asymmetry is the story's primary dramatic engine: she knows how civilizational collapse happens, in granular detail, and she is watching the prologue. The pre-catastrophe window is the ticking clock.

## Power mechanics (partially settled — specifics pending OQ resolution)
Law: Her parahuman power is not simply "gone" — reincarnation is a soul-persistence event, and her power is shard-linked in ways Worm's lore established. The question of what form the power takes in a new body is an OQ, but the assumption that it is simply absent is ruled out. The story requires her to have capability; the exact form is what the OQ resolves.
Law: Power mechanics must obey Worm's established rules for what shards do and do not do. The worm-canon-pedant is on the panel. No unexplained extensions of her toolkit.
