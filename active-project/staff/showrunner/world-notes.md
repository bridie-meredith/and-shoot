# World Notes — Decided Constraints
# Internal. Written by showrunner in step 1a. One constraint per line. Law/Lore/Behavior prefix where applicable.

## FROM BRIEF (settled on intake)

Law: Capes whose existence as that identity was conclusively ended between S9 formation and Taylor's death are eligible for arrival in Westeros. "Ended" includes canonical death AND identity-death where the person survived but the parahuman persona was permanently concluded (e.g., Bonesaw surrendering under Contessa — Bonesaw as an identity died). Auditor flag resolved: Riley/Bonesaw retained under this amended law.
Law: Shards arrive with their host. A shard cannot operate through any other host than the one it arrived with. No new bonding, no shard transfer.
Law: Shards cannot gain new hosts under any circumstances in this setting. The host they arrived with is the only host they will ever have.
Lore: Arrival point is roughly 30 years before the Dance of Dragons (Targaryen civil war, ~120 AC). Aegon I's conquest was ~2 centuries prior.
Lore: Westerosi do not know what parahumans are. They have a working vocabulary of magic (maesters know greensight, fire magic, the old gods, dragons) but nothing resembling Worm power taxonomy.
Behavior: Westerosi characters process cape powers through their existing frameworks: sorcery, curses, demon-bargains, gifts of the gods, dragon-kin, the supernatural. They do not adapt rapidly to modern Earth-Bet thinking.

## FROM 1b RESOLUTION (appended as each OQ is decided)

# OQ-1: ARRIVAL MECHANIC — decided: Three-mode system, host-type determines entry form
Law: Three arrival modes exist simultaneously. Hosts may arrive as: (A) adult drop-in with a new body whose appearance reflects their power; (B) reincarnated infant or child retaining power and fragmented shard-memory but no episodic Earth-Bet memories; (C) hatched from a wild dragon egg, no longer human, shard persists and functions through the new body with deep disorientation.
Law: The mode of arrival is determined by the shard, not the host's preference. The shard selects the mode it believes best positions its power for expression and development in the new environment.
Law: Type-A arrivals (adult drop-in) have full Earth-Bet memories and know exactly what they are and what happened to them. Their bodies are new but the memories are intact.
Law: Type-B arrivals (reincarnated) have no episodic memories of Earth-Bet. They have shard-dreams — recurring nightmare-visions of Earth-Bet events that feel like prophecy rather than memory. The shard pushes these through.
Law: Type-C arrivals (dragon-born) cannot speak human language until they choose to learn it; they retain abstract shard-understanding (threat assessment, power activation) but not language. Their inner experience is deeply alien.

# OQ-2: SHARD BEHAVIOR NEAR WESTEROSI MAGIC — decided: Shards are fascinated and hungry, but destabilized
Law: Shards register Westerosi magic (dragon fire, greensight, the weirwood network, wildfire) as anomalous data — not Earth-Bet parahuman powers, not recognizable threat categories. This produces a state of intense shard-interest that manifests as pressure on the host: intrusive shard-attention, amplified power expression, occasional involuntary activations near magic sources.
Law: Weirwood trees and the greensight network produce the strongest shard-reaction: the network resembles the Entity's own structure enough that shards attempt to interface with it and fail. Hosts near weirwoods may experience shard-activity spikes and bleedthrough of the shard's alien perspective.
Law: Dragon fire is novel enough that no cape has pre-adapted to it. It works on them.
Law: Wildfire produces a weaker version of the same shard-fascination as weirwood — chaotic energy with no recognizable pattern.
Behavior: Shards do not understand the Westerosi magic system. They process it as anomalous data and try to categorize it as threat/neutral/useful. This processing takes time. Hosts experience this as their power behaving oddly near magic sources.

# OQ-3: POLITICAL POSITION OF ARRIVED CAPES — decided: No unified faction; scattered, unknown to each other at series start
Lore: Arrived capes have no knowledge of each other's presence at series start. Each believes they may be alone in Westeros.
Lore: No organization knows about the capes. The Maesters have no record. The crown has no record. They are unknown quantities.
Behavior: Arrived capes must construct their own position in Westerosi society or live outside it. They have no support structure, no PRT, no gangs, no allies who understand what they are.

# OQ-4: POWER FUNCTION IN WESTEROS — decided: Powers work, but with environmental friction
Law: Powers function in Westeros. There is no suppression field, no Manton effect override, no fundamental change to how powers operate.
Law: Trigger conditions and power expressions that relied on Earth-Bet context (e.g., powers linked to bugs specific to North America, powers calibrated to modern urban environments) may need to find Westerosi analogs. The shard adapts the expression; the power type persists.
Law: Shards still provide the secondary sense, the passive awareness, the combat precognition — all the background functions continue. The host retains them fully.

# OQ-5: WHICH DEAD CAPES ARRIVE — decided: Series uses 4-6 protagonists plus NPCs; initial cast selection below
Lore: Not every dead cape arrives. The mechanism is selective but the selection criteria are unknown to anyone in-world. From the outside it looks like coincidence or divine selection.

# OQ-6: DEATH RETENTION — decided: They know they died
Law: Type-A and Type-B (through shard-dreams) arrivals retain knowledge that they died on Earth-Bet. Type-C arrivals do not — the dragon body has no framework for the concept of a prior life.
Behavior: The knowledge that one died and is now here is not psychologically neutral. How each character processes this is character-specific and must be paid for in the text.

# OQ-7: SEASON 1 FOCAL REGION — decided: Reach and the Stormlands, converging toward King's Landing
Lore: Season 1 is set primarily in the Reach and the Stormlands, approximately 30 years before the Dance. The Red Keep exists; the Targaryens hold the throne; the succession conflict that will become the Dance is a distant political rumor. Dragons are alive and known but rare in the south.
