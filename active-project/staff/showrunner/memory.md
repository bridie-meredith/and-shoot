series:
  theme: Self-authorship when the author is an alien intelligence — can you choose who you become when the thing that made you has its own agenda?
  laws:
    - Arrival modes: Type-A (adult drop-in, full memory), Type-B (reincarnated child, shard-dreams only), Type-C (dragon-born, no language/memory); shard selects
    - Shard-host lock absolute: no new bonding, no transfer, no new parahumans in Westeros
    - Shards treat Westerosi magic as anomalous data; attempted interface fails; produces host pressure
    - Powers work with environmental friction on Earth-Bet-specific expressions; shard adapts expression
    - Dragon fire novel — no pre-adaptation; works on capes
    - Eligible arrivals include canonical deaths AND identity-death (Bonesaw amendment: surrendered capes count if identity was conclusively ended)
  lore:
    - Setting: ~120 AC, ~30 years before Dance of Dragons; Iron Throne held by Targaryens
    - Capes have no knowledge of each other at series start; each believes they may be alone
    - No Westerosi organization knows about the capes at series start
    - Season 1 focal region: Reach and Stormlands, converging toward King's Landing
    - Weirwood network produces strongest shard-response; resembles Entity structure enough that shards attempt interface and fail
    - All five "trace" stories from s01 converge on an unnamed collector in King's Landing
  behaviors:
    - Westerosi characters process cape powers through existing frameworks (sorcery, curses, gods, demon-bargains)
    - Jack does not reveal what he is without extreme dramatic justification
    - Riley does not know what she is — shard-dreams only, no Earth-Bet vocabulary
    - Crawler has no human language at series open
    - Clockblocker's humor armor always running; silence is the signal
    - Noelle's suppression cost appears in every scene she occupies
    - Shatterbird's grandiosity is survival architecture, not simple villainy
  plot:
    start: Six dead capes, isolated in a medieval world with no framework for them, constructing provisional identities in the wreckage of the ones they had
    end: The Dance of Dragons forces a reckoning — each cape declares what they are, permanently; not all answers include survival
    protagonist_arc: From fractured identity-in-hiding to chosen identity under maximum pressure; the choosing is the arc
    series_question: Can you choose who you become when the thing that made you has its own agenda?
  cast_roster:
    - jack-slash: psychological operator running the show without instruments, Reach minor lordship cover
    - bonesaw: child reincarnate age 8, Reach village, tinker power re-emerging, does not know what she is
    - crawler: iridescent-black dragon in Stormlands coastal cliffs, Type-C, no language, adaptation hunger active
    - clockblocker: Ward on the wrong road, Stormlands market town → Kingsroad, father-grief without object
    - noelle: walking west toward King's Landing, hunger suppressed at high daily cost, six weeks in
    - shatterbird: Yi Ti noblewoman cover, Highgarden-bound, glass-deprived, Alec's death underneath everything
  stage_elements:
    - reach-smallfolk-village: Riley's home; weirwood at the edge is the shard-pressure site
    - stormlands-coastal-cliffs: Crawler's den; Targaryen expedition convergence point
    - kings-landing-exterior: season 1 convergence destination; trace-collector is here
    - reach-smallfolk-village: Jack's initial operational territory (minor lordship nearby)
