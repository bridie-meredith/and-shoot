# 1b — OQ-2 Options: Power scope, onset curve, range, multi-body, series arc
# Status: PROPOSED — awaiting orchestrator decision

---

## Option A — Degraded-and-recovering: shard operates at fraction of canon ceiling from breath one; Khepri-mantle sealed until earned

**Story open — what is available:**
Taylor has insect control and full-body proprioception across every arthropod within a roughly 300-meter radius at story open — functional, usable, unmistakably her power. Range is sharp-bounded: past that edge, nothing. She feels the cut-off the way you feel a wall in the dark. Pain-tolerance is intact. She is not fragile. She can do meaningful things from the first episode.

**What is sealed and why:**
Three things are absent: (1) the multi-body human coordination that defines the Khepri-mantle, (2) the extreme-range diffuse-swarm mode she operated in at Gold Morning (continental-scale ambient awareness), and (3) the fine-detail neurological threading that let her suppress friendly-fire and run thousands of simultaneous bodies without lag. The shard explains the seal internally as infrastructure. In canon Worm, shards run on Entity network bandwidth — distributed processing across a peer-to-peer web of entities. In Westeros, that network does not exist. The shard is running local-only. It has everything it needs for basic operation — the trigger event happened, the shard seated fully — but the processing overhead for Khepri-tier operations requires bandwidth it is borrowing from nowhere. The shard is not broken. It is air-gapped.

**Recovery curve over the series:**
The shard adapts. It is, after all, a piece of an entity that evolves through use. Three unlock mechanisms operate across the series arc:
- Range expands organically as the shard builds local caching — Taylor's insect-reach grows from ~300m at story open toward a hard ceiling of roughly 1.5km by late season 2. Beyond that ceiling, she needs external amplifiers (see below).
- Khepri-mantle human coordination unlocks as a discrete threshold event in mid-series — the trigger is not emotional but informational: the shard achieves sufficient local-processing capacity once Taylor has run sustained high-density bug-control operations for long enough that it self-optimizes. This is earned through use, not a revelation moment. The audience sees it coming before Taylor names it.
- The extreme diffuse-range mode remains permanently foreclosed without external infrastructure. This is the load-bearing limitation: Taylor cannot operate at Gold-Morning scale in Westeros unless she builds or finds something that functions as a relay. Valyrian glass-candles are the in-world candidate — and this is where the arcane experimentation becomes structural, not flavor. The research into glass-candles is partly her trying to give the shard something to route through.

**Range characteristics:**
Hard-bounded sphere, radius expanding over time. Within range: full control, full proprioception, individual targeting. At range threshold: soft fraying, not a cliff — bugs at the edge are sluggish and imprecise. She knows her range precisely at all times; it is the most reliable thing she has.

**Multi-body status:**
Insects: full, from breath one. Humans: sealed at story open. Khepri-mantle unlocks mid-series as described. When it arrives, it operates at closer range than canon — she can coordinate perhaps a dozen humans at peak, not thousands. This is the degraded-but-recovered version of Khepri. Full-scale human army coordination remains out of reach without external relay infrastructure.

**Framings most enabled:**
Power-as-instrument-vs-power-as-self (the degraded-range forces intimacy — she has to be physically present, embedded, close); The Arcanist's Bargain (glass-candle research is structurally load-bearing, not cosmetic); The Organizing Story (300m radius makes her an embedded network node, not an aerial observer — she has to be in Flea Bottom, not above it); Fear as tool vs. fear as identity (what she can demonstrate with degraded power is still more than Westeros has a frame for, and she knows that gap is her leverage).

## Option B — Full-scope-but-range-capped: shard operates at near-canon ceiling from story open; Khepri-mantle always available; the constraint is purely spatial

**Story open — what is available:**
Taylor has full-spectrum insect control from breath one, including Khepri-mantle multi-body human coordination. Pain-tolerance intact. The shard is not degraded — it reseated clean. What is missing is not capability but reach: her range is hard-capped at approximately 50 meters at story open. Inside that sphere, she is operating at or near canon ceiling. Outside it, she has nothing.

**What is sealed and why:**
Range only. The shard's air-gap problem (no Entity network) manifests not as processing degradation but as signal attenuation. The shard can do everything it ever did — it just cannot reach. The 50-meter wall is the fundamental constraint of operating in isolation: the shard cannot bootstrap ambient signal from a parahuman-absent world because there is no ambient signal. It is not broadcasting into a network; it is broadcasting into silence, and the signal falls off fast.

**Recovery curve over the series:**
Range expands as the shard learns to amplify through available signal — the mechanism is environmental rather than use-based. Valyrian magic residue (specifically dragon proximity, blood-price rituals, and glass-candle scrying fields) acts as partial infrastructure. Taylor's range doubles near a dragon. It extends further inside a space where blood magic has been actively worked. This means her range map is dynamic and location-dependent: 50m default, 100m near Dragonstone's walls, 200m+ inside an active glass-candle session she is participating in. By late series, her ceiling in optimal conditions reaches ~500m; in null conditions (clean Flea Bottom alley, no magic nearby) she stays at 50m.

**Multi-body status:**
Available from story open at full capability. She can coordinate humans within range from episode one. This means the Khepri-weight is present and operational, not a reveal — it is a permanent feature of her toolkit and a permanent feature of what she is to herself.

**Framings most enabled:**
The Khepri problem (always available means always present as identity-weight, not a thing she earns or discovers — the horror of what she can do is there from scene one); The Faustian Inversion (she goes into arcane spaces and near dragons deliberately to extend range — the research motivation is self-expansion, not pure curiosity, and that is visible to an honest reader from early on); The Horror of Being the Solution (her full-capability demonstration is available early, which means the political-fear dynamic can ignite in season one and the story shifts to managing consequence rather than building capability).

## Option C — Qualitatively transformed: shard has reseeded differently; insect-control is present but Khepri-mantle is gone and replaced by something new

**Story open — what is available:**
Taylor has insect control — range approximately 200m, clean and functional, expanding slowly over time. Pain-tolerance intact. Standard surveillance and swarm capability is fully operational. What is present but new: the shard, running isolated, has begun using Westerosi magical residue as a second data channel. Taylor does not have Khepri-mantle human coordination. Instead she has partial read-through of magical states — she can sense, through insects in contact with a person, whether blood magic or Valyrian binding is active in that person's blood. Not mind-reading. Not control. Recognition: she can feel the difference between a Targaryen with active dragon-binding in their bloodline and a Targaryen without it, the way you can tell the difference between a live wire and a dead one by touch through rubber gloves.

**What is sealed and why:**
Khepri-mantle human coordination is genuinely gone, not delayed. The shard determined on reseeding that multi-body human coordination was the capability that, in the prior world, most directly enabled Taylor to become Khepri and to do Gold Morning. The shard is not sapient, but it responds to use-patterns and prior-world-data. It sealed that pathway because the isolation environment + prior-world-data creates a high-probability path to host overreach. What replaced it is the magic-residue channel — an emergent adaptation to the specific world the shard found itself in.

**Recovery curve over the series:**
No Khepri-mantle unlock. Instead, the magic-residue read-through deepens: Taylor goes from basic magical-state recognition (alive/dead, bound/unbound) in early series to fine-grained arcane analysis (what the binding costs, where it draws from, whether it is failing) by late series. This is the research-substrate function actualized: she becomes the only instrument in Westeros capable of empirically examining Valyrian blood magic from inside, because her insects are in contact with the subjects and the shard is reading the magic-residue channel directly. Range on the magic-read expands with arcane proximity, following the same logic as Option B.

**Multi-body status:**
Permanently foreclosed for humans. Insect multi-body: full, as always.

**Framings most enabled:**
The Arcanist's Bargain (the magic-read channel is literally built for this — she IS the research apparatus, not just someone using a power as one); Research ethics in a world with no research ethics (if she can read blood-magic states through insect contact, she can research people without consent in a way that has no Westerosi analog and very little Worm analog either); Fading vs. hoarded vs. taboo magic (she can see what is dying and what is being suppressed — the Citadel's suppression becomes visible to her directly).

---

## Recommendation: Option A

Option A is the right structural choice because it gives the story a genuine power arc without tying that arc to location-logistics (Option B's range-expanding-near-dragons commits the story to keeping Taylor near Valyrian infrastructure to grow) or foreclosing the Khepri identity problem (Option C removes the thing that makes Taylor most dangerous to herself).

The brief's central pressure is the Faustian Inversion and the Khepri problem — the question of what she will do when she decides she has to. That question requires Khepri-mantle to be a live possibility throughout the series, not a permanent absence. Option C's permanent foreclosure makes the story safer and more contained, which is the wrong direction for grimdark-procedural-horror.

Option B front-loads capability too aggressively. If Khepri-mantle human coordination is available in episode one, the fear-demonstration that stops the Dance is available in season one, and the story becomes about consequence management rather than capability-as-cost. That collapses the research arc: if she already has everything, the glass-candle work is curiosity, not necessity.

Option A's air-gap model earns the Khepri unlock as a use-derived threshold — not a reward, not a revelation, but an adaptation that the audience can see accumulating. It keeps Taylor physically embedded (short range forces proximity; proximity forces smallfolk relationships; smallfolk relationships are the story's moral ground). It makes the glass-candle research load-bearing: she needs external relay infrastructure to reach Gold-Morning scale, and the story can make the cost of acquiring that infrastructure visible and real. And it preserves the full Khepri-mantle as an eventual capability while making clear that reaching it requires committing to the kind of sustained, high-intensity operation that is itself a Faustian cost.

The series arc under Option A: seasons 1–2 are embedded, range-limited, the shard adapting and Taylor using what she has; Khepri-mantle unlocks mid-series as a threshold crossed through use; late series, she is working the glass-candle infrastructure problem, knowing that reaching full-Gold-Morning scale means building something in Westeros that has never existed — and the story asks whether she should.
