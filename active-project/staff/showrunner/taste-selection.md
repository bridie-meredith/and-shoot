## Story-type pick
historical-irony tragedy
reason: This shape makes the reader's foreknowledge of the Dance the engine of dramatic irony — the gap between what Taylor believes she is preventing and what the reader can already see she is producing is the mechanism by which the end-requirement (protagonist's own good-intent choices cause the bad outcome) becomes structurally legible rather than editorially delivered.

## Archetype picks
- Protagonist characterization: zealot-of-prevention — the vow to prevent another Gold Morning is the specific, reconstructible cause-chain that makes every bad act locally defensible, which is exactly the architecture the road-to-hell pattern requires.
- Foil: true innocent untouched by apocalypse-logic — the structural contrast between Taylor's escalation-trained reasoning and a Westerosi who has never needed it makes the cost of Taylor's competence visible without requiring the narrative to name it.
- False-ally: a Targaryen patron whose interests align until they don't — the alignment-phase earns Taylor's trust and the divergence-phase is what forecloses exits, which is the compounding structure the story type demands.
- True-ally: a single low-status loyalist whose loyalty is the cost-bearer — specificity of attachment (not symbolic solidarity) means the reader knows exactly who pays when Taylor's choices compound, making the cost legible rather than abstract.
- Antagonist-as-person: ultimately Taylor herself — the recognition scene that is the climax of historical-irony tragedy requires the antagonist-as-person to be the same agent whose good intent drove the plot; displacing it onto a Targaryen claimant weakens the causal chain the theme requires.
- Antagonist-as-institution: the inevitability of the Dance — this is the structural system Taylor is trying to prevent and thereby precipitates; reader foreknowledge makes it load-bearing, not decorative.
- Victim-by-which-cost-is-paid: a named innocent in Taylor's care — named and in-care specificity ensures the cost is personal and reconstructible, not diffuse realm-scale abstraction that lets the reader evade the accounting.
- Witness: a maester-chronicler — a chronicler survives the act, frames its meaning in-world, and closes the dramatic-irony loop: the reader has been ahead of Taylor; the chronicler now confirms, in-world, what the reader already knew.
- Opposite-number: a true-believer who acts badly for good reasons and is proven right — this figure makes Taylor's failure a choice, not a fate, because the structure now contains a mirror who ran the same logic and got the opposite result; the gap between them is where the theme lives.

## Notes
The protagonist-as-antagonist pick creates tension with the false-ally role: if Taylor is her own antagonist by the climax, the Targaryen patron's betrayal must land early enough that it is a compounding factor, not the climax itself — otherwise the two roles compete for the recognition beat. The pipeline may want to fix the false-ally's betrayal as a mid-structure turn, not the ending. Advisory only.
