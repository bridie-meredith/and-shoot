**Comprehensibility: PASS.** The SVO bones are legible throughout. Who does what to whom chains correctly. The officer-arrives-surveys-enters-records-departs spine is readable without prose.

**Primary complaints by window:**

- W11: Ten-line gap mid-confrontation makes the central administrative exchange a black box. Board state after this window cannot be determined from SVO alone. Two "speaks" events with unknown content and a chin-lift closing beat. Needs at minimum one action-verb between the speech exchanges to show what moved — officer makes a notation, or produces a document, or Taylor reaches for something, or the septon stirs again. Right now it is two people talking and then one person lifting their chin.

- W14: Post-departure decompression window with no parting complication. The septon closing his eyes is the last thing that happens before Taylor moves to the window — this should be a board event (is he dying? has he just surrendered to something?) but without an action verb that registers as a consequence, it reads as scene wind-down. A single beat here where Taylor reacts to the septon's closed eyes with something consequential — taking something from his hand, noticing something the officer left behind, seeing the scroll's impression still on the floor — would convert this from decompression to a carry-forward hook.

- W15: The chapter closes on nine beats of transit and kneeling. No clock is started. No new threat is named. The chapter goal was "the machine notices her" — the machine has noticed, but the chapter closes before we see Taylor reckon with what that means in actionable terms. A single board-change beat at the close — the riders did not just record a name, they left something behind (a copy of the notation, a date for the next visit, a mark on the gate) — would convert this from cool-down to chapter-close hook.

**Consecutive TOLERATED note:** W1-W2-W3 are three consecutive TOLERATED windows, clearing the chapter's arrival sequence with no board-live event until W4. The setup toll here is two windows too long. The gap at IDs 17-29 (between W2 and W3) suggests prose content is already there; if that prose delivers the riders' dust on the horizon or the first distant sound, the SVO gap does not represent a pacing problem at the prose level. If those IDs are genuinely absent proto-lines, the pre-arrival section is structurally overlong and the riders should be visible at the end of W2.
