## Fault diagnosis

### Primary fault: Chapter 03 — the chapter fails to orient the reader

**Location:** W24 (ch03:1–10), W25 (ch03:11–21), W26 (ch03:22–33)

**What went wrong:** Chapter 03's stated goal is "something has closed upstream without Taylor knowing it — she senses the disturbance but cannot reach what caused it." This is a valid narrative premise. But the chapter has not established for the reader what Taylor is trying to reach, why she is trying to reach it, or what the upstream closure is. The reader only has:
- She climbs the bell tower (W24)
- The raven lifts and fails (W24, repeated at W25 twice more)
- She nosebleeds twice (W25)
- She goes to the sept and does nothing (W26)

The chapter has omitted the orientation layer: what is the information channel, what does the upstream node represent, and what is the closing event that has severed the channel? Without that layer, the mechanical failure-state (can't reach) reads as technical difficulty, not as stakes.

**In terms of the worm-canon-pedant's pattern flags:** "The fauna network becoming visible must be shown as a coherence signal" — Chapter 03 inverts this correctly (the network is being shown failing), but the pattern flag assumes the reader knows which node has gone quiet. If the reader cannot identify the upstream node from prior context, the chapter is legible as physical-cost drama but not as the "closed upstream" beat the chapter goal describes.

**Impact:** Three windows of below-threshold entertainment. The BORED window (W26) is the hard stop.

### Secondary fault: Chapter 03 — the BORED window contains zero functional content

**W26 specific:** Loft boards creak. A cart passes the sept. She crosses the yard, enters the sept, kneels, rises, exits. None of these actions changes the situation. No new information enters. No cost escalates. No enemy action occurs. This is a literal marking-time window with no justification for its existence.

**Prescriptive fix:** The creak and cart are the only observable external events in this window. The chapter could use the cart (a specific cart — someone's cart, carrying something relevant) to deliver information that she processes through the fauna net, rather than having the cart pass unmarked. Alternatively the window could be cut and replaced with a direct transition to the septon-contact beat (W27), which at least has human texture.

### Secondary observation: Chapter 08 compression (W54)

**Status:** TOLERATED, not BORED. Not a cap violation. But the pattern of large mid-chapter blanks (27–60, 64–88) means the chapter's stated "board-worsening close" is understaged. The raven striking the beam (line 96) is a strong closing image, but it arrives after compression that has reduced reader engagement. If this chapter is later expanded (prose pass), the compression zones should be explicitly populated rather than left as time-skips.

**Worm-canon-pedant specific concern:** The chancel-steps stop at line 63 is underdetermined. Taylor stops at the chancel steps and then the scene cuts. In Worm terms: she has reached a decision point and the text does not show what she finds. This could be intentional (she finds nothing, which is information) but the proto-line does not signal which it is. At the current skeleton level this is a flagged ambiguity, not a fault.

### Note: Chapter 01, W15 — minor timeline ambiguity

The concurrent-event sequence at the end of Chapter 01 (lines 132–140) reads as structurally ambiguous: the officer is finishing with the septon inside while the woman is speaking to Taylor outside and both events appear to be in the same window. If this is a deliberate parallel-cut structure, it needs a staging cue in the proto-line (e.g., a blank separator between the two tracks). If the woman and the final officer actions are sequential, the sequencing implies Taylor was inside with the septon when the woman arrived and then exited — but the prior lines show her kneeling in the sept at line 130. The continuity gap is small but present.
