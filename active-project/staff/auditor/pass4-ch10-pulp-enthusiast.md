### Specific Problems

**W1 (entries 51–55): Dead opening.**
The chapter opens with two blank/stub entries and three approach-choreography lines. No threat is present, the board is not live, and the characters are just walking. This persona's fatigue triggers on arrival scenes before the complication is present. The chapter's opening five lines have no board-activation event.

Recommendation: Cut entries 51 and 52 (they appear to be ordering artifacts, not content). Reorder so the first substantive line opens inside the hall with Celtigar's confrontation already live — or open on Taylor's approach with the crown agent's presence already known as the reason the approach is tense.

**W12 (entries 47–50): Dead close.**
Three body-language aftermath beats with no forward hook. The chapter closes on Taylor's private release after the administrative act is complete. This is the correct emotional content but the wrong escalation shape for this persona. The season-end chapter must leave the board in a state that makes Season 2 necessary — not a mood landing.

Recommendation: Replace at least one of the three body-language beats with a forward-facing board state. Either: Taylor's internal acknowledgment of what the ward entry means as a tactical instrument she can deploy (reframe closure as new leverage), or a late environmental beat that names an approaching threat (the network she now has access to, the crown's continued attention, something that does not let the board go quiet). The chapter can close — it just cannot close flat.

**W10 (entry 37): Comprehensibility flag.**
"Taylor holds the feet" does not parse from SVO bones. If this is a body-language action (gripping the feet of a stool? holding herself still from the feet up?), the SVO form is not self-explaining. Flag for clarification at prose stage — either rename the action or add an object that makes the image legible (e.g., "taylor holds the stool-feet" or "taylor holds the floor with the feet").

**W2–W3: Slow assembly.**
Two consecutive TOLERATED windows before the first ENGAGED is a setup-tax problem. The chapter spends two windows assembling characters and activating the confrontation. This is structurally necessary given the chapter's goal, but the bones as written do not compress the assembly. If W1 is restructured to open inside the confrontation, W2 and W3 would each be re-evaluated — the assembly windows would become part of a live-board scene rather than a pre-board arrival sequence.

### What Lands

W4 through W11 (minus W10's partial comprehension issue) is a strong run. The complication-density structure of the three-party administrative confrontation, the scroll's appearance, the writing of the entry, and the progressive room-clearing is exactly the escalation-toward-irreversibility geometry this persona engages with. The chapter's middle is working. The problems are structural: the opening does not start on a beat, and the close does not leave a hook.
