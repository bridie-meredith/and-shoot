# Season S01 — Dark Fantasy Reader Trim Review

## Persona stance

Patient. Has been burned by false darkness. Reads for consequence that propagates, for enemies who don't stop, for a world that was there before the protagonist. Rejects violence without aftermath, hope that costs nothing, and any moment where the world bends to protagonist convenience. Will tolerate one chapter that merely marks time. Two marks REVISE. Any BORED marks REVISE.

---

## Per-chapter verdicts

### Chapter 01 — Census at the Sept

**ENGAGED**

The machine arrives before she's ready. The septon falls mid-scene and she cannot stop the notation. Plumm's man doesn't wait; the riders don't pause; the scroll gets filled and rolled and taken away. Every line of bureaucratic procedure here is actual threat — the census officer is not a villain doing villain things, he is a system that does not need to care about her to destroy her. The proto-line sequence from line 44 through line 113 (riders arrive, enter, record, depart) is the dark fantasy engine running clean: institutional violence, no aftermath, the protagonist survives by doing nothing dramatic. The raven resettling at line 122 after she's already absorbed the cost is the chapter earning its keep.

---

### Chapter 02 — Plumm's Man in the Field

**ENGAGED**

The gap between what Taylor thinks she controls and what she has made visible is the chapter's entire engine, and it works. The oc-plumms-man's ledger entries land as physical facts — each notation is irreversible from the moment the stylus lifts. The physical cost accumulation (nosebleed, lines 52–53) arrives mid-surveillance, not at the end, which is correct: the cost doesn't pause for narrative convenience. The chapter's final image — fauna dispersed, ledger set, no event to point at — is exactly right for a dark fantasy readership. What has happened cannot be unmade. No body to count, no wound to dress, and that's worse.

---

### Chapter 03 — The Report Upstream (Taylor POV)

**TOLERATED**

The chapter's conceit is legitimate: she senses disturbance she cannot reach. The problem is the conceit exhausts itself quickly (line 5, the first raven lifts; line 47, she drives a raven toward the road and it drops) and then repeats. The structure is: hold, bleed, raven-fails, hold, bleed, raven-fails, hold, bleed. The prose bones show this clearly — the chapter is 47 lines and most of them are iterations of the same sensation. The physical cost register is appropriate but there's no escalation of the helplessness; she ends where she starts, slightly bloodier. What rescues it from BORED is the chapter's function in the sequence: the fact that the report travels faster than she can track is a genuine dark fantasy beat. But the chapter is thinner than it needs to be at the skeleton level. Would skim.

### Chapter 03-Interlude — Plumm's Records Clerk

**ENGAGED**

Short and tight. Ser-harwick-plumm trying to retrieve the ledger (lines 14–26) and being stopped by the records clerk — this is the dark fantasy machine operating at its sharpest. The clerk's refusal is not malicious; it is procedural. Plumm cannot get the record back not because someone opposes him but because the record is now property of the institution. The fact that it ends with oc-plumms-man walking the road — not triumph, not defeat, just continuation — is correct. The report lives upstream now. Nothing to be done.

### Chapter 04 — The Witnessed Anomaly (Castellan and Inspector Visit)

**ENGAGED**

The chapter that establishes the raven-on-arm moment (lines 52–57: raven descends, perches Taylor, castellan steps back, Plumm grips the stylus). This is the first time the world is actually frightened of her, and the chapter earns it by having the fear expressed in procedural language — the castellan doesn't scream, he retreats; Plumm doesn't draw steel, he marks the page. The fear registers as the right flavor: superstition, not curiosity. The inspectors do not adapt; they document and leave. Westerosi reaction to something they cannot name — they process it through their administrative structure, which is exactly how this world would respond. The raven launching at line 84 while the castellan is still facing the bell tower is a clean horror beat. Strong chapter.

### Chapter 05 (Interlude) — Rowan's Intercession

**ENGAGED**

The chapter's structure is the dark fantasy trap perfectly executed: good faith, worse outcome. Rowan walks to Plumm to shrink the target and instead Plumm writes two names where there was one (lines 47–62). The scene with the record book is the heart — each time Plumm lifts the stylus it's another irreversible fact. The ending (lines 72–88: Rowan stops, turns, returns to the sept, lowers the eyes, drops the eyes) is the chapter paying the bill. He knows what he did and the proto-lines show it without naming it. No false comfort. The protection has made the thing more visible and Rowan understands this before he speaks again.

### Chapter 06 — The Succession Clock (Taylor Fights the Machine)

**ENGAGED**

The raven-harassment sequence (lines 59–105) is the chapter's engine and it earns its length. Taylor disrupting couriers through controlled fauna movement, the cost accumulating in real time (nosebleed at line 82, drops at line 103), the second courier exiting before she can recover — this is action that feels dangerous because the protagonist is physically limited and the machine keeps generating new vectors. The chapter's structure also pays off the season's first active-resistance beat cleanly: she fights, she uses her network, and then she drops and presses her hands to the ground. That's the bill. The death of the septon (lines 1–13) opening the chapter before the active sequence is correct placement — loss first, then action, then physical cost.

### Chapter 07 — The Refusal Logged

**TOLERATED**

The chapter's function is clear and correct: the cover is removed in real time. Rowan's claim stamped refused, Plumm's counter-claim entered, Taylor exiting through the postern gate as this happens without her knowledge. The structural sequence works. What makes this TOLERATED rather than ENGAGED is the middle of the chapter — lines 37–71 — which is mostly Taylor processing the outcome. She crosses the approach road, stops, faces Rowan, takes the chair, exhales, flips the document, grips the wall, kneels in the sept, rises, grips the doorframe. The dark fantasy reader can absorb one chapter of aftermath processing, but this sequence has no escalation — she is digesting rather than moving. The final image (Plumm takes the document, line 93) is the right note to end on, but it arrives after too much interior cycling for a reader who reads for consequence propagation.

## Required cuts / recasts

### Chapter 03 — The Report Upstream

**Problem:** The conceit (helplessness, disturbance she cannot reach) is valid but the chapter cycles through the same sensation three times without escalation. Hold, bleed, fail, hold, bleed, fail. The physical cost accumulates but the helplessness does not deepen — she ends the chapter in the same epistemic state she started it, and the reader registers this.

**Required recast:** The chapter needs at least one beat of escalation that changes the texture of the helplessness. Options: (a) a second attempt at a different fauna vector that fails for a different reason — the cost curve shifts, she tries something she hasn't tried before and it costs more than she expected; (b) a concrete sensory intrusion that arrives via the fauna network and tells her something wrong without telling her what — not disturbance, but data that implies something specific and bad; (c) compression — cut the iteration count from three cycles to two, then use the recovered space for a single beat where she tries to push further and the physical cost forces a complete stop, not just a bleed. The chapter must escalate from "I sense something wrong" to "I know something has moved and I cannot reach it" — those two states feel the same in the current skeleton, which is the problem.

### Chapter 07 — The Refusal Logged

**Problem:** The chapter's middle section (lines 37–71) is aftermath processing without escalation. Taylor crosses the road, stops, faces Rowan, takes the chair, exhales, flips the document, grips the wall, kneels, rises, grips the doorframe. These are interior cycling actions. The dark fantasy reader will absorb them once but this sequence takes too long to arrive at the chapter's final image.

**Required recast:** The middle of the chapter needs at least one beat that compounds the state rather than digests it. Options: (a) an additional institutional event she observes (or hears, or senses via fauna) during this period — something that tells her the machine has already moved again while she is still processing the first move; (b) compression of the cycling section and insertion of a concrete new pressure, even minor, that arrives before she can finish absorbing the refusal; (c) the document-flip (line 63) should carry new information she did not know — if she reads something in that document that changes the stakes, the chapter pulls forward instead of marking time. The final image (Plumm takes the document, line 93) is correct; the approach to it needs one additional beat of escalation.
