## Seam-1 — Mira @6 "her gaze tracks the yard before her mouth opens"

**Seam-class: MODERATE**

**Attack vector:** The Phase 2 audit defends "tracks" as a "direct body-action verb (gaze movement), not a feeling-name." The Phase 2 mira fork characterizes it as an enactment of §Look "Eyes that move slightly faster than her face does." But there is a structural form question the defense does not fully close: the rubric's AP16 bans "original-figure metaphor" that names the feeling through a figure. "Tracks" is a working-language verb commonly applied to gaze — "her eyes tracked the movement," "his gaze tracked across the room." The edric audit at @57 worried about "finds" on the same axis and concluded it was a biomechanical-locator. The question is whether "tracks" passes the same test. "Track" has a hunting-and-pursuit etymology and carries a directionality-with-purpose connotation that "scan" or "move across" does not. The Phase 2 audit does not defend on this axis at all: it dismisses the form question in a single sentence ("'Tracks' is a body-action verb (gaze movement), not a feeling-name") without checking whether "tracks" is a working-language idiom versus a figure. A hostile reading can argue: "tracks" imports a predator-register (hunter tracking prey) that is figurative-naming through working-language-adjacent idiom — the feel of purposeful pursuit — which AP13 (idiomatic naming through metaphor) could catch if the form-committee is looking for it. The pulp-enthusiast flagged VOICE-MIXED on this exact word and said "the verb 'tracks' is doing signature-carrying work," which is an implicit acknowledgment that "tracks" is doing more than literal gaze description.

Beyond the AP13/AP16 surface: the Phase 2 audit's Q2 defense rests on the claim that the fire establishes mira's signature "at the scene's first-impression beat." But the corpus puts mira's introduction as @5/@6/@7 — she sets the bucket, straightens, speaks. The fire is at @6, between a neutral action and a speech act. The scene's structural weight is at @7 (mira speaks to the yard) and at @11+ (officer arrival). Firing at @6, where the tens is 1 and no confrontation structure has arrived, means the Q2 defense leans entirely on "character establishment" — which the rubric's functional-register catalog covers under "painting-characterization" but which requires two of four functional-register hits. The fire claims two (painting-characterization + social-commentary). An adversarial reading presses the social-commentary claim: "smallfolk woman performs positional-read reflex without announcing it, against §'she is not advertising' Default Stance." This is interpretation-heavy — where is the social commentary in "her gaze tracks the yard"? Social commentary in the rubric's sense requires the audience to receive a comment on the social world, not just a character doing a characteristic thing. The defense must distinguish painting-characterization (she is this kind of person) from social-commentary (her being this kind of person says something about the world she lives in). The fire conflates the two.

**What the writer-fork must defend:** (1) That "tracks" is working-language-literal, not idiomatic-predator-register, and that it passes the AP13 test. The defense cannot rest on "it's a body-action verb" — it must address the directionality-with-purpose connotation and distinguish it from AP13. (2) That the social-commentary functional-register hit is substantively distinct from painting-characterization — i.e., that the fire's somatic-tell carries a comment about mira's world, not only about mira's personality. If the defense can only name one of the four functional registers with precision, the fire falls to 1/4, and with painting-characterization + painting-characterization-under-another-name the multi-justification slot 5 fails the ≥2-of-4 gate.

**Defense outcome:** If "tracks" passes the AP13/AP16 surface and social-commentary is substantiated as distinct from painting-characterization → DEFEND as-is. If "tracks" carries idiomatic-register risk → REVISE to a less-loaded gaze verb that still carries the "eyes faster than face" signature. If social-commentary fails to be distinguished from painting-characterization → REVISE the functional-register claim to name a cleaner second hit (e.g., realization-register: the audience realizes at the character's introduction that the space-read is structural, not casual).

## Seam-2 — Taylor @39 "her shoulders go down and back"

**Seam-class: STRONG**

**Attack vector:** The AP6 non-redundancy defense is the most aggressively defended claim in the entire Phase 2 output. It is defended twice — in the fork, in the aggregate audit — and the defense is precise: NI @39 fires at the feet-locus (cognition-class), the feeling-flag fires at the shoulder-locus (somatic-show-class), and the rubric calls these "distinct jobs." The two-sentence summary at Phase 2 audit §AP6 states: "NI = registration of cognitive state; feeling-flag = show of physical discipline. These are non-overlapping interior-layers." This is where the strongest seam lives.

The attack is not about the loci being different body parts — that is true. The attack is about whether feet and shoulders, at @39, are delivering the same interior to the audience or genuinely different interior. NI @39 says "the feet are set where the calculation had set them; what arrives next is his to spend." This is not only feet-description — it is a description of the completed calculation and the assignment of the officer's next cost. That is fully cognitive. The feeling-flag says "her shoulders go down and back," which the fork calls "the trained pre-action discipline showing through the Westeros mask." The fork claims this delivers "the body executing the trained-down-and-back shoulder-set, which is the cape-trained pre-action discipline signaling calibration-not-fear." But the audience, reading both NI @39 and the feeling-flag together, receives: her calculation is complete (NI) + her trained body is executing refusal-posture (FF). The question is whether "calculation complete → body executing" is one interior-layer or two. An adversarial reading argues it is one: the NI already tells the audience the calculation is done and the officer will spend the cost; the feeling-flag tells the audience the body is executing what the calculation decided. These are sequential-stages of the same interior event, not non-overlapping layers. The rubric's AP6 test is whether the audience would receive "the same interior from each" — and the defense answers "no, because the jobs are cognitively vs somatically distinct." But the AP6 test is about interior-content, not about anatomical-loci or cognitive-vs-somatic class. If the audience already knows "she has decided and is holding her position," the shoulder-set adds emphasis, not new interior.

A second angle: the calibration anchor C4 is load-bearing for the Q2 defense ("episode's structural refusal pivot"). But C4 was pre-loaded into the rubric before Phase 2, meaning the writer-fork that fired @39 was firing toward a target it had already been told was correct. The corpus said "body-commit; distinguishes from NI's cost-tracking on adjacent beats; episode's structural refusal pivot" — so the fork's Q2 defense is partially circular: it cites corpus C4 as proof of Q2, and corpus C4 was authored partly as a prompt to fire @39. An adversarial reader asks whether C4 pre-licenses the fire or validates it. If C4 is treated as a calibration anchor rather than a Q2 proof, the Q2 defense must stand on its own without the corpus cite.

A third angle (mild, raised by pulp-enthusiast): the tell is sourced directly from the base card's §Non-verbal tics verbatim ("shoulders go down and back"). The dialect audience cleared this as a virtue ("direct vocabulary lift"). But the rubric says the somatic-tell should be authored, not lifted. The rubric says "matches the character's persona-card §Voice / §Signature Moves / §Look vocabulary" — vocabulary match, not verbatim-copy. A verbatim lift from the card into the facet entry means the stitch will produce a sentence that is, word-for-word, already in the character's documentation. The question is whether this constitutes vocabulary-match (rubric-compliant) or template-substitution (authoring-flat). This is a soft concern, not a formal AP violation, but Phase 4 should address it.

**What the writer-fork must defend:** (1) That NI @39 and the feeling-flag at @39 deliver non-overlapping interior — not just that they use different somatic loci or different functional labels (cognition vs somatic), but that the audience receives a distinct interior-content from each that neither provides alone. The defense must show that "calculation complete; cost delegated to him" (NI) and "body executing trained-discipline-not-instinct-fear" (FF) are genuinely separable audience-perceptions, not sequential description of a single event. (2) That C4 operates as validation rather than pre-licensing — i.e., that the Q2 defense can survive without the corpus cite. (3) Optionally, that the verbatim-card-lift in the somatic-tell is vocabulary-match rather than authoring-flatness — or acknowledge this as a Phase 5 stitcher caveat.

**Defense outcome:** If the non-overlapping interior case is made at the content level (not just at the anatomical-loci or functional-label level) → DEFEND as-is. If the defense rests only on "shoulder ≠ feet" → the AP6 seam is unresolved and the entry should be REVISED to use a tell whose content more cleanly separates from NI's interior. A revised tell might use a different somatic register entirely (breath-control, hand-position, eye-line) to put more distance between the feeling-flag's interior-claim and NI's cognition-content.

## Seam-3 — Edric @57 "his hands settle at his sides and his weight finds the back foot before the step"

**Seam-class: STRONG**

**Attack vector:** The Phase 2 audit and fork both flag "finds" as "the closest form question in the file" and both defend it as "biomechanical locator." The AP16 defense in the fork is: "finds = literal weight-shift locator." Flag-002 in the Phase 2 audit names this as the primary Phase 3 seam. The adversarial press is this: AP16 bans "original-figure metaphor" — figures that picture the feeling through a non-idiomatic original construction. The defense distinguishes this from working-language usage. But "his weight finds the back foot" is not working-language for weight-distribution in the way "his weight shifted to the back foot" is. "Shifts," "moves," "settles," "presses" are the standard biomechanical vocabulary for weight-distribution. "Finds" is the standard narrative vocabulary for searching and locating — it imports the semantic register of search-and-discovery into a description of body-weight. In "his weight finds the back foot before the step," "finds" makes the weight an agent doing the searching, and the back foot the object being discovered. This is exactly the AP16 pattern: an original figure that attributes agency to a physical-mass transfer, making the weight-shift feel like a seeking rather than a mechanical process. The fact that no feeling is explicitly named does not rescue the figure — AP16 bans the figure itself, not the named feeling. The fork's defense says "finds = literal weight-shift locator" but does not explain why "finds" is literal rather than figurative when applied to weight. The auditor in Phase 2 noted it passes "because the biomechanical reading is primary and no feeling is named through the figure" — but AP16 does not require the feeling to be named; it requires the figure to not be deployed for feeling-show. The weight-as-agent-finding-destination is a figure regardless of whether a named feeling appears.

The seam on form competes with the seam on §Form structure: the rubric's content shape calls for "subject + verb + (object/locator)" and specifies "one clause." The entry has two clauses joined by "and": "his hands settle at his sides" and "his weight finds the back foot before the step." The Phase 2 dialect audience (pulp-enthusiast, VOICE-MIXED) flagged this as a two-clause tell with pacing cost and offered that either clause is sufficient alone. The rubric's form discipline says "somatic-tell-one-clause" — does the "and"-joined construction violate this? The aggregate audit did not flag this. The mechanic-auditor in Phase 2 did not flag this. But the rubric's form requirement is "somatic-tell-one-clause" in the content-shape specification. Two independent clauses joined by "and" is not one clause; it is two clauses. If form discipline is hard, the entry is in technical form violation regardless of the somatic-tell content.

Additionally: the Q2 defense rests on the scene's "witness-or-not pivot for Edric." The corpus confirms this at @57. But the adversarial press on Q2 is: the proto-line @57 "edric steps back through the door" is itself structural — the act is the pivot. The feeling-flag is supposed to license interior the proto-line cannot carry alone. If the proto-line already carries "the pivot" (the retreat IS the scene's pivot; the audience reads "he retreated" as the pivot-event), the feeling-flag is adding render-weight to a beat that already has structural weight, which is different from adding interior that would otherwise be invisible. The Q2 gate asks "is the interior meaningful enough to render?" — the interior here is "his body is executing his exit-check calculation." The audience who reads @57's proto-line knows he retreated. The audience who reads the feeling-flag additionally knows that his hands settled and his weight found the back foot. The question is whether "how his body executes the retreat" is interior-meaningful or just the prose-texture of the retreat already on the page. This is a soft Q2 pressure, not a hard one, but it should be addressed.

**What the writer-fork must defend:** (1) That "finds" is biomechanical-working-language and not an original figure — specifically, that weight-as-agent-finding-destination is a literal description of weight distribution and not a figure attributing agency to physical mass. The defense must do this at the word-register level, not at the "no feeling is named" level. The strongest defense is to cite working-language uses of "finds" for weight-distribution in physical description (e.g., "the foot finds its purchase," "the weight finds the ground") as established-idiom rather than original-figure. If this defense cannot be made, the verb should be revised to a non-figure biomechanical verb. (2) That the two-clause construction is permissible under the form rule — either by arguing that two clauses joined by "and" constitute one compound somatic-tell (the rubric's "one clause" is a shape guideline, not a hard count), or by acknowledging the two-clause concern and invoking the stitcher-option (pulp-enthusiast's note: either clause works alone). If the form rule is hard, one clause must be selected. (3) That the feeling-flag's interior-content is distinct from the proto-line's structural content — i.e., that "the cost-of-the-act" is genuinely interior and not a re-description of the act already visible in the proto-line.

**Defense outcome:** If "finds" is successfully grounded as working-language and the two-clause form is permissible → DEFEND as-is. If "finds" carries AP16 risk → REVISE the verb to a literal weight-distribution verb ("his weight settles to the back foot," "his weight draws to the back foot before the step"). If the two-clause form is a hard violation → REVISE to one clause (the "weight/back-foot" clause is the stronger structural carrier; the hands-settle clause, while card-faithful, can be held for a later episode's first-on-page fire of the hands-awareness signature).

## Curve Seam

**Seam-class: MODERATE**

**Attack vector:** The Phase 2 aggregate reports tens distribution as t=1 (@6), t=3 (@39), t=2 (@57) — presented as a meaningful distribution across "quiet-beat, peak-beat, mid-curve." The rubric's tens-independence rule says feeling-flags do NOT gate on tensometer ≥2, and the audit presents the three-rung distribution as evidence of non-peak-monoculture. But the adversarial press is: the fact that the three fires hit three different tens values (1, 2, 3) is a clean match for the rubric's expected distribution language — almost too clean. The rubric says "expected file-shape distributes fires across t=1, t=2, t=3 per the natural distribution of somatic-show in the episode." Three fires and three tens rungs hitting one each is minimum-compliance with the letter of the rubric but not necessarily with its spirit, which is tens-independence, meaning fires should be where the somatic-show is warranted independent of tens. The pattern "one fire at each tens level" could be produced by a writer that started from "I need one quiet, one peak, one mid" rather than from somatic-show necessity. The Phase 2 audit does not address this; it simply reports the distribution as passing.

The stronger curve pressure is on the file's overall sparsity: 3 fires on 77 beats = 3.9%, the lower-middle of the 2-5% band. The Phase 2 audit concludes "no hard SKIP-MISSED; two borderline cases defensible under default-to-silence." But the file-shape observation is that Scene 4 is entirely unfired (0 of 4 beats Taylor walks through the threshold — @70, @71, @72, @73, @74, @75, @76, @77 — produce 0 feeling-flags). The threshold-cross sequence is the episode's emotional terminus. Taylor walks from yard to sept interior, carrying the letter that has just been marked parallel-margin, past the scene of Osmynd's absence. The rubric names "threshold-cross before-the-act" as an expected quiet-beat feeling-fire class. Zero fires in Scene 4 is not a cap violation — the cap was available — and it is not a sparsity violation — the file is within band. But Scene 4's emotional terminus being entirely silent at the feeling-flag level is a file-shape pressure that should be named explicitly.

**What the writer-fork must defend:** That the three-rung distribution is tens-independent (fires are where the somatic-show is warranted, not where the rubric's distribution example says to put one), and that Scene 4's complete silence is a correct conservative reading of default-to-silence rather than a structural omission. The Taylor fork's @73 refusal on AP6 grounds is the load-bearing defense for Scene 4 silence; it must hold under the adversarial test that NI @73 is environmental-cognitive, not somatic-show, and therefore cannot block a feeling-flag at a distinct somatic locus.

**Defense outcome:** If tens-independence is genuinely held (fires are not placed to satisfy the rubric's distribution example) → DEFEND. If Scene 4 silence stands under the @73 AP6 re-examination → DEFEND. If @73 refusal fails → see SKIP-MISSED section.

## Cross-Facet Seam

**Seam-class: STRONG**

**Attack vector:** The AP6 contract for POV is the cross-facet contract that matters most, and the Phase 2 defense of @39 is the load-bearing AP6 claim (addressed above in Seam-2). But there is a second cross-facet seam that Phase 2 did not examine: the state-updates file at @57 writes two entries — `studio.actors_in_yard: officer+taylor+mira+edric -> officer+taylor+mira` and `actor:edric-cray.sublocation: yard (near sept door) -> sept interior (past threshold)`. The feeling-flag at @57 fires on Edric's interior-of-the-exit. The state-updates rubric says "mood / register / voice-tone are NOT canonical state per state-updates rubric. Feeling-flag is selection-signal only." But the state-updates entries at @57 do not write mood or voice-tone — they write location. The feeling-flag at @57 is writing the interior-cost of the location-change that state-updates records. These are licensed as distinct: state-updates = canonical-state-write; feeling-flag = selection-signal for interior. The Phase 2 audit confirms the separation.

The seam is more subtle: state-updates @57 entry 9 records `actor:edric-cray.sublocation: yard (near sept door) -> sept interior (past threshold)`. This is a location-state canonical write. Entry 8 records `studio.actors_in_yard: officer+taylor+mira+edric -> officer+taylor+mira`. Both are phase-of-action writes for Edric's exit. The feeling-flag at @57 licenses "the interior cost of the act." But the state-updates writes already tell the stitch-time reader that Edric exited — and the cross-facet contract note for state-updates says state-updates entries "are load-bearing for canonical memory and should render at high weight." If the stitcher renders both the state-updates @57 writes AND the feeling-flag @57 at high weight, the stitch produces a sequence that covers Edric's exit three times: (1) proto-line @57 "edric steps back through the door" [act], (2) state-updates @57 [canonical writes], (3) feeling-flag @57 [interior of the act]. The question is whether this triple-coverage of the same beat creates a render-density problem at stitch time that the feeling-flag's Q1 defense does not account for. Q1 asks whether the audience can read the interior from proto-line + cross-facets alone; the Phase 2 audit says no — the interior is not in state-updates because state-updates doesn't write mood/register. That is correct. But the stitch-time render-weight problem is not a Q1 problem; it is a stitcher-instruction problem. The feeling-flag at @57 does not have any render-weight caveat; it fires clean. When the stitcher hits @57 with proto-line + state-updates (two entries, high-weight signal) + feeling-flag, the beat could over-render.

**What the writer-fork must defend:** That the feeling-flag at @57 adds interior not present in the state-updates writes or the proto-line, and that the stitcher's triple-coverage of @57 is manageable — either because the facets are read as complementary (proto-line = act, state-updates = canonical registration, feeling-flag = interior) rather than as redundant, or because the feeling-flag carries a stitcher-instruction note that it should integrate with state-updates @57 rather than layer on top of it.

**Defense outcome:** Q1 holds by contract (state-updates does not write mood). The render-weight issue is a stitcher-instruction concern, not a firing-decision concern. → DEFEND the fire; add a cross-facet note for the stitcher at @57 flagging the triple-coverage and specifying that feeling-flag @57 integrates (not stacks) with state-updates @57 entries 8 and 9.

## Q1-Interior-Integrity Seam

**Seam-class: MODERATE**

**Attack on Mira @6:** Mira is non-POV. No NI fires at @6 (NI is POV-only and Taylor is not on stage in Scene 1). No sensory fires at @6. No state-updates at @6. The Phase 2 audit uses the absence of cross-facet corroboration as a strength ("no cross-facet source already carries the interior"). The adversarial press is the opposite: the absence of any cross-facet corroboration means the Q1 defense rests entirely on the claim that "the audience cannot read the positional-read reflex from proto-lines @5/@6/@7 alone." The three proto-lines are: "mira sets the bucket" / "mira straightens" / "mira speaks to the yard." The Phase 2 audit argues that the audience reads "a woman who straightened and then spoke" but not "the assessment-pause that precedes mira's speech." The seam: is this correct? The sequence bucket → straighten → speak is a very ordinary narrative sequence. The specific question is whether a reader who has mira's persona card loaded (as the stitcher and downstream consumers do) would read the assessment-pause from these three proto-lines without the feeling-flag. The rubric is clear that Q1 is evaluated from the audience's perspective, not from the card-holder's perspective. An audience member reading the stitched prose (not the proto-lines) will read: mira set the bucket, straightened, and then spoke to the yard. Without the feeling-flag, nothing in those three actions marks the assessment-beat as distinct from any other set-bucket/straighten/speak sequence. Q1 holds: the pause is not in the actions. The seam closes.

The Q1-integrity challenge for mira @6 that does have teeth: the proto-line @7 "mira speaks to the yard" is what comes after the feeling-flag's somatic-tell ("before her mouth opens"). The feeling-flag fires at @6 and its temporal-clause references @7 implicitly. If the stitcher renders the feeling-flag at @6 and then renders @7's speech act, the audience receives the assessment-pause as a bridge between @6 and @7 — which is correct. But if the stitcher renders @7 before or alongside the feeling-flag @6 content, the sequence breaks. The feeling-flag has no render-sequencing instruction. This is a stitcher-instruction gap, not a Q1 problem. Worth noting for Phase 5.

**Attack on Edric @57:** State-updates @57 writes edric's sublocation change. The Q2 defense says "the cost-of-the-act" is interior and not in state-updates. The Q1-interior-integrity press: does the proto-line @57 + state-updates @57 combination, together, convey the cost-of-the-act to the audience? Proto-line: "edric steps back through the door." State-updates: sublocation change + yard-composition change. These two tell the audience "he left" at the canonical and action levels. The cost-of-the-act — specifically that the body is executing the calculation rather than a fear-response, and that the hands-awareness and weight-find are the somatic register of his specific exit-check signature — is not in either. Q1 holds. State-updates is categorical and canonical; it does not carry the quality of the act.

**Defense outcome for both:** Q1 holds on inspection. Mira @6 should add a stitcher-sequencing note (render feeling-flag before @7 speech). Edric @57 should add the stitcher-integration note described in the cross-facet seam. Both are Phase 5 stitcher-instruction caveats, not Q1 revisions.

## Vocabulary Saturation Seam (AP7)

**Seam-class: THIN**

**Attack vector:** Three fires, three anatomical registers: gaze/eyes (mira), torso/shoulders (taylor), hands/weight-foot (edric). No shared verb across entries. The Phase 2 audit confirms this explicitly and the dialect audience confirms it. The vocabulary distinctness is the cleanest outcome in the file. The adversarial press worth making is at the semantic-register level rather than the vocabulary level: all three fires are in the "controlled-body-discipline-before-action" semantic register. Mira's gaze-tracks-before-speaking is body-discipline (eyes complete their work before mouth opens). Taylor's shoulders-go-down-and-back is body-discipline (trained pre-action set). Edric's hands-settle-weight-finds-back-foot is body-discipline (body completing calculation before step). All three characters are displaying disciplined physical control at their respective fire-beats. This is not vocabulary saturation (different words, different anatomies, different characters) but it is semantic-monoculture at the register level. The three characters the file fires on all happen to be the three characters whose persona cards foreground somatic-discipline-as-characterization: mira's stillness-beat, taylor's trained-shoulder-set, edric's stillness-of-calculation. The file does not produce a fire on distress (uncontrolled somatic), impulse (reactive somatic), or involuntary-tell (below-conscious somatic). This is a consequence of the per-character per-scene cap and the corpus's character-structure, not a flaw, but it is a file-shape observation that Phase 4 should acknowledge: all three fires are in the same functional register (controlled-before-action), which is not what the per-season distribution note imagines when it says "at least 3 different character-slugs across episodes 1-6." If e02-e06 continue firing only controlled-body-discipline tells, the season-level vocabulary will be semantically flat even if lexically distinct.

**What Phase 4 should address:** Not a per-episode fault. Advisory for future-episode authoring: the file has no fire in the distress-register (involuntary tell, reactive somatic), the realization-register (sudden somatic that precedes cognition), or the cost-after-the-act register (somatic that arrives after a decision completes rather than during or before). Future episodes should be watched for semantic-monoculture at the controlled-discipline register.

**Defense outcome:** No AP7 violation in s01e01. Advisory note for future-episode character distribution. → PASS with caveat to forward to future-episode feeling-flag authors.

## SKIP-MISSED Candidates (File-Shape Seam)

### Taylor @73 — flag-001 (SKIP-MISSED pressure)

**Seam-class: STRONG within SKIP-MISSED candidates**

The Taylor fork refused @73 on AP6 grounds: "NI @73 fires 'the frame's shadow takes her and what is on its other side stays the size it has been' — refusal-to-look + cost-frame." The fork argues this is "environmental-cognitive" and that "the somatic of stepping-into-shadow is fused with the NI registration." The Phase 2 audit flags this as "borderline-SKIP-MISSED" with explicit notation that "NI @73 is environmental-cognitive (refusal-to-look class), not somatic-show of Taylor's body."

The adversarial press: the Phase 2 audit's own flag-001 analysis identifies the seam correctly: NI @73 is environmental-cognitive, not somatic. NI @73 = "the frame's shadow takes her" — this is Taylor's perception of the shadow taking her, which is the environmental-cognitive channel (NI fires on what the narrator's attention lands on). It is not Taylor's body-bearing-of-the-cost. The feeling-flag's job is to show the somatic. If NI @73 registers "the shadow takes her" (environmental-perceptual), a feeling-flag at @73 using a body-locus that NI does not occupy (breath, hand-on-frame, foot-stop, posture-change at the threshold) would not duplicate NI @73's content.
