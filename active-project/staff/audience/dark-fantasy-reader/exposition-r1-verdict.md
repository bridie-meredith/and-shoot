# Verdict reasoning

The three surviving entries pass on reach but fail on grip. exposition:1 names Flea Bottom as an institution — "no titles and no guild rolls, subsistence-class permanent" — and that's the right skeleton; it reads like a building code, not a ward. A Planetos-specific slum smells like rendered fat and shit-channel, operates on specific fear (the Gold Cloaks at the Hook's bend, the sept's tithe-collectors, the thing you don't say to a stranger), runs on specific social physics the gloss does not name. "Subsistence-class permanent" is sociological framing, not Planetos local color. exposition:5 on Wren is serviceable — the ward-arrangement is correctly identified as a Planetos institution — but names it neutrally, without weight; the arrangement exists because children are cheap and the stitch-maker needs hands that don't ask wages. That cost is absent and the cost is the Planetos part.

# Entry-level callouts (revise / fail only)

- [exposition:1] @1 — "no titles and no guild rolls, subsistence-class permanent" lands as sociological summary, not Planetos slum-physics. A Westerosi Flea Bottom ward is specific: Gold Cloak territory, sept-adjacent, copper-star informal economy that exists because no lord administers this block. The gloss orients to generic medieval-poor without naming what makes this ward Westerosi rather than any other low-end district in any other dark fantasy. Revise to name one specific institutional fact that is Planetos-specific — the Gold Cloak circuit, the septon's absence, the copper-star as the only currency that functions here — something a reader arriving from a generic fantasy setting would not already know.

- [exposition:2] @4 — the simile "the way buildings accumulate moss" is the most workable image in the facet, but it earns its keep as figurative compression and I notice it is doing the work that Planetos-specific texture should be doing. Coll works the net on a Flea Bottom corner under Gold Cloak shadow. The gloss tells me he is a fixture-not-confidant (correct) and gives me a nice simile; it does not tell me what kind of fixture. In Flea Bottom specifically, the persistent presence on a corner is not neutral — a corner-holder has a relationship to the Watch, to the building-keeper, to whoever runs the lane. The gloss reads as generic-medieval-fixture when it could read as Westerosi-fixture. Not a fail — the functional orientation is there — but the Planetos specificity is absent.

- [exposition:5] @22 — "a Flea Bottom arrangement where the household takes a child for light work in exchange for shelter and two meals" is the gloss that most nearly arrives at Planetos-specific institutional weight, but it stops one clause short. The reader who came from a generic fantasy setting sees domestic service. The reader who knows Planetos sees a specific class of arrangement — economically motivated, socially invisible, the child with no house name and no claim — that operates on a specific set of stakes (Wren cannot leave; there is nowhere to go; the stitch-maker's household is better than the alternative, which is the street). The gloss implies the stakes without naming them; "light work in exchange for shelter and two meals" is the description of the contract, not the description of what the contract means in this ward. Revise to include one clause naming the absence the arrangement implies — no house, no name, no next step — which is the Planetos-specific fact that makes Wren's position load-bearing rather than generic.

- [exposition:--] @6 — proposal: needed gloss for the tallow-stall — Taylor passes it and the sensory facet picks it up, but as a dark-fantasy reader I want to know what a tallow-stall is doing in Flea Bottom specifically. Generic medieval has tallow. Planetos Flea Bottom has a tallow-stall that operates in the open because no guild has jurisdiction here. - [exposition:--] @18 — proposal: the Watch at the Hook is DELETEd from exposition on the grounds that loc-state:6 covers it, and I do not dispute that loc-state carries the naming. But what loc-state cannot carry is the institutional weight: "four-body patrol in gold cloaks" is geometry and livery. As a dark-fantasy reader I need to know what the Gold Cloaks mean in a ward like this — not their name, which I can infer, but their operational reality (they are not here to protect the ward; they are here to tax it informally, or move product, or enforce someone else's interest). The institutional weight of the Watch in Flea Bottom is a Planetos-specific fact that distinguishes this from generic city-guard fantasy. The deletion is defensible on schema grounds; I flag the gap it leaves.
