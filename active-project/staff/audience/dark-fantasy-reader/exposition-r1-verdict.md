# Verdict reasoning

The preamble earns its place: "a war the small folk do not yet have a name for moving toward them under the surface of the court" is exactly the right dark-fantasy register — unnamed danger, felt before it is labeled, pressure under the surface of ordinary life. The Flea Bottom description as "the part of the city the rest of the city forgets on purpose" is the institutional-cruelty detail I needed, not generic poverty atmosphere. Coll's fifteen years on the same corner establishes the block as a place with its own prior order, not a backdrop built for the protagonist's arrival. The Watch gloss gives me institution-as-predictable-threat rather than random-danger, which is how a functioning dangerous world works. Wren's entry — I know she is eleven, I know the Hook keeps her in subsistence labor, I know the structure even if not the name. Dark-fantasy does not need every institution captioned; the function of the Hook is transparent from the sentence, and a world that names things without explaining them is the world I want to be in.
