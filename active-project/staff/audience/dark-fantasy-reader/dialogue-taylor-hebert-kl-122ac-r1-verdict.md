## Stage 2 — V3 adversarial seam (dark-fantasy lens)

### The dark-fantasy attack

The line passes Q1 and Q2 cleanly. The named death is concrete. The cold-utilitarian register holds. The body of the line — from "There's one in the interval" through "it was a burial, not a call" — is the most honest prose in the chapter. Nothing softened. Nothing performed.

The seam is in the last two sentences.

**"She's the first name in the count. She's why I'm in Flea Bottom at all."**

The auditor flagged this as a suasion-edge (fault-011). The R2 taylor judge defended it as ledger-filing, not pleading. I am going harder.

These two sentences do something the rest of the line carefully avoids: they convert the named dead child into justification for the arrangement. The line's body (Wenna Cobb, the fever, the burial-not-call) is an honest accounting of a death the slower method produced. That accounting is neutral and complete — a specific cost, named exactly. Then the last two sentences assign the child a function in Taylor's narrative. "She's why I'm in Flea Bottom at all."

**This is the complicity-softening move.**

Wenna Cobb is now doing two things simultaneously:
1. She is the cost the slower method produced.
2. She is the justification for the faster method Taylor chose.

The dark-fantasy problem: using a dead child as both evidence of systemic failure AND as your own moral licensing creates a self-serving accounting. The child's death is recruited into the argument twice — once as the cost of the slow way, once as the reason the quick way is necessary. The child is made to carry both sides. She is an instrument.

The R2 judge defended this by saying the card's closing-clause-twist is a documented signature and the reframe stays in filing register (she is logging why the count is hers to keep). I understand the defense. It is insufficient.

Here is what the defense misses: **the filing register does not neutralize the self-licensing.** Taylor is not the intended recipient of her own filing. She is speaking. Halvard is present. "She's why I'm in Flea Bottom at all" is said aloud to a person who just argued that her method compounds the corruption. The cold-utilitarian register is not protection against the self-justification-to-the-room prohibition when the room is the person who just indicted you.

The card prohibits self-justification-to-the-room. The defense argument is that "she's why I'm in Flea Bottom at all" is not self-justification because it stays in filing register. But filing register is a style, not a substance. The sentence's functional act — in the context of this conversation, said aloud to Halvard — is to tell the indicting party why her method is necessary. That is self-justification. It is self-justification written in cold-utilitarian syntax.

**The additional dark-fantasy charge:** The sentence instrumentalizes Wenna Cobb's death more completely than any sentence in the line. The body of the counter — name, age, street, fever, eleven days, burial-not-call — gives the child back her specificity: she was a real person who died a real death with a real mechanism. "She's why I'm in Flea Bottom at all" converts her from a real death into a founding myth. She becomes the origin story. The dark-fantasy reader does not accept a dead child becoming an origin story. Dead children do not exist to justify the protagonists who couldn't save them.

The correct dark-fantasy version of this line stops before the last two sentences: "By the time someone was found to carry it, it was a burial, not a call. The slower way kept its principles intact for the eleven days it took her to die." Full stop. The death lands as cost. Taylor made her counter. The silence after is the bill.

"She's the first name in the count. She's why I'm in Flea Bottom at all." These sentences convert the cost into a foundation. They make the death useful. A dark-fantasy text that is serious about its complicity does not make the death useful.

### Why the seam closes to REVISE

The auditor's defense (fault-011 TASTE-FLAG) and the R2 judge's defense (sub-threshold, KEEP) both work within the rubric's cost-tracking register. My charge is not rubric-mechanical. It is: does the last sentence let the protagonist use the dead child's death as the license for her method, and does the text endorse this use by framing it as filing rather than self-justification?

I am finding that it does. "She's why I'm in Flea Bottom at all" is Taylor's interior ledger becoming her public defense. The card says no self-justification to the room. The R2 judge says this is filing not plea. I say: the functional act of the sentence, in the context of this conversation, is the plea. Filing syntax does not change the functional act.

The chapter's moral core requires that Taylor's counter land as cost-acknowledgment, not as vindication. The body of the line does this. The last sentence undoes it by making Wenna Cobb the reason Taylor's method is necessary. If Wenna Cobb is the reason Taylor's method is necessary, then Taylor's method is justified, and the chapter has a winner.

**The chapter cannot have a winner.**

This is the single sentence in the chapter that risks giving it one.

**[dialogue-taylor:1] @19** — REVISE. The last two sentences should be reexamined. "She's the first name in the count" is acceptable ledger-position. "She's why I'm in Flea Bottom at all" converts the dead child into a justification and violates the no-self-justification-to-the-room prohibition in substance even if not in syntax. At minimum the closing sentence should be removed or rewritten to stay in pure cost-register without asserting that the cost licenses the method.
