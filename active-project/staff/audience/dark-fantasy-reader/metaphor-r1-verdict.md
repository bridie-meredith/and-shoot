# Verdict reasoning

Zero fires. My question is whether this chapter has a beat of sufficient weight that a metaphor would have named something the other facets cannot, and whether its absence is a loss or a discipline.

The chapter's substance contract is: first deployment of the insects in the field, technically within the prohibition, already bent enough that the accounting cannot close. The held hand at @28. The struck-and-underlined ledger entry at @25 and @26. These are the beats where the world is pressing back against the protagonist in a way that costs something. In a dark fantasy chapter, those are the beats where the prose earns its weight.

I am going to work this adversarially.

The beat that troubles me is @25 — "taylor-hebert-kl-122ac strikes the line." The memory fire at @25 carries the displacement-clamp construction: a tongue that outlived the hand that set it, monument-conquest-charter-language. That is the figurative vehicle the chapter gives this moment. And the strike of the line is the action — a decision written and then unwritten. This is Westerosi register (a conquered document, a word that persists past the authority that made it). For a dark fantasy reader, this is exactly the texture I want: the weight of written record, the fear of what cannot be unmade.

Does the zero-fires call on @25 leave a gap? The AP4 analysis says the memory entry already carries the figurative correspondence. I accept that argument on grounds of the rubric, but I am going to push on it from where I sit. The memory entry (mem:2) carries a monument-gloss. A metaphor at @25 would have made explicit what the monument-gloss implies structurally — that the ledger-strike is the same gesture as conquest-charter, that Taylor is participating in the same economy of written violence as the historical forces whose monuments she recognizes. That is a different claim than the memory entry makes. The memory entry says: she has felt the weight of this kind of word before, in a tongue that outlived the hand that set it. A metaphor could say: the struck line is the same offer every archive makes — to outlive the hand that unmade it.

But here is where I stop pushing: that metaphor, rendered in Taylor's voice with Westerosi overlay, would require the kind of explicit-comparator structure the rubric demands — "X is Y" or "X like Y" — and the figure I am imagining ("the struck line is the same offer every archive makes") is exactly what narrator:5 @25 should be doing if it is working. The NI entry carries the cognitive registration. If narrator:5 is landing correctly in prose, the reader already gets the weight. The metaphor would be a second landing on the same pad.

AP4 is not just a mechanical block here — it is the correct editorial call. When the other facets already produce the full weight of a moment, a metaphor is not an amplifier; it is an explanation. This chapter does not want to explain. It wants to record and leave the record open.

@28 — the held hand. The deleted meta:1 carried an allegory that was unresolvable (memory:@9 not in the locked graph) and that closed the somatic register that narrator:6 + feel:1 hold deliberately open. The G5 deletion ground is the one I weight most heavily: an allegory that closes what the chapter holds open is not just a rubric failure. It is a category mistake about what this chapter is. The chapter ends with the ledger closed and the accounting not closed. A metaphor that completed the gesture would have lied about the chapter's nature. Zero-fires at @28 is not absence — it is the correct silence.

The world of this chapter does feel like a place that was there before the protagonist arrived. The insects, the watch-sweep, the near-witness, the ledger — these are all things that do not bend to accommodate the protagonist's needs. That atmosphere is achieved through the loc-state, state-updates, and vibes layers without any metaphor. Adding a metaphor here would have made the world feel explained rather than inhabited. My standard for dark fantasy is: does the world push back in a way that costs something real? This chapter clears that standard without figuration. Zero is correct.

One note I carry forward: if b01c03 or later chapters contain the same dense accounting-sequence without any metaphor ever firing in this register, I will begin to wonder whether the charter-language / monument register is being maintained or left inert. The cost-tracking vocabulary is doing heavy lifting (auditor AP-001 flags it). A metaphor-free run of three or more chapters in this register would register as a missed opportunity for the single figurative move that earns its keep. Flagging this as a carry-forward concern, not a b01c02 finding.
