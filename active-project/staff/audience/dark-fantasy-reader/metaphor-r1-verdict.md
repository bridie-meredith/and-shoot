## [meta:1] @23 — `1 @23 simile: the assessment opens like a door she has already decided to leave shut | licensed-by: feel:1 + mem:2`

verdict: accept

This lands. Barely — and the "barely" is worth naming before the accept.

What the simile does: it makes the prohibition feel like something that was paid for. The decision to close the door is prior to this beat; the door still opens anyway (the assessment auto-initiates, the shard doesn't ask). The consequence is that she is standing at a door she paid to keep shut while it opens. That is suffering with a specific texture — not pain, not fear, not grief, but the particular grimness of a self-imposed rule catching itself doing exactly the thing the rule forbids. Dark humor is the right register for that. The irony is cold and accurate: the rule is working perfectly, and that working-perfectly is its own kind of burden.

Position: @23 is the chapter hinge. This is where the prohibition-test beat lands (pattern-read auto-initiates, rule catches it). The feel:1 + mem:2 anchors are both backed and co-located at this bone. The simile is placed at the moment of maximum interior load — not before the beat (setup), not after (aftermath), but at the instant the door opens and closes simultaneously. That placement is defensible and, for this genre, correct. The moment of grim recognition deserves its one figurative move.

The Westerosi overlay does not interfere: doors are universal. The comparison does not lean on any Planetos-specific color, which is appropriate here — this is Taylor's interior, not the environment's voice. The simile isn't dressed in false darkness; the irony is structural, not atmospheric.

What almost caused a revise:

The simile's second half — "she has already decided to leave shut" — is doing two things at once: naming the prohibition AND naming her agency in establishing it. That is a lot of freight for a door. If the "already decided" reads as self-congratulatory (she's proud of the rule) rather than as flat self-observation (the rule is inventory, not virtue), the grim-irony register collapses into something more optimistic. The rule is working; she notices; the noticing is not warm. If the stitcher's voice-transform pass adds any warmth to this beat — any small satisfaction at the prohibition succeeding — the simile will have paid for something it shouldn't.

That is not a facet fault. The facet correctly declares dark-humor functional register and the text supports it. The risk lives downstream at /and-stitch, where warmth can contaminate flat self-observation if the prose-register pass drifts. Carry-forward advisory: the local-flow pass should render @23 as inventory, not self-approval.
