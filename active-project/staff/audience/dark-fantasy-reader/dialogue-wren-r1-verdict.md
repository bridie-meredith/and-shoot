**Seam 2 — @26: The flies-line and the wrong flavor of response.**
The strongest hostile reading from this reviewer's hot_buttons: Westerosi characters reacting to Worm-world elements with the wrong flavor of shock — specifically, curiosity instead of superstition, adaptation instead of fear. Is Wren's response to the flies-anomaly the correct Planetos-flavor response?

The Westerosi response to inexplicable things tends toward superstition and category-allocation ("glamoured witch," "cursed," "the old gods") rather than precise empirical observation. Wren's line is precise and empirical: she counts proximity, she names the comparison, she stops. Does this precision register as wrong-flavor for a Flea Bottom child?

This is the seam this reviewer cannot fully close on Stage 2 inspection. The witness-vocabulary card provides the cultural frame (trained-attention-before-action; reports observation before interpreting), and the drafts sidecar argues this behavior is the card's signature rather than an anachronistic register. But there is a tension: a Westerosi smallfolk child in 122 AC who notices that flies are not on a person when the nearby food-source is closer would, in the Planetos register, be more likely to file this as "there is something wrong with her" or "the old gods mark her" or simply back away — not to report it flat as a proximity-calculation. The flat-empirical-observation delivery is the card's signature, but it sits adjacent to a Worm-register delivery of "I observe and report data points without superstition-framing."

This reviewer does not revise on this seam — the card's authority overrides the world-flavor concern, and the close-attention training is the in-world justification for the empirical register — but the seam is named as the strongest adversarial pressure point from this lens.
