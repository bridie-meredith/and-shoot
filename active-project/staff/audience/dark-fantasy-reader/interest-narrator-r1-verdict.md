# Verdict reasoning

I read this with one question running the whole time: does the world push back, or is it set dressing for a protagonist who is always in control?

The attack surfaces named going in are flag-018 (past-perfect saturation) and flag-015 (AP-001 at-cap). My read adds one more: does the file accumulate weight, or does it resolve too cleanly?

narrator:7 @3 — "the holding cost a quarter of her at the count's edge, and she paid it on principle every morning." She pays daily. Not in a heroic-suffering way. In a maintenance way, the way you drain an infected wound because you know what happens if you don't. "On principle" is the dark note: it's not desperation, it's discipline. A world that costs you a quarter of yourself every morning before anything has happened yet. That's the texture I need. Yes.

narrator:1 @4 — "the swell was the suppression slipping by a quarter measure; she had pulled it back before the count completed." The prohibition nearly didn't hold. She recovered it, but barely. The world is pushing back — her own capabilities are pushing back. The slip is real. The recovery doesn't erase the slip. I'm watching something that is not reliably under control. Narrow yes, but yes.

narrator:2 @8 — "the ground had carried the child's breath before the crowd had registered the absence of one." The ground carries information. Her perception extends through a medium the crowd doesn't notice and can't use. This is not the protagonist being special and competent. This is the protagonist being ontologically different from everyone around her in a way that cannot be undone. The world, from her vantage, is made of a different substance. This is what I mean by the smell of a world before someone puts a name to it. Yes.

narrator:3 @11 — "the lane-mouth pressure was already priced — the dozen she would need, and the second dozen she would not." The second dozen she would not. She has already written off half the people in this lane before a single insect has moved. Not because she doesn't care. Because the cost was calculated and found to exceed the available budget. This is suffering that shapes decision-making without melodrama. The bleakness earns. Yes.

narrator:4 @12 — "the propagation went where she had told it to go, and the telling was a thing she would not name." The AP-001 instance. I was going to attack this. "A thing she would not name" can be cheap — the protagonist refusing to name the dark thing as an aesthetic gesture rather than a psychological necessity. But read against the file: she has been paying a quarter of herself every morning, barely holding the swell, writing off a second dozen already. By @12 the not-naming is not an aesthetic choice. It is the only way she can keep operating. The clinical-of-the-horrible register holds. The gap between "the propagation went where she had told it to go" (technical, dry) and "the telling was a thing she would not name" (refusal at the seam between competence and prohibition) is load-bearing. Yes.

narrator:5 @21 — "the elder had taken the lane-mouth a week before he would have the word for it." The categorization is already in motion before the categorizer knows it's happening. This is superstition specific to this culture — the categorization that is assembling, the word that is being built without the speaker knowing they're building it. The world moves against her before anyone decides to move against her. That is the kind of danger that doesn't pause because the protagonist needs it to pause. Yes.

narrator:6 @27 — "the ward's face had found hers; she filed the registration in the same fold where she kept the things that did not enter the ledger." The ward-as-secret-cost, filed in the same register as everything she cannot acknowledge. The ledger of the prohibited keeps expanding. There is no resolution here. The weight is accumulating, not releasing. Yes.

narrator:8 @24 — "she set the body to the alley-mouth so the stitch-house lane would not need to be registered again." "Set the body." Third-person relationship to her own physical form. This is not a healthy dissociation. This is a person who has been managing herself as an asset for so long that the body is an object of logistics. The phrasing doesn't perform this or comment on it. It just states it. The world wins this one without swinging a punch. Yes.

File-level: The weight accumulates correctly. Doubled register is present throughout — the Earth-Bet shadow in the cost-tracking, the body-as-asset, the non-naming, the ledger. No catharsis before the weight lands. The world is not pausing. The past-perfect saturation (flag-018) is a stitch-time risk — five consecutive past-perfect render-anchors may read as a single sustained bleak monotone in prose — but the weight inside each entry is genuine and the rhythm question is for the stitcher.

What I was watching for and did not find: hope that costs nothing. Enemies pausing at convenient moments. Magic treated as a reliable tool. The suppression slipping at @4 names the unreliability. The second dozen she would not at @11 names the cost. The ledger-expansion at @6 and @27 names the accumulation. This is correct darkness.
