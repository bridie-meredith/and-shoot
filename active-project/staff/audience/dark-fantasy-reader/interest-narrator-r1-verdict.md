narrator:4 @17 — "the ward-junction contact filed itself in the fold she kept for the entries that did not enter the ledger, and the filing took less hesitation than it had the first time she did it."

"Less hesitation than it had the first time." Here is where I stop.

The comparative requires weight I have not been given. The weight of the first time should be heavier than anything that comes after — the first time you file a person in a ledger you cannot acknowledge is when the act costs the most. If it cost less this time, the reader needs to have felt the cost of the first time in order to feel the diminishment of the second. Where is the first time? If it exists in b01c01, the chapter has not pointed me there. I am being handed a moral diminuendo without the initiating note.

"Filed itself" earns — passive construction for an act she's technically doing is clinical-of-the-horrible. But the comparative clause tells me a weight should have accumulated and implies that it hasn't quite — which requires me to have felt the starting weight. Without that anchor, the "less hesitation" reads as atmospheric rather than load-bearing. Atmosphere that costs nothing is a hot button.

REVISE. Cut the comparative clause or anchor it. "The filing took less hesitation than it should have" would carry the weight without requiring the reader to supply the prior event.
