# Verdict reasoning

Seven entries in twenty-four bones is one too many, and the file earns the breach — but not evenly. The doubled-register test is where this file earns real credit: narrator:7 @23 (the rule catching the pattern-read) is the kind of moment I wait for in dark fantasy, where the character's own constraints become the danger. The problem is that the file does not pay for the quiet bones between entries. Three of the seven entries use the same inverted-predicate construction, and when a stylistic tell repeats at that rate in a sparse file, it stops reading as character and starts reading as author. The doubled register should feel like a gap between what she knows and what she can show; what I'm getting in narrator:2 and narrator:4 is a narrator translating the rule into English, not a narrator whose interior is running a different program from her exterior.

# Entry-level callouts

- [narrator:2] @8 — "useful without controlling is what the threshold means today" — this fires on a flagstone-insect beat with no transition and no behavior-pack trigger I can point to. The rubric calls this approach-zone permitted-sparse; the entry is written as a definition of a rule she already holds, not a registration of something the beat surfaced. It reads like commentary placed over the scene rather than interior cognition that landed in response to something.

- [narrator:3] @12 — "the block reads itself through the flies — corridors, dead-walls, the where-people-aren't — and the reading is the whole of what she will take" — the em-dash list is the most evocative entry in the file, genuinely in-register, and "the where-people-aren't" is doing real dark-fantasy atmospheric work. But "the reading is the whole of what she will take" adds an accounting-tail that deflates the image. The image earns the fire; the closing clause doesn't trust it.

- [narrator:6] @24 — "face, not node, is what she holds" — the content is strong: she is suppressing her own read, which is the kind of cost that earns a fire. But "is what she holds" is the third inverted-predicate landing in seven entries, and by here I am reading the construction before I am reading the content. The discipline act she's performing deserves a harder close.
