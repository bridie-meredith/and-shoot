## Central question: @173 mother

The answer is yes, but the margin is narrower than I'd prefer.

What makes it land: "stained crease" is earned specificity. The thumb carries this woman's labor — she is a tanner's wife; the stain is the work that has defined her life since before the daughter died. The fact that this particular thumb passes across this particular edge is materially specific in a way that generic grief-gesture is not. It does not say grief. It presents a body caught in a transition — weight not yet off the bench, one more contact with the surface before the standing. That is the correct register for a grief that has been maintained as practice, not felt as emotion in the moment. She is not breaking down. She is completing a posture.

"Passes once" is correct restraint. Not twice. Not slowly. Once. The economy of it is right for a woman of this class who has never been given the language or the space for performed grief. The passage is private. Expressed: partial means attentive observers might catch it — the reader catches it; Taylor probably catches it through her swarm.

What makes the margin narrow: this is a heavily co-cited node (11 other facets firing at @173). The feeling-flag is reading into an extremely loaded moment where the weight has been built by state-updates, sensory, mem, narrator, vibes all converging. The somatic-tell does not have to build the weight by itself — it arrives into accumulated weight and gives it a body-exit. In that context the tell is sufficient. But if the reader arrives at @173 cold (if the prior facets have not built the accumulation), the thumb-on-bench would be too sparse to carry the full moment alone.

Verdict on @173: CARRIES. Not over-carries, not under-renders. The restraint is correct grimdark discipline — the weight is there; the body acknowledges it once and stands.
