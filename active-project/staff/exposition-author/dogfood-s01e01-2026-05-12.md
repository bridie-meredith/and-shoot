## Where Phase 2 trimmed and it's a problem

**All 11 scene-open-orient entries: REMOVED.** Phase 2's logic: NI + loc-state + sensory already carry time/place at scene-opens; explicit scene-bridges would be wallpaper.

This is **the structural question the dogfood surfaces.** Phase 2 is making a pure-routing-decision based on what the graph carries. But the user's actual reader-feedback was "what yard" / "I'm losing track of what's going on and why" — the scene-bridges DID help on their reading of the polish.

Two interpretations:

(a) **The Phase 2 author is right architecturally and the user-flag is about something else.** If NI + loc-state carries the scene-open orientation, then the felt-confusion at scene-boundaries is actually about *lens-facet density not carrying at scene-open* — meaning the fix is upstream (more NI fires at scene-opens, more loc-state at-establishment, more sensory-baseline-cues). Exposition correctly stays out.

(b) **The scene-open-orient scope belongs to the Stitcher's render-time, not exposition-author's facet-time.** A render-time decision: "is the next scene's opening anchor sparse enough that the reader will lose orientation? If so, the Stitcher's Phase 0.7 generates a micro-bridge from loc-state + episode-chunk." This keeps scene-orient out of the upstream facet graph and into the downstream renderer.

(c) **scene-open-orient remains an exposition scope but with a stricter fire-rule**: only fires when loc-state doesn't fire at the scene-open anchor AND the time-skip-blank is >0 AND no NI carries time/place at the first 2 anchors of the new scene. Conditional fire.

**My read:** (c) is the right design. (a) would require structural changes upstream that aren't in scope right now; (b) puts orientation back into the Stitcher's invent-or-not decision space, which is exactly what the bone-faithfulness fence forbids. (c) keeps the audit-able-upstream property while making the scope honest about when it should fire.

## Phase 1 vs Phase 2: the dogfood's central lesson

Phase 1 (no lens facets) over-budgets. It identifies 25 candidate gaps because it has no signal about what the graph already covers. It defaults to glossing every first-mention term, every place-name, every scene-boundary.

Phase 2 (full graph) under-budgets relative to reader feedback but accurately routes. It identifies 8 gaps because it can see NI:25 carrying King's Landing, NI:17 carrying institutional weight, mem:7 carrying foreknowledge-clamp. It refuses to gloss when the graph already does the work.

**The audience-modeling works.** Both runs converged on the union-of-personas gap-test. The cape-fic-reader + worm-canon-pedant need the Westeros institutional glosses; dark-fantasy doesn't; union = yes-gloss. Both runs converged on the same 4 institutional roles.

**The graph-trust principle works.** Phase 2 demonstrates that an exposition-author with full canonical graph can correctly route reader-gap content to whichever facet should carry it — and stay out of the way when another facet already does.

**The cross-episode register concept is validated.** Both dogfood runs propose the same set of glossed terms for cross-episode write-back (reeve, lords-man, maester, Watch, log, Flea Bottom). Future episodes can lift these from the register and not re-gloss.
