## Flags

- L5: "My father went still. ... My father went still again, and my mother went still."
  reason: "went still" lands three times in one paragraph. The peak is @22 (father's step-back, tens:3); the surrounding stillness-repetition diffuses the body-committed-withdrawal punch the scene is built around. The reader can feel the rhythm flattening exactly where it should crack.
  scene: scene-A
  category: variance

- L5: "My father stepped back, his weight on his rear foot, gaze on the strap at his hip."
  reason: the tens:3 peak-bone (@22) reads as one observation among many in a long unbroken paragraph. The asymmetry is there — father's verdict enacted as posture, the Tya-shape made bodily — but the prose buries the beat inside the wage-exchange run. Consider whitespace before the step-back, or trimming the surrounding "still"s, so the peak-shadow bones don't compete with the peak.
  scene: scene-A
  category: pacing

- L13: "He turned and faced me square, working pitch, the placement-look set on his face. The carter rolled out of the junction."
  reason: pronoun ambiguity. "He" follows the elder's dialogue and "the wasps brought the pass," but the only physical actor in motion in the prior sentences is the carter (implied). Reader has to backtrack to confirm that "He" is the elder turning to Taylor after the carter exits — and the order of those two beats (elder pivots to Taylor / carter rolls out) reads as scrambled. Asymmetry-tracking reader wants the elder-faces-Taylor beat clean because it is the placement-look — the social asymmetry registering across the table.
  scene: scene-E
  category: confusion

- L15: "the wasps were on the Fish Gate — the southern dock-side gate of King's Landing, the city's busy fish-trade mouth — holding the perimeter"
  reason: inline Fish Gate gloss interrupts a relay-quartet that is otherwise tracking power-extension cleanly. Cape-fic reader skims worldbuilding inserts when tactics are live; this one drops into the middle of the perimeter sweep and dilutes the relay-asymmetry beat. Either trim ("the wasps were on the Fish Gate, holding the perimeter") or move the gloss to a parenthetical adjacent to the relay block.
  scene: scene-F
  category: pacing

The cost is implied (breath, repeated writing), but the rule-connection — that the exhale is the price of range expansion — is not named or shaped distinctly enough for this reader to register it as a cost-of-power beat versus a quiet exposition beat. (Carry-forward watch: relay-load-accumulation must be legible as cause; this is the first installment of that promise.)
  scene: scene-F
  category: pacing

  reason: "clean and remembered" reads as slightly literary for Taylor's tactical-observational POV register.   scene: scene-F

- L19: "The flies relayed the lord's-man's man — an ear pressed to a wall."
  reason: ambiguous antecedent. The em-dash phrase "an ear pressed to a wall" can attach to "the lord's-man's man" (the enforcer has his ear to a wall) or to "the flies" (the fly itself is the ear-pressed-to-the-wall, i.e. insect-as-instrument). Cape-fic reader normally rewards the second reading — that's the asymmetry mechanic — but the construction does not disambiguate, and the reader's first parse goes to the enforcer. Worth a rewrite that lands the fly-as-ear reading unambiguously.
  scene: scene-H
  category: confusion

- L23: "I walked the perimeter and wrote the entry — wrote it again — flexed my hand. I woke. The stylus dropped."
  reason: stylus-drop is the @125 peak-bone — the first physiological cost of 400m, the cost-written-to-body-not-log moment. The sequence "I woke. The stylus dropped." reads scrambled. Did she wake then drop the stylus, or wake because the stylus dropped, or drop the stylus during sleep? The cape-fic reader needs the causal chain (range-load → headache → micro-faint → stylus drop) to be recoverable from the text. As written, the cost lands but the mechanism is opaque — and the live watch on "unlock cause must be legible as relay-load accumulation" extends downstream. This is where the s01e02 reader first learns that cost is real; the rule architecture asks the prose to make the causal step visible.
  scene: scene-J
  category: confusion

- L25: "I held my feet still."
  reason: scene-K is loc-eastern-quarter-apothecary but Taylor is at base (Flea Bottom), reading through beetles. The line implies a physical posture, and the reader does not get an explicit confirmation that Taylor is at base while beetles work the apothecary wall. Fixed-anchor live-watch (carried from OQ-4/5 plan review): the prose must establish the distance from base to maester at least once. This line is one of the natural places to do it — Taylor's feet are still at base, beetles are at the wall, the asymmetry is the gap between them. Without that anchor, scene-K reads as if Taylor might be physically near the apothecary.
  scene: scene-K
  category: confusion

- L25: "I held my feet still. He went out by the upper room, by the stairwell, by the side-alley door."
  reason: route-triple protected pattern lands, but the visitor's exit is given immediately after "I held my feet still" with no separator. The visitor's three-part exit is the structural mirror of the entrance in the same paragraph; placing it directly adjacent to Taylor's stillness collapses the two beats. Wants a break — paragraph or em-dash — so the asymmetry (visitor moves through three thresholds while Taylor's body does not move at all) is shaped, not crammed.
  scene: scene-K
  category: pacing
