# Exemplar passage — voice prime (alt-auto-1)

I counted the coppers twice before I let him cross. The third boat of the morning, and the river still running fast from the rains upstream, and the man wanted to bring his mule. I told him the mule cost extra. He said he knew. He did not know. He was watching the far bank the way a man watches a door he is not sure will open, and I marked that and said nothing and took the coin.

The ferry was my uncle's before it was mine. My uncle had a way of asking after a passenger's business that sounded like courtesy, and a way of remembering the answers that did not. I had not yet learned the courtesy part. I had learned the remembering. I poled us out into the current and watched the man and watched the mule and watched the bank we had left, because three things at once is what the work asks of you, and a fourth if there is weather, and there was no weather that morning but there was the man and his quiet.

He paid me in copper but his boots were good. The boots told me he had been someone, or knew someone, or owed someone. I did not need to know which. The ferry only needed to know which bank, and the bank was the north bank, and the north road from there went two places, and only one of them was a place a man with good boots would go in a hurry. I put us in at the north landing and let him off and did not watch him take the road.
