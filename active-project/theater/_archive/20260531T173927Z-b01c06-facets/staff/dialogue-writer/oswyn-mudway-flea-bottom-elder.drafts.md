## Affirmative card-signature citations

- **Sentences end with the fact, not the implication.** Final clause is "Up the hill twice this month" — a count and a cadence, no flag of significance. The receiver (Taylor) finds the significance; Oswyn states the count.
- **No filler.** Three sentences, no connective tissue. Verb-elided where the slotting is doing the work ("Up the hill twice this month" — no "I've seen him," because the slot already implies the observer is him).
- **Vocabulary: names of streets and people, seasonal patterns.** Name=Corwick. Pattern=this-month. Geography=up the hill.
- **Gallows only when specific.** "Runs errands for someone above his station" — the class-fact is the dry edge; he names it without commentary, the way the exemplar names Halek putting salt in the back room.
- **Exemplar cadence match.** Compare exemplar's "Tallow-works hasn't come down the alley in three days. Either they're shut or they've gone the Long Lane. I'd check the Long Lane." — period-broken, observation-then-pattern, no semicolons. Draft C uses the same structural rhythm: noun-slot. fact. cadence-count.
- **Categorized-not-curious.** Corwick is a slot in Oswyn's frame ("runs errands for someone above his station"). The slot is the whole interest. Mirrors the card's "You're the foreign woman who knows where the rats are" — the categorization is the report.

- **No curiosity about the listener.** Did not phrase as "thought you'd want to know" or "in case you were watching for him" — that would betray awareness of Taylor's surveillance use, which is a HARD FENCE violation (card Hard Fence 1: he does not know he is part of a coverage architecture).

The card's "Didn't see anyone from the tallow-works on the alley this morning" elides the "I" — Oswyn's reports are spoken from the slot of the observer, not announced as observations. Draft C matches that elision more cleanly than Draft B.

## Why Draft C beats A and B

Draft B fixes the punctuation but keeps the observer-clause, which front-loads Oswyn as the reporter; the exemplar consistently lets the fact stand without the reporter announcing themselves. Draft C name-first, then slot-fact, then count-and-cadence — three short statements, each a fact, no connective tissue, matches the exemplar's "noun. fact. cadence." rhythm exactly. The slot-categorization shape ("Corwick. Runs errands for someone above his station.") also mirrors the card's "You're the foreign woman who knows where the rats are." structure: the name, then the slot, then the supporting count.
