## Stage 2 — V3 Adversarial Seam from Cape-Fic Lens

Here's where I have a real problem, and the auditor flagged it correctly as a TASTE-FLAG but I'm landing harder on it.

**Entry :1 — aphorism-strain at the crooked-house closer:**

"A thing built crooked doesn't come straight because you lean on it gently. It grows crooked at the rate it was always going to grow."

The auditor called this "a maxim wearing work-clothes" and said the R2 judge countered that the image stays inside carpentry/debt concrete. I read it differently through my lens. The problem is not the image — the image is fine. The problem is the *structure* of the sentence: "It grows crooked at the rate it was always going to grow." That is a sentence that could only be said by someone who has rehearsed the argument. "At the rate it was always going to grow" is a formally constructed clause — it has the shape of a polished aphorism, not an honest working-through. It is too clean for someone who is "working it through honestly."

Halvard's card tells me he: pauses between premise and conclusion, names his own uncertainty, is slower than Taylor. "It grows crooked at the rate it was always going to grow" violates the uncertainty-naming tell — it's stated as a certainty, and as a certainty that has clearly been thought through in advance. The auditor's defense (R2 judge said "bounded by the surrounding ledger") doesn't answer the specific problem: the sentence sounds like a prepared closer, not an honest stumbling-toward.

Compare to entry :2's "I don't know where it stops. That's the truth of it." — that's Halvard's actual register. Uncertainty named, stated plainly, not dressed. The :1 aphorism does not sound like the same man as "I don't know where it stops."

From my cape-fic lens: a character-with-established-register speaking in a way that breaks the register is the thing that makes me pause and reread. Halvard's register is slower, plainer, less polished than this sentence. The Lane man argument earns up to "The slow way costs him more now — he'd go hungry a while, refusing. It costs him less after. The debt doesn't sit there gathering." That's his voice. "It grows crooked at the rate it was always going to grow" is not.

This is a REVISE, not a fail. The argument works; the specific aphorism-sentence should be recalibrated to Halvard's honest-uncertainty register.
