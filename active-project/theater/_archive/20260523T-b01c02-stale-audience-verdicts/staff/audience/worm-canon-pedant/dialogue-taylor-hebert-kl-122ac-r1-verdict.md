## Strongest hostile counter-argument — lens: worm-canon-pedant

### Entry 1 seam

**The half-honest calibration anchor is correct — but "That is all it was" may over-close.**

The worm-canon-pedant's strongest V3 attack on Entry 1: "That is all it was" is a closing assertion that functions as a minor lie-by-framing. The canonical Taylor Hebert does engage in exactly this kind of partial-truth deflection. But the worm-canon-pedant tracks *how far* the deflection goes. "That is all it was" frames the entire event — including the lane-mouth closures and the insect deployment — as explainable by the watch passing. Wren just named the pattern: "the lane went quiet after." Taylor's response does not address the pattern; it addresses only today's incident.

The V3 seam: is this a canon-Taylor move? Yes — she has always been willing to let partial truths stand. But the specific construction "That is all it was" is a finality assertion that an observant reader (or Wren herself) can test against Wren's prior statement. The question is whether canon Taylor, under operational conditions with attachment forming, would deploy a finality assertion rather than a redirect. A redirect ("The watch comes through most nights") would be less falsifiable and more operationally sound. The finality assertion closes the conversation but leaves the factual gap visible. This is a character-plausibility seam: canon Taylor tends to favor the less falsifiable move when managing information. "That is all it was" is the slightly riskier operational choice.

The sidecar addresses draft C's "Nothing happened" as an over-claim (which is correct). The sidecar does not address whether "That is all it was" itself over-closes relative to canon Taylor's information-management discipline. This is the live seam.

**Severity:** the move is within the card's behavioral range (half-honest pattern, §Voice tells). The seam is narrow. The worm-canon-pedant notes it but does not REVISE on Entry 1 alone.
