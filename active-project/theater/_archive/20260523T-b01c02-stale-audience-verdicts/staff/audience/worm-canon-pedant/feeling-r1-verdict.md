# Verdict reasoning

Two entries. I am running the Taylor entry against her canon voice register and the rubric's one-clause discipline. I am running the Wren entry against the card and the form. Both have issues, but the Wren entry's issues are form-level and recoverable. The Taylor entry has a form violation I cannot pass.

feel:1 @28 (taylor, expressed:no): "her hand closes short of the page and does not finish the reach."

The one-clause form discipline from the rubric is exact: subject + verb + object/locator, one clause. This entry has two predicate clauses joined by "and": (1) "closes short of the page" and (2) "does not finish the reach." The rubric's REJECT signatures include: "Compound somatic-tell exiting body-register" — and the specific note is that the connector "and" joining a body-show to a "temporal-latency observation about timing" is the failure mode.

The question is whether "does not finish the reach" is a body action or a temporal-latency observation. I read it as the latter. "Closes short of the page" is the body action — the hand closes, stopping before the page. "Does not finish the reach" names the non-completion as a fact about the reach rather than naming a discrete body-state. "Does not finish" is completion-accounting, not body-register. This is close to the pattern the rubric promoted to AP-entry after b01c01: the second clause whose subject is the action-of-the-body rather than the body-doing-the-action. "The reach" is the abstraction; "her hand" is the body. The second clause observes the reach did not finish; the first clause shows the hand closing short. The rubric's REJECT signature says: "A compound where the second clause's subject is the turn, the moment, the silence, the gap — any abstraction-noun that is not the character's body." "The reach" is exactly that abstraction-noun.

Second issue: canon voice. Wildbow's Taylor in Worm uses economical, terse interior notation. When a body action is clipped, it is stated once. "Closes short" already communicates non-completion; "does not finish the reach" restates it at greater length. Canon Taylor's somatic tells tend toward the single clean verb, not the doubled form. "Her hand stilled above the page" is the Wildbow register. "Closes short of the page and does not finish the reach" is two ways of saying the same arrested motion. That is not Taylor's register. That is author-voice expansion of a single body-state into two clauses.

The failure is form-level (one-clause discipline, second-clause abstraction-noun) plus register-level (Wildbow/Taylor's terse somatic economy). This is a REVISE on feel:1. The slot is correct — Taylor's one fire in scene-C is the right allocation. The beat at @28 is the right beat. Replace the compound form with a single body-action clause that carries what both clauses are reaching for, or that carries the cost-vector the dark-fantasy-reader independently flagged as underspecified.

feel:2 @15 (wren, expressed:partial): "her eyes go to the alley-mouth before her head turns to it, then settle back on Taylor and stay."

This has three sequential motion units: eyes go to alley-mouth; head turns to alley-mouth; eyes settle back on Taylor and stay. The form is compound-complex. The rubric requires one clause. Three motion units is not one clause.

However — and I am holding this separately — the tell itself is character-accurate for an original Westerosi Flea Bottom character. Street-trained attention management: the eyes lead the threat-track, the head follows, the eyes re-anchor on the person being evaluated. That is a plausible behavioral sequence for someone who lives in Flea Bottom. No canon violation (Wren is not a Worm character). The interior it discloses — Wren's deliberate reanchoring of attention onto Taylor, the "stay" being the tell — is the correct interior to show at the crystallisation beat.

The form violation is real (three motion units, subordinate temporal clause, "then" second-predicate, trailing "and stay"). But the content is right. The REVISE verdict on this entry is form-only: compress to one clause that carries the decisive motion. The "stay" is the load-bearing tell; the subordinate lead-in (eyes before head) is supporting. A single-clause reconstruction might be: "her eyes come to rest on Taylor and do not leave" — though the impersonator-fork should solve the revision, not me. The point is: the content of feel:2 passes; the form does not.

Aggregate: two entries, both needing revision. feel:1 on form (one-clause discipline, second-clause abstraction-noun violation) and voice-register (doubled restatement in non-Wildbow form). feel:2 on form (three-unit compound-complex, not one clause). Content of feel:2 is sound; content of feel:1 is questionable (see dark-fantasy-reader's independent Q1 attack). Verdict: REVISE.
