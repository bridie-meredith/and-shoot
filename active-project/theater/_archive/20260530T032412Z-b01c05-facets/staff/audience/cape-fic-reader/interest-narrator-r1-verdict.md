# Cape-Fic Reader — Adversarial Review: interest-narrator b01-c04

---

## Reading stance

This is an operational chapter. Taylor is running a four-ward insect-feed while routing intelligence through a courier she has now formally committed to. The setup is that she has a power with information asymmetry baked in — she sees the wards before anyone else can, and the ledger she is not writing is the real ledger. That is the right genre chassis. The narrator-interest layer is supposed to be where that doubles — where the inside track shows.

Reading for: does every fire register something the reader could not see without access to her interior? Does it cost something, or reveal something about the rules? Is the interior channel doing work the external channel cannot?

Rejecting on: fires that tell me things the SVO already told me. Fires that explain the stakes I already clocked from the setup. Fires on beats I would have read past anyway.

AP10 chassis carry (narrator:3 @9 + narrator:7 @31) is a special ask. I am reading both against this question: does the template feel like a register tic or does it feel like the author needed a way to make the beat sound weighty?

## Per-entry readings

**narrator:1 @6** — `the feed had him a span before the lane-mouth did; the courier's gait was the gait already mapped`

Pre-calc surfacing. She had him located before he came into visual range. That is the asymmetry channel — she knows first, always. The past-tense on "was already mapped" is doing the right work. This is not telling me she noticed him; it is telling me the notice happened before the scene started. The information edge is legible.

Accept.

**narrator:3 @9** — `the lever is no longer a question of whether; it has just become a question of how much`

AP10 chassis. "The lever is no longer X; it has just become Y." Definitional-collapse on the acceptance moment.

"Lever" is doing real work as her cost-tracking vocabulary — leverage, threshold, whether vs. how-much is her resting register. The inversion resolves a specific tactical fact: commitment has flipped from conditional to parametric. She is now pricing how much exposure rather than whether to expose. The chassis delivers the flip economically. For a peak-bone at the acceptance moment, her interior should register exactly this kind of binary-to-parametric shift.

The template is the vessel for the thought here, not a substitute for it. This reads as register tic: a character who thinks in levers and thresholds would clock this exact transition in exactly these terms. Accept on the AP10 question.

Accept — AP10 carry reads as voice-consistent on this entry.

**narrator:4 @18** — `the elder enters the feed at second-ward range; she does not let the next thought finish`

Bone is "the insect-feed returns oswyn-mudway-flea-bottom-elder." Two clause-units in sequence: perceptual (range read) and behavioral (thought interrupted). The rubric says one observation per fire.

The second clause signals withholding — she cut off her own reasoning mid-step. In capefic, that usually means the character knows something the scene is not delivering yet. But the facet does not name the edge of what was interrupted. The reader gets told a thought was killed without knowing what the thought approached. That is mood-texture, not information. The first clause is clean; the second clause is a void where there should be content.

If the interrupted thought has a payoff elsewhere in the chapter, this entry can earn it. If it is purely atmospheric, the second clause is underdelivering against the genre expectation that interior interruptions signal suppressed knowledge.

Revise-flag: second clause underdelivers. Either name what the thought approached, or cut to the first clause alone.

**narrator:6 @22** — `the ward registers in the feed at second-ward range; the anchor stays outside the ledger by the same hand that wrote the ledger`

Bone: insect-feed returns Wren. Chapter's load-bearing plant — protection and trap are the same operation.

The semicolon is structural here (cause-to-consequence): she registers Wren → the registration forces the architecture into view. The first clause is perceptual. The second clause is the cost-tracking read — the anchor is outside the ledger because the ledger's author excluded her by structural design. "Same hand that wrote the ledger" folds the protection/trap duality into one image.

This is the kind of entry that pays off a setup. The chapter's thesis earns this fire. Accept.

**narrator:7 @31** — `the half-step of yard-air between her hand and his is the exposure she has just paid in full`

Second AP10 chassis carry. "X is the Y she has just Z." Definitional-collapse on the hand-off.

The bone is "taylor-hebert-kl-122ac delivers the report-sheet." The half-step of yard-air is the physical gap during the hand-off. "Exposure she has just paid in full" is the cost read.

Here is my problem. Narrator:3 @9 used the chassis to encode a binary-to-parametric state flip — a specific cognitive event with a mechanical name ("lever," "whether," "how much"). This entry uses the chassis to describe a physical action that just happened. "The exposure she has just paid in full" is accounting-language applied to a delivery event. The mapping is correct for her voice, but the chassis is doing heavier lifting. The entry does not tell me something I could not assemble from the bone: she delivered the sheet; that cost exposure. The chassis dresses it up as interiority but the registration is thin.

The half-step/yard-air image is strong. That physical detail is doing real work. But the AP10 frame attached to it dilutes the image into a cost-accounting summary rather than letting the image carry its own weight. Narrator:3 had a cognitive event the chassis could encode. Narrator:7 has a physical image the chassis is flattening.

Two AP10 carries in 11 entries where only one delivers the specificity that justifies the template: the second reads as authorial pressure, not voice. The auditor named @31 as the priority revise target. My read confirms it — this is not a close call.

Revise-flag: AP10 chassis at @31 is coasting. Verb-driven closure that preserves the half-step/yard-air image would make this its own registration instead of a template echo.

**narrator:8 @36** — `the sheet has just entered a channel the feed cannot reach into; the operation has cycled for the first time at scale`

Bone: Jarvis exits the cooper's yard. World-axis peak — intelligence is now in Otto's channel.

"A channel the feed cannot reach into" is the information-asymmetry channel inverted: she is now on the wrong side of her own advantage. The feed, which gives her everything, stops here. That is the structural reveal the chapter has been building toward. "Cycled" reads clinical and operational — her register stays flat at the peak, which is correct.

Two clauses, but these are cause-to-consequence: the sheet entered an unreachable channel, therefore the operation cycled. The second clause names what the first clause means. Tight. Earns its position.

Accept.

**narrator:9 @38** — `the ward returns in the feed at stitch-house range; the four-ward read continues; the one observation that does not enter any report continues with it`

Bone: insect-feed returns Wren a second time. Chapter close.

Three clause-units. The first is perceptual (Wren in range). The second is operational status ("the four-ward read continues"). The third is the load-bearing close (the unlogged observation).

The second clause is AP2 — it paraphrases the bone. The bone says Taylor is running the four-ward feed; the NI entry confirms the four-ward read continues. No additional registration. The rubric names this: summary-of-the-beat.

Strip the second clause and the entry is clean: perceptual clause + unlogged-observation clause. That is the double-register close — she sees Wren again, she keeps not-logging her. The @22 architecture calls back without the middle clause muddying it.

Revise-flag: delete "the four-ward read continues." First and third clauses carry the chapter close.

**narrator:10 @7** — `the yes goes across the yard-air in the cadence of a settled count; the modification carries the only parameter the prohibition still holds`

Bone: Taylor speaks to Jarvis (the acceptance).

The first clause is her perceiving her own spoken output — "cadence of a settled count" is self-monitoring against her internal register. That is the split-channel observation where her tactical cognition watches her own performance. The second clause is the tactical fine-print: the modification carries the one constraint that makes the yes not a total commitment. That is information asymmetry — she knows the parameter; Jarvis has to work with it without knowing what it fences.

Both clauses earn. The second clause is not a summary; it is the content the SVO does not surface (the modification's hidden constraint). Accept.

**narrator:11 @15** — `the range opens one ward outward; the suppression-cost has just shifted to a load she had already priced`

Range extension + cost-shift. Pre-calc tense ("had already priced") is correct — she knew the cost before paying it. Clean two-part structure. Accept.

**narrator:12 @23** — `taylor-hebert-kl-122ac holds the feet`

The bone is "taylor-hebert-kl-122ac holds the feet" and the NI entry is the same content. That is AP2 straight — the entry repeats the SVO without adding interior layer. No perceptual channel named. No cost-register. No additional registration. Lonely in the cite-index (no co-citations). The scene-map protected-pattern designation for this bone is already carried by the scene-map facet; the NI is not required here and is not earning its fire.

Hard reject. AP2 anti-pattern, no channel, no earn. Delete.

**narrator:13 @27** — `the fourth ward opens to the feed; the saturation-cost has gone past the load she has carried before`

Structurally parallel to narrator:11 @15: range opens + cost-tracking. Two fires with the same chassis on range-extension bones is persistent-narration adjacent. But the rubric permits a second fire if it registers change, and the content diverges at the threshold: at @15 the cost "shifted to a load she had already priced" (pre-calculated, within expectations); at @27 the cost "has gone past the load she has carried before" (now in unpriced territory). That is a mechanical state-change — she is operating outside her pre-calc margin.

Established costs being respected at their limits is exactly what this persona reads for. The distinction is clean and the genre-mechanical implication is real (unpriced territory means she does not know what this costs yet). Accept.
