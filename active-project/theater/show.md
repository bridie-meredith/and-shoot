The stone behind me is the cold the dusk laid down and the night pressed deeper, and I take it through the overdress and the linen because moving off it would tell the yard a thing I do not want the yard to know. The bird is on the gatepost. I put it there. Down the line that runs from this wall to that post, I am holding it the way a hand holds a wire that has not yet been asked to carry weight, and the wire is humming clean — body forward into the four-point grip, head set on the road, the small held tilt that is not roosting and is not rest. The road north is empty. The road north stays empty. Half a league up the dark, the gatehouse lantern reads as a smear at the distance a point-source loses its point — the amber bleed where Harrenhal has not gone to bed and is not going to, the one warmth in a field of cold stone and frozen earth, and I am watching the smear through the stillness on the gatepost because I cannot watch the smear from here. Underneath: the yard's own birds at the lowest weight the feed delivers, the small things in the seams of the wall, the frozen ground giving back nothing through the soles. The clock is running. The cost is on a hook behind a door that is not yet open. The morning has not started for anyone but me, and the only instrument the yard holds is a bird I have set on a post to look at a light I cannot see past.
A mouse-shape steps in the seam at my left hip — wall-weight, the small one, repositioning at the pace of a thing that has had no reason to learn caution — and the passive feed takes the spike of it the way the feed takes any movement against a still field, too loud for the half-second of the crossing, the warmth of it brief against the cold mass of the wall before it sets down again into the next seam. I do not turn. I do not lift the hip off the stone to watch what my eye could not have seen anyway, because following a mouse with the head would tell the yard's own perimeter the wall has a mind on it that knows where to look, and the yard does not need to know. The wall takes another measure of the warmth I have been giving it and I let it take the measure. The small thing finishes the seam. The feed comes back to its still plane. The wire to the post is still humming the one note. The smear at half a league has not moved.
