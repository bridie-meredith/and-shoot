-- scene: great hall, minor Reach lordship, late afternoon --

The room had the look of a weighing — the merchant across from Lord Edwyn Fossoway-minor, the steward to the lord's left with a ledger and a quill that had been running since before the merchant sat down, and by the door two men-at-arms, one of whom had been giving the merchant more of his attention than the other for the past quarter-hour, which the merchant had catalogued and the man-at-arms did not know.
He started with the wool — undyed, three bolts, Tumbleton sourced, quoted at a number that gave the lord room to feel shrewd — and let that settle before he mentioned the pickled fish from the Arbor, which he described with the mild distaste of a man who moved it for profit rather than love, and only then, as if it were a small thing, the spice-wax, which was not a small thing, and which arrived through Tumbleton because he had particular friends in Tumbleton, and he said this with the tone of a man who had no particular interest in the lord knowing more than he needed to.
Lord Edwyn nodded, brought two fingers of his left hand down on the table twice in a light tap, and cut his eyes to the steward.
"Your steward will know the arrangement better than I can describe it," he said, settling back as though the lord's signal had released him from a formality, "Aldric mentioned that the household prefers fixed price to margin — I've come prepared for that."
The steward's quill came up off the page and stayed there.
The lord held the silence for a moment the way a man holds a card, then let a smile cross his face, said the arrangement suited him, and lifted a hand toward the far end of the hall to bring wine.
The cup was the weight of ordinary metal and ordinary ceremony; he let his thumb find the rim's edge and felt Lord Edwyn's smile the same way — placed, duration-controlled, technically correct, the kind of expression a careful man produces when he has already decided to find out everything about you.
Lord Edwyn turned his shoulder toward the steward and said a single word at a volume that died somewhere between the two of them.
The steward set the quill down across the ledger's spine, pressed the cover flat, and went out through the side passage without haste.
He raised the cup he hadn't touched, a small courtesy he could not have been expected to drink from, and set it back down with the deliberate care of a man returning a borrowed thing, then told Lord Edwyn the road to the Reach was long enough that an early start served better than any comfortable bed, thanked him for the hall and the fire and the very fine wine, and said he imagined their paths would not cross again — meaning it as an ending, offering it as a gift, watching the lord's settled face the whole time for the flicker that didn't come.
The two men-at-arms near the door moved their weight from one foot to the other and did not step aside; they stood there in the gap until Lord Edwyn lifted one hand, open-palmed, and then they did.
He lifts the latch and the door opens the way doors do when no one is stopping you, which is not the same as cleanly.
The hall doors came together behind him, and inside, where the steward's ledger still sat closed on the table, the steward had not come back.

-- scene: market square, Stormlands town, midday --

The square is a working square in the middle of a working day — livestock pens ranked along the eastern wall with their smell arriving well before their noise, a bread stall planted at the center like something permanent, the well coping beaded with water from the morning's use, and a sell-sword taking his ease against the well post with the particular stillness of a man who is paid to watch.
He corrects the angle of the top crate, settles it, adds another, and tells himself: good job, great work, no notes — the joke lands in his own head with all the warmth of a training exercise, because there is no one to hear it and the stallholder is watching her count the way people watch counts when they are deciding whether to trust you with a second one.
The cart horse gets one look at the dog coming through the pen-gap and that is enough — four hooves scrambling for purchase on wet stone, finding none, the cart swinging wide and sideways until the wheel catches the boy between its rim and the well coping and he stops making noise.
He bit the fingertip of his left glove, pulled it free with one sharp backward jerk, and his bare hand was already moving toward the cart's side panel before the bread hit the cobblestones.
The cart stops — not settles, not slows, stops, wheel hung mid-skid at an angle that has no relationship to balance, the horse's foreleg lifted and held there, the harness chains slack in the air with nothing pulling them and nothing letting them fall.
With the wheel locked and the pressure gone, the boy drops to the cobblestones beside it and lies there, not making noise.
He works the glove back onto his right hand with his teeth still in his lip, fingers finding the seams by feel while his eyes stay on the square, on the frozen wheel still tilted wrong, on the pigeon that doesn't know yet, on all of it — and he doesn't look at his hand, because if he looks at his hand he will have to think about the rest of it.
The cart is still wrong — wheel locked at that impossible mid-arc, the horse foreleg still up, the chains still hanging in their slack suspension — and the square has gone quiet in the way that markets go quiet, which is all at once and from the outside in.
Then the freeze breaks: the horse lurches into its next step, the chains drop with a crack of iron links, and the wheel comes down onto the well coping with the full weight of the cart behind it.
The sell-sword pushes off the well post, gets himself upright, and watches Dennis across the square without taking a step toward him.
The septon crosses to the boy's mother and leans in close, and the word he puts in her ear is short and very old — older than the Seven, older than the Andals who named the Seven, a word out of whatever the Stormlands was before it had a name.
The boy's mother looks at Dennis, and then at the septon, and then back at Dennis, and then she does not look anywhere else.

-- scene: sea-cliffs, Stormlands coast, morning --

The cave mouth opens onto grey water and grey sky and no clear line between them. Salt comes in off the Narrow Sea flat and constant, and underneath it something scorched, old heat worked into the rock. The light does not come from anywhere — it is simply present, diffuse, arriving from the whole sky at once without casting a shadow. A quarter-mile out, a trading cog works east: a small dark shape, sail and mast readable against the grey, moving at the unhurried pace of a vessel with somewhere to be and time to get there. From inside the cave the sea looks still. The cog does not look at the cliff. Not yet.
The dragon moved toward the light. Claws found the rock floor with no sound beyond the dry click of contact — four points, then four more, a slow crossing of the cave's width with the salt getting sharper at each step and the grey opening ahead getting wider. At the mouth it stopped. The cog was out there: sail, mast, a direction of travel, the small regular motion of something that displaced water. Mobile. The shard logged it. The eyes held it. Nothing else moved.
On the cog, one of the small figures stepped to the rail and something in its hand caught the grey — a brief flat glint, brass or polished metal, there and then not — and the line of it came back toward the cliff. Two of the other figures turned the same direction the glass was pointing.
The feet moved. Three strides and the cave was behind — the scorched dark, the salt held in rock, the ceiling's weight — and what replaced them was the whole sky arriving from every angle at once, cold light on scale, the body suddenly fully in the open above three hundred feet of open water, the Narrow Sea grey and full below and the cog a quarter-mile out with the glass still fixed and no part of the scene changing. The shard noted the shift in the exchange: before, one party observing and one party unobserved; now both parties with a clear line of sight, the data running both directions at the same quality, the glass resolving the body's full length against the flat sky the same way the eyes on the cliff were resolving the cog's sail, its mast, its deck, the small figures and their turned shoulders. The chest held its warmth. The wings folded against the body. The body held still above the water and let the grey light read it.
On the cog, one of the small figures raised an arm toward the cliff — a line held for less than a breath, then pulled back — and the ship's heading changed: no order crossing the water, only the sail's face rotating as the cog came two points west and the hull's angle opened away from the cliff, the vessel drawing itself quietly out of whatever proximity it had decided was too much.
The body turned. Three strides and the grey fell away behind — the whole sky's flat weight lifting off the scales as the cave mouth rose around it, the cold arriving from fewer angles and then from none, the salt dropping off sharply with each step inward until it was only a trace, a memory in the rock, and then the scorched smell came up from underneath: old heat, worked stone, the specific dark that had been here before the body was here and would be here after. The claws found the cave floor and the ceiling's weight returned above — not oppressive, just present, a known pressure, a context the body had been calibrated to. The light changed: the diffuse grey that had read the body from every angle at once closed down to a narrower thing, interior and sourceless, casting nothing. The shard logged it: classification complete — mobile, crewed, glass-equipped, withdrawal-response confirmed, no aggression, no pursuit. The cog continued west somewhere behind the eyes in the dark, diminishing at a predictable rate toward a predictable destination, and it meant nothing further.
Maester Pylos went below, and the looking-glass did not come back up. The deck returned to itself — a few sailors moving about their work in the ordinary way of men on a vessel that has somewhere to be — and the cog continued west, its sail holding the grey, nothing on deck directed toward the cliff anymore.
Below decks, in the close light of a lamp, Pylos had parchment flat on a plank table and a quill already inked. The first line went down. The report was addressed to the Citadel, with a copy to the Hand's office in King's Landing.
