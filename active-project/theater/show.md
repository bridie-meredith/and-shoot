The chest drew in — shallow, slower than the last, and the interval after it simply did not end.

Across the wall, in the nave, a held weight — body forward into the perch, head-line set toward the cottage side, the posture of an animal that finished its registering some while ago and is waiting on what comes after; the cold arrives second, jaw and hands and the strip of air above the blanket-edge taken in as a fact that was decided hours before I had any say in it; and the wall, the wall the breathing came through as a small regular pressure I had absorbed the way I absorbed the cold, is not transmitting; eyes still closed, mat still under me, the conclusion already in the body before there is a word for it.

Two fingers found the inside of his wrist before my knees had fully taken the floor — the placement automatic, from a hand that had done this in another life. The skin was warm, alcove-warm, sealed-room warm, the wrong kind of warmth and noted as wrong; I held the count through to its number and through the number after, and nothing came up under the fingers to meet it. I straightened.

The doorframe took both shoulders and the nave's open-roofed cold came across my face in the same breath, the spent-tallow warmth of the alcove falling off my back; the raven at the far end held its weight forward, head-line set, and my mouth stayed closed and my hands stayed at my sides.

Three steps in and the rushes took the weight under my heel the way they had taken it for three months, the dry give and the dry sound returning through the foot before the dark resolved to anything the eye could read; the cold deepened by the margin a sept's nave is colder than a sealed alcove and I drew the breath that measured the margin and let it out; the raven's mass slid past on my left as I went, weight forward into its place, head-line still set, and I did not turn to it and it did not call; the altar table's edge met my hip where my hip had learned to expect it, and I went around; my hands arrived at the shelf a half-beat before the shelf finished arriving as a shape, and the register was there under them, the binding rough, the bulk of it carrying the cold of the room in its covers — a cold that had been sitting in it long enough to be in it — and I took it down off the shelf with both hands and drew it out against my chest.

The candle came off the chancel table and the wick took the spill at the second pass — small yellow committing to itself, the tallow finding the shape it would burn in, and the page resolved out of the grey in the radius the flame allowed; I set the register flat on the table and turned through the sections by the feel of the leaves more than the look of them, succession running where it had always run, third quartering, and the clause arrived under the thumb before the eye had fully arrived at it. I read it the way a hand reads a knife — for the edge first. The first sentences were the frame I already knew, the ward-of-the-sept language, the standing of the sept's authority, the precedence under the Faith; my eye took them at the speed they deserved and went on. Then the operative paragraph, and the room narrowed to the column of light over it. A vacancy in the cure-of-souls; the wardship not transferring with the office because the office was not a chattel; the sept's standing over its dependents lapsing on the day the cure was declared vacant unless a successor of permanent appointment was seated and his hand entered against the line — permanent appointment, the words taking on weight as I read them, not a visiting brother, not a brother sent to attend an illness, not a road-dusty man with a motherhouse credential and no seat, a successor in the cure, and the cure declared vacant by the district recorder upon the filing of notice of death; and the clock ran from the filing not from the death, which sounded like a mercy and was not, because the filing was already moving — Plumm's man would file or compel a filing on his own circuit and the circuit was on its road; and the term was named in the clause as a count of days, and the count of days was the count Plumm had already named to me in the yard, the same number, sitting on the page in the dry hand of whoever had drafted the form a generation ago, having been the number the whole time; on expiry the wardship reverted to the lord of the precinct in whose ground the sept stood, the standing of any other claim — kin, motherhouse, sworn guardian — admissible only if entered before the term ran and accompanied by a seated successor's hand on the same page; and below that the language for what reversion meant in practice, the disposition at the lord's discretion, no further hearing required, the record sealed and traveling with the next administrative carry to Harrenhal — sealed, traveling, the same verbs that had been used about the document with my name on it three days ago. I came to the bottom of the clause and stopped. The flame had not moved. The shape of the next several months was on the page under my hands and it was not the shape I had been working toward; the deadline was the count Plumm had named and the requirement was a seated successor and Rowan was not a seated successor and the filing was already on a horse. I let the breath out without lifting my eyes off the page.
