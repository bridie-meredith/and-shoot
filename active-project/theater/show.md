The cart sat outside the timber gate with the lord's banner furled against the crossbar and the horse still hitched to the traces, and my body understood what the beetles in the flagstone gaps had been telling me all morning — that it had arrived in the dark, that it had not left, that it was not going to.

Mira set the bucket down on the flagstone where the sound would travel, straightened, and spoke across the yard at the carry of a woman calling a price. "When he comes through that gate, he'll take the unattached ones first — anyone without a man or a holding to name — and he'll have them on the list before the sun's off the wall, so whoever that is wants to be elsewhere by then."

Edric kept his eyes on the road past the cart and saw the clerk instead, paper already unrolled, counting the yard before the officer had bothered to come through the gate.

He came through and I felt the distance to the well go from irrelevant to the only number in the yard.

He stepped to the center where the yard opened, and his voice reached the sept wall without strain: "I carry commission from the lord's administration for the impressment assessment of Harrenhal district, and by that commission all wards, unattached residents, and those without sworn guardiants are required to assemble in this yard. That requirement stands now."

I crossed the twelve feet on packed dirt gone soft at the center from the morning's foot traffic, came in among the others — wool and sweat close on both sides, Mira's elbow a hard angle near my shoulder — and the clerk's ledger was open on a board he'd balanced against his hip, the top line already filled, a name already taken, and I stopped when I had nowhere further to go.

The officer worked the line with the steady rhythm of a man whose eye had learned to move faster than the forms required, three questions to each figure—name, lineage claim, sworn guardian resident—and the clerk's stylus followed the dictation without pause, and by the time the officer was halfway through the second-to-last entry his gaze had already separated from the present ward and fixed on the final shape at the yard's far end: a girl alone in the line, the space where a guardian's hand should have rested visibly empty.
