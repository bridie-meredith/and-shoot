The sept door is open and the candlelight from the altar table is reaching out across the threshold-stone in a wedge that was not there at first light, and the wedge is moving — not the light, the woman inside it, the neighbor from two doors down passing between the table and the wall with her hands occupied at the arrangement, and the arrangement is what neighbors do for a body, and the door I pulled to before any of this opened to let her in without my hand on it. The folded leaves are in my right hand with the wax raised under the thumb where the thumb has learned the seal in the hours since Plumm's saddle, and Rowan is in the yard with the folio under his arm, and the bench is where the bench is, and the eastern track is empty in the direction the horse went, and none of these are the operative fact. The operative fact is the door. I pulled it shut. I was two miles east on the protocol of a clock that had already started without me, and while I was arguing the start of the clock, the settlement opened the door I had closed and put a woman at the altar table with Osmynd, and the door is still open, and the open door is the vote — the settlement has decided what kind of death this is and the deciding does not require me to be told. Sparrows in the eaves taking the candlelight as the second thing in their feed, after the woman's moving warmth — passive only, the cost-curve already paid for the day; the wedge of light reaches the stone and the stone holds it the way stone holds anything that was not asked of it.

The folded leaves shifted in my right hand as I turned a quarter into the yard's east angle, putting the open road on the cart's side of my shoulder and putting Rowan in the corner of an eye that had stopped using him as the principal datum some hours ago. He had drawn a breath that had the weight of a man finding the shape of his next sentence in his mouth before releasing it; the folio came forward against his ribs by the small lift the arm makes when the hand is about to move, and the words for the motherhouse were assembling behind his teeth in the orderly file a brother of his training keeps them in. I said it before he did, flat, at the cadence I would have used to read out a line of a list. "The next recorder's session in this district closes before any motherhouse letter can come back over that road." The breath he had been keeping for his sentence went out without the sentence. The folio stayed against his ribs at the lifted angle and did not move forward into the yard. The eastern road held the silence the way the threshold-stone was holding the candlelight — without giving anything back.

The folio arm came down as I spoke, the document settling against the side of the leg where it was no longer being held forward as anything, and I gave the sentence to the yard at the cadence I use for a line that is meant to be heard once and not argued with. "I am writing to the motherhouse tonight regardless." Not to her, exactly; not to the empty road; not to the sept door at my back. To the place between the three where a thing said aloud takes up room it does not take up unsaid. The Father is the patron of records that outlive the men who keep them, and a brother who puts down the only instrument left in his hand because the road is too long has chosen a silence he will be asked about later by someone he cannot answer with the excuse of speed.

My right hand came across my body and down with the folded leaves, and the leaves found the threshold-stone seal-up where the wedge of candlelight from the altar table had been resting since the door went open without me — wax raised, district mark squared into the light, the thumb that had learned the seal in Plumm's saddle leaving it and the leaves at the same instant, fingers lifting clear and the right arm coming back to my side empty for the first time since first light. The wedge took the wax the way the stone had been taking the wedge: without giving anything back, without asking for it, without a hand to read it. I straightened off the placement and did not stoop to it again. The leaves stayed where they were laid; the seal stayed up; the candle inside the door went on reaching across the stone and finding the wax instead of the empty wedge it had been finding before; sparrows in the eaves registered the new still object at the threshold and folded the registration into the feed without lifting. The hand that had held it for hours hung at the seam of the skirt, light at the wrist with the weight gone, and stayed there.
