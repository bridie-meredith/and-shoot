-- scene: jack --

The hall smells of the meal that just finished — something roasted, grain, tallow burning down in the candles they've left on the table. The lord's at the far end, cup between his hands, moving it slightly. Two men-at-arms at the door behind me. I sat down without looking at them; I can tell from the breathing that the one on the right is more alert than he's being paid to be. The steward's got a ledger. The lord hasn't smiled. He's been waiting.

My broadcast shard fires. Returns nothing, the same nothing it has returned every day for three months in this world without parahumans. I stopped expecting something. I didn't stop noticing the absence.

The dog under the table has put its chin on my boot.

"My Lord," I said, "the arrangement I've found most useful coming east from Oldtown — and your steward's brother Aldric would know this trade, he spent two years in that city's cloth quarter before he came north to your household — is to hold the Myrish work separate from the Lyseni imitation, because the touch tells you nothing in low light, but the weight does."

I said it the way you say anything that is supposed to be common knowledge: naturally, without emphasis, as though I assumed he knew.

I felt the moment it landed wrong before his face told me anything. It was in the air. Like a sound slightly off-pitch that you notice three notes after it happens.

Lord Edwyn's left hand moved. Not much — it settled flat on the table, fingers loose, close to his wine cup. The steward's quill stopped in the middle of a character. Neither of them looked at each other, which meant they had agreed in advance on what to do if something happened.

The two men-at-arms by the door didn't move. They hadn't caught it. That was something.

My shard fired. Returned noise.

I kept my face on the arrangement.

The question was a probe, not a request. He'd mentioned the cloth distinction yesterday in passing, in a room with four other people, and now he was asking me to explain my expertise. He already knew I'd paid attention. He wanted to know how much.

I held it a half-beat too long before I answered. I know I did. There's a version of this where I don't — where the answer comes smooth and immediate the way it would have if I had my instruments. I didn't have my instruments.

"My first years were in the Free Cities," I said. "Pentos, then Lys for a season. When you buy from both sources for long enough you start to feel the weave difference before you name it."

I watched him. His face arranged itself into something that was neither convinced nor unconvinced.

Lord Edwyn nodded to his steward. The arrangement was closed, at least as far as the ledger was concerned. I gathered my things — nothing that required looking down for long — and stood.

He watched me cross the room. Not the way you watch a merchant leave. The way you watch a door close on something you're going to want to remember the position of.

The dog followed me to the door. The steward was still writing.

-- scene: dennis --

Three hours I've been in this square and I still don't know what kind of labor exchange I did to get the bread. I moved some crates. I think. There were crates and someone pointed and I moved them and they gave me half a loaf. That passed for a transaction here.

The square is — okay. The square is what every town center has been since before I had a concept of what a town center was. Market day. Goats. A farrier at the far end with a queue of horses and their owners looking impatient. Two dogs in a territorial dispute over what might be a dead rat. A man on the water trough who hasn't moved in an hour and has the specific posture of someone who is watching the crowd professionally.

I have the bread. I don't have a plan past the bread.

The bread was okay. Dense. The kind of thing that made you want something to drink, and what I had was the stream water I'd collected this morning in the cupped hands of someone who does not have a water bottle and has made peace with that.

I thought: *I would kill for a Gatorade.*

And then I thought: what is Gatorade. It's a drink. It's a specific drink with a specific name that exists on a specific Earth that is — okay, do the math — several centuries in the future and also an entirely different world, so functionally it does not exist anywhere. The joke required the concept of Gatorade. The joke did not translate.

I ate the bread. The two dogs were now actively fighting.

The noise from the pen — something sharp, a goat or the gate — and the horse went sideways like it had been hit. The cart swung. The boy by the well was in the wrong place at completely the wrong time, and then the cart was against the well coping and he was between them and he was shouting.

The crowd went from market to something else in about two seconds.

I was already moving before I made a decision about it.

I had the glove off before I registered taking it off. Right hand. The glove was in my left hand. I was running.

There's a voice in my head that's always running calculations about exposure — *gloves on, touch nothing, you're a time bomb, don't be a time bomb* — and that voice was there and I was also running at the cart and taking the glove off, which was not what the voice was saying.

Ward reflex. Vista used to say I had the fastest threat-response time in the team and I used to say that was because I was already thinking about running.

I wasn't running. I was running toward it.

Contact. The wheel stopped. The whole cart stopped — horse mid-lean, cart mid-swing, a tableau of arrested motion that had no business standing at that angle without something holding it. The boy scrambled free while everything was still.

I was aware of the square in a way I hadn't been three seconds ago. The people in the three-meter radius around me had stopped talking. Stopped moving. The two dogs were still frozen in their thing.

I put the glove back on.

*Forty-some seconds*, I thought. *Could be forty. Could be forty-five. Don't predict it. Never predict it.*

The boy was out. He was at his mother. He was crying.

The cart hung at its impossible angle in the stopped instant.

The sell-sword was on his feet off the water trough. He hadn't drawn anything. He was looking at the cart with the expression of a man who has survived a lot of things and is currently deciding what category this goes in.

The market women had stopped arguing about price. One of them had her hands up, not praying — just up, as if she'd been interrupted mid-gesture and hadn't finished lowering them.

The septon at the edge of the square had raised his hands, but that one looked like praying.

The boy's mother was staring at the cart. She'd started at relief and ended somewhere else — the cart was hanging at an angle that didn't make sense and it had been hanging there for what was starting to feel like a long time, and nobody had touched it, and the boy was out, and the relief had curdled into something she didn't have a word for yet.

Nobody in the radius was talking. Nobody was looking directly at me. They were all looking somewhere that was not directly at me, which was worse.

"Stuck in a rut," I said, which was technically something you could say about a wheel in a road, and it was not the real explanation for anything that happened, and everyone in the radius could see that it was not the real explanation, and I said it anyway because my armor was running and my armor produces words.

The joke I added after was something about how they should see the ruts in some road I was trying to invent on the fly that existed somewhere in this world. It didn't work. It had never been a good joke. I was making it worse in real time.

The cart was still hanging.

The cart dropped. The horse screamed — that specific horse scream that I had now heard twice in three days here and it was still wrong in the same way — and the wood cracked against the well coping and people were finally moving, finally talking, finally doing the things people do after something.

I moved to the edge of the square. I was at the edge within eight seconds of the cart dropping. That was a good time. I was proud of it in the specific hollow way you're proud of things when the thing you're most proud of is not panicking.

The septon got to the mother. He was already talking to her. I couldn't hear the word he used — I was too far, and there was too much noise — but I could see her face when he said it, and she nodded. Like she'd been waiting for someone to name it.

I didn't know what the word was. I knew it was a word about me.

-- scene: crawler-sighting --

The cave mouth holds warmth from the day, salt-smell, the smell of the last kill three days ago in the back of the cave where the bones are. The sea is running a medium swell. The light is going orange-grey, which changes the threat-visibility profile of the clifftop in a direction that is neutral.

A vessel on the water. Configuration: trading cog, single mast, running south under partial sail. Speed: slow. Distance: three hundred body-lengths or more. The assessment is fast and the result is low.

Warm-in-chest. Fire-ready. The chest warmth is not decision yet. Decision requires closer range.

The clifftop above is clear. The beach below is wet. This is the assessment, complete, in the time it takes the wind to shift once.

The vessel moves. Three figures on deck: small-heat signatures, human-sized, one near the stern coiling rope, two near the front. A fourth figure below deck, estimated by weight distribution. Captain configuration: single command source on the upper part. Five humans total, probable.

The fire is ready but the calculus is complete: range too far, wind wrong, return-to-water path poor. Not a hunting decision. The shard is noting the configuration anyway. It notes everything that moves.

The vessel is below threat-threshold. The vessel is not novel. The bones in the back of the cave are more interesting than this vessel.

Movement on deck. The figure coiling rope stopped. Its attention direction changed — oriented toward the cliff face. Toward here.

A second figure joined it. Then a third. Three figures now oriented toward the cliff face. Their movement patterns had shifted from task-movement to observation-movement. This is a distinction the shard registers: they were working, now they are watching.

A fourth figure came up from below deck. This one moved differently: deliberate, unhurried, the movement of authority. It was handed a long metal object — smaller than a weapon; cylindrical. The shard catalogued it as non-threat.

Four figures on deck, all oriented toward the cliff face.

Assessment update: I have been perceived.

This does not change the calculus.

Movement invites engagement. Staying invites observation.

Observation is not threat. Observation at this range from a vessel on water at this wind-bearing is not threat.

The warm-in-chest continues. Not decision. Readiness.

The fourth figure — the one with the cylindrical object — held the object up and looked through it for a long time. Then it lowered it and was still. The quality of its stillness had changed from the other figures. The other figures were still in the way of things that have stopped. This one was still in the way of things that are working.

The assessment stays. Range too far. Novel stimulus: low.

The cliff holds the warmth of the day. This is good.

The figures on deck were moving again. Not toward the cliff face — away from it. The bearing changed: the vessel was moving at a wider angle from the cliff line, gaining distance. This is a withdrawal pattern.

Withdrawal-pattern means: non-aggressive. Withdrawal-pattern means: they are not coming to the cliff.

The fourth figure — the working-still one — went below deck. Something was being transferred: the cylindrical object, a small object, a flat object. Materials for a task.

The assessment reached its conclusion. They were going to tell other humans. Telling-other-humans is a known behavior-pattern following a sighting event. It is logged in the same category as the hunting parties from the inland lords: event that produces future-events. Not relevant to current cycle.

The cave held warmth. The bones in the back of the cave were where they had been. The sea ran its swell.

He turned from the cave mouth and settled back into the dark.
