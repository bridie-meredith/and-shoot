## Seam 1 — Per-entry, @73 AP4 structural level vs domain level

**Target:** `1 @73 simile: her breath goes out like something set down at a threshold it has crossed before`
**Severity:** STRONG

**Verdict: DEFEND**

The rubric's AP4 gate is explicitly a domain-level gate, not a structural-level gate. The rubric §Anti-patterns reads: "AP4 — Figurative-already-in-memory. Metaphor that doubles a figure the memory-flag callback *already carries*." The operative test is whether the **figure** — meaning the specific comparison, domain, and vehicle — is already present in the memory-flag's text. Memory:3's figure is: threshold / shape / body-recognition. The comparator domain is **body-as-shape-fit** — the threshold is rendered as a form the body has previously occupied. The simile's figure is: breath / deposit / cost-at-recognized-threshold. The comparator domain is **breath-as-expenditure-at-familiar-threshold** — a different vehicle (breath, not shape), a different comparison mode (deposit-as-action, not fit-as-state), and a different image-domain (cost-currency, not morphological recognition).

The Phase 3 seam argues that the *recognition-structure* — `[threshold] + [prior-acquaintance clause] + [body as subject of that acquaintance]` — is shared between memory:3 and the simile, even though the vehicle domains differ. This argument would make AP4 a structural gate that blocks any metaphor whose logical shape echoes the anchor's logical shape. The rubric does not support this reading. A gate that operated at the recognition-structure level would be nearly impossible to satisfy, since any memory-callback metaphor must, by the rubric's functional-register requirement, invoke the recognition-of-prior-acquaintance structure — the callback is, definitionally, a recognition-move. An AP4 gate reading that bars all comparisons using the recognition-structure would effectively bar the callback register from the facet entirely, which is the opposite of the rubric's design. The rubric intends AP4 to prevent the metaphor from restating memory's specific figure; it does not intend AP4 to prevent the metaphor from participating in the same semantic occasion.

The simile does not restate "not a new shape for her body." It adds "her breath goes out like something set down" — the breath-as-deposit image, which memory:3's text does not contain. Domain-level AP4 check: clear. The Phase 3 seam's structural-level reading is the harder reading but is not the rubric's reading.

**Seam 1 resolved: the AP4 gate operates at domain level. @73 fire holds.**

## Seam 2 — Per-entry, @73 AP5 label-fence vs figure-fence

**Target:** `1 @73 simile: her breath goes out like something set down at a threshold it has crossed before`
**Severity:** STRONG

**Verdict: DEFEND**

The AP5 hard fence is a label-fence: "Hard fences absolute — no Earth-Bet proper nouns in description." The rubric specifies **proper nouns** as the prohibited category and makes the distinction explicit: "Memory facet uses fenced gloss `(earth-bet: ...)` in target-reference field; metaphor's text field cannot use that gloss." The contrast is between the target-reference field (where the gloss is permitted) and the text field (where the gloss is forbidden). The fence keeps the **name** out of the prose-facing text field; it does not require that the Earth-Bet referent be erased from the structural logic of the comparison.

The Phase 3 seam argues that the worm-canon-pedant's approval confirms the locker-displacement figure is "present in the simile as an unlabeled structural shadow," and that this is precisely the violation rather than the justification. This argument proves too much. The rubric's memory-flag system is built on the premise that Earth-Bet monuments can be invoked and activated in the prose without naming their labels. The memory-flag schema's own gloss format `-> (earth-bet: locker-displacement)` is the mechanic that separates the monument-invocation (in the memory facet) from the label-appearance (in the prose). The metaphor facet operates downstream of this separation: it licenses from the monument (via the memory anchor) and keeps the label fenced.

If the AP5 fence were a figure-fence — if it barred the structural import of the Earth-Bet figure into prose — then every memory-callback metaphor licensed by a memory-flag carrying an Earth-Bet gloss would be AP5-barred by definition. The memory facet would authorize the callback and the metaphor facet would immediately refuse on AP5 grounds. This would make the memory facet's Earth-Bet-glossed entries useless as metaphor anchors, which contradicts the rubric §Cross-facet contract: "memory-flags @33 / @52 / @73 are the s01e01 metaphor-eligible memory-anchors," with @73 being the strongest. The rubric explicitly designates a gloss-carrying entry as the strongest metaphor anchor in the episode. A figure-fence reading is structurally inconsistent with this designation.

The doubled-register that the worm-canon-pedant describes — prose that is readable at both the Westerosi surface register and the Earth-Bet monument register simultaneously — is the intended design of the memory-monument system. It is not a violation of AP5; it is AP5's correct operation. The label is fenced; the resonance is the point.

**Seam 2 resolved: AP5 is a label-fence (proper nouns only). @73 fire holds.**

## Seam 4 — Per-skip, @33 SKIP-MISSED (verdict-body domain)

**Target:** @33 REFUSE decision
**Severity:** MODERATE

**Verdict: DEFEND the REFUSE**

The Seam 3 resolution establishes the principle that monument-licensing extends to the full semantic domain of the monument, not only to the image-domain the memory-flag's text deploys. Applying this principle to @33: memory:1's monument is the dying-tutor cluster (locker-adjacent + annette-death-adjacent). The semantic field of this monument extends beyond the recognition-of-prior-pattern shape that memory:1 deploys. Does it extend to the verdict-body domain — "the door-staying-shut as the text of a sentence already written, not merely a recognized series-member"?

Yes, in principle the monument admits this domain. But three independent obstacles block the fire, and each is sufficient to refuse independently.

**Obstacle 1: tens=2 at @33 is not trailing-edge.** The mechanic audit (fault-006) notes that @33's tens=2 is a stakes-visibility reading. The Phase 3 seam acknowledges this: "the tens=2 justification would need work even if the domain argument holds. The @38-@39 peak cluster is six beats away — this is not trailing-edge; this is mid-scene sustained pressure." Rubric §Tens-curve discipline: "tens=2 (pressure): acceptable IF the beat is a trailing-edge of a peak (post-3-cluster) and the metaphor renders the recoil/aftermath, NOT the pressure itself." @33 precedes the @38-@39 peak by six beats; it is not trailing-edge. The pressure reading at @33 is rising-to-peak, not recoiling-from-peak. tens=2 mid-scene-rising is not the acceptable-zone configuration. This is a rubric-rule independent of domain arguments.

**Obstacle 2: AP4 operates with greater force at @33 than at @52.** Memory:1's text is: "a closed-door-over-a-failing-tutor is not the first such door her body has stood at." Unlike memory:2 (which uses the flagstone as the image), memory:1 uses the door itself as the image. Any metaphor at @33 is almost necessarily in the door domain. The verdict-body candidate — "the door as completed sentence" — is still in the door domain. Domain-level AP4 is at risk: both memory:1's figure (door-as-series-member) and the candidate figure (door-as-verdict-text) are door-comparisons. The vehicles are different (series vs sentence), but the tenor (the door staying shut) and the domain (the door as the figure-center) are shared. The Phase 3 seam notes this risk ("approaching AP8 territory; may be too architecturally complex"). At minimum the AP4 domain-level proximity to memory:1's own door-figure is stronger here than at @52, where flagstone (memory:2's image) and peer-silence-as-choosing (the candidate) are genuinely distinct domains.

**Obstacle 3: AP8 structural risk.** The verdict-body candidate — "the closed door IS the failing-tutor's death-register, not the announcement of it" — requires structural correspondence between the closed door (spatial object), the verdict (social act), and the tutor's failing/death (third-party event). This is a multi-element allegory needing more than a single-clause simile can carry without collapsing or becoming opaque. Rubric §AP8: "Allegory that requires multiple `@<pid>` anchors collapses to single anchor (strongest beat) or refuses at this facet." A single-clause metaphor rendering three-element correspondence at a single anchor would either be under-developed (reading as obscure) or fully allegory (requiring more development than a facet entry permits).

All three obstacles hold independently. @33 REFUSE is upheld.

## Seam 5 — Cross-facet, @73 simile vs state-updates @77 + NI:19 beat saturation

**Target:** `1 @73 simile: her breath goes out like something set down at a threshold it has crossed before`
**Severity:** MODERATE

**Verdict: DEFEND**

### Vector A — simile pre-rendering state-updates @77

The Phase 3 seam argues the simile may "pre-render" the mask-thin at @77: if the breath-as-deposit means the cost has already been accepted and the body is already past resistance, the simile is functionally co-present with the mask-thinning, making the state-update's mask-thin a restatement.

The cross-facet hardest-cull rule (rubric §Cull discipline) applies to **contradictions**: "any metaphor that contradicts a state-update... gets deleted." The rule does not extend to metaphors that precede or lead to a state-update. The rubric's own cross-facet contract notes this sequence explicitly: "State-updates @73: the simile at @73 renders the interior breath-cost of the threshold-crossing. State-updates @77 carries the canonical mask-thinning write... The simile is upstream of the @77 state-update (breath empties at @73; mask-thinning is at @77). No contradiction." The mechanic audit confirms: "fault-008... No cross-facet contradiction found."

The pre-rendering concern mistakes directionality for redundancy. The simile at @73 renders the cost-recognition (breath as deposit); the state-update at @77 records the outcome (mask-thinned). The causal direction is: breath-cost at @73 → crossing at @73 → mask-thin at @77. The simile is not a redundant restatement of the state-update; it is the registering of the cost that produces the state-update. This is the normal upstream-downstream relationship between a figurative facet and a state-update facet. A metaphor that licenses the interior event at @73 and a state-update that records the canonical consequence at @77 are not redundant; they are a sequence.

The cross-facet hardest-cull rule does not cover pre-rendering as such. Extending the rule to cover "metaphors that lead to state-updates" would prohibit any figurative rendering of an internal event whenever that event produces a canonical consequence, which would collapse the distinction between the figurative-rendering facets and the state-update facet entirely.

**Vector A resolved: pre-rendering at @73 is not functional redundancy with the @77 state-update. The simile holds.**

### Vector B — four-facet citation density at @73

The Phase 3 seam notes: NI:19 + memory:3 + feeling:4 + metaphor:1 all fire at @73 — the highest facet-concentration in the episode by a wide margin. The concern is stitcher load: can the stitcher coherently integrate four independent figurative layers at a single beat?

This is a stitcher concern, not a facet-entry correctness concern. The rubric does not impose a per-beat facet-density ceiling; the facet entries are authored independently and the stitcher cross-cuts them. The mechanic audit does not identify beat-saturation as a fault. The question is whether the four entries at @73 are individually correct (they are — AP sweeps confirmed for all) and whether their combination produces collision at the stitcher level.

A stitcher note is appropriate. The four @73 entries are: proto-line @73 (physical action: stepping into shadow) + NI:19 (shadow takes her + size holds) + memory:3 (shape-recognition callback) + feeling:4 (breath somatic tell) + metaphor:1 (breath-as-deposit simile). The stitcher's task is to render the closing beat with all of these available without producing a five-clause pileup. The entries occupy distinct channels: physical action (proto-line), environmental-perceptual (NI:19), monument-recognition (memory:3), somatic (feeling:4), figurative-cost (metaphor:1). The channels are distinct; the stitcher can layer them rather than stack them.

The stitcher instruction carried forward: the closing beat at @73 should render these channels in priority order with the simile as the payoff register. **Attempt 2: a weight / a stone**
"Her breath goes out like a weight set down at a threshold it has crossed before." The weight/stone figure is generic enough to avoid Earth-Bet proximity and Westerosi-register-too-specific problems.
