# Round 3 — Maester Defense

Persona: westerosi-traveling-maester
Behaviors: westeros-maester (leaf) + westeros-grrm-mannerisms (overlay)
Era: HotD-adjacent / post-Dance traveling circuit.
Method: read seam, defend or revise. Sharpen, don't soften.

---

### M-1
**Seam:** "The one above your finger" as the deictic specifier assumes Taylor's finger is the spatial reference point, but the intent says the maester places his own finger on the line — and if the maester's finger is the anchor, the spatial instruction sends Taylor's gaze to a position above the maester's hand, which is either a deliberate misdirection in the test or a spatial ambiguity the line cannot resolve without clarifying which finger is the reference.

**Decision:** REVISE.

The seam is load-bearing. The intent puts the maester's finger on Taylor's-name-line in the steward's column; the round-2 line then says *the one above your finger*, which forces a reading where Taylor's finger is on the page. Two finger placements cannot both be the anchor and the line refuses to choose. Worse, the persona card is explicit on the test mechanic: *he watches the eyes, not the hands*. The maester's signature move is to point so that the candidate's gaze does the reading, not to invite the candidate's hand onto the page. *Help me to the name above your finger* mis-stages the test by routing it through Taylor's hand. Revise so the maester's finger is unambiguously the deictic anchor and the eyes are what the line is fishing for.

**Draft A**
**Line:** "Be so kind, child. The steward writes a small hand. My finger is on the line — the name above it, if you would. Read it back to me."
**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Be so kind, child.* — institutional courtesy, Citadel composure, classless address (maester card §Vocabulary, Voice)
- Polite-brief, polite-brief: four short sentences, no subordinated spine
- Citation-as-interruption: *the steward writes a small hand* lands as procedural footnote anchoring the page (maester card §Syntax)
- *My finger is on the line* — the maester's finger is the spatial anchor, named explicitly; resolves the seam
- *The name above it* — *it* refers to *the line* (and to the finger on it); deictic is unambiguous
- *If you would* — modal courtly subjunctive, register-locked maester (maester card §Vocabulary)
- Saxon morphology throughout: *be so kind, set down, read it back* (no Latinate *locate, identify, recite*)
- Em-dash used once, not as spine
**Why rejected:** *Read it back to me* is an explicit literacy instruction — the test surfaces as a test. The persona card is categorical: *He has never in his career said "can you read." He does not intend to start.* *Read it back* is the same instruction in courtesy clothing. The card refuses it.

**Draft B**
**Line:** "Be so kind, child. The hand is small here, and my own finger is in the way. Help me to the name just above it — the entry the steward has set down."
**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Be so kind, child.* — institutional courtesy, classless vocative (maester card §Vocabulary)
- *The hand is small here* — citation-as-interruption, the document anchored as object before the request lands; addresses the dark-fantasy challenge that the line opens on the request rather than on the document (maester card §Syntax)
- *My own finger is in the way* — maester's finger named as the spatial reference; resolves the seam without naming the test. The phrasing also mildly self-effaces (the finger is *in the way*, an apology-frame that masks the test as inconvenience)
- *Help me to the name just above it* — embedded literacy test: never *read*, never *identify*; *help me to* is the canonical maester-card formulation (maester card §Direct samples; persona card §Action Menu)
- *The entry the steward has set down* — Saxon morphology *set down* (overlay card §Anglo-Saxon prefixes); names the document as procedural artifact, locating the assessment as routine rather than targeted
- Polite-brief stack: three short clauses, parallel; no em-dash spine, em-dash used once
- The eyes, not the hands, do the work — the line invites Taylor's gaze to track from the maester's finger to the line above; the test is in where the gaze lands
- Authority without announcement: no *I am a maester*, no chain-claim — the chain is doing the talking (maester card §Register markers)

**CHOSEN: Draft B.**

Draft B resolves the seam by making the maester's finger the named spatial reference (*my own finger is in the way*), addresses the dark-fantasy complaint about opening-without-context (*the hand is small here* anchors the document before the ask), and refuses the worm-canon-pedant push to surface dramatic irony in the line — that is a scene-architecture concern, not a line concern, and surfacing it inside the line would put the maester out of register. The maester does not perform irony; he runs the test. Taylor's parallel processing is her work, not his.

### M-2
**Seam:** The maester's death-registration of Osmynd ("may the Father judge him true") is delivered as a sub-clause under his breath while the next question is already arriving — which is correct Citadel composure — but whether Taylor receives it as a death-registration signal or as a general Faith-invocation depends on her current working knowledge of Westerosi courtesy-forms, and nothing in the adjacent lines establishes which it is, making the line's most important informational move invisible to the character it most directly affects.

**Decision:** DEFEND.

The seam mis-locates the work. Whether Taylor parses *may the Father judge him true* as a death-registration formula or as general Faith-invocation is a Taylor-card question, not a maester-card question. The maester's job in M-2 is to do what a Citadel man does when a child goes silent on the named septon at the foot of the column: register the silence as bereavement-shaped, mark the dead in courtesy form, and continue the assessment without pursuing what he has just deduced. That is the line's content and the line delivers it.

The maester's discipline on this exact point is on the persona card: *He has placed smallfolk before. Not many… he sees the smallfolk stratum as a resource the realm manages badly, not as a gallery of suffering individuals.* And: *he does not pursue the silence, he moves on, and the moving-on is itself the assessment recorded.* The dark-fantasy challenge wants composure-as-performance surfaced; the card refuses that — *he is visibly nothing. Inwardly it registers as a resource-allocation failure.* The composure is not performance; it is the register. Surfacing the seam inside the line would make the maester perform composure, which is the register break the persona card explicitly forbids (*pretended interest that is not actual interest; folksy warmth*).

The pulp-enthusiast's complaint that the question (about the register, the literacy-source) is the *least interesting* possible thread is the strongest of the three challenges, but it is also wrong on the card. The card's M-2 mechanism is *the pivot is the structure*. The most-interesting thread — the dead septon, the silent ward — is exactly the thread the maester does not pursue, because pursuing it would tell Taylor he had noticed. Filing the death and pivoting to the literacy-source *is* the move; following the death thread would collapse the assessment-asymmetry the persona card §Action Costs warns against losing. The pulp reader wants the half-beat surfaced; the card says the maester refuses to give it.

The worm-canon-pedant point about Taylor's working knowledge of Westerosi courtesy-forms is real, but it is correctly resolved at the Taylor-fork: Taylor's leaf card is where her current command of the Faith-form is established. The maester's line cannot — and should not — carry both ends of the signal. He delivers the form; she receives at her own card-fluency.

**Line stands:** "Mm. Yes, yes. The sept will have its register — every sept does — and Septon Osmynd, may the Father judge him true, will have set your letters there. Was it the register he taught you from, child?"

**Card features demonstrated (re-anchored under hostile pressure):**
- *Mm. Yes, yes.* — under-breath double-affirmation; the canonical reflex (maester card §Cadence; persona card §Voice — *the yes-yes reflex*). The card is explicit: *escapes before he forms the full thought.* Comes when something has been spotted before assessment is complete. The silence on Osmynd's name is what was spotted.
- *Every sept does* — citation-as-interruption in institutional-we shading; procedural footnote (maester card §Syntax)
- *May the Father judge him true* — Faith aside as form, not piety; institutional courtesy on naming the dead (maester card §Vocabulary §Religious register: institutional, and §Direct samples — *the late Lord Bracken, may the Father judge him true*)
- *Will have set your letters there* — Saxon morphology *set* (overlay card §Anglo-Saxon prefixes); future-in-the-past tense recounting what the dead-named has done
- *The register he taught you from* — pivot to the literacy-source thread; the maester is not abandoning the assessment for the more dramatic thread (persona card §Hard Fences — *the steward holds the census scroll; the maester holds the assessing gaze*)
- Vocative *child*, classless register maintained (maester card §Vocabulary)
- Polite-brief, polite-brief: four short clauses; no em-dash spine; em-dash used once
- The pivot is the structure: he files the death and continues; persona card §Triggers explicitly: *he will ask the steward a quiet question after the interview, not during. During is not the time for that.* M-2 is during.
- Authority without announcement; the chain is unmentioned

The line is doing exactly what the card says the maester does in this moment. Held.

### M-3
**Seam:** "I have his courtesy and thank him for it" — the verb "have" in "I have his courtesy" is not a Westerosi idiom and it exposes the substitution-frame (courtesy for bribe) rather than concealing it, making the acknowledgment-as-courtesy move audibly like a translation rather than a register-native formulation, which is the one thing Citadel composure is not supposed to sound like.

**Decision:** REVISE.

The seam is load-bearing. *Have his courtesy* is the only phrase in the round-2 line that reads as paraphrase rather than register. GRRM's Westeros uses *receive, accept, take note of, thank for the kindness, mark the courtesy* — *have* in the abstract-noun-as-object sense (*have his courtesy*) does not occur. The seam is correct: the *have* exposes the substitution. A Citadel man closing a refusal does not telegraph the substitution; he performs the substitution so cleanly that the substituted-for thing is never legible. *Have his courtesy* leaves a fingerprint.

The pulp-enthusiast's *Good day to you* push is a soft challenge that I partially absorb in the revision — the closer can be sharper without breaking register. The worm-canon-pedant's standing-of-the-Citadel challenge is real but is also outside the line's scope: M-3 is a refusal, not a treatise on the Citadel's institutional reach. The Citadel-independence claim is the move the card prescribes (maester card §Direct samples — *The Conclave speaks for the Citadel on such matters. I serve the Citadel.*). The persona card §Hard Fences specifies the maester does not stage faction and deflects with institutional formula; that is the register-correct refusal whether or not the claim is, in the wider canon, fragile.

**Draft A**
**Line:** "Tell your lord his kindness is marked, and I thank him for it. The assessment is set down. The record is the maester's — by the chain, and by no other warrant. I trust the lord understands."
**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Tell your lord his kindness is marked* — Westerosi-idiomatic acknowledgment of the bribe-as-courtesy without the *have* substitution; *marked* is in-register (maesters mark, note, set down)
- *I thank him for it* — Saxon-shaped, register-native
- *The assessment is set down* — Saxon morphology, register-locked maester (overlay card §Anglo-Saxon prefixes; maester card §Direct samples — *set it down clearly*)
- *The record is the maester's* — institutional-third-person; the office, not the man (maester card §Register markers — *the institutional we / the chain speaks; the man does not*)
- *By the chain, and by no other warrant* — chain-as-authority closer; *no other warrant* is institutional-legal Saxon; refuses Bracken without naming Bracken
- *I trust the lord understands* — Citadel-composed closer with edge; addresses the pulp-enthusiast push that *Good day to you* was inert; carries political weight without abandoning politeness
- Polite-brief stack: four short sentences; no em-dash spine; em-dash used once
- No addressee-grammar for the rider (no *ser*, no honorific) — the maester is speaking past the courier
**Why rejected:** *I trust the lord understands* is sharper than *Good day to you* but the *I trust* construction nudges into a personal-stake register the maester does not occupy — he is not trusting anything; the institution is. It also faintly threatens, which the persona card §Hard Fences forbids: *He carries no violence and will not produce any.* The edge wants to come from the institutional formula itself, not from a personal undertaking.

**Draft B**
**Line:** "You may tell your lord his kindness is noted, and the maester thanks him for it. The assessment is set down. The record is the Citadel's, and the chain answers for it — by no other warrant. The day is yours, as is the road."
**Card features demonstrated:**
- *You may tell your lord his kindness is noted* — Westerosi-idiomatic; *noted* is the maester's reflex verb (persona card §Action Menu — *Note the result in the smaller notebook*); resolves the seam by removing *have* and replacing it with the maester's signature operation
- *The maester thanks him for it* — institutional-third-person; the man does not thank, the office does (maester card §Register markers)
- *The assessment is set down* — Saxon morphology (overlay card)
- *The record is the Citadel's, and the chain answers for it* — institutional-we and chain-as-authority compounded; *answers for it* is Saxon-legal in-register (overlay card §Anglo-Saxon prefixes — *answer-craft* family)
- *By no other warrant* — institutional-legal Saxon; refuses Bracken's claim without naming Bracken
- *The day is yours, as is the road* — closer; replaces inert *Good day to you* with a register-locked dismissal that is sharper without threat. *The road* names the rider's actual instruction (he is to leave) without telling him to leave; classic maester deflection — *he does not say "go"; he says "the road is yours"*. The closer absorbs the pulp push.
- No addressee-grammar; the rider is a courier, the maester speaks past him
- Polite-brief, four sentences; em-dash used once, not as spine
- No *I*, no personal stake; the man does not own the refusal, the chain does (persona card §Voice — *Authority without announcement*)
- Faith-form absent; the refusal is institutional, not religious — correct register for a chain-claim against a noble (maester card §Vocabulary — *partisan vocabulary refused*)

**CHOSEN: Draft B.**

Draft B removes *have his courtesy* (the seam), removes the personal-stake *I* (Draft A's smaller seam), sharpens the closer without threatening, and increases the institutional-third-person density. The chain is named once; the Citadel is named once; the man's *I* is named never. This is the register the persona card §Voice prescribes for an institutional refusal under pressure: *the chain speaks; the man does not.*

## Authoring notes

- **Seam disposition:** M-1 revise (spatial ambiguity was real, and the test-mechanic was being mis-staged through Taylor's hand instead of her eyes). M-2 defend (the seam mis-locates the work — composure is the register, not a performance the line should crack). M-3 revise (*have his courtesy* was the one paraphrase-fingerprint in the round-2 maester batch).
- **What sharpened:** M-1's revised line names the maester's finger as the deictic anchor and reroutes the test through the gaze (which is what the persona card prescribes). M-3's revised line replaces the paraphrase-verb *have* with the maester's signature operation *noted*, drops the personal *I* in favor of the institutional third-person, and replaces an inert closer with a register-locked dismissal (*the day is yours, as is the road*).
- **What was held:** M-2 in full. The pivot-as-structure is the maester's move; surfacing the dramatic-irony or the composure-as-performance would put him out of register, against the persona card's explicit forbidden registers.
- **Chain naming:** Now distributed M-1 (none, chain is doing the talking by the institutional vocabulary alone), M-2 (none, the Faith-form does the institutional work for the dead-naming), M-3 (named once and made to *answer for* the record). Same total chain-density as round 2; better placement.
- **Em-dash discipline:** Each chosen line uses the em-dash exactly once, never as the structural spine. Polite-brief, polite-brief held throughout.
- **Anti-pattern check:** No clinical English (*the assessment is complete* refused for *set down*); no Latinate operating verbs; no semicolon-spine; no *I am a maester* register; no folksy warmth; no Faith-pious overflow. *Have his courtesy* removed. *Read it back* refused at draft stage.
