# Phase 4 Defense — Edric @57 (feeling-flags)

## @57 verdict: REVISE-FORM-AND-VERB

The Phase 2 entry was:

```
3 @57 edric-cray: his hands settle at his sides and his weight finds the back foot before the step | expressed: partial
```

Two of three Phase-3 attack lanes land hard enough to require revision. One lane (cross-facet triple-coverage) is held by contract but warrants a stitcher note. Re-justification follows.

---

### Lane 1 — "finds" (AP16): REVISE.

The Phase-2 defense ("finds = literal weight-shift locator / biomechanical") cannot be sustained on inspection. The hostile reading is correct on the merits:

- Working-language for weight-distribution is **shifts / settles / moves / presses / draws / takes / sinks onto / rocks back onto**. These verbs describe the mechanical process of mass transfer without animating the mass.
- "Finds" is the standard narrative vocabulary for **searching and locating**. The collocation "the foot finds its purchase" / "the hand finds the rail" is familiar — but in those constructions the *agent* (foot, hand) is the limb performing the search; "finds" attributes a small purposive seeking to a body part already understood as actor-of-its-own-motion.
- "His weight finds the back foot" inverts that: the **weight** is the subject doing the finding, the **back foot** is the destination being discovered. Weight is non-agent (a quantity, not an actor). Attributing seek-and-locate to weight is a figure — small, but a figure. AP16's net catches "original (non-idiom) metaphors that picture the feeling" through agency-attribution to non-agents. The cleanest case in the rubric's anti-pattern set is "the floor pulled at his shoes" — same shape: non-agent given verb-of-volition. "Weight finds back foot" is in the same family, lower amplitude.
- The fact that no feeling is named through the figure does not rescue it. AP16 bans the figure when it is deployed for feeling-show, regardless of whether the named feeling appears. The figure's purpose at @57 is to render Edric's exit-check **as something the body's mass is doing under cognition** — that purpose IS feeling-show by figure.

Revision: **takes**. Working-language for weight-distribution; verb-of-position-change without animating the mass. "His weight takes the back foot" = his weight goes onto / rests on / is borne by the back foot. No agency attributed. Cleanly biomechanical. Fallback options if "takes" reads light: **settles onto**, **shifts to**. "Takes" is preferred for register reasons (low, plain, no softening) and because it does not echo Mira's gaze-tell ("tracks") or Taylor's shoulder-set ("go down and back").

### Lane 2 — two-clause form: REVISE to single clause.

The rubric's content-shape specification is **somatic-tell-one-clause**. "His hands settle at his sides AND his weight finds the back foot before the step" is two independent clauses joined by "and." The Phase-3 reading is correct: this is two clauses, not one compound somatic event, regardless of whether both clauses share an unstated subject (the body executing the exit-check).

The pulp-enthusiast's Phase-2 advisory pre-flagged this and offered the stitcher option ("either clause works alone"). That is the right move; the form rule pulls it forward to authoring rather than render-time.

Selection between the two clauses:

- **Hands-clause** ("his hands settle at his sides") — card-faithful (§Look "always slightly aware of where his hands are"). Solid. But "settle at his sides" reads as the resolution of an unspecified prior hand-position; on the page in this episode, the hands have not been on stage as a register. Firing the hands-awareness signature on its first appearance, in a clause that refers to a return-to-default, asks the audience to read the default position as significant without the contrasting prior. The hand-awareness signature lands harder when it is held for a beat where the hands have been doing something specific and then resolve.
- **Weight-clause** ("his weight takes the back foot before the step") — card-faithful (§Look "stillness of someone running calculations" rendered as weight-distribution; §Signature §1 "the exit-check first" — the back-foot is the body's pre-registration of the exit, the weight already in the direction the calculation has chosen). The "before the step" tag carries the **calculation-runs-through-body-before-act** which is the entire point of the fire. This clause is the structural carrier.
