## Proto-line discipline

> Detailed episode planning decides the subject, object, verb of each action. Bullet points (proto-lines) are at max a single sentence of subject verb object construction. Adjectives and adverbs used sparingly if at all.

> The bulk of the previous 'shoot' effort will now be spent to ensure the proto-lines (bullet points) are the bones of a chapter. Critical that at this time only minimal sentences exist and that each proto-line is only a single sentence. Subject verb object pattern, no fragments or modifiers or statements.

## Multi-pass review

> Review of proto-lines in several passes, each pass a different purpose [delete, re-arrange, constraint-check, behavior-check, entertainment-check, ...] maybe others, maybe in different orders, maybe more than once.

## Dialogue marker in proto-lines

> In places where a character speaks, instead of including the spoken lines, the bullet-point will be `<character-identifier> speaks to <subjects-identifier> about [abbreviated content list] for [speaker's objective list]`.

## Dialogue as its own phase

> With planning detailed and locked in, move to dialogue. This part deserves its own section really, but objectively, if we know what each dialogue is meant to achieve, and if we know how the speaker should sound, then we can assemble the dialogue independently of everything else. We don't even need immersed impersonators for this, just the character card, the character memory, a sampling of character dialect, then the critic audience and a coach. We go through a character's entirety of screen time and collect all dialogue for that episode, edit and revise with several passes, continuing until all checks are passed, then progressing to the next character. These dialogue lines are kept separate from the proto-lines and from each other. They should be citable from the proto-lines. Or rather, the proto-lines should have a pointer to dialogue line.

## Facets

> At this point we move to the next phase, not yet assembling proto-lines and dialogue, but instead beginning to consider other facets. Each facet (and dialogue could probably be considered a facet too) is created independently and is citable to proto-lines.

> Possible facets (flesh out in planning, could be added to or modified):
> - **tensometer**: at each bullet point, how tense is the moment, scale 1-3
> - **interest flags**: at each bullet point, what is interesting to audience
> - **interest flags**: at each bullet point, what is interesting to narrator
> - **memory flags**: at each bullet point, does the narrator have a call-back
> - **loudness flags**: anywhere volume spikes or drops
> - **feeling flags**: what is felt by non-narrator and would it be expressed
> - **metaphor flags**: comparisons, similes, allegories, etc. limited and almost never used in end state unless dark humor or memory callback
> - **state updates**: where does state change, capture at protoline citation
> - **vibes updates**: where does vibes change, capture at protoline citation

## Cross-facet consistency pass

> By this point the protolines have many references to external files (not in same document as protolines). All facets and dialogue should be considered comprehensively to catch inconsistencies between facets, states, protolines, dialogue, etc. Similar to a constraint check. Delete inconsistencies. Delete boring. Delete inane. (Deletions happen in proto-lines document by removing either line or citation.)

## Stitch and polish

> At this point, the shoot is considered done. Next phase will be stitching the best most applicable facets and dialogue and protolines into single doc. This doc is then revised until polished.
