## What the user-supplied requirements added

### Pre-Phase-0 framing (handoff message)

The user named memory-flags as the **licensing layer** for metaphor + callback in the stitched output. Per `schemas/facet.schema.md` §metaphor: "sparse by design — almost never used in end state unless dark humor or memory callback." Memory-flag fires are the *callback* half of that license; without a memory-flag co-citation, the stitcher produces no metaphor and no callback on a beat regardless of how charged it reads.

Plus the **inverted-tens-density** rule: memory-flags fire in tens=1 zones and tens=2 trailing-edge zones; default-forbidden at tens=3. This is the *inverse* of narrator-interest's tens-alignment. Memory-flags is the quiet-beat facet; reflection happens in the gaps, not in the strikes.

These two framings are the rubric's structural addition relative to prior facets. The licensing-layer reshapes memory-flags from a passive callback-tracker into an active gating layer for the entire stitched output's figurative content. The inverted-tens-density rule reshapes the curve-shape rubric from peak-aligned (narrator-interest) to gap-aligned.

### Mid-Phase-4 nudges (five iterative tightenings)

Mid-Phase-4 the user supplied five additional rubric constraints:

1. **"Apply flags sparingly."** Sparsity floor revised from 5–12% to **1–5%**.
2. **"More than 1 a scene is too many."** Hard per-scene cap; doubled-register exemplars on a single beat forbidden.
3. **"Meaningful to both narrator and audience."** The audience-meaningful gate. Interior-only foreknowledge fires (Westerosi monuments invisible to non-fluent readers) fail by default.
4. **"At a moment of realization, grim humor, social commentary, painting characterization."** The four functional-register jobs a fire serves; ≥2 of 4 required.
5. **"If there isn't several justifications for attaching flag, deny."** Multi-justification gate. Default to silence.

These together pulled the file from 8 entries (Phase-4-first-pass) to 3 entries (Phase-4-re-run). The pipeline absorbed the rubric change in Phase 4 alone — no Phase 1/2/3 rework needed.

The five tightenings collectively transform memory-flags from "a sparse facet at 5-12%" to "the sparsest facet at 1-5%, gated four ways." This reflects the licensing-layer's structural function: a layer that gates figurative content for the entire stitched output must be far sparser than any registration facet.

## What worked

1. **The five-phase pattern absorbs mid-tuning rubric retunes cleanly.** Same trajectory shape (~19% baseline → ~85% writer-fork → 100% post-revise) as prior runs, with the addition of a mid-Phase-4 retune that triggered Phase 4 re-run only. Phase 1/2/3 artifacts remained valid; the rubric change lived in Phase 4 file shape and Phase 5 audit. The pipeline pattern tolerates rubric tightening if the tightening lands before Phase 5; later tightening would require restart.

2. **Licensing-layer-as-gate is the right structural framing.** Memory-flags is now positioned as the gating boolean for the metaphor facet's stitcher-licensed entries. The schema's §metaphor "sparse by design" rule plus the audience-meaningful gate at memory-flags together guarantee that any shipped metaphor inherits both the sparsity discipline and the audience-meaningfulness gate by construction. Downstream metaphor-facet tuning will validate this consumer-side.

3. **Inverted-tens-density is the right curve-shape rubric.** Phase 1 baseline fired 4 entries on tens=3 beats (peak-density-on-tens-3); Phase 5 fires zero. The naive instinct (transferred from narrator-interest) breaks memory-flags entirely. The rubric explicitly inverts the tens-alignment; the writer-fork honors it on first pass.

4. **Behavior-pack-as-rubric-authority transfers from narrator-interest.** The base + variant cards' §"Memory monuments" sections are THE authority. Monument extension goes through margit. The rubric is POV-character-specific. Pattern transfer from narrator-interest is clean.

5. **Audience-meaningful gate produces structural finding.** Westerosi monuments are by-construction interior-foreknowledge (Taylor knows what's coming; nobody else does). Under audience-meaningful gating, Westerosi-monument fires require a beat where the audience also feels something concrete (e.g., when Aldric mentions the Conquest aloud; when smallfolk speak Harrenhal's curse). s01e01's proto-line file does not surface Westerosi-content in dialogue/setting strongly enough; the foreknowledge-clamps Taylor experiences happen on procedural beats invisible to non-fluent readers. **Result: s01e01 ships single-register (Earth-Bet only).** This is rubric-correct, not a coverage failure. Per-season doubled-register check pending future episodes.

6. **Multi-justification gate produces honest fires.** Each of the 3 shipped entries defends with 5-6 converging justifications: monument-trigger + spine-present + audience-meaningful + ≥2 functional-register hits + scene-eligible. The discipline of naming several justifications forces the author to either earn the fire or refuse. Most of the Phase 4 first-pass strips were single-justification or two-justification entries that didn't survive the gate.

7. **Per-scene cap forces selection across competing fires.** At @73 the doubled-register exemplar (Harrenhal-precinct + locker-displacement) was two strong fires on one beat under Phase 2 rubric; under per-scene cap, only one survives. Selection criterion: audience-meaningful + multi-justification ranks. Locker-displacement (universally legible body-memory) wins over Harrenhal-precinct (ASOIAF-fluency-required); the Westerosi register loses one entry, the file becomes single-register.

8. **Hybrid mechanic + dialect-audience review with independent gates held.** Phase 2 dialect MIXED on @33 monument-target was real and load-bearing; Phase 4 retarget (cluster gloss) resolved cleanly. Dialect audience reactivated for memory-flags fidelity-only without bleeding into mechanic-domain adjudication. Same architecture as narrator-interest.

9. **Hard fences held end-to-end.** Zero Earth-Bet proper-noun leaks in description fields across baseline, Phase 2, Phase 4, Phase 5. The variant card's "Hard fence: never named in any Westerosi-set scene" rule is internalized by the writer-fork (and even by the rubric-blind naive baseline; the schema's permissive target-reference field allows naming, and the description discipline is independently maintained).

## Co-deployment note

Per dialogue, loc-state, tensometer, narrator-interest, and state-updates packages: writer + reviewer ships as a co-deployed unit. For memory-flags, the unit has four components reflecting the hybrid review architecture:

- **Writer:** dialogue-writer fork for the POV character, in monument-licensing output mode. Loads: base behavior card (§"Memory monuments / Earth-Bet"), variant behavior card (§"Memory monuments / Westerosi + local-current"), persona card, locked tensometer (inverted-tens-density check), locked narrator-interest (mandatory spine co-citation), locked location-state (soft alignment), this rubric, the corpus-selection note. Two-pass authoring: per-beat → file-shape audit. Per-beat decisions evaluate the multi-justification gate (monument-trigger + spine-present + audience-meaningful + ≥2 functional-register + scene-eligible); file-shape audit verifies sparsity (1-5%), per-scene cap (≤1 per scene), inverted tens-density (zero peaks), hard-fence held, spine 100%.

- **Reviewer (mechanic auditor):** single mechanic auditor with the rubric as authority. Per-entry verdicts (CORRECT / INCORRECT-{axis-or-anti-pattern}); per-skip verdicts (SKIP-CORRECT / SKIP-MISSED); curve verdict (SHAPE-OK / SHAPE-FAIL); cross-facet contract pre-ship check (tensometer-inversion, NI spine, behavior-pack authority). Voice fidelity is checked at the axis level but the reviewer does not have the dialect audience's calibration to base-card register samples.

- **Reviewer (dialect audience, voice-fidelity-only mode):** worm-canon-pedant primary (Earth-Bet monument fidelity); dark-fantasy-reader and pulp-enthusiast secondary (Westerosi-monument fidelity + figurative-reach taste). Per-entry verdicts: VOICE-OK / VOICE-FAIL / VOICE-MIXED with citation to behavior-pack §. Domain restricted to monument-fidelity and displacement-cue voice; does not adjudicate firing decision, quiet-beat anchor, licensing-discipline curve, audience-meaningfulness gate (mechanic does), per-scene cap, or cross-facet contract.

- **Verdict combination:** mechanic + dialect verdicts are independent gates. Both must pass for ACCEPT. They cannot substitute. Phase 5 confirmed independent-signal architecture working: mechanic catches structural / discipline / curve faults; dialect catches voice / monument-target asymmetries. Phase 2's @33 MIXED was dialect-caught and mechanic-missed; Phase 4 retarget resolved both.

- **Adversarial pass (Phase 3):** same mechanic auditor in hostile mode, one strongest seam per entry plus one curve-level seam plus one cross-facet contract seam plus one licensing-layer-integrity seam. Catches what passes naive mechanic review and surfaces structural seams worth Phase 4 attention.

The four parts (writer + mechanic + dialect-audience + adversarial-pass) are not separable. The writer's affirmative-citation discipline (multi-justification) only works because the mechanic auditor tests citations. The dialect audience's voice-fidelity verdict only works because the writer produces entries that *demonstrate* monument-fidelity rather than merely avoid violations. The mechanic auditor's strict rubric is only meaningful because the writer can produce entries that survive multi-justification ceiling-defense. The adversarial pass surfaces what the other three accept.

The dialect audience IS part of the memory-flags pipeline — fidelity-only, voice-only, no scope creep into mechanic adjudication. Calibration to base-card §"Memory monuments" preserved across the run.

The mid-tuning-run rubric retune (5 user-supplied tightenings landed mid-Phase-4) is the structural addition for this facet. The pipeline's tolerance of mid-tuning rubric changes is a load-bearing finding: rubric tightening through Phase 4 lives in the writer-fork's response (Phase 4 re-run); rubric tightening at Phase 5 or post-ship would require a tuning restart. The natural place to absorb iterative rubric tightening is mid-Phase-4 — the writer-fork is the tightest contractor for rubric changes.
