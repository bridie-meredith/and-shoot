Era: 120 AC (HotD-era) — slightly more *shall*, slightly more inversion, *moons* and *fortnight* native.

### NC-1 (speaker: census-officer)
**Intent:** Open the yard. Establish the lord's commission and require wards / unattached / unguardianed to come forward. Public-authority register, audible at carry-distance.
**Rung:** functionary-courtly (working a yard under a lord's-hand commission)

**Draft A — chosen.**
**Line:** "By commission of the castellan, and under the lord's hand, you shall hear me. All wards within the bounds of this sept, all souls without a holding, and any man or woman that cannot name a guardian seated in the cure — shall come forward to the board, and shall be set down by name, before this yard is quit. The roll closes at midday. None shall be entered after."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- Periodic structure: long opening establishes the conditions (*By commission... and under... you shall hear me*); the load-bearing main clause arrives after the tricolon.
- Tricolon: *all wards... all souls... any man or woman...* — parallel clauses, not raised volume.
- *Shall*-modal three times (*shall hear, shall come forward, shall be set down*) — the commission-register *shall*, not *will*.
- Honor / lord's-right vocabulary: *by commission of the castellan, under the lord's hand, the bounds of this sept, the cure*. Authority sourced to lord and Faith, not to procedure.
- Saxon/courtly compound: *holding, guardian, midday*. *Quit* (Anglo-Saxon) for *finished*.
- Time-vocabulary: *at midday* (not *at noon*, not *by twelve o'clock*).
- Avoids modern compliance-English: no "labor-eligibility," no "register process." The instrument is the lord's hand.

**Draft B — rejected (too short, loses periodic shape).**
"You shall hear me. By the lord's hand I am commissioned, and the roll is open: every ward, every unguardianed soul, every man without a holding to name — to the board, before midday."

### NC-2 (speaker: plumms-man)
**Intent:** Single curt instruction to a child ward holding a raven on her arm: send the bird. Cold, no honorific, expects compliance.
**Rung:** mid-functionary (Plumm's circuit-man, commission from a knight, addressing a ward downward)

**Draft A — chosen.**
**Line:** "Loose her, child. The bird shall fly, and you shall stand back from the perch."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Shall*-modal twice — the instrument of command in noble-functionary register, paired structurally (*the bird shall... you shall...*).
- Parallel clauses (two-part, the courtly minimum-parallel) without raising volume. Cadence under pressure: tightens, does not loosen.
- *Loose her* — Anglo-Saxon imperative, not Latinate *release*.
- Address-by-station, no honorific: *child* — placement of the addressee in the procedural hierarchy. Persona's voice tell: placeholder descriptions over names.
- Restrained register — flat, procedural, no warmth, no menace. Authority is in the form, not the volume.
- Avoids modern functionary-English: no "step away from the perch, please" — the *shall* and the bare imperative carry it.

**Draft B — rejected (too elaborate; this is a single curt order, persona voice is *says no more than needed*).**
"The raven flies now. Loose her, and step you back — the perch is not yours to hold."

### NC-3 (speaker: district-recorder)
**Intent:** Deny Rowan's wardship claim on procedural grounds (provisional appointment, protocol requires permanent) while continuing to write. The refusal *itself* is a courtesy formula — polite procedural finality.
**Rung:** institutional-bureaucratic in courtly form (eleven years in post; the form animated)

**Draft A — chosen.**
**Line:** "The claim is heard, septon, and the claim is refused. A provisional appointment does not seat a man in the cure; the protocol shall not recognize it, and I shall not enter it against the line. Bring the letter of permanent appointment, and the matter shall be heard again."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Shall*-modal three times (*shall not recognize, shall not enter, shall be heard again*) — the procedural *shall* with the weight of the ledger behind it.
- Tricolon by clause-pairing: *the claim is heard / the claim is refused / the matter shall be heard again* — opening, ruling, closure. Structural parallelism, not emphatic.
- Periodic structure across two sentences: conditions named, judgment delivered at the close of each.
- Judgment-ownership: *I shall not enter it against the line* — the verb of judgment owned, in the recorder's flat first-person.
- Westerosi register over modern HR-speak: *seat a man in the cure*, *enter against the line*, *the protocol*, *the letter of permanent appointment*. Faith-and-office vocabulary, not "credentials" or "documentation."
- Honor-vocabulary substrate: *the cure* (a soul-charge, not a job description); *the matter* (indirection — courtly habit of not naming the sensitive thing directly).
- Address-by-station: *septon* — full form, uncontracted, calibrated to standing. Polite finality without warmth.
- Avoids em-dash + semicolon-spine. Uses two semicolons (the recorder's hand, not Taylor's voice) but the chassis is parallel-clause and periodic, not the dash-pivot.

**Draft B — rejected (closer to the recorder's persona-voice short-declarative, but loses the courtly periodic shape the rung calls for).**
"The claim is refused. A provisional letter does not seat a man in the cure. Return with the letter of permanent appointment; the protocol shall hear the claim then, and not before."

### NC-4 (speaker: ser-harwick-plumm)
**Intent:** Answer Rowan's appeal to Faith jurisdiction by re-grounding on the procedural requirement the recorder named — a successor *seated in the cure* (hand entered against the line in a recorder's session before the term expires). A letter of permanent appointment names a man to a cure but does not seat him. Periodic, tricolon, judgment-ownership.
**Rung:** knight-administrator (highest register; the calibration target)

**Draft A — chosen.**
**Line:** "The good septon mistakes the ground, and I would not have him stand upon it longer than is needful. The recorder did not speak of jurisdiction; the recorder spoke of seating — of a hand set against the line, in a recorder's session, before the term is run out — and a letter of appointment, however lawful, however solemn, however well-witnessed by the motherhouse at Stoney Sept, names a man to a cure but does not seat him in it. I find the protocol satisfied in its plain reading; I find the cure unseated; and I find the matter to lie, this morning, where the recorder set it down."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- **Periodic sentence (sentence two):** the long opening establishes the conditions through three nested *however*-clauses (*however lawful, however solemn, however well-witnessed*); the load-bearing main clause arrives at the close — *names a man to a cure but does not seat him in it*. Textbook periodic structure.
- **Tricolon, twice.** The *however*-tricolon inside the conditions; and the closing tricolon of judgment-ownership: *I find... I find... I find...* — anaphora as rhetorical structure, not emphatic.
- **Judgment-ownership three times:** *I find the protocol satisfied / I find the cure unseated / I find the matter to lie...* — the noble's verb of judgment, owned. Cadence locks the line as a knight-administrator's ruling, not a notary's citation.
- **Subjunctive intact:** *I would not have him stand upon it longer than is needful* — *I would have* (subjunctive softening of command); *needful* (Saxon-suffix, in-register).
- **Inversion / current high register:** *and a letter of appointment... names a man to a cure but does not seat him in it* — the construction *names... but does not seat* is a courtly correction-pattern (the difference between two verbs is the whole ruling).
- **Honor / Faith / lord's-right vocabulary:** *the good septon, the cure, seating, the term, the motherhouse at Stoney Sept, the matter.* The courtly indirection (*the matter*) is current.
- **Westerosi institutional register, not modern compliance-English.** The line refuses *jurisdiction* explicitly as Rowan's frame and pivots to *seating* — the act of being placed in the cure, hand against the line. The mechanism is articulated as Faith-and-office craft, not as bureaucracy.
- **GRRM mannerisms (overlay), used naturally not pasted:** *needful* (Saxon-suffix); *the term is run out* (idiomatic, not *expires*); *this morning* (placement-in-time within the proceeding); the indirect *the recorder set it down* (Saxon verb-particle, not *recorded*).
- **No em-dash + semicolon-spine chassis.** The em-dashes used (*— of a hand set against the line, in a recorder's session, before the term is run out —*) are parenthetical apposition specifying the prior noun (*seating*), not voice-pivot dashes. The sentence chassis is periodic-with-tricolon, not dash-driven.
- **Rung-distinction from NC-1 and NC-3:** Plumm does not invoke *the lord's hand* directly (NC-1, the functionary, must) and does not speak as the protocol itself (NC-3, the recorder, is); Plumm reads the ruling, names what was done, and owns the judgment. He is the knight standing above both.

**Draft B — rejected (cleaner, but loses the third *I find* and weakens the calibration target's tricolon).**
"The good septon mistakes the ground. The recorder did not speak of jurisdiction; he spoke of seating — of a hand entered against the line, in a recorder's session, before the term is out — and a letter, however lawful and however solemn, names a man to a cure but does not seat him in it. I find the protocol satisfied, and the cure unseated, and the matter to lie where the recorder set it down."
