### Shippability Assessment

**The pipeline is substantially ready to ship. It is not fully bulletproof. Here is what the user needs to know before relying on it.**

**What the pipeline does well:**

The round-trip demonstrated a writer that responds to seam-pressure at the correct grain. When the seam named a structural failure (architecture invisible as a slip; spatial ambiguity in a deictic; *have* as a paraphrase-fingerprint), the revision addressed the named seam rather than shifting the failure elsewhere. The three Taylor revisions in particular demonstrate a writer that can relocate a card-demonstration from engineering (invisible to the crossover reader) to surface lexis (a single wrong word). That relocation is the hard move, and the writer executed it across TH-1, TH-2, TH-3, and TH-4 without losing the card chassis in any of them.

The defenses for NC-3, NC-4, and M-2 are the pipeline's proof-of-concept for a second capability: the writer can distinguish between a seam that identifies a real failure and a seam that identifies an intended feature. NC-4's *however*-tricolon is not a structural gap; it is the knight-administrator's move. M-2's pivot is not a missed escalation; it is what the card forbids pursuing. The defended lines held under hostile re-examination.

**Where the pipeline has remaining risk:**

**Risk 1: Defense quality is variable.** The NC-3 defense (passive-institutional / first-person-owned toggle as eleven-years-in-post precision) is the weakest of the three defenses — it is convincing on the character logic but does not fully resolve whether a reader at speed experiences the toggle as precision or as register-drift. The defense is rationalization-adjacent in a way the NC-4 and M-2 defenses are not. In the current round, this is accepted because the card citation is real and the character logic holds. In a longer project, NC-3-style defenses should be flagged for the writer to monitor: the line works, but the defense did not fully close the seam.

**Risk 2: Cross-line dependencies require explicit management.** The TH-3/S-2 cross-line pair was the highest-stakes coordination problem in this round trip. It resolved correctly because both writer forks addressed the dependency in parallel and their chosen lines were designed to load-bear each other. In a longer project with more interspersed cross-behavior dependencies, the pipeline requires explicit identification of these pairs at the planning stage, not the defense stage. The round-3 defense documents handled this well — the septon writer's S-2 revision explicitly cited "with TH-3 now reading 'Twelve. Counted.'" — but that coordination is load-bearing for the reader's experience and should be formalized.

**Risk 3: The "invisible to crossover reader" failure mode required three rounds to resolve in Taylor lines.** Round 2 accepted TH-1 on the rubric (parallel structure as engineering-slip) and rounds 2's reviewer identified the crossover-reader seam correctly in round 3. This means the pipeline's v2 strict rubric did not catch the crossover-reader problem on the first pass — the rubric needed the adversarial pressure of round 3 to surface it. For a shipped pipeline that does not include a round-3 adversarial phase, this is a gap: the line will pass v2 strict and fail for a significant portion of the actual readership. The recommendation is to add a crossover-reader check to the v2 rubric as a named fourth criterion alongside the existing three: *does the card-demonstration require source-material fluency to perceive, or is it accessible to a reader with only Westerosi fluency?*

**Risk 4: Scene-architecture obligations exposed at the line level are not resolved by the line.** M-2's reception (does Taylor parse the death-registration formula?), SF-3's surveillance-gap (does Taylor process Mira's warning?), NC-2's adjacency pressure (what does Taylor's response register look like after "child"?), and S-3's continuity obligation (do Taylor's post-S-3 lines account for being publicly named in a jurisdictional dispute?) are all real obligations that the individual line defenses correctly identify as outside their scope. The pipeline's current design resolves these at the Taylor-fork level during the shoot. That is the correct architecture — but it requires the showrunner to track the obligations and ensure the Taylor fork has loaded them. If the showrunner's memory management misses a scene-architecture obligation exposed at the line level, the continuity failure will not be caught until the wrap review.
