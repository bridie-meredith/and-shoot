# Audience Review — Originals v2 (Strict Rubric)

**Rubric change from v1:** A line ACCEPTS only if it affirmatively demonstrates at least one signature feature of the assigned behavior card AND does not violate it. A line that is merely inoffensive — a short fragment with no register markers, a generic confirmation any English speaker could utter, a one-syllable answer — REJECTS. The card must be doing visible work on the line's surface area. If it isn't, reject.

Persona lenses:
- **DFR** (dark-fantasy-reader): Is the register atmosphere earning itself? Does this line carry Planetos-specific grain?
- **PE** (pulp-enthusiast): Does this line land and move? Does it complicate the board?
- **WCP** (worm-canon-pedant): Does Taylor sound like Taylor? Is the doubled register present? Are we getting the right behavior card in the right speaker?

### What failed (the reject pattern)

**The uniform failure mode across all five cards:** Short declaratives and fragments that could come from any speaker in any register. "Rowan." "Disposition." "Mm." "She is." "Yes, ser." "I am going now." "East. Toward the hall." "Send the bird." — these were authored at the same syntactic skeleton level regardless of who is speaking. A corpus where the character names are removed would be very difficult to sort back into their registers on these lines. That is the problem.

**The fragment trap:** Fragments can be on-card (Taylor's "Twelve." would earn if the full calculation sentence preceded it on the same line; Mira's "before the sun's off the wall" is on-card because it is a smallfolk time-marker embedded in a full thought). But fragments that carry no register information — direction words, names, affirmatives, noises — demonstrate nothing.

**The administrative-vs.-noble-courtly confusion:** Census-officer and most plumms-man lines are not noble-courtly. They are administrative-functionary. The corpus assigned them to the noble-courtly card but the register of their lines is a different class: they use bureaucratic vocabulary, not Latinate-for-elegance; they command without courtesy-frame; they are missing *shall*, *I find*, periodic structure, honor-vocabulary. If the card assignment is correct, the lines need to be rewritten. If the lines are correct, the card assignment is wrong.

**The septon's observation lines:** Three of Rowan's lines (L54, L82, L95) and Osmynd's single line are plain investigative questions and plain observational statements. A septon's register does not disappear when he is gathering information — the card's *discernment as constant background* tells describe how a septon frames his attention even in neutral lines. These lines show no evidence of that framing.

**The maester's test instructions:** Most of the traveling-maester's corpus is test-instructions issued in their most stripped form. The card expects even the instructions to carry the polite-brief courtesy-wrap — "Perhaps you could" (L36) earns because it has the wrap; "Come and sit," "Which mark follows this one," "The sum at the foot — is it correct?" do not, because the wrap is stripped. The maester's register is not silent between the *yes, yes* moments.
