## PATTERNS — Systemic Failure Modes

The following failure modes appear across the corpus with enough frequency to constitute systemic problems, not isolated line failures.

---

### 1. The Administrative-English Register Bleed (corpus-wide, severe)

Nearly every speaker in every behavior category defaults at some point to contemporary administrative-bureaucratic English: "labor-eligibility," "cognitive assessment," "procedural grounds," "documented pattern of interest," "the clock runs on both from the filing," "is calendared at a date," "anomalies logged," "status classifications," "capability notations."

**Diagnosis:** The writer's voice is corporate English with Westerosi nouns swapped in. The skeleton of nearly every complex sentence in the corpus is a contemporary HR or legal document. This is not a vocabulary problem — it is a syntax-and-register problem. Westerosi administrative language, even at its most formal, operates through lord's-right claims, Honor-vocabulary, Faith-authority framing, and GRRM's Latinate-Anglo-Saxon compound vocabulary. It does not operate through compliance-process language.

**The test:** If the line could appear unchanged in a modern employee handbook, a court motion, or a project-status email, it is in the wrong register for every card in this corpus.

---

### 2. The Semicolon-Spine / Em-Dash Hinge on Every Register (corpus-wide)

The base Taylor-Hebert card uses the em-dash for sensory specification and the semicolon for habit-to-consequence chaining. These syntactic tools are bleeding across every register in the corpus — noble-courtly, smallfolk, septon, and even maester all reach for em-dash insertions and semicolon spines as sentence connectors.

**The problem:** The em-dash insertion is a base-Taylor voice tell. When Ser Harwick Plumm, a Westerosi knight-officer, produces "the notice has been filed at the recorder's hall this morning and the proceeding stands recorded; you will gather what the ward carries" — the semicolon-spine is the Taylor register, not the noble-courtly register. The noble-courtly card calls for subordinated periodic sentences, tricolon parallel structure, and *shall*-modal. It does not call for semicolons linking parallel imperatives.

**The smallfolk problem is the inverse:** The one smallfolk line in the corpus (Mira, s01e01:L9) produces an em-dash insertion in exactly the Taylor-base-register style, which is the dominant-voice contaminating the only class-register that should explicitly lack this tool.

---

### 3. Taylor's Adult Planning Register Delivered Unmasked (taylor-hebert-westeros, severe)

Five Taylor lines in the corpus deliver full adult tactical-planning sentences in the adult base register, to adults, in high-stakes public situations, without surrounding prose marking them as mask-slips:

- s01e04:L28 (second) — "the count of days against the wardship runs from the filing, not from the death"
- s01e04:L60 (second) — "the reversion clock had not formally started when the document left the register"
- s01e04:L82 — the twelve-day route analysis
- s01e05:L8 — "the next recorder's session in this district closes before any motherhouse letter can come back over that road"
- s01e01:L37 and L41 — operational witness-securing in a public yard

The card is unambiguous: long planning sentences delivered aloud to adults are the slip, not the default. The slip must be marked. If no surrounding prose marks it, the line is an unintended register failure. The corpus has produced a Taylor who speaks at adult base-register almost every time the stakes go up, which is the opposite of what the doubled register is supposed to do.

---

### 4. "GRRM Time-Vocabulary" is Entirely Absent (corpus-wide)

The GRRM-mannerisms card flags time-keeping vocabulary as the *highest-priority anchor*. Audit any Westerosi line that says *month, week, birthday* — these are register breaks. The corpus produces:

- "twelve days" (s01e04:L78, L82) — should be "twelve days" which is fine, but "nine days" (s01e04:L76) and "ten days" (s01e05:L88) also appear with no moon-count equivalents
- No "moons turn," no "sennight," no "fortnight," no "name-day" in any of the administrative lines
- "Within ten days of receipt" (s01e05:L88, Celtigar letter) — a formal Small Council commission would count in days (fine) but would not use the modern "within X days of receipt" legal-notice formula

The GRRM number-construction ("two-and-twenty") appears nowhere in the corpus. The compound-word habit is nearly absent. The corpus uses plain-numeral notation throughout.

---

### 5. The Septon Register is Stripped of Faith Vocabulary (westeros-septon)

The septon card's defining register markers are: "child," homiletic three-and-close, plain-Anglo moral closers, doctrinal vocabulary (*the Seven, the Mother, the Father, the Stranger, the Faith, sin, mercy, grace*), and pastoral versus doctrinal modes.

**The specific failure:** Septon Rowan has no homiletic rhythm, no plain-Anglo closer, no invocation of the Seven even in moments of doctrinal dispute where the card says the register *tightens* into doctrinal certainty. The Faith is entirely absent from the Faith-register speaker.

### 6. The Noble-Courtly Card's Defining Markers are Absent
