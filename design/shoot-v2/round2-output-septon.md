### S-1 (speaker: septon-dying-protector)
**Intent:** Osmynd, on his pallet, warns Taylor that a man has come asking after the rats. Each phrase costs breath; the warning carries pastoral warmth and the moral weight of *the rats* as a thing the Faith should not be silent about.

**Line (draft A — chosen):**
"Child. Sit by me. There is a man come down to the village... asking after the rats. Asking close. The Mother sees, child, what is in such asking. The Father judges it. We — we do not pretend it is nothing."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Child*-vocative used twice — pastoral universal address, dying septon to ward
- Homiletic three-and-close structure: *The Mother sees / The Father judges / we do not pretend it is nothing* — three short clauses, plain-Anglo closer
- Seven invoked by aspect (the Mother, the Father), correctly: Mother sees, Father judges
- Imperative-with-permission: *Sit by me* (the pastoral opening pattern from the card)
- Breath-cost visible in the syntax — clauses break at the comma, the half-repetition *Asking close* is the labor of speech, the *we — we* stutter is breath gathering
- Plain-Anglo moral closer: *we do not pretend it is nothing* — Saxon vocabulary, no Latinate
- Faith-conversational, not deposition register — the warning lives inside pastoral framing, not as a witness statement

**Line (draft B):**
"Hush, child. Hear me while I have it. A man is asking after the rats — close-asking, the way a man asks who already knows. The Father sees a man so. So must we. Keep within doors a sennight, child. The Mother is here."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- Comfort-register repetition (*Hush, child*) deployed institutionally
- Compound-formation reflex (*close-asking*) — GRRM register
- Time-vocabulary: *sennight*
- Three-and-close again, with Mother-comfort closer
- Seven by aspect, doctrinally clean

*Chosen: A.* The valedictory weight lands harder; the *we do not pretend it is nothing* is the moral weight Osmynd needs to spend his breath on. B is cleaner in cadence but less heavy.

### S-2 (speaker: septon-rowan)
**Intent:** Walking the eastern track. Taylor has just said *twelve* against Rowan's *nine*. He asks how an eleven-year-old ward knows the road to Stoney Sept is twelve days and not nine. Pastoral indirection — the question of a man who has just realized the ward in his keeping is not what she presents as. Septon's question, not interrogator's.

**Line (draft A — chosen):**
"Twelve, child. Now there is a number for a man to sit with. Tell me — for I have walked this road and you have not — how is it that an eleven-year-old ward of my sept comes to count it twelve, and the Father grant me wit to hear the answer."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Child*-vocative — pastoral universal, even mid-investigation
- Pastoral framing of an investigative question: not *how do you know* but *how is it that... comes to count it* — the soft passive a brother of the Faith reaches for
- Faith-aphorism opening: *a number for a man to sit with* — the septon's habit of treating an utterance as a thing to be weighed, not just data
- Self-located authority cited honestly: *I have walked this road and you have not* — refusal of false claim, the septon does not pretend not to know what he knows
- Father-invocation in the closer: *the Father grant me wit to hear the answer* — conditional comfort form (the septon does not assert what the gods will do; he asks)
- Parallel-coordinated, not dash-and-semicolon — the structure runs on *and* and on the comma list, which is the sermon-rhythm
- No notarial register: this is a man asking a question with the Father in the room, not a recorder setting up a discrepancy

**Line (draft B):**
"Twelve. Sit with me a moment, child, and tell me how the road counts twelve in your mouth and nine in mine. The Mother asks gentler than I do; let her be the one asking, then, and not me."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- Imperative-with-permission (*sit with me a moment*)
- *Child*-vocative
- Mother-aspect deployed correctly (mercy, gentleness)
- Pastoral self-effacement (the septon yielding the question to the Mother) — the *I have not the right* pattern in modified form
- The doubled-mouth construction (*twelve in your mouth and nine in mine*) is plainly Faith-conversational, not deposition

*Chosen: A.* The *Father grant me wit to hear the answer* is doctrinally correct (conditional, not assertive) and lands the moment as pastoral inquiry rather than challenge. B is gentler but less load-bearing for the scene's weight.

### S-3 (speaker: septon-rowan)
**Intent:** Public sept yard. Rowan asserts that ward succession in a matter touching the cure of a soul lies with the Faith, and that Stoney Sept's motherhouse holds the jurisdiction the recorder's hall has this morning declined. Doctrinal challenge to a knight, in front of the village. Faith-as-authority register, homiletic structure, plain-Anglo moral closer. Brother making a *theological* argument, not a notary citing a statute.

**Line (draft A — chosen):**
"Ser. Hear me before the yard, for it is the yard's business as well as ours. A child's keeping is not the recorder's matter when the keeping touches a soul; the cure of a soul is the Faith's, by the Father's law and not the king's. The motherhouse at Stoney Sept holds the seven-pointed star over this child, and your hall this morning has set its hand aside from her. So be it. The Faith will not."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- Homiletic three-and-close cadence: *A child's keeping... the cure of a soul... by the Father's law* — three weighted clauses, then the plain-Anglo closer *The Faith will not* lands the moral
- Faith-as-jurisdiction framed *doctrine first, law second* — the argument is about the cure of a soul, not about a procedural rule
- Seven invoked through *the seven-pointed star* (the symbol-and-book noun), used correctly
- Father-aspect: *by the Father's law* — judgment-aspect, doctrinally clean
- Plain-Anglo moral closer: *So be it. The Faith will not.* — five words, Saxon, the homily's habit of dropping to plain speech at the moment the moral lands
- Public address frame: *Hear me before the yard, for it is the yard's business as well as ours* — the parishioner-attention claim, septon-public register, not court oratory
- Compound-coordinated, not dash-driven; the spine is *and / when / by / and not* — parallel-coordinated, aphoristic
- *Cure of a soul* is the Faith's term of art, used twice deliberately — doctrinal precision over moralizing warmth
- Motherhouse and Stoney Sept named with proper Faith register (motherhouse is a canonical compound on the GRRM card)
- Refusal of secular faction-register: he does not say *I oppose the lord*; he says *the Faith will not* — institutional duty, not personal stance

**Line (draft B):**
"Ser, with your leave. The recorder's hall keeps lands and tallies, and the Faith keeps souls; this child's keeping is the second sort and not the first. The motherhouse at Stoney Sept holds her seven-pointed star, as it has held it. The Father judges what is owed and to whom. Set your hand aside from her, then, and the Faith will not."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *With your leave* — courtly-courtesy frame, refusal underneath firm (canonical card pattern)
- Two-domain parallel (lands-and-tallies / souls) — sermon-rhythm
- Seven-pointed star invoked
- Father-aspect: *judges what is owed and to whom*
- Plain-Anglo closer: *and the Faith will not*
- Doctrine-first / law-second order

*Chosen: A.* The *Hear me before the yard* opening makes the public-address theology explicit and gives the line the homiletic frame Rowan reaches for under doctrinal challenge. B is courtlier but slightly closer to the courtly-bureaucratic register the audience is told to reject; A is more clearly *brother of the Faith making a theological argument in a yard* and less *clerk citing jurisdictional grounds*.
