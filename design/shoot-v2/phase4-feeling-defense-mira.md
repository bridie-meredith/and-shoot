### Seam-1.1 — "tracks" etymology / register check

**The hostile case.** "Track" carries a hunting-and-pursuit etymology (Middle English *trak*, animal-trail / footprint; verbal sense of "follow by sign or trace" emerging from the noun). In contemporary usage applied to gaze, the verb has two attested registers: (a) working-language for purposeful directed eye-movement, used in optometry / kinesiology / surveillance / animal-behavior writing without figurative weight ("the eyes track the moving stimulus"); (b) a literary register imported from the hunting-sense, where "her gaze tracked X" carries predator-connotation deliberately. The pulp-enthusiast's note ("the verb 'tracks' is doing signature-carrying work") recognizes that something more than ordinary eye-movement is being conveyed.

**The corpus comparator.** The Phase 0 corpus's "fauna-track tilt" (POV behavior pack, taylor) is a fixed-noun-compound naming a specific cape-trained scanning pattern; it is etymologically the same root but is functionally a different construction (it names a learned movement-pattern). It does not license "tracks" as a verb-form for non-cape, non-trained gaze.

**The narrow defense.** Mira's gaze IS purposeful, directed, and disciplined — §Proactive Drives "takes a positional read of any new space within sixty seconds" + §Look "Eyes that move slightly faster than her face does." She is conducting a working-language activity, not an instinctive scan. The working-language register (a) is therefore the primary reading.

**The hostile rebuttal.** The working-language register (a) is the primary reading only inside specialist registers (kinesiology / surveillance / hunting trade). In general literary prose, "her gaze tracks the yard" reads against register (b) — it imports the predator-flavor by default unless the surrounding prose has established a specialist register. The proto-line context here is a Riverlands sept yard at first encounter; no specialist register is established. Default-reader will receive the predator-flavor; this is figurative-register import even if no feeling is named. AP13 (idiomatic-feeling-naming through working-language-adjacent idiom) becomes a live concern at audit time.

**Verdict on Seam-1.1.** The defense is not strong enough to survive a hostile mechanic auditor in Phase 5. The verb is doing work in two registers simultaneously, and the rubric's response to ambiguous register is silence-or-revise. "Tracks" carries enough AP13 risk to revise. (Note: this is a tighter call than Phase 2 made; the Phase 2 dismissal at "tracks is a body-action verb (gaze movement), not a feeling-name" was correct on the surface but did not address the directionality-with-purpose connotation that the seams report names.)

### Seam-1.2 — functional-register: distinguish or fail

The Phase 2 fire claimed painting-characterization + social-commentary. The seams report presses: are these distinct functional registers, or does social-commentary collapse into painting-characterization?

**Strict definitions per rubric §functional-register catalog:**
- **Painting-characterization** = the somatic-tell renders the character's voice / signature / personality — "this is who she is."
- **Social-commentary** = the somatic-tell renders something about the world / structure beyond the character herself — "this is what her world is."

**Phase 2's social-commentary claim:** "smallfolk-woman-performs-positional-read-reflex without announcing it, against §'she is not advertising' Default Stance; the reflex itself is survival infrastructure."

**Test:** For social-commentary to fire distinctly from painting-characterization, the audience must receive a comment about the world, not only about Mira. Does "her gaze tracks the yard before her mouth opens" license a world-comment?

The reflex IS survival-infrastructure for the smallfolk stratum. But the somatic-tell as written shows mira doing the thing; the world-comment ("smallfolk women do this; the Riverlands has shaped a stratum into reflex space-readers") is not on the page. It is in the card. To extract the social-commentary, the reader must already hold the card-context that this is not idiosyncratic to mira but characteristic of her stratum. Without that context, "her gaze tracks the yard" reads as "mira is observant" — pure painting-characterization.

A somatic-tell at @6 that would ALSO carry social-commentary independently would need to either (a) be visible as stratum-coded (a marker the reader recognizes as smallfolk-survival-coded by the proto-line's surrounding context), or (b) put the reflex against something the world expects (the gaze does something the surrounding social register would not predict). Neither is on the page in the Phase 2 fire.

**Verdict on Seam-1.2.** Social-commentary collapses into painting-characterization. The fire as written hits 1-of-4 functional registers, not 2-of-4. The multi-justification slot 5 fails. The fire fails the gate.

**Cleaner second-register options examined:**
- *Realization* — "the audience realizes the space-read is structural, not casual." This works only if the somatic-tell creates the realization in-beat. The Phase 2 phrasing does not — it shows a competent assessment without surfacing that the assessment is reflex-not-choice. Realization requires the tell to make visible something the reader would not have predicted; assessment-pause from a competent woman is predicted.
- *Grim humor* — n/a; the beat carries no register-gap comedy.
- *Painting-characterization* — already counted; cannot be double-counted.

No clean second-register hit available at @6 with a phrasing that stays within mira's card vocabulary. Fire fails the gate.

### Revision

Two options were considered: STRIP entirely (Phase 2's instinct toward sparsity; default-to-silence), or REVISE to a phrasing that (a) replaces "tracks" with a non-AP13 verb and (b) opens a clean second functional register.

**STRIP** is the conservative move. It would take mira's file to 0 fires on s01e01, which is permitted (rubric §per-episode sparsity 2-5%; 0 is below band only at the file aggregate, not per-character). Aggregate file would drop from 3 fires to 2 (taylor @39, edric @57) = 2.6%, still within band.

**REVISE** is permitted only if a defensible fire exists. Examining the alternatives the brief offers:

- "her eyes move ahead of her face" — direct §Look instantiation; "move ahead of" is working-language for sequential body-coordination (boxing / dance / surveillance vocabulary); no AP13 import. But: still single-register (painting-characterization only); social-commentary still collapses; same 1-of-4 fail.
- "she stills before she speaks" — §Voice "Assessing — visible as a slight stillness" + §Signature Moves §1 "The stillness beat." Direct card-vocabulary lift. The stillness IS the card's named tell. But: "stills before she speaks" parallels mira's introduction beat at @6 → @7 and so carries painting-characterization cleanly. Second-register check: is there grim-humor, social-commentary, or realization? The stillness-before-speaking against a yard-with-three-other-people-and-a-counting-clerk is the §Default Stance "off-center" + §"not advertising" reflex made visible — but again, the social-commentary is in the card, not on the page.
- "her eyes find the door before they find the wards" — this is interesting. It enacts §Look + §Default Stance "a place from which she can see the door" + §Signature Moves §1 stillness-as-information-gathering. AND the ordering ("door before wards") is a world-comment legible without card-context: in a sept yard with religious wards visible, the survivor reads the exit before the holy. This is social-commentary that lives on the page. The reader receives both "this is who Mira is" (painting-characterization) and "this is what survival in the Riverlands has made of religious space" (social-commentary that wards-second-after-doors is structural, not idiosyncratic). 2-of-4 functional-register hits cleanly.
- "she takes the count of who is in the yard" — paraphrases the clerk's literal action (the clerk is counting); mira-doing-the-clerk's-act is interesting but blurs the vocabulary distinctness with the clerk and creates a register echo that the rubric's anti-pattern catalog does not name but that file-shape audit would flag. Also "takes the count of" carries an idiom-import (taking-count = census-register) that is exactly what the clerk's procedural register does — semantic-collision, not separation. Reject.

**Selected revision:** `her eyes find the door before they find the wards`.

Verb-check: "find" is the same verb the seams report flagged as biomechanical-locator at edric @57. Phase 2/3 cleared "finds" for edric on the working-language-locator reading. For mira-eyes-find-the-door, the verb is even cleaner: "eyes find X" is established narrative idiom for purposeful gaze-arrival (compare card §Signature Moves §1 for edric: "his eyes find the door"). The card uses the exact verb. AP13/AP16 risk: low; the construction is established working-language for gaze-targeting. The hostile reading (eyes-as-agent-finding) is the same pattern that cleared at edric @57; no new ground.

Functional-register check: 
1. **painting-characterization** — direct §Default Stance + §Signature Moves §1 + §Look enactment.
2. **social-commentary** — door-before-wards ordering. In a sept yard, religious wards are the social-register-default object of attention (the yard is consecrated; the wards are what one is supposed to see). Mira sees the door first. The reader does not need card-context to receive this: the proto-line context (sept yard, clerk counting, officer-arrival imminent at @11) supplies the surround. The ordering is a world-comment: this is what surviving the Riverlands has done to the relationship between the holy and the practical for smallfolk women. Distinct from painting-characterization — this is not "Mira specifically is wary," it is "the stratum reads doors before wards." 2-of-4. PASS.

Q1 check: proto-lines @5/@6/@7 are bucket / straighten / speak. NI no fire. Sensory no fire. State-updates no fire. The door-first-then-wards ordering is not on the page elsewhere. Q1 holds.

Q2 check: introduction beat for mira; establishes the register at first-impression; structural to the character's place in the episode and the series. Q2 holds.

Form check: subject + verb + locator + temporal-clause. No labeled-feeling. No hedge. No simile. No metaphor. The "find" verb is cleared above. Two-clause-via-coordination (and: "before they find the wards") — single SVO with a temporal-comparison clause; rubric §form "subject + verb + (object/locator)" + "one clause" is held (one main clause; the temporal phrase modifies it).
