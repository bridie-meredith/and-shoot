## Internal-consistency response

### @48 vs. @64

The seam is correct to flag this as the central consistency challenge. Both @48 and @64 are stylus-commits that make something permanent in the record. The Phase 2 distinction — that @48 records a determination already made while @64 IS the commit of the bureaucratic sub-arc — is the intended distinction, but it requires sharper rubric grounding.

The distinction is this: **what question does the beat answer, and is that question still open at this beat?**

At @48 ("the officer dictates taylor's name as provisional labor-eligible"), the question of Taylor's labor status was settled at @38-@43 — the officer took the letter, read it, and returned it. The registration here is bureaucratic documentation of a turn that occurred four beats earlier.

At @64 ("the stylus marks two parallel lines beside taylor's entry"), the question of *what the parallel marks mean* is the stakes-bearing question. Unlike the dictation at @48, there has been no prior beat where the outcome of the bureaucratic notation sub-arc was declared and handed to Taylor. @64 is not documenting a prior determination; it IS the determination, expressed in permanent marks whose meaning the audience and Taylor must read simultaneously. The turn has not happened before @64 — the marks *are* the turn.

This distinction is rubric-consistent: stakes-visibility 3 requires that "the registration" be "of an irreversible turn" where the registration IS the event of turning, not the documentation of a prior event. @48 documents; @64 turns.

**Conclusion:** DEFEND @64 at 3, DEFEND @48 at 2. The distinction is: @48's dictation records a determination externalized at @43; @64's marks are themselves the determination, with no prior beat where the parallel-marks outcome was handed to Taylor as a visible decision. The formal action (stylus on board) is similar, but the narrative position is different: one follows a declaration, one is the declaration.
