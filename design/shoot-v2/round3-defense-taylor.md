# Round 3 Defense — taylor-hebert-westeros

Author fork, defense phase. Read the seam, then defend or revise. Sharpen, don't soften. Cards reloaded fresh; corpus and show files unread.

The doubled register is the primary attack surface. Every seam below converges on the same demand: *can the slip be observable on the line itself?* Where the round-2 line answered "yes, in the engineering" and the audience came back with "engineering invisible to a non-Worm reader," the right move is to relocate the slip into surface diagnostics that any Westerosi-fluent reader catches — a wrong word, a wrong proposition, a too-precise unit, a register-rung the ward should not own. The mask-tells stay. The mask-engineering becomes mask-surface.

### TH-1
**Seam (audience):** The structural slip depends on a reader knowing eleven-year-olds don't enumerate parallel indexed clauses — but in a GRRM-register story where characters routinely speak in rhetorical parallels, that knowledge is not guaranteed, and a reader who attributes the structure to the setting will read past the doubled register entirely. (worm-canon-pedant secondary push: enumeration-out-loud is canon-Taylor's *planning* voice, not her *delivery* voice — she resolves to the ask before opening her mouth.)

**Decision:** REVISE

The seam is load-bearing on two axes. (1) GRRM-Westerosi prose normalizes parallel clauses, so the parallelism cannot be the slip-carrier. (2) The pedant is right that canon-Taylor compresses to the ask under time-pressure rather than externalizing the bullet-list. The fix is to push the line toward *delivery-with-planning-shape-implicit*: the ask compressed to one cost-frame, and the slip moved out of architecture and into a single lexical item that an eleven-year-old sept ward should not own — a unit, a word, a register-rung the role does not have access to. Mira (smallfolk neighbor) is not adversarial; she is the witness Taylor needs. The line should sound like a child asking for help, with one word in it that does not belong to the child.

**Drafts.**

- *Draft A (rejected — too close to round-2, still over-architected):* "Goodwife Mira. Before he closes his board — only what you saw. The letter held out, the septon called, the door not opened. That is all. I would not ask if I had the time."

- *Draft B (rejected — closes the slip; reads as fully in-mask):* "Goodwife Mira, if it please you. He will close his board soon. Could you say what you saw — the letter, the septon, the door? I would not ask."

- *Draft C — chosen.* "Goodwife Mira. If it please you, only what you saw — the letter held out, the septon called for, the door not opened. Before he closes his board. I cannot afford the risk that he rides without it."

- *Draft D (rejected — *afford the risk* is too clean a slip; pairs with *cannot* for double exposure):* same as C with "I would not wish to leave it at his hand alone" replacing the closer. Loses the load.

**Chosen:** "Goodwife Mira. If it please you, only what you saw — the letter held out, the septon called for, the door not opened. Before he closes his board. I cannot afford the risk that he rides without it."

**How the seam is answered:**
- *Architecture compressed.* The three items are now an em-dash apposition inside the *only what you saw* frame, not three indexed *that*-clauses. The ask lives in one sentence; the items are recoverable inside it. This is delivery-voice with planning-shape implicit, per the pedant's canon-Taylor diagnostic: she has resolved to *only what you saw* before opening her mouth, and the three-item appositive is the compressed receipt, not the open enumeration.
- *Slip relocated to surface lexis.* "I cannot afford the risk that he rides without it" is the audit-trip. *Afford the risk* is base-card cost-language (§Vocabulary "cost, paid, leverage, budget, ceiling, threshold, exposure") in a phrasing an eleven-year-old sept ward of the Riverlands does not have. A child of her station says *I cannot let him*, *I am afraid he will*, *please, before he goes* — not *I cannot afford the risk that*. That is a managerial-modal that belongs to the underlying adult. The smallfolk-witness register would land at *please* or *afore he rides*; the line lands at *afford the risk*, and Mira hears something is wrong even if she has no name for it. The slip is a single phrase, not a structural diagnosis the reader has to perform.
- *Pulp-rhythm corrected.* The opening softener is one beat ("If it please you,") rolled into the load-bearing clause rather than two dead beats before the architecture. The first em-dash arrives by word eight, not word fifteen.
- *Worm-pedant satisfied.* The enumeration is no longer *the request* — *only what you saw* is the request; the three items are scope-clarification inside the request. This is canon-Taylor's *Did you see it?* with the items recoverable, not enumerated as the ask.

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Smallfolk address held* — Goodwife Mira, if it please you (leaf §Vocabulary).
- *Em-dash as sensory/scope specifier, not chassis* — single em-dash; spine is sentence-stack.
- *Subject-implied trailing fragment* — "Before he closes his board." stands alone, base-card §Cadence "trailing fragment endings — sentences end on the load-bearing word."
- *Cost-language in the closer* — *afford, risk, without it* — base-card §Vocabulary cost-frame; this is the surface slip.
- *Closing-clause-twist rationed to one* — "I cannot afford the risk that he rides without it" reframes the ask as cost-bounded, base-card signature.
- *Uncontracted Westerosi-formal* — *I cannot, were not, did not* (leaf §Vocabulary).
- *No anachronism, no Earth-Bet noun, no childish-affect performance.*
- *Cognitive-age slip is now lexical, not structural.* A reader without Worm-context catches *afford the risk* in a child's mouth as wrong-for-the-role; a reader with Worm-context catches the cost-language as base-card-pure. Both readings converge.

### TH-2
**Seam (audience):** A line that demonstrates competence entirely through what it refuses to add requires the reader to already know what competence in this register looks like — three spare declaratives may simply read as a child giving a spare answer, with no observable doubled register at all.

**Decision:** REVISE

The seam is partially right and I want to be honest about it. The pure-restraint defense (round 2: *the absence of the closing-clause-twist is the card move*) holds for a Worm-fluent reader and collapses for a crossover reader. The fix is small: keep the cooperative, non-volunteering posture (the line's job per intent), but install one surface diagnostic that registers as wrong even to a reader who has never met Taylor. The candidate diagnostic for mask-ON is *register-precision the role should not have* — a word that is correct for the situation but selected at adult-precision rather than child-vocabulary, sliding past the inspector's pen but available to a careful reader.

The intent says *demonstrate the child-surface, not the adult underneath.* I read this as: the child-surface must be intact at the inspector's reading, but the adult must be perceptible to the audience. That is a doubled-register requirement, and the round-2 line met it only on the inspector's side.

**Drafts.**

- *Draft A (round-2 original — kept as a baseline):* "Septon Osmynd, ser. He is in the alcove. He has not risen today."

- *Draft B (rejected — overcorrects into mask-OFF; *attends* is too far over the line):* "Septon Osmynd, ser. He attends the alcove. He has not risen today."

- *Draft C — chosen.* "Septon Osmynd, ser. He keeps to the alcove. He has not risen since yestermorn."

- *Draft D (rejected — *yestermorn* without *since* is a register-paste, not a slip):* "Septon Osmynd, ser. He is in the alcove. Yestermorn was the last."

**Chosen:** "Septon Osmynd, ser. He keeps to the alcove. He has not risen since yestermorn."

**How the seam is answered:**
- *Surface diagnostic installed.* "He keeps to the alcove" is the audit-pinpoint. A Westerosi sept ward of eleven says *he is in the alcove*, *he is abed*, *he has not come out.* *Keeps to* is a habitual-aspect verb-phrase that frames the alcove as Osmynd's chosen confinement — adult observational register, not child report. The inspector hears a clean cooperative answer; the reader hears a child describing her dying guardian's pattern with the kind of phrasing a steward would use.
- *Temporal precision sharpened past child-register.* "Has not risen since yestermorn" replaces "today." *Yestermorn* is GRRM time-vocabulary (cross-cutting card §Time-keeping) — available to a literate sept ward, but pairing it with *since* and *not risen* delivers a duration-bracketed statement: it answers a question the inspector did not quite ask (*how long*) at the same time it answers the question he did ask (*where*). Volunteering one register-step further than asked, in temporal-bracket form, is base-card §Voice tells "time-keeping interior" leaking into spoken — the precision is wrong for the role even though the vocabulary is correct.
- *Stacking pattern preserved.* Three short declaratives, named-source / located / stated-condition, matches authored anchor "There is a man on the road. Past the orchard. He has not turned in." Mask-ON cadence holds.
- *Loaded silence at the tail preserved.* Line ends on *yestermorn*. No softener, no Faith-formula. Base-card §Voice tells "the held silence after a question."
- *No closing-clause-twist deployed.* Mask-ON; the twist would be Draft B-territory. Held back per leaf-card §Syntax "the closing-clause-twist is preserved but rationed."
- *Crossover reader gets the slip.* A reader who has never opened Worm catches "keeps to" + "since yestermorn" as language a child shouldn't quite have, even if they cannot name what the wrongness is. That perception was the seam's demand.

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Three short declaratives, stacked* — leaf §Cadence, base §Cadence "short sentences when afraid" extended to mask-ON cooperative-under-pressure.
- *Honorific correct, uncontracted formal* — *ser*, *He is, He has not*.
- *GRRM time-vocabulary integrated* — *yestermorn* (cross-cutting card §Time-keeping).
- *Habitual-aspect verb-phrase as register-leak* — *keeps to* — leaf §Vocabulary "she does not have a Westerosi vocabulary for these concepts," inverted: she has selected a Westerosi-correct phrasing at adult precision.
- *Cognitive-age slip surface-visible* — duration-bracketed answer to a where-question, base §Voice tells "time-keeping interior" surfacing.
- *Subject-implied is avoided* — *He is. He has not.* — mask-ON correctness.
- *No anachronism, no Earth-Bet noun, no childish-affect performance.*

### TH-3
**Seam (audience):** The late vocative is supposed to carry the slip — cold number first, child-register catching up — but "Twelve, septon." reads as one polite correction in conventional ward-register tail-address form. The vocative placement demonstrates ward-register performing correctly rather than the mask re-engaging after a failure. The slip and recovery are simultaneous; there is no observable slip.

**Decision:** REVISE

The pedant is right and the patch was the wrong fix. In canon, Taylor's slips are *unrecovered in the moment*. "Twelve, septon." compresses slip and recovery into the same breath, which is the opposite of what the intent wants. The intent reads: *the number comes out cold before the child-register can soften it. The slip is the line.*

The right move is to honor the intent fully — let the number land cold, with no softener at all — and earn the card surface by *what the cold number does to the line that follows it*, not by softening the cold number itself. But that demand applies to S-2 (Rowan's response), not to TH-3 itself. Within TH-3, the question is whether the bare number can demonstrate the card on a one-word surface.

The leaf-card answer: a one-word fragment from Taylor *is* a card-pure form (§Direct samples "Status." / "Coffee."), but the leaf-card layer requires that the spoken surface read as either child-abrupt or adult-authoritative, with the ambiguity acceptable. The seam's complaint is not that "Twelve." fails to demonstrate the card — it is that "Twelve, septon." patches the failure by adding a softener that closes the slip.

The fix: replace the softener with a *second word* that does not soften — a word that doubles down on the cold number rather than retreating. A word that confirms the count rather than apologizing for it. The two-word form survives; the politeness vanishes; the slip stays unrecovered.

**Drafts.**

- *Draft A (round-2 original "Twelve."):* one-word; rejected by audience round 2 as not demonstrating the card on a sub-three-word surface.

- *Draft B (round-2 patch "Twelve, septon."):* rejected here per the seam.

- *Draft C (rejected — "Twelve days" reads as helpful clarification, not as slip):* "Twelve days."

- *Draft D — chosen.* "Twelve. Counted."

- *Draft E (rejected — *I have counted* re-introduces subject-pronoun softening):* "I have counted. Twelve."

- *Draft F (rejected — "Twelve, by the road" is the methodology preview that belongs in TH-4):* "Twelve, by the road."

**Chosen:** "Twelve. Counted."

**How the seam is answered:**
- *Slip is unrecovered.* The second word does not soften the first; it confirms it. *Counted* is past-tense report — she has already done the counting, before Rowan opened the question. This is base-card §Voice tells "pre-calculation announcements" surfacing in spoken form: past-perfect-equivalent for an action completed before the present beat. The mask is supposed to mediate this kind of admission; it does not. There is no recovery within the utterance.
- *Slip is observable on the surface.* A Westerosi child of eleven correcting a septon would say *twelve, septon* (the patch), *twelve, I think* (Draft C-territory in TH-2's hedge-register), or *twelve days, septon, by my count*. She would not say *Counted* as a bare past-participle confirmation. The participle stripped of subject and auxiliary — *I have counted* compressed to *Counted* — is base-card §Syntax subject-implied openings pushed past child-register acceptability, and it is the leaf-card §Authoring notes "cognitive-age slip" diagnostic at one-word grain. A reader does not need Worm fluency to feel that *Counted* is wrong-in-the-mouth of a sept ward; they need only Westerosi fluency to know that *counted* is the wrong register-rung for a child correcting a cleric.
- *Pulp-hinge restored.* "Twelve. Counted." stops Rowan cold the way "Twelve." did, and the second word does the work the patch was trying to do (demonstrate the card surface) without softening the first. The reversal pulp-enthusiast wanted is preserved; the seam pulp-enthusiast flagged ("polite correction in the same breath") is closed.
- *S-2 dependency repaired.* The cross-line dependency the methodology log flags — Rowan's pastoral alarm in S-2 needs TH-3 to actually register as wrong-for-her — is now load-bearing on Rowan's perception. *Counted* is the word that justifies *now there is a number for a man to sit with.* Rowan heard the participle, and the participle is what alarmed him. S-2's weight is earned.

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Bare-fragment correction* — base §Direct samples "Status." / "Coffee." / "Dark." Two fragments instead of one; both load-bearing.
- *Subject-implied opening, extreme* — *Counted* with subject and auxiliary both dropped. Base §Syntax.
- *Pre-calculation announcement, in dialogue* — base §Voice tells. Surfaces in spoken form, which is itself the slip.
- *No softener, no honorific, no Faith-formula* — leaf-card mask-thins-with-Aldric pattern extended to Rowan.
- *Cognitive-age slip surface-visible* — *counted* as a bare past-participle in a child's mouth reads as wrong even to a reader who has not loaded Taylor's base card.
- *Numeric specificity preserved as voice tell* — *twelve* unhedged, base §Vocabulary.
- *Plain number not X-and-Y form* — slip is in speed, not in register-display; *two-and-ten* would be performed register, *twelve* is the number arriving before the mask catches it (round-2 reasoning preserved).
- *The slip is the line's job.* Mask-state SLIPPING honored by removing every softener the mask would supply *and* by adding a second word that confirms-rather-than-mitigates.

### TH-4
**Seam (audience):** The mask-FULLY-OFF state delivers an adult-operator road-assessment with no internal registration of wrongness — the speaker is supposed to know she has over-exposed, but nothing in the line's surface carries that self-awareness. The reader gets tactical information without the affective cost the dark-fantasy register requires. (Pulp secondary: forty-eight words of geography buries the wrongness under content. Worm-pedant secondary: the level of unsolicited operational detail is Director-Costa-register, not pastoral-walk register.)

**Decision:** REVISE

All three challenges converge on the same fix. (1) Dark-fantasy wants self-aware-over-exposure on the line. (2) Pulp wants the over-exposure to land in three sentences or fewer. (3) Worm-pedant wants the operational detail dialed back to *answering the question* rather than *proving the methodology*. The fix is one move: cut the geography from three obstacles to one, deliver the count by deduction not by tour, and install a self-aware crack — a word she walks back, a fragment that registers her own over-exposure as it happens.

The signature requirements stay: long-sentence-when-planning chassis, comma-chained run-on, closing-clause-twist that reframes Rowan's count, no Latinate, no Earth-Bet noun. What changes: the geography compresses, and the line carries an internal tell that the speaker hears herself.

**Drafts.**

- *Draft A (round-2 original):* 105 words across seven sentences. Rejected per all three seams.

- *Draft B (rejected — closer is too dry-funny, reads Earth-Bet):* "Nine is the count for a rider who is not actually on the road."

- *Draft C (rejected — opens with a softener that the OFF state forbids):* "I am sorry, septon —"

- *Draft D (rejected — geography compressed but no self-aware crack; same seam unaddressed):* "The Kingsroad is the count he is making, septon. The river road is the road, and the fords above Harroway are still high — a rider on a tired horse fords or swims, and either way he loses the morning to it. A day for that, and a day for the cut past Harroway, and another for the climb above Cox's Ford. Twelve, not nine. Nine is the count if a man does not have to dismount."

- *Draft E — chosen.* "The Kingsroad is the count he is making, septon. The river road is the road, and the fords above Harroway are still high from the melt — a rider on a tired horse fords or swims, and either way the morning goes to it. A day there, a day for the cut past Harroway, another for the climb. Twelve, not nine. — That is more than I should know, I think. Nine is the count if a man does not have to dismount."

**Chosen:** "The Kingsroad is the count he is making, septon. The river road is the road, and the fords above Harroway are still high from the melt — a rider on a tired horse fords or swims, and either way the morning goes to it. A day there, a day for the cut past Harroway, another for the climb. Twelve, not nine. — That is more than I should know, I think. Nine is the count if a man does not have to dismount."

**How the seam is answered:**
- *Self-aware crack installed.* "— That is more than I should know, I think." The em-dash-prefaced fragment is the speaker hearing herself one beat too late. *That is more than I should know* is the line registering its own over-exposure on the surface; *I think* is the mask re-engaging after the fact, in the same shape the round-2 audience said TH-3's patch falsely claimed to perform. Here the recovery comes after the slip, not simultaneous with it — the slip has already happened (the obstacle-list is complete; *twelve, not nine* has landed), and the recovery is a separate beat. This is the canonical Taylor pattern the pedant pointed to: slip, then a separate moment in which the slip is acknowledged. The acknowledgement is in-line rather than in narration, which is what the doubled register needs to do without external instrumentation.
- *Rhythm corrected.* The geography is now ~30 words across two sentences before *twelve, not nine.* The pulp-rhythm seam (forty-eight words of subordinated geography) is closed by compressing three obstacles to one full obstacle (the fords) plus two named-only obstacles (cut past Harroway, climb). The fords get the tactical detail because they are the load-bearing reason the count differs; the other two are *a day there, a day for the cut, another for the climb* — counted, not toured.
- *Worm-pedant satisfied.* Operational detail is now dialed to *answering* rather than *proving.* The fords get one clause of methodology (*from the melt — a rider on a tired horse fords or swims*); the rest is enumeration-of-cost. This is closer to canon-Taylor under direct question (*"the chain-ring. how old is it?"* — minimum needed, no methodology display).
- *Closing-clause-twist preserved at full weight.* "Nine is the count if a man does not have to dismount." Reframes Rowan's count as the count for an idealized rider. Base §Syntax signature, deployed at full weight, mask-OFF.
- *Dark-fantasy affective cost paid.* The line now costs the speaker something on its own surface. *That is more than I should know, I think* is the tremor the dark-fantasy reader was looking for — over-exposure registering as self-recognition, in-line, before Rowan answers. The mask-OFF state has consequence-logic now.
- *Crossover reader served.* The crack is in plain English. A reader who has never opened Worm catches *that is more than I should know* as a child realizing she has said too much. The doubled register is now visible without the reader needing to know what eleven-year-olds typically don't enumerate.

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Long-sentence-when-planning chassis* — base §Cadence diagnostic rhythm. Comma-chained run-on in the fords-clause.
- *Comma-spliced run-ons in tactical exposition* — *a rider on a tired horse fords or swims, and either way the morning goes to it.* Base §Syntax.
- *Closing-clause-twist at full weight* — *Nine is the count if a man does not have to dismount.* Base §Syntax signature.
- *Self-aware-recovery beat, in-line* — *— That is more than I should know, I think.* Leaf §Vocabulary "*I think* is a removable softener — the line underneath is in base register" deployed precisely as an after-the-fact mask re-engagement, not as a softener inside the load-bearing clause.
- *GRRM time and geography vocabulary, integrated* — *the morning, a day there, the melt, the count, fords, the cut, the climb.* Cross-cutting card §Time-keeping + §Compound words.
- *Saxon morphology, no Latinate* — *Kingsroad, fords, melt, cut, climb, dismount.* No *alternative route, terrain assessment, methodology* (leaf §Vocabulary forbidden).
- *Period-formal phrasing, uncontracted* — *is the road, has not, does not have to.* Leaf §Syntax.
- *Honorific at the head only* — *septon* in the first sentence, dropped for the rest. Mask-OFF tell at fine grain (round-2 reasoning preserved).
- *Em-dash as sensory specifier and as crack-introducer* — two em-dashes; one inside the fords-sentence (causal-aside), one prefacing the self-aware fragment. Spine remains comma-chain + sentence-stack, not em-dash chassis.
- *Numeric specificity at adult cadence* — *twelve, not nine, a day there, a day for the cut, another for the climb.* Base §Vocabulary.
- *No Earth-Bet noun, no anachronism, no cape-register.*
- *The speaker-knows-it requirement now demonstrated, not asserted.* The round-2 line claimed the speaker knew; the round-3 line shows her knowing on the surface.
- *Could not come from Plumm's mouth.* Plumm's register is periodic-sentence Latinate-courtly with judgment-ownership; this is comma-chain Anglo-tactical with mid-line self-recognition. The two registers remain syntactically incompatible.

## Author notes — round 3

- Three revisions, one defended-then-revised. TH-2 is the closest to a defense — the round-2 logic was correct on the Worm-fluent axis but the seam was right that the crossover reader gets nothing, so the revise installs a single surface diagnostic (*keeps to* / *since yestermorn*) without disturbing the mask-ON posture.
- The pattern of the three revisions is consistent: **relocate the slip from architecture to surface lexis**. Round 2 hid the slip in engineering (TH-1's indexed clauses, TH-3's bare number, TH-4's adult-monologue chassis); round 3 puts the slip in a single observable item per line (TH-1's *afford the risk*, TH-2's *keeps to* + *since yestermorn*, TH-3's *Counted*, TH-4's *That is more than I should know, I think*).
- The doubled register now lands without external instrumentation. Each line carries its own crack visible to a reader who has Westerosi fluency but not Worm fluency. The leaf-card framework is preserved; the demonstration mechanism shifts from *inferable engineering* to *observable lexis*.
- TH-3's revision retires the round-2 audience-recommended patch in favor of a different two-word form. The reasoning: the audience's seam-finding round identified a deeper failure mode (slip-recovery simultaneous) than the round-2 review caught. *Counted* is unrecovered.
- TH-4's *— That is more than I should know, I think.* deliberately lifts the leaf-card softener *I think* and weaponizes it. Round 2 listed *I think* as a removable softener for child-ward register; round 3 deploys it not as a softener inside the load-bearing clause but as an after-the-slip mask-recovery, which is the mechanically correct placement the leaf card implies.
- All four chosen lines hold the writing-bar requirements: no em-dash + semicolon chassis (no semicolons appear; em-dashes are sensory/causal/recovery specifiers, never spine), per-line voice incompatible with Plumm's register, no Earth-Bet nouns, no anachronistic idiom.
