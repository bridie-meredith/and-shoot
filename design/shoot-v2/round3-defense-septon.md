### S-1 (septon-dying-protector)
**Seam:** *We do not pretend it is nothing* sources the moral obligation to collective personal will rather than to the Faith. For a dying septon spending breath on a moral warning, the closer should be most explicitly theological, and instead reaches for secular-moralist phrasing. Compare *The Faith will not* (S-3): the Faith named as agent.

**Decision:** REVISE.

The seam is load-bearing. Osmynd is dying; this is the closer he spends breath on; the closer should attribute the moral to the Seven, not to a *we* who refuses to pretend. The card (§Refuses to say: *Self-claim of holiness*; §Voice tells: *plain-speech moral closers... the moral lands in plain speech inside the head, the same way it lands in plain speech in a homily*) supports a Faith-as-agent closer in plain Anglo. Easy fix; the rest of the line earns.

Secondary seam from pulp-enthusiast (the ellipsis as actor-direction in punctuation rather than syntax) is also fair. A breath-cost line should carry the breath-cost in the *grammar of the speech* — broken sentences, restarts, short stutter-fragments — not in a typographic hint. Revise both at once.

**Line (draft A — chosen):**
"Child. Sit by me. A man is come to the village. Asking after the rats. Asking close. The Mother sees such a man, child. The Father judges him. The Faith does not look away from him. Nor will you."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Child*-vocative twice — pastoral universal (§Vocabulary: forms of address — *Child is the septon's universal address in pastoral mode*)
- Imperative-with-permission opening: *Sit by me* — pastoral opening (§Direct samples: *Come. Sit by me. Tell me what troubles you*)
- Breath-cost in syntax, not typography — short declaratives stacked, the half-repetition *Asking close* is the labor of speech as grammar (§Direct samples for septon, §Voice tells for breath-cost in a dying mouth)
- Homiletic three-and-close: *The Mother sees... The Father judges... The Faith does not look away* — three parallel theological clauses, plain-Anglo Faith-as-agent closer (§Cadence: *Homiletic three-and-close*; §Direct samples: *The Father judges. The Mother forgives. The Stranger waits*)
- Seven invoked by aspect, doctrinally clean — Mother sees (mercy), Father judges (judgment) (§Vocabulary: *the Seven, named and in aspect*)
- Faith-as-agent closer replaces the round-2 secular-moralist *we do not pretend* — the Faith is named, and Taylor is folded into the Faith's gaze with *Nor will you* rather than into a collective *we* of personal-moral will
- *Nor will you* is the pastoral injunction-with-permission: not *you must look*, but *the Faith does not look away, nor will you* — the obligation is sourced to the Faith and Taylor is named into it (§Cadence: *Imperative-with-permission*)
- Plain Anglo throughout: *come, village, asking, rats, close, sees, judges, look away* — Saxon spine (§Vocabulary: *Plain-Anglo register alongside the Latinate*)

**Line (draft B):**
"Child. Hear me. There is a man asking after the rats. Asking close. The Father sees a man so. The Mother sees the asking. The Stranger waits, child. He waits for him as he waits for me. The Faith is not silent on such a man."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- Three-aspect anaphora (Father / Mother / Stranger) — full sermon-rhythm
- Stranger-aspect deployed correctly (death and the unknown), folded into doctrinal closer that *the Stranger waits as he waits for all of us*
- Faith-as-agent closer (*The Faith is not silent*)
- *Hush*-pattern register-feature absent here; comfort-register held back because the line is warning, not comfort

*Chosen: A.* B is doctrinally richer (three-aspect anaphora) but spends more breath than Osmynd can afford and the *Stranger waits* invocation pulls the line toward Osmynd's own dying rather than toward the warning about the man. A is shorter, breath-cost reads cleaner in the grammar, and the closer (*The Faith does not look away from him. Nor will you.*) does the seam-fix work without overspending.

### S-2 (septon-rowan)
**Seam (as given):** S-2 depends on TH-3. Rowan's pastoral concern reads as correct perception only if the slip in *twelve* was actually observable. The Taylor writer fork has revised TH-3 to *"Twelve. Counted."* — a stronger unrecovered slip.

**Decision:** REVISE — but to point harder at *what specifically Rowan heard*, not to soften the line.

With TH-3 as *"Twelve. Counted."*, the slip is now load-bearing on the page: an eleven-year-old ward in the Riverlands does not say *Counted* about a road she has not walked. *Counted* is a verb of operational reckoning, not of childhood report. The cross-line load is now cleaner — Rowan has heard a real register-break — but the round-2 S-2 does not name what he heard. It generalizes (*how is it that... comes to count it twelve*) when it could specifically register the slip-word. A revision that points harder at *Counted* does two things at once: it earns its weight against the new TH-3, and it shortens the line (addressing the pulp-enthusiast seam about fifty-word mid-walk delivery).

The dark-fantasy seam (the Faith-aphorism as stalling mask vs. genuine pastoral pause) is real but is the *intended read* per the persona card (§Voice: *Privately alarmed by his own theology... a slight shift — a fraction slower, a fraction more formal — that a careful observer might notice*). Rowan reaches for the aphorism *because* he is buying himself the breath of a step. That is in-character, not a craft seam. I keep the aphorism move; I make it shorter.

**Line (draft A — chosen):**
"*Counted*, you say. Now there is a word. Walk a moment, child — slower, with me. How is it that an eleven-year-old ward of my sept counts a road she has not walked, and counts it *true*? The Father grant me wit to hear the answer."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- Names the specific word that broke register: *Counted, you say* — quoting the slip back at the speaker is the septon's pastoral correction-by-attention move (§Vocabulary: *Doctrinal precision over moralizing warmth — A septon corrects... gently but firmly*; the same attention applied to a register-slip in a child's speech). This addresses the seam directly: Rowan heard *Counted*, and the line says so.
- The aphorism is shortened: *Now there is a word* (five syllables) replaces *Now there is a number for a man to sit with* (twelve syllables). Still in-register (Faith-aphorism habit, treating an utterance as a thing to be weighed) but no longer fifty words of embedded parenthetical mid-walk. Pulp-enthusiast seam addressed.
- Imperative-with-permission, walking-scene sized: *Walk a moment, child — slower, with me* (§Cadence: *Imperative-with-permission* — the imperative is real; the permission is also real)
- *Child*-vocative — pastoral universal even mid-investigation
- Pastoral framing of an investigative question: *how is it that... counts a road she has not walked, and counts it true* — the soft passive, Faith-conversational, not interrogator's
- Self-located authority left implicit (he has walked the road; he does not need to recite it; the question carries the asymmetry)
- Conditional-Father closer preserved: *the Father grant me wit to hear the answer* — doctrinally precise, conditional not assertive (§Syntax: *Conditional comfort... The septon does not assert what the gods will do*)
- Two-clause question is shorter than round 2 (~thirty-five words vs ~fifty), parses cleanly mid-walk
- *Counts it true* is the doctrinal handle: the Father judges what is true (§Vocabulary: Father-aspect = judgment); pulling *true* into the question lets the Father-closer land on something the question has already named

**Line (draft B):**
"*Counted.* Now there is a word for a man to sit with. Tell me, child — for the road has twelve days in your mouth and nine in mine — which of us has walked it? The Father grant me wit to hear the answer."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- Same slip-word foregrounding (*Counted*)
- Doubled-mouth construction (*twelve in your mouth and nine in mine*) — Faith-conversational, not deposition
- Shorter parenthetical
- *Which of us has walked it* is the genuine pastoral question (he knows the answer; he is asking her to hear herself answering it)
- Same conditional-Father closer

*Chosen: A.* B leans on the doubled-mouth move (which is good) but the question *which of us has walked it* is rhetorical-trap territory; Rowan does not lay traps (§Voice: *Direct questions, sincerely meant... Not rhetorically. Not to make a point*). A asks the question Rowan actually wants answered: *how does an eleven-year-old ward count a road she has not walked*. That is the question on the persona card. A also pairs *counts* and *counted* across the line to keep the slip-word in the air without overworking it.

**Cross-line note:** With TH-3 now reading *"Twelve. Counted."*, the seam is closed. Rowan heard a real register-break (an operator's verb in a ward's mouth), and S-2 names what he heard. The *Now there is a word* aphorism reads, in this context, not as stalling but as Rowan's habit of treating the spoken word as the unit of attention — which is exactly what septons do (§Voice tells: *Discernment as the constant background... the narration does not announce this; it surfaces in attention*). The dark-fantasy reader's complaint (composed-vs-caught) becomes the intended read: Rowan is composed *and* caught, the aphorism is the breath he takes between the two.

### S-3 (septon-rowan)
**Seam (as given):** S-3 commits institutional Faith-will from a traveling septon's standing. *The Faith will not* overreaches. Card check: a brother newly appointed under provisional authority making a *Faith-will* claim against a knight in the lord's yard.

**Decision:** REVISE.

The card check is decisive. Per NC-3 intent, the recorder denies Rowan's wardship claim because *Rowan's appointment is provisional*. Per persona card §Stats: *institutional_loyalty: 3 — He serves the Faith; he does not trust the institution*. Per §Voice: *Forbidden registers: ...performance of certainty he does not hold*. Per §Hard Fences §3 of the seam: a traveling septon under provisional authority cannot commit the Faith's institutional will in his own mouth. *The Faith will not* is exactly the move Rowan is structurally barred from making — it is the High Septon's sentence in a village septon's mouth.

What Rowan *can* do — and what the card explicitly licenses (§Direct samples: *I have not the right to refuse the dying their comfort. Nor the living theirs. The sept stands open*) — is refuse-of-personal-authority *couched as* institutional duty. The form is: *I have not the right to do otherwise; the Faith stands; the motherhouse will hear of this*. The closer should source the refusal to *what Rowan cannot do*, not to *what the Faith will not do*. That is the doctrinally honest move for a man whose appointment is provisional and whose institution has not yet ruled.

The pulp-enthusiast seam (*set its hand aside* as elegant abstraction over procedural specifics) is also fair. A revision can name the recorder's specific refusal (*declined to enter her name*) without losing the homiletic register.

The worm-canon-pedant seam (Taylor's adjacent register obligation) is real but is a continuity concern across lines, not an S-3 concern; it lives in the Taylor fork's lines after S-3, not in S-3's surface.

**Line (draft A — chosen):**
"Ser. Hear me before the yard, for it is the yard's business as well as ours. A child's keeping is not the recorder's matter when the keeping touches a soul; the cure of a soul is the Faith's, by the Father's law and not the king's. The recorder this morning would not enter her name. So be it. I have not the right to set the cure of a soul aside on a recorder's word. The motherhouse at Stoney Sept will hear of this before the moon turns, and the motherhouse will speak. I will not, before then, abandon her."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *I have not the right* — refusal-of-personal-authority couched as institutional duty (§Direct samples: *I have not the right to refuse the dying their comfort... The sept stands open*). This is the canonical card-pattern that lets a village septon refuse a knight on Faith grounds without overreaching his rank.
- *The motherhouse will hear of this... and the motherhouse will speak* — sources institutional voice to the body that has it (the motherhouse at Stoney Sept), not to *the Faith* in the abstract or to Rowan's own mouth. Rowan is not committing the Faith's will; he is announcing that the motherhouse will be heard from. Provisional-authority-aware.
- *I will not, before then, abandon her* — Rowan's personal commitment, in his own name, scaled to what he actually has standing to commit (his own conduct as ward-keeper). Plain-Anglo closer (§Cadence: *The plain closer*).
- Time-vocabulary: *before the moon turns* — GRRM moons-not-months (§GRRM card: *Time-keeping vocabulary*: *moons / moon's turn*)
- Procedural specificity addresses pulp seam: *the recorder this morning would not enter her name* — names the specific failure
- Homiletic frame preserved: *Hear me before the yard, for it is the yard's business as well as ours* — public-address theology, parishioner-attention claim
- Seven-pointed star not redeployed (was decorative in round 2); replaced with the institutional-duty register
- *By the Father's law and not the king's* preserved — Father-aspect (judgment), Faith-vs-secular jurisdiction stated as doctrine
- Sermon-rhythm three-and-close runs across the second half: *I have not the right... the motherhouse will hear... I will not, before then, abandon her* — three weighted clauses, plain-Anglo personal closer
- Doctrinal precision: *the cure of a soul* used twice, term of art (§Vocabulary: *Faith vocabulary*)
- The closer is now *I will not, before then, abandon her* — Rowan in his own voice committing what he has standing to commit (his own conduct), with a time-bound qualifier (*before then* = before the motherhouse speaks). This replaces the round-2 *The Faith will not* — the institutional will is now properly located in the motherhouse, and Rowan's will is in himself.

**Line (draft B):**
"Ser, with your leave. A child's keeping is not the recorder's matter when the keeping touches a soul; the cure of a soul is the Faith's. The recorder this morning would not enter her name; so be it. The motherhouse at Stoney Sept appointed me to this sept, provisionally, and provisionally I serve. But provisionally or no, I have not the right to set a child of my keeping aside on a clerk's hand. The motherhouse will hear of this before the moon turns. Until they answer, she is in my care."

**Card features demonstrated:**
- *With your leave* — courtly-courtesy frame, refusal underneath firm (§Direct samples: *With your leave, I will not speak to it further*)
- Names his own provisional standing aloud (*the motherhouse appointed me... provisionally, and provisionally I serve*) — doctrinal honesty, addresses the standing-overreach seam by naming the standing explicitly
- *Provisionally or no, I have not the right* — pivots from his own qualified standing to the unqualified Faith-duty he carries within it. The card pattern (*I have not the right*) lands harder when the speaker has just acknowledged his own provisionality.
- Plain Anglo closer: *Until they answer, she is in my care* — five-and-six words, Saxon, the moral lands on Rowan's own conduct
- Time-vocabulary: *before the moon turns*
- Procedural specificity: *would not enter her name*, *on a clerk's hand*
- Doctrinal precision: *cure of a soul*, *the Faith's*

*Chosen: A.* B is more transparent about provisionality (*provisionally I serve*) which is doctrinally honest, but the explicit self-declaration of provisional standing in a public yard *in front of the knight whose recorder just used that provisionality against him* is tactically a concession Rowan does not need to volunteer — the recorder has named it; Rowan does not need to ratify it on the record. A handles the overreach by *not committing the Faith's will at all* (substituting the motherhouse for the Faith as the agent of institutional voice, and substituting Rowan's own conduct for any Faith-wide claim). The seam is fixed by the structure of the closer, not by self-declaration. A is the more disciplined move; B is the more transparent move; for a septon under doctrinal challenge in a yard he can lose, disciplined wins.

**Closer-line note:** Round 2's *So be it. The Faith will not.* is replaced with *So be it. I have not the right to set the cure of a soul aside on a recorder's word. The motherhouse... will speak. I will not, before then, abandon her.* The plain-Anglo moral closer is preserved (*I will not, before then, abandon her* — Saxon spine, Rowan in his own voice). The institutional will is sourced to the motherhouse, with a time-bound (*before the moon turns*); the Faith-will claim is removed; Rowan's personal commitment is what he has standing to make. The dark-fantasy reader's seam — *too confident for a man in Rowan's position* — is addressed by relocating institutional voice to the body that holds it and reducing Rowan's own claim to the scope his rank actually permits.
