## Rubric carry-back validation

The Phase-4 author named 3 rubric carry-back candidates. For each, the audience adjudicates whether it is a real rubric ambiguity or a defensive construction:

**1. Tens=2 zone-class needs scene-frame anchor rule.**

Confirmed as real rubric ambiguity. The adjudication of mem:3 and mem:8 both required external resolution (cite-index back=Y classification) to settle zone-class disputes that the rubric's text alone — "2 trailing edge — defensible with cue; 2 rising edge — contested" — cannot resolve without knowing which class a specific tens=2 beat falls into. The rubric §quiet-beat test states the distinction but provides no mechanical rule for classification when scene boundaries are not formally dramatist-locked. The Phase-4 defense constructed an "episode-arc release-zone" argument for mem:8 because the micro-beat read was ambiguous. The cite-index's back=Y resolved both cases in this round, but that field's availability is not guaranteed for all productions. Confirmed carry-back: rubric needs either (a) a requirement that dramatist-locked scene-frames precede memory-flag authoring, or (b) explicit guidance that the tensometer's back= field is the mechanical classifier for zone-class, with priority over scalar value alone.

**2. Mask-bleed vs clinical-of-the-horrible distinction.**

Confirmed as real rubric ambiguity. The mem:4 defense required distinguishing somatic-fact registration ("warm" = thermoreceptor data) from voice-register performance (mask-bleed). The rubric anti-pattern §10 defines mask-bleed as "apologetic, deferent, if it please you ser tone" — but does not explicitly carve out somatic-fact registration as a non-bleed class. DFR's STRONG seam on mem:4 demonstrates that under audience pressure, the two signatures (clinical-of-the-horrible's flat surface-fact clause vs. mask-bleed's surface-impression intrusion) are confusable at first read. The rubric's adjacent ACCEPT signature ("clinical-of-the-horrible: the gap between the content and the register is the load") and REJECT signature ("mask-bleed: interior performing the surface affect") do not sharply separate the case where the surface clause registers a physical sensation as fact vs. performs a warmth-affect. Confirmed carry-back: rubric should add a positive definition distinguishing somatic-fact registration (licit: body registers physical sense-data as interior evidence) from mask-bleed (prohibited: description adopts the affective tone of the surface impression rather than the clinical interior register).

**3. Stage-named-cue anti-pattern generalization to analytical-frame primary-nouns.**

Confirmed as real rubric ambiguity, though narrower than the Phase-4 author framed it. The existing rubric anti-pattern §3 names "she remembers... / a memory rises..." as stage-named-cue — labeling the act of remembering. The Phase-4 author proposes extending this to any analytical-frame noun (map, apparatus, performance, system, mechanism) as the description's primary figure. This is confirmed by the evidence: mem:1 ("old map"), mem:2 ("long apparatus"), mem:6 ("performance has rehearsed") all failed on analytical-frame primary-nouns, and the rubric's §3 definition alone did not catch mem:1 and mem:2 (the seam documents invoked §12 author-vocabulary leak and §displacement-discipline rather than §3 stage-named-cue for those two). The rubric has a gap: §3 catches act-of-remembering labels but does not generalize to analytical-frame primary-nouns that name the monument's mechanism or structural role without naming the act of remembering. Confirmed carry-back: extend anti-pattern §3 (or add §3a) to cover description fields where the primary figure is an analytical-frame noun naming the mechanism or structural role of the cue rather than producing the cue's somatic or evidential shape.
