# Round 2 — Maester Output

Persona: westerosi-traveling-maester
Behaviors loaded: westeros-maester (leaf) + westeros-grrm-mannerisms (overlay)
Era: HotD-adjacent / post-Dance traveling circuit. Speaks to a child in the steward's column.

---

### M-1
**Intent:** At the trestle, opening the assessment, the maester sets his finger on the line where Taylor's name appears in the steward's column and asks her to help him locate the name. Polite-brief, the test framed as courtesy; should sound like a Citadel man who has done this in a hundred villages.

**Draft A**
**Line:** "Child. Be so good. The hand here has run a little — help me find the name, just there, where the steward has set it down."
**Card features demonstrated:**
- Vocative *child* (institutional, classless maester register)
- Polite-brief chassis: short clauses, courtesy frame
- The embedded literacy test: *help me find the name* — never *can you read*
- *Just there* with deictic — invites the eyes to track, which is the actual test
- Saxon morphology: *be so good*, *set it down* (no Latinate *locate/identify*)
- The chain doing the talking: no self-announcement
- Avoids em-dash + semicolon-spine; clause-and-clause parallel

**Draft B (chosen)**
**Line:** "Be so kind, child. The steward writes a small hand. Help me to the name — the one above your finger, if you would."
**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Be so kind, child.* — institutional courtesy, Citadel composure, classless address
- Polite-brief, polite-brief: three short sentences instead of one periodic
- Citation-as-interruption: *the steward writes a small hand* lands between request and ask, anchoring it as procedural rather than a test
- Embedded literacy test: *help me to the name* — never *read it*
- *If you would* — modal courtly subjunctive, register-locked maester
- *The one above your finger* — the eyes, not the hand, are the data; the line invites the gaze that confirms literacy
- No clinical English; no em-dash spine; parallel and elliptical

**CHOSEN: Draft B.**

---

### M-2
**Intent:** After Taylor's silence on Osmynd's name at the foot of the column, the maester gives a small interior-noted *mm* and pivots to the next item — the sept's register, did the septon teach her her letters from it. Yes-yes register kept inside the teeth; citation-as-interruption pivot; the working maester filing an observation under his breath while the next question is already arriving.

**Draft A**
**Line:** "Yes, yes. Just so. The sept will keep its register — it is the custom — and Septon Osmynd, by his lights, will have taught his ward from it. From the register, child. Was it so?"
**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Yes, yes. Just so.* — canonical double-affirmation reflex, kept low (the *under-breath* signature)
- Citation-as-interruption: *it is the custom* breaks into the sentence as procedural footnote
- *By his lights* — Saxon-Anglo idiom; not *in his judgment*
- *Septon Osmynd* — institutional pairing of patient-and-condition: he names the man and the office in one breath
- Polite-brief stack: four short clauses, parallel, elliptical
- The pivot before the silence has fully landed — yes-yes filed under-breath, next question already arriving
- Vocative *child*, repeated; classless-register maintained
- No em-dash spine; *—* used once, not as chassis

**Draft B (chosen)**
**Line:** "Mm. Yes, yes. The sept will have its register — every sept does — and Septon Osmynd, may the Father judge him true, will have set your letters there. Was it the register he taught you from, child?"
**Card features demonstrated:**
- *Mm. Yes, yes.* — the under-breath double-affirmation, the maester filing the silence as data without naming it
- *Every sept does* — citation-as-interruption, institutional-we register
- *May the Father judge him true* — Faith aside as form, not piety; the maester registering Osmynd as the dead, in courtesy form (he has read the silence as bereavement-shaped)
- *Set your letters there* — Saxon morphology; *taught you to read* is the Latinate version refused
- Polite-brief; closes on the question, the previous observation un-spoken-of
- The pivot is the structure: he does not pursue the silence, he moves on, and the moving-on is itself the assessment recorded
- Vocative *child*; no clinical English; no semicolon-spine

**CHOSEN: Draft B.**

---

### M-3
**Intent:** After reading and refolding the Bracken bribe note, the maester — without looking at the rider — tells whoever sent him that the assessment is complete and the record is the maester's. Polite-brief Citadel composure under pressure; institutional refusal a brother of the Citadel has rehearsed; neither hostile nor warm.

**Draft A**
**Line:** "Tell your lord I thank him for the courtesy. The assessment is set down. The record is the Citadel's, and the Citadel's alone. By my chain."
**Card features demonstrated:**
- Polite-brief stacked declaratives, four sentences, no subordination
- *I thank him for the courtesy* — the bribe acknowledged as courtesy, refused without naming the refusal (the canonical maester deflection)
- Institutional-we shading into institutional-it: *the Citadel's, and the Citadel's alone*
- *By my chain.* — the chain doing the talking, closer-tag, register-locking
- No self-announcement, no anger, no warmth
- Anglo-Saxon *set down* over Latinate *recorded/finalized*
- Avoids em-dash spine; tag-clause closer per GRRM register

**Draft B (chosen)**
**Line:** "You may tell your lord I have his courtesy and thank him for it. The assessment is set down. The record is the maester's — by the chain that was forged for it, and by no other warrant. Good day to you."
**Card features demonstrated:**
- Polite-brief, polite-brief: three short sentences, parallel; the periodic only at the second clause and short at that
- *You may tell your lord* — institutional courtesy refusing the bribe without naming it; the bribe is folded into *courtesy* and handed back
- *I have his courtesy and thank him for it* — Saxon-shaped acknowledgment; the bribe acknowledged-and-closed in a single clause
- *Set down* — Anglo-Saxon, register-locked maester
- *The record is the maester's* — institutional-third-person; the man does not own it, the office does
- *By the chain that was forged for it* — chain-as-authority, the Citadel's investment language; *forged* is in-register (chain links are forged at the Citadel)
- *No other warrant* — institutional-legal Saxon; refuses Bracken's claim without naming Bracken
- *Good day to you.* — closer, tag-clause, classless-courtesy; no honorific, no warmth, no chill
- Without looking at the rider: the line carries no addressee-grammar (no *ser*, no *my lord*, only *your lord*) — the rider is a courier, the maester is speaking past him to the sender
- Avoids clinical English (*the assessment is complete*) by reaching for *set down*; avoids em-dash + semicolon chassis; em-dash used once, not as spine

**CHOSEN: Draft B.**

---

## Authoring notes

- Across all three lines I held the polite-brief, polite-brief rhythm and used the em-dash sparingly (never twice in a line, never as the structural spine).
- M-1 and M-2 keep the *child* vocative — the classless maester register, addressing minors directly regardless of station. M-3 drops it: he is no longer in the assessment; he is closing the record.
- *Yes, yes* placed where the card prescribes — under-breath, before the question is fully formed (M-2). M-1 does not deploy it; the assessment has not yet found its tell.
- The chain is named once, in M-3, where the institutional weight is the entire content of the line. Naming it earlier would be self-promotion register, refused.
- Time-keeping vocabulary did not have an organic seat in any of these three intents (the lines are in-room and present-tense). The X-and-twenty form likewise did not surface naturally. The GRRM overlay sits in the morphology (*set down, set your letters, no other warrant, by my chain, good day to you*) rather than in time/number markers.
- Faith-aside in M-2 (*may the Father judge him true*) does the work of acknowledging Osmynd-as-dead in courtesy form — the maester reads the silence and registers it institutionally, not affectively.
