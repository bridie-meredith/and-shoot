## Author's note

What I found myself reaching for, repeatedly, was the *meaning-of-the-beat* read — every external action got pulled toward a tactical or social interpretation, because that is where this voice lives. I fired heavily on officer-and-clerk beats (entry, gaze, stylus, foot, shoulder) because each of those is a positional move in a transaction Taylor is reading at adult depth, and skipping them felt like silencing the lens. I hesitated most on the pure-environment beats at the open (cart, banner, horse, beetles) — they are not events, but Taylor's passive sense and her habit of reading a room before entering it made it feel wrong to skip them entirely; I fired on all four and was uncertain about that volume so early. I skipped the bare locomotion beats (13, 26, 30, 40, 44, 47, 49, 51, 59, 62, 66, 71, 72, 76) where the action is either someone else's speech-act with no new content for her or her own foot/hand on a continuous motion she had already decided about — those felt like the load-bearing moments are the framing beats next to them, not the motion itself. I fired and was unsure on a few interior-bordering beats (29, 38, 48) where the registration is closer to a verdict than a perception; the line between "she notices" and "she concludes" thinned out in those, and I let it. The inventory tell (corvids/beetles/rats) surfaced less than I expected — only at 4, 34, 69 — because the beats that explicitly named fauna were where it landed cleanest, and forcing it elsewhere would have been imposed.
