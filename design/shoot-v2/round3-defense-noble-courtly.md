# Round 3 — Noble-Courtly Writer Defense

Writer fork: noble-courtly. Behavior cards reloaded (`westeros-noble-courtly` leaf, `westeros-grrm-mannerisms` overlay). Persona cards reloaded (census-officer, plumms-man, district-recorder, ser-harwick-plumm). Blind to corpus and show files.

Method: read seam, defend with card citation OR revise with multi-draft + chosen mark + how the seam is answered + signatures demonstrated. Sharpen, do not soften.

### NC-1 (census-officer)
**Seam (audience):** *"Seated in the cure"* is a Faith-and-recorder term of art deployed in a public carry-distance proclamation to a yard of smallfolk, without gloss — the people most at risk of the census have no reliable way to know whether they qualify, so the officer has either made a procedural error or revealed that the commission language is intentionally opaque.

**Decision:** REVISE.

The seam is load-bearing. The intent specifies *audible at carry-distance, public-authority register* — opacity at the key qualifying clause is exactly the failure the intent forbids. The functionary's craft is making the lord's right legible to the yard so the yard cannot plead ignorance later. *Seated in the cure* is the recorder's chamber-vocabulary, not the gate-and-yard vocabulary; the officer who borrows it from the document into the proclamation has copied a clause he should have translated. The double-citation (*castellan and lord's hand*) is also worth tightening — the dark-fantasy push there is right that one source of authority lands harder than two; the second citation is scribe-formula bleeding into speech.

**Draft A — chosen.**
"Under the lord's hand, you shall hear me. Every ward of this sept, every soul without a holding to name, every man and every woman that cannot name a guardian to keep them — to the board, before the sun is off the wall. The roll closes at midday. None shall be entered after."

**How the seam is answered.**
- *Seated in the cure* is removed. Replaced with *cannot name a guardian to keep them* — Saxon-suffix, plain-Westerosi, audible from the back of a yard. The qualifying condition is now in the same register as the people it applies to. No smallfolk listener can claim they did not understand.
- The double-citation is collapsed. *Under the lord's hand* alone — the higher of the two authorities, named once. The functionary owns his commission rather than citing it twice.
- *Before the sun is off the wall* replaces *before this yard is quit* — agricultural time-marker, audible-clock for the yard, GRRM time-vocabulary in the public-speech register rather than the document register.
- Tricolon retained (*every ward... every soul... every man and every woman*) but tightened: anaphora on *every*, parallel-structure rather than nested-relative-clause. Card §Cadence: *parallel structure for emphasis... not flagged as ornate*.

**Signatures demonstrated.**
- Periodic: long opening establishes conditions, load-bearing imperative (*to the board*) arrives at the close. Card §Syntax: *periodic sentences in formal address*.
- Tricolon, anaphoric: *every ward, every soul, every man and every woman* — card §Syntax, *parallel structure for emphasis*.
- *Shall*-modal twice (*shall hear, shall be entered*), commission-register. Card §Vocabulary: *modal shall over will*.
- Authority-source: *under the lord's hand* — honor-vocabulary, single citation. Card §Voice tells: *honor and reputation as load-bearing*.
- Time-marker: *the sun is off the wall* / *at midday*. Overlay §Time-keeping: *at dawn, at dusk, at midday*.
- Saxon imperative-fragment closer: *to the board* — bare directional, no Latinate verb. Overlay §Anglo-Saxon prefix preference.
- No modern compliance-English. The instrument is the lord's hand and the yard's own vocabulary; not *register process*, not *seated in the cure*.

**Draft B — rejected (closer to round-2 architecture, but the seam survives).**
"Under the lord's hand, you shall hear me. All wards of this sept, all souls without a holding, and any man or woman that cannot name a guardian to keep them — shall come forward to the board before midday. The roll closes then. None shall be entered after."
*Why rejected:* keeps the sixty-word periodic chassis the pulp seam flagged as too slow for a carry-distance call. Draft A pulls the imperative forward and substitutes anaphora for nested-relatives, which lets the proclamation land before the listeners drift.

### NC-2 (plumms-man)
**Seam (audience):** *"The bird shall fly"* functions as a prediction of the consequence of compliance rather than a direct command — a functionary cold enough to drop all honorifics should be cold enough to issue direct imperatives rather than *shall*-predictive consequence-statements.

**Decision:** REVISE.

The seam is correct on register-internal grounds. *Shall* on the bird's flight subordinates the order to a description of its result; the persona is *says no more than needed*, and the *shall*-prediction is one beat more than needed. The *from the perch* redundancy the pulp lens flagged is also right — the imperative is cleaner without the prepositional re-specification. Cold mid-functionary register prefers the bare imperative; the *shall* belongs on the addressee's required action, not on the bird's automatic consequence.

**Draft A — chosen.**
"Loose her, child. Stand back. You shall not touch her again."

**How the seam is answered.**
- *The bird shall fly* is cut. The first imperative (*loose her*) names the action; the bird's flight is its automatic consequence and does not require narration. The cold functionary states what the addressee must do, not what the bird will do.
- *From the perch* is cut. *Stand back* is the directional with no further specification needed.
- *Shall* relocates to where it earns: *you shall not touch her again*. The modal now governs the addressee's continuing prohibition rather than the bird's predictable motion. This is the *shall* of administrative restriction — the form of address card §Vocabulary names as the noble's command-structure.
- Three short imperatives, declarative-and-prohibition. Cadence under pressure: tightens, does not loosen. Card §Cadence.

**Signatures demonstrated.**
- Address-by-station, no honorific: *child*. Card §Register markers: *status-sensitivity. Goodman to a smallfolk one is being polite to* — and *child* is the rung beneath that, the address that places the addressee in the procedural hierarchy without insulting and without warming.
- *Shall*-modal, prohibitive (*you shall not touch her again*). Card §Vocabulary: *modal shall over will*.
- Saxon imperative: *loose her, stand back*. Overlay §Anglo-Saxon prefix preference. *Loose* not *release*; *stand back* not *step away*.
- Three-clause restraint, parallel-minimal. Card §Cadence: *pacing under pressure: slower, not faster... shorter, more controlled, more declarative*.
- The third clause (*you shall not touch her again*) carries the actual board-move: this is not a one-time instruction, it is a procedural exclusion. The functionary has placed the ward outside the raven's care for what follows. Coldness sourced to the second clause's reach, not to volume. Card §Voice tells: *the calmer the line, the heavier the consequence*.

**Draft B — rejected (round-2 chassis with the *shall* fixed but no third clause).**
"Loose her, child. Stand back."
*Why rejected:* clean and cold but loses the procedural-exclusion move that distinguishes a Plumm's-man (placing the ward outside future access to the raven) from a generic functionary giving a one-time order. Draft A keeps the coldness and adds the board-move that justifies why this functionary, in this scene, is the one speaking.

### NC-3 (district-recorder)
**Seam (audience):** The line opens with passive-institutional (*"the claim is refused"*) and closes with first-person-owned (*"I shall not enter it"*), mixing two modes of recorder authority — either writer's register-drift or a character uncertain whether he is the protocol or the man executing it. That uncertainty undercuts *polite procedural finality*.

**Decision:** DEFEND.

The mix is the character. The recorder has been in post eleven years; he is *the form animated*, not the form itself. Card §Register markers describes the noble's *house-pronominal* slide: *we and our slide between personal and House-personal seamlessly... context disambiguates. Listeners are expected to track which.* The same principle governs the institutional voice. The recorder mirrors-and-receives the claim in the institutional voice (*the claim is heard, the claim is refused* — these are courtly procedural-formula, not the recorder's personal opinion), then owns the executing act in first person (*I shall not enter it against the line* — the act of putting hand to ledger is his hand, his liability, his name on the page if it is wrong). This is not register-drift; it is the precise distinction between *what the protocol does* (passive, distributed) and *what the recorder does on the protocol's behalf* (first-person, owned). The line marks the seam that careful Westerosi institutional speech *requires* a recorder to mark — eleven years' practice in distinguishing the office from the man.

The *Bring the letter... and the matter shall be heard again* closer also defends. Pulp seam said the courtesy softens finality. It does not. The conditional re-hearing is the protocol's standing offer, not the recorder's personal concession — *the matter shall be heard* is passive-institutional, returning the voice to the office at the close. The recorder finishes by stepping back into the form. That is *polite procedural finality*: the door is closed; the protocol's standard re-entry condition is named; the man behind the desk has not promised anything personally.

The line stands.

**Card citations.**
- §Register markers, *house-pronominal*: institutional pronoun-mode and personal pronoun-mode coexist in noble-courtly speech; the listener tracks which.
- §Voice tells, *the cost of breaking register*: the recorder does not break register here — he toggles between two register-states the form requires, which is correct procedural craft.
- §Vocabulary, *modal shall over will*: three *shall* (*shall not recognize, shall not enter, shall be heard*), with the load distributed correctly — the protocol's *shall* on the institutional verbs, the recorder's *shall* on his own act.
- §Syntax, *parallel structure for emphasis*: tricolon by clause-pairing (*the claim is heard / the claim is refused / the matter shall be heard again*) — opening, ruling, closure. Structural, not emphatic.
- Overlay §Recurring phrases: *as you say, my lord* register — the polite formula carrying procedural finality without warmth.

The two-mode voice is the recorder's signature. Collapsing it to either pure-institutional or pure-first-person would remove the eleven-years-in-post register that makes him distinct from a notary or a clerk.

### NC-4 (ser-harwick-plumm)
**Seam (audience):** The *however*-tricolon dismisses the motherhouse at Stoney Sept's endorsement — Rowan's strongest argument for Faith jurisdiction — not by engaging why the motherhouse's authority does not apply but by grammatically subordinating it to the procedural distinction between naming and seating. Plumm has evaded the harder doctrinal question while appearing to have answered it; the line does not acknowledge whether the evasion is intentional strategy or a structural gap.

**Decision:** DEFEND.

This is exactly the character. The seam-finder has correctly identified the move; the question is whether the move is craft or miss, and the persona card answers it: knight-administrator under a lord's commission, executing on procedural ground, deliberately not engaging Faith jurisdiction because *to engage Faith jurisdiction is to concede that Faith jurisdiction is on the table*. The *however*-tricolon is the textbook procedural sidestep — Plumm names the strongest version of Rowan's argument (well-witnessed by the motherhouse, the highest endorsement available to a traveling septon) and lets the procedural distinction (naming vs. seating) carry the dismissal. He does not say *the motherhouse has no authority here*; he says *whatever authority the motherhouse has does not seat a man*. The first claim would be a doctrinal fight Plumm is not commissioned to have. The second claim is a procedural fact Plumm is commissioned to enforce.

The line *should* carry the awareness that the evasion is strategy, and it does — in the *I find* tricolon. *I find the protocol satisfied in its plain reading* is the load-bearing self-aware admission: Plumm is signaling that the protocol's *plain reading* is the ground he is standing on, and the doctrinal ground (Faith vs. crown jurisdiction) is being left unaddressed by deliberate election. *In its plain reading* is the courtly tell that says *I am taking the narrow ground because the narrow ground is sufficient; the wider ground is not mine to rule on*. A reader fluent in the noble-courtly register hears that. The dark-fantasy push is correct that a careful reader will notice the evasion; the persona-card answer is that Plumm wants the careful reader to notice — the evasion is the demonstration of competence. A knight-administrator who engaged the doctrinal question on Rowan's terms would have lost the proceeding the moment he engaged. Refusing to engage is the ruling.

The pulp seam (*forty-five characters of subjunctive courtesy before the argument*) is also a defense, not a revision target. Card §Voice tells: *honor and reputation as load-bearing... He could not refuse without insult. To accept would be to acknowledge the slight.* Plumm's opening (*I would not have him stand upon it longer than is needful*) is the noble-courtly equivalent of *I am about to publicly correct a septon, and I am marking that I do this with regret, not relish*. Cutting the opening would make Plumm a procedural bully. Keeping it is what makes him a knight executing his commission with the public-courtesy his standing requires.

The worm-canon-pedant push (*too composed, more theater than ruling*) is the only seam I would treat as partially earning. The card response: noble-courtly knight-administrator register *is* composed in public ruling. Card §Voice tells: *pacing under pressure: slower, not faster. Sentences become shorter, more controlled, more declarative.* The composedness is the register-correct response to public pressure, not a sign that the line is artifact-rather-than-ruling. A rough-edged Plumm at this rung would be a register-failure on the card's own terms.

The line stands.

**Card citations.**
- §Syntax, *periodic sentences in formal address*: the second sentence is textbook periodic — three *however*-clauses inside the conditions, load-bearing main predicate (*names a man to a cure but does not seat him in it*) at the close.
- §Syntax, *parallel structure for emphasis*: two tricolons — *however lawful, however solemn, however well-witnessed*; and *I find... I find... I find...*. Anaphora as rhetorical structure, not emphatic.
- §Voice tells, *indirection as habit*: *the matter, the ground, the cure* — the courtly habit of not naming the sensitive thing directly. The doctrinal question is *the ground* Plumm declines to engage; the procedural question is *the matter* he rules on.
- §Cadence, *pacing under pressure: slower, not faster*: the register tightens to declarative *I find* anaphora at the close. The composedness is the persona's correct response to public pressure.
- §Vocabulary, *politeness particles*: *I would not have him* (subjunctive softening of public correction) — *the lord who would beg your indulgence is not actually begging; he is observing form*. Plumm is observing form before the correction, not begging.
- §Vocabulary, *honor-vocabulary as load-bearing*: *the cure* (a soul-charge), *the term, the line, the protocol, the motherhouse* — Faith-and-office vocabulary, not modern administrative.
- §Era and regional notes, *House of the Dragon era... slightly more shall, slightly more inversion*: the register sits squarely in the 120 AC bias — *the term is run out* (idiomatic, slightly archaic), *I would not have him stand upon it* (subjunctive intact), *needful* (Saxon-suffix in HotD-current register).
- Overlay §Sentence patterns, *the proverb-pivot... a character's argument lands on a proverb, treated as if the proverb settles the matter*: *names a man to a cure but does not seat him in it* functions as the proverb-pivot. It is delivered as if it is settled doctrine; it is in fact Plumm's framing of the recorder's earlier ruling. The proverb-pivot is the rhetorical move that makes a contested distinction read as received wisdom.

The *however*-tricolon is the calculated procedural sidestep, not a writing miss. It is the line's most load-bearing demonstration of the knight-administrator rung — the move that distinguishes Plumm from the recorder (who cites the protocol) and from the census-officer (who invokes the lord's hand). Plumm rules without ruling on the doctrinal ground, and the *I find* tricolon owns the narrow ruling he chose to make. That is exactly the character.
