### fault-001 — Aggregate non-monotonic IDs

- decision: ACKNOWLEDGE-AND-ESCALATE
- justification: The auditor identifies 21 IDs in the 900-range (922, 924, 930, 931, 916, 935, 926, 925, 927, 928, 932, 919, 920, 921, 923, 904, 905, 906, 907, 910, 911) appearing interspersed within the 1–250 content region of the aggregate. The schema's rule is "stable — once assigned, never reused, never reassigned" plus "re-ordering preserves IDs." These two rules are in tension when content is moved in the aggregate during prior passes: if a bone was originally at aggregate position ~900 and was relocated to an earlier narrative position during a pass-2 or pass-3 reorder, it retains its original ID (stable) but now sits out of monotonic sequence. Under the schema's "stable" rule, this is legal as long as the ID is not reused or reassigned — the bone kept its identity. Under the "re-ordering preserves IDs" rule (which says reordering does not change IDs), the presence of 900-range IDs at narrative positions 32–248 is schema-consistent if these bones were moved from later positions to earlier positions via a rubric-sanctioned reorder. However, the fixer formula `aggregate_id = aggregate_range_start + episode_id - 1` assumes a contiguous monotonic mapping that is broken by these survivor IDs. Any downstream fixer computation using this formula for any episode that covers the out-of-order region will produce incorrect aggregate-to-episode mappings.
- the two interpretations cannot be reconciled without human review of the pass history: if these IDs are reorder survivors, they are schema-compliant (stable rule honors them) and the fixer formula needs to be acknowledged as inapplicable to non-monotonic ranges; if they are data artifacts from a file-generation error, they are schema violations requiring correction. This distinction requires human decision.
- cascade and impact on downstream fixer formula: if confirmed as legal-deletion-gap survivors, the fixer formula must be documented as a named limitation — it is not generally applicable to the aggregate; episode-level re-numbering (1..M per episode) means the per-episode files are self-consistent and the only mapping affected is aggregate_id lookup by formula. Phase H should document: "fixer aggregate_id formula applies only to contiguous monotonic aggregate ranges; for non-monotonic survivor IDs, aggregate lookup must use a direct scan of the aggregate file rather than arithmetic offset."
- if confirmed as reordering artifacts (schema violation), fixer must renumber the out-of-order bones in the aggregate to their correct monotonic positions, re-running the per-episode aggregate_range header validations afterward.
- recommendation for human reviewer: confirm whether the 21 900-range IDs at aggregate positions ~32–248 are the result of a rubric-sanctioned reorder during prior passes (legal survivors) or a generation artifact (violation). The prior pass audit files (phase2_pass2_constraint, phase2_pass3_shape) should contain evidence of whether any bulk reorder moved these bones from their original positions.
