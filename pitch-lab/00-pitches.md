# 33 Fantasy–Sci-Fi Pitches

Single-sentence loglines for science-fantasy stories. Each is seeded against one of the project's eight premise lenses (relational / political / interior / penitential / escape / vocational / structural / accidental) so the field has dramatic variety, and each implies a cost structure (a road-to-hell engine) rather than only a premise — that is what the downstream plan must deliver on.

Format: `P## [lens] — logline`

---

P01 [relational] — A bonewright who can grow living prosthetics from her own marrow keeps her dying sister walking by replacing her piece by piece, until the sister who finally stands is no longer the person she was saving.

P02 [vocational] — The empire's last star-cartographer discovers that the constellations she charts are a language, that reading them aloud rewrites the sky, and that her life's masterwork is slowly erasing the stars sailors steer by.

P03 [penitential] — A field-surgeon who once amputated a plague-arm too late now carries a hive of healing wasps in his chest, and every life he saves costs another villager the memory that proves the disease was ever real.

P04 [political] — On a tide-locked world where the noble houses are ranked by how long they can hold their breath underwater, a commoner girl who never needs to surface is adopted as a weapon — and learns the houses drowned the last person like her to keep the rankings honest.

P05 [escape] — A deserter from the dream-war stows away inside a sleeping god's nightmare to reach the one country no army can invade, not knowing the god wakes when its last dreamer leaves.

P06 [interior] — A clocksmith compelled to rewind any death he witnesses has saved the same stranger forty times, and is beginning to suspect the stranger is the one who taught him the compulsion in the first life he cannot remember.

P07 [structural] — Caught between a machine-priesthood that prints souls and a wild magic that grows them, an orphan whose soul was half-printed and half-grown becomes the treaty both sides will burn the city to sign.

P08 [accidental] — A glassblower idly shapes a lens that focuses moonlight into a single perfect note, the note wakes something asleep under the kiln-district, and the city begins to remember a war it was built to forget.

P09 [vocational] — A debt-collector for a guild that lends years off your lifespan is the best in the city because she can hear the exact hour a person will die — until she's sent to collect from a child whose death-hour is the same as her own.

P10 [relational] — Two sisters share one body across alternating days, and when one of them falls in love, the other begins quietly sabotaging the lover so she'll never have to wake up alone in a stranger's grief.

P11 [political] — The colony's terraforming engine runs on confessions, the largest sins yield the most fertile soil, and the new governor must decide whether to seed the dying crops with a crime she has not yet committed.

P12 [interior] — A man who cannot stop counting builds a perfect ledger of every breath the city takes, and when the count tells him exactly how many people the coming famine will kill, he realizes the ledger has been choosing them.

P13 [penitential] — A war-witch who unmade an army with a single word now teaches grammar to children so the word can never be spoken again, until one of her students is born already knowing it.

P14 [escape] — A courier flees across the rooftops of a vertical world by trading memories to the wind for speed, and arrives at sanctuary having forgotten why she ran, what she carried, and the face of the child waiting there.

P15 [structural] — A bridge-keeper between the living city above and the archived dead below is forced to ration who crosses when both halves of the world begin sinking toward each other.

P16 [vocational] — A scent-engineer who designs the perfumes that keep a refugee fleet calm discovers her newest blend is being used to make the passengers forget which planet they're owed, and that she has been breathing it longest.

P17 [accidental] — A boy trades his shadow to a salvage-drone for passage off-world, the shadow keeps living his life better than he did, and by the time he returns home everyone he loved has chosen it over him.

P18 [relational] — A mother who can fold space carries her son across a galaxy one impossible step at a time, but each fold ages her a decade and shortens his by a year, so the closer they get to safety the more he becomes a stranger raising her.

P19 [political] — In a senate where votes are cast in years of remaining life, the youngest senator wins every motion by spending recklessly, and must choose between her cause and the months she has left to see it pass.

P20 [interior] — An archivist forbidden from feeling grief swallows the city's sorrows to keep the peace, growing a second silent self out of everything she refuses to mourn, until that self begins signing her name.

P21 [penitential] — The pilot who fired the shot that cracked a moon now ferries its falling pieces back to orbit one shard at a time, knowing the work will outlast her by a thousand years and that finishing it would prove the moon could have been saved.

P22 [escape] — A girl raised inside a story-engine that writes the kingdom's official history rewrites herself a way out, only to find that everyone she meets beyond the margins is a character someone else is trying to escape into.

P23 [vocational] — A translator for the things that live in the gaps between languages is hired to broker peace with an invading silence, and realizes that to make it understood she must teach it the one word that lets it want.

P24 [structural] — A child born on the seam between a kingdom that runs on sunlight and one that runs on its absence can survive in neither, and is courted by both as the only ambassador who can stand in the dusk where their war is fought.

P25 [accidental] — A locksmith picks a door that was never meant to open and lets out the previous tenant of the world, who is polite, grateful, and quietly resuming a life everyone else has been living in the meantime.

P26 [relational] — A man keeps his drowned wife alive as weather, and the small kindnesses he does to keep her gentle — the warm rains, the soft winters — are starving the coast that depended on her storms.

P27 [vocational] — The realm's finest forger of divine signatures is commissioned to fake a god's blessing on a war, succeeds so perfectly that the god begins to believe it, and must now forge the deity's change of heart before the belief becomes true.

P28 [political] — On a generation ship governed by whoever holds the most descendants, a barren navigator who alone remembers the destination is being voted out of the only role that could land them.

P29 [interior] — A cartomancer who reads the future in the wear-patterns of well-handled cards realizes the deck is wearing toward a single dealt hand, and that every reading she gives to prevent it deals it one card closer.

P30 [penitential] — A famine-saint who multiplies bread by taking the hunger into herself is asked to feed an entire besieged city, and the hunger she absorbs begins to multiply too.

P31 [escape] — A conscript in an endless trench-war between two color-kingdoms defects into the no-man's-land's grey, where deserters from both sides have built a third country that neither army can perceive — and that neither will let exist once it learns the grey is winning.

P32 [structural] — A weather-priest caught between farmers who pray for rain and a sky-guild that sells it must choose whose drought to end, knowing the rain he calls down anywhere will be invoiced everywhere.

P33 [vocational] — A demolitions-mage who brings down the towers of the old magic to make room for the new is the only one who can still read the warnings carved in their foundations, and the towers she has left to fell are the ones that say what falls next.
