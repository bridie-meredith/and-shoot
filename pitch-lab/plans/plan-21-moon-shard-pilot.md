# P21 moon-shard-pilot

LOGLINE: The pilot who fired the shot that cracked the moon now ferries its falling shards back to orbit one piece at a time, knowing the work will outlast her by a thousand years and that finishing it would prove the moon could have been saved.

LENS: penitential

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Cael takes every ferry run herself because the guild offered her a desk and she refused it; moving the shards is the only act that feels proportional to the crack.
- anchor: The original targeting station on the dark side — still standing, still logged, unmanned — where the fire order came from her hand.
- escalation: The shards she has already returned are not stable in orbit; they are drifting back together, and when they reconverge they will reform a moon with a fault line that makes the original whole moon's structural integrity look sound.
- trade: She can stop ferrying — let the shards fall where they will, diffuse impact over centuries — or she can keep working, building a reconstituted moon that proves the original was salvageable and therefore that she destroyed something that did not need destroying.
- irony: The only way to finish the work is to prove the work should never have started.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The moon was cracked by a focused gravitational lance fired from an orbital platform — a weapon designed to shatter asteroid threats. Cael's ship is a medium-tonnage hauler retrofitted with a gravity-well anchor that lets her latch onto a shard up to eight hundred meters across, pull it to a stable burn, and sling it into a calculated orbit. The shards are not inert: the moon had a magnetic core, and pieces larger than fifty meters still carry residual pull toward one another. Cael plots each trajectory to avoid convergence, but the work is self-defeating — the more shards she places, the stronger the aggregate pull becomes, and the margin for safe placement shrinks. Her navigation system flags trajectories that will cause convergence in under two hundred years as ORANGE; under fifty years, RED. The board is now all red.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: moral-framework → slow collapse (each run confirms the evidence she is building against herself); capability ↑ as a pilot, ↓ as a planner (she flies better than anyone; the larger problem she cannot solve); legibility ↑ (the reconstituting moon is a visible record of what she did)
- cost_ledger: Run 1–40 → margin narrows, board goes orange / Run 67 → first RED trajectory accepted anyway / Run 89 → she files the stability report under a false date / Final run → she must choose to place the last shard that triggers reconvergence or abort and scatter
- antagonist_pressure: The Guild of Restoration has begun publishing her run logs as proof of redemption-through-labor; each log entry becomes public record; by act three a memorial commission wants to name the reformed moon after her, making the cover-up impossible
- dramatic_shape: Cael takes run 90 → stability report surfaces → commission announces the memorial naming → she reads the original fire order for the first time since the crack → she makes run 91 knowing it will trigger convergence → she files her last log entry honestly

## Protagonist arc
Cael begins running to avoid the station log — physical motion as a substitute for reading what she signed. She ends sitting in the targeting station for the first time since the shot, reading the order aloud to no one, with the commission's plaque already cast. The single decision: she tells the commission the moon reconverges in forty years, not a thousand, and she files run 91's log with the real trajectory data.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Cael anchors shard 91-C and slings it into the calculated slot; the board updates: seven trajectories go from ORANGE to RED; she logs the run, marks the trajectory correct, and does not note the upgrade.
2. The Guild publishes her first forty run-logs as a redemption archive; a journalist asks her to confirm the moon will be stable at completion; she says yes.
3. The stability report — commissioned two years ago and suppressed — resurfaces in the Guild's records request; Cael has six days before it is read.
4. The commission sends her the plaque rendering: her name, the moon's name, the completion date. She holds it in the cockpit during run 92 and does not latch onto the shard.
5. She flies to the targeting station instead. She reads the fire order — her own authorization, timestamped, logged. She sits there for four hours. Then she calls the commission, tells them the reconvergence timeline, and sends the unredacted stability report. She does run 93 anyway, because someone has to.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Cael grips the anchor lever and the ship shudders as the gravity-well latches onto the shard — she feels it in her teeth, a low vibration that means the piece is live and pulling. She slings it wide, releases at the apex, watches it arc into the dark. Then she checks the board. Three more trajectories go red. She marks them and starts the burn home.

## Novelty claim
The act of penance is physically self-undermining in a documented, mathematical way — the reconstruction is a visible proof of the original crime's senselessness, and the protagonist's competence accelerates the revelation she is trying to outrun.
