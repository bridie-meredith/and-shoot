# P08 moonlight-note

LOGLINE: A glassblower shapes a lens that focuses moonlight into a single sustained note, the note vibrates something awake beneath the kiln-district, and the city begins to remember a war it was built to forget.

LENS: accidental

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: He blows glass for a living and shapes the lens as a curiosity — a test of his precision, nothing more.
- anchor: His neighborhood — the kiln-district, whose residents include his daughter, his customers, and the families who built their lives on deliberately not knowing what the ground holds.
- escalation: Each night the note sounds, the city remembers more: first old street-names, then faces, then the specific acts of violence that were buried with the dead under the kiln-district foundations.
- trade: Destroying the lens stops the remembering — but the thing awake underground has already started moving toward the surface, and it will arrive whether the note sounds again or not.
- irony: He shaped the lens to prove his skill; proving his skill has made it impossible to contain what his skill proved he was capable of.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
Moonlight carries a residual charge from surfaces it has touched over centuries — it accumulates witness. A lens that focuses moonlight tightly enough concentrates that accumulated witness into a frequency that resonates with the specific mineral composition of war-dead buried under the kiln-district's clay. The note is not metaphorical: it is a physical standing wave that passes through the clay and vibrates the bones of the dead. When the bones vibrate, the memories encoded in them surface in the dreams and waking moments of anyone within the resonance field — the whole district. The thing awakening is the composite will of all those dead, cohering into a single body from the clay around their bones. Each successive sounding makes the body more complete.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability inverts (the lens he made becomes a liability he cannot unmake) / legibility falls for the city (remembered history surfaces against civic will) / social-tether cracks (neighbors turn on each other as they remember whose family did what)
- cost_ledger: First sounding → district-wide nightmares → neighbors lose sleep and function; second sounding → specific memories surface (names, faces, acts) → civic infrastructure begins breaking as people refuse to work alongside each other; each subsequent sounding → composite body more fully formed → nearer to the surface
- antagonist_pressure: Round 1 — city authority confiscates the lens and locks it; Round 2 — the composite body begins sending up proxy-agents (dead-clay hands through kiln floors) to force the lens to sound, removing the city's option to simply suppress it; Round 3 — the composite body names a specific living person responsible for the original burial order, who is still alive and in a position of civic power
- dramatic_shape: setup (lens shaped, note sounds, district dreams begin) → escalation (memories surface, neighbors recognize each other's histories, city authority acts) → midpoint reversal (composite body sends proxies up; suppression is no longer viable) → cost spike (the named living official is his daughter's employer; her safety depends on the silence) → climax (he carries the lens to the body's site and lets it sound one final time, fully) → aftermath (the composite body surfaces, names the living, and the city holds a reckoning or does not)

## Protagonist arc
Start: skilled, incurious, proud of precision. End: he understands that precision without context is just a sharper instrument of accidental harm — and he chooses, once, to let the harm he started complete itself honestly rather than suppress it. The flip: he carries the lens to the burial site himself rather than helping the city bury it a second time.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. He finishes the lens at dawn, holds it to the window as the moon sets, and a clear single note fills the workshop for four seconds; his daughter, asleep upstairs, wakes and asks who was crying.
2. The district wakes with matching dreams: a particular street name, cobblestones laid over something that shifted; he hears three separate customers describe the same street, the same smell of clay.
3. City authority arrives with a story about resonance hazard in glass near gas-lines and takes the lens; that night, a hand made of compacted kiln-clay pushes through the floor of the civic depository and the note sounds for eleven seconds.
4. He traces the resonance field with a borrowed sextant and discovers the burial site directly under the oldest kiln — the one his grandfather built — and finds a city survey map from sixty years ago that shows the original excavation order.
5. The composite body surfaces a partial torso from the clay at the district's center well; it points at a building; his daughter works in that building, for the man whose family signed the excavation order.
6. He retrieves the lens, walks to the well, holds it to the moon, and lets the note sound until the composite body is fully surfaced and has said every name aloud.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
He holds a glass lens at arm's length between the moon and a flat surface; a tone rises from the clay floor of his workshop — audible, pitchable, sustained. Neighbors within two streets hear it through closed windows. Bones forty feet underground vibrate at that frequency; sleepers in the district twitch and speak names they have never heard. The composite body assembles itself from the clay around those bones, gains a hand, then an arm, then a face, each night the note sounds.

## Novelty claim
Moonlight-as-memory-archive is not new; the specific mechanism of a glassblower's precision accidentally concentrating accumulated witness into a frequency that physically assembles a composite body from war-dead bones is the novel combination. The protagonist's culpability is purely accidental — the road-to-hell turn is that he never pursued anything except craft.
