# P10 sisters-alternating-days

LOGLINE: Two sisters share one body on alternating days, and when one falls in love, the other begins quietly sabotaging the lover so she will never have to wake up to someone else's grief.

LENS: interior

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Calla wants a continuous life — dates that span more than one day, a lover who doesn't have to be re-explained every 48 hours.
- anchor: She meets Dov, who is patient enough to learn both sisters and decides he loves Calla specifically.
- escalation: Renne, the other sister, realizes she will wake every other day to Dov's presence, his expectations, and the residue of Calla's feelings — indefinitely.
- trade: Renne engineers Dov's withdrawal by impersonating Calla coldly, returning his letters unopened, leaving his belongings outside the door.
- irony: Renne's sabotage works; she wakes alone as she wanted — but the body she shares now wakes on Calla's days already grieving, and Renne inherits that grief every other morning without any of the love that produced it.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The sisters share one body through a clean alternation: Calla has odd days, Renne has even days. The switch happens at midnight — one goes under and the other wakes in the same bed. They communicate through a shared notebook kept on the nightstand, written in two different inks. The body carries physical continuity — injuries, hunger, arousal, exhaustion — but memory does not transfer except through the notebook. Neither sister can override the other's day or read the other's experience directly; they know only what is written down and what they find when they wake: the state of the room, the set of the jaw in the mirror, a smell on the sheets. The notebook is the only channel and it is voluntary — either sister can leave a page blank.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: social-tether (Calla's rising, Renne's eroding); moral-framework (Renne's self-defense framing collapses into deliberate harm); legibility (each sister to the other, degrading as Renne begins writing less)
- cost_ledger: Renne's first cold impersonation gives her one quiet morning — cost: Calla writes three pages of confusion that Renne must read. Each subsequent sabotage act pushes Dov further — cost: Renne's notebook entries get shorter until she stops writing to Calla at all, and the silence is a second betrayal. The final withdrawal of Dov — cost: Renne wakes to a body that has been crying in its sleep on Calla's days, and she cannot stop it.
- antagonist_pressure: R1 — Dov's patience itself is the pressure; he keeps showing up, so Renne must escalate. R2 — Calla begins to suspect the body is being used against her and starts locking drawers, hiding the notebook, writing in code. R3 — Calla stops writing in the notebook entirely; Renne wakes to a body she cannot read, doing things she cannot track, and the isolation she engineered is now hers too.
- dramatic_shape: Setup — Calla meets Dov and fills the notebook with him / Escalation — Renne begins her quiet campaign, day by day / Midpoint reversal — Renne reads Calla's entry describing how Dov said "I feel like I met you twice and only one of you likes me" / Cost spike — Renne impersonates Calla at his door and tells him Calla ended it / Climax — Calla wakes to find Dov gone and the notebook with only Renne's entry: "I'm sorry. I couldn't." / Aftermath — the sisters stop writing; the body wakes in silence on both sides.

## Protagonist arc
Renne starts as a sister trying to protect her half of a shared life from annexation by someone else's love story. She ends as the architect of a mutual silence she cannot escape — the single decision being the night she goes to Dov's door herself instead of writing another cold letter, because that is the one act that cannot be undone or explained away.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Calla writes twelve pages about Dov in one night; Renne wakes to ink-smeared hands and a notebook so full she has to start a new one to reply.
2. Renne meets Dov on her day — he is waiting outside with coffee for Calla — and she is polite but does not let him in; she writes nothing about it to Calla.
3. Renne returns Dov's gift (a small book of maps he left for Calla) with a note that says "she doesn't want this" — in Calla's handwriting, because they share the same hand.
4. Calla finds the returned gift on an even day when she goes to Dov's apartment; he won't open the door; she fills four pages that night demanding to know what Renne did.
5. Renne reads the four pages, goes to Dov's door that evening, and tells him face-to-face that Calla ended it and won't be explaining herself; she watches him believe it.
6. Calla's next entry is one line: "I know it was you." Then nothing. Renne's next entry is "I'm sorry. I couldn't." Then nothing. The notebook sits open on the nightstand between them.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Renne picks up Dov's gift with her hands, carries it to his building, and leaves it on his step with a note she writes in Calla's ink. She goes to his door and speaks to him — her voice, Calla's face. He closes the door. She walks back and puts the notebook on the nightstand and does not open it. In the morning, Calla opens it and reads nothing from Renne's side.

## Novelty claim
The sabotage mechanism is entirely mediated by a shared handwriting — there is no way for Calla to prove Renne forged anything, and no way for Dov to know, because the imposture is anatomically perfect; the villain and the victim are indistinguishable by any external instrument.
