# P09 year-debt-collector

LOGLINE: The guild's best debt-collector can hear the exact hour a person will die — until she is sent to collect from a child whose death-hour is the same as hers.

LENS: vocational

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Sera collects lifespan-loans because the hearing gives her control: she knows exactly how long anyone has, so she is never surprised, never cheated, never caught short.
- anchor: The guild's ledger room, where Sera's own loan terms are sealed in a vault she signed at nineteen and has never reopened.
- escalation: The child, Pol, rings at the same pitch as Sera — same hour, same note — and every standard collection method fails because the guild's contract requires Pol to surrender years he does not have.
- trade: To satisfy the contract, Sera must transfer the remaining years from her own account into Pol's — which means her own death-hour moves forward to fill the gap.
- irony: The woman who controlled every death-hour by listening to it loses her own hearing the moment she trades her years away; she finishes the collection deaf and on schedule.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The guild lends years off a person's natural lifespan in exchange for a fixed fee. The debt registers as a physical vibration in the borrower's sternum — a specific pitch that rises in frequency as the remaining loan-years drain. Sera hears these pitches as clearly as speech; she can identify a borrower across a crowded market by the note they carry. When a loan comes due, she attends the debtor in person, and the guild's collection instrument — a tuning rod she presses to the debtor's chest — transfers the outstanding years back into the guild's reserve. Years cannot be destroyed, only moved. A debtor with no remaining years cannot pay; the rod instead drains from whatever account is in contact with it. Sera has never touched the rod to her own chest.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability ↓ (the hearing that makes her effective transfers out with her years); moral-framework ↑ then crisis (she has never asked whether the contracts are just); position ↓ (she ends outside the guild's structure entirely)
- cost_ledger: Collection visit 1 → Sera hears Pol's pitch and knows; Collection visit 2 → she stalls, first time she has ever stalled; Rod-contact attempt → her own pitch rises, years draining; Final transfer → hearing goes, she completes the collection in silence
- antagonist_pressure: The guild dispatches an auditor to observe the collection; each delay Sera manufactures triggers an escalating review; by act three the auditor holds the rod and Sera must take it back physically to make the trade on her own terms
- dramatic_shape: Sera receives the assignment → she identifies Pol's pitch as her own → she delays and investigates → the auditor arrives → she reads her own sealed contract → she takes the rod and places it against her own chest → she finishes the work deaf

## Protagonist arc
Sera begins as the guild's instrument: efficient, unquestioning, defined by the hearing. She ends having used that instrument once on herself, on purpose, which is the only collection she has ever made that cost her something. The single decision: she takes the rod from the auditor and presses it to her own sternum while the auditor watches.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Sera locates Pol in a tannery district by his pitch alone — she hears him through a wall before she sees him; the note matches hers exactly, which has never happened.
2. She reads the contract: Pol borrowed five years to pay for surgery at age seven; he is now nine; the loan is due; he has lived two of the five years and cannot return the other three.
3. Sera visits the guild's vault under the pretext of a records check and finds her own sealed contract; she does not open it but she counts the years remaining by sound through the vault door.
4. The auditor, Voss, arrives and witnesses Sera's third postponement; he picks up the rod himself and moves toward Pol.
5. Sera steps between them, takes the rod, and places it against her own chest; the transfer takes forty seconds; she hears her pitch rise sharply and then goes silent; she hands the rod back to Voss and writes the collection closed.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Sera presses the tuning rod flat against her sternum, under her coat, so Voss cannot see the angle. The rod hums. The pitch she has carried since nineteen rises — she hears it climb one note, two, five — and then the sound cuts out entirely, the way a fire goes out, not fading but gone. She pulls the rod away, marks the ledger entry, and hands Voss the completed form. He stamps it without looking at her face.

## Novelty claim
The death-hearing is not a gift or a curse but a professional credential that the protagonist weaponizes against herself — the same instrument used to drain debtors drains her, and the loss of the power is the mechanical proof that the payment was real.
