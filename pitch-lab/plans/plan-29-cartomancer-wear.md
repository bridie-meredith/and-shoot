# P29 cartomancer-wear

LOGLINE: A cartomancer who reads the future in the wear-patterns of handled cards discovers her deck is converging on a single dealt hand — and that every reading she gives to prevent it brings that hand one card closer to the table.

LENS: structural

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: prevent the hand the deck is converging toward, which the wear-patterns tell her will end her practice and the community that depends on it
- anchor: the physical cards themselves — their worn edges, ink-fade, and crease-lines encoding probability through use
- escalation: she tries shuffling deeper, reading less, charging for fewer sessions; each intervention leaves new wear that the deck incorporates
- trade: she reads the deck one final time to identify the exact sequence that would prevent the hand — and that reading is the dealing of it
- irony: the convergence is not fate punishing her; it is causation: the deck wears toward the hand because she keeps reading it to find out how to avoid it

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
Cartomancy in this world is empirical. Cards wear differently depending on how often they are drawn, in what positions, and in sequence with which other cards. A skilled cartomancer reads the wear-gradient of a deck — the difference in thickness between the upper-right corner of the Tower and the lower-left corner of the Wheel, the groove along the spine of the Ace of Cups — as a probability-density map of near-future events. The more a deck has been used by a single reader, the more accurate the wear-pattern becomes, because the reader's habitual grip and shuffle encode the decisions they unconsciously weight. A hand dealt from a fully-converged deck is not a prophecy: it is a record of every choice the reader made while holding the cards. To prevent a hand means not making the decisions that wear the relevant cards — but identifying which decisions wear which cards requires reading the deck, which requires handling it, which wears the cards. The cartomancer works in a market stall; her clients pay for sessions; the deck is thirty years old and shaped entirely to her hands.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability ↑ then ↓ (her skill is what creates the trap; the more expert she becomes, the faster it closes); legibility ↑ then ↓ (she sees more clearly into the mechanism, which makes her less able to act outside it); moral-framework — flat (no moral dimension — the trap is purely structural)
- cost_ledger: each session to prevent the hand = measurable new wear on the convergence cards, quantified against the baseline she measured at the start; each shuffle = her hands leave oil on the card surface she can see under lamplight; the final reading = dealing the hand in front of a client who did not ask for it
- antagonist_pressure: the convergence is not an agent but it escalates with mechanical precision — each session she logs the wear against her baseline; the delta between current and convergent state decreases at an accelerating rate because prevention attempts are more frequent and more deliberate than normal use; by the midpoint she is handling the deck seventeen times per day trying to arrest what she is accelerating
- dramatic_shape: setup (she notices the convergence while cleaning the deck at closing time; measures it against her notes from three years prior) → escalation (she tries reduced sessions, altered shuffles, lending the deck to an apprentice — each produces new wear in the convergence pattern) → midpoint reversal (she maps the wear-rate against the session log and realizes prevention is the accelerant; stopping entirely is impossible because she has clients depending on her) → cost spike (she tries to destroy the deck; she cannot; the cards are thirty years of her hands and she cannot put them in the fire) → climax (a client sits down and she reads them, watching the convergent hand rise through the shuffle into her palm one card at a time) → aftermath (the hand is dealt; she documents what it says; she begins a new deck with a stranger's cards and starts counting wear from zero)

## Protagonist arc
Starts as an expert who believes mastery means seeing ahead of consequences. Ends as someone who learned that mastery in a feedback loop is its own trap — and that the answer was never to see further ahead, but to put the cards down. The flip: when she cannot burn the deck, she admits the cards are not a tool. They are a record. She deals the hand and reads it honestly.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. She holds the Wheel of Fortune under the lamp after her last client leaves and measures the wear-gradient with calipers; the numbers match the convergence pattern from six months ago, tighter.
2. She halves her sessions for two weeks. She measures the deck on a Thursday and finds the convergence has advanced faster than during full operation — the deliberate handling of prevention reads as intention.
3. She lends the deck to her apprentice for four days. The apprentice returns it and she measures it: the apprentice's grip is different; the convergence has scattered. She takes the deck back. The scatter resolves in three days of her hands on it.
4. She maps every session against every wear-measurement in her ledger across thirty years and draws a curve on paper with a pen. The curve reaches the convergent hand on a specific date approximately five weeks out. The date is also when her oldest client is due for a reading they have been scheduling for two years.
5. She holds the deck over the kitchen fire. She stands there for four minutes. She does not drop it.
6. The client sits. She shuffles. The cards come to her palm in the order the deck has been wearing toward for thirty years — she can feel them arriving like a sentence she has been building word by word. She lays them out and reads them. The client asks what it means. She tells them exactly and truthfully. She charges her usual rate and writes the session in the log.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
She measures the cards with calipers. She logs numbers in a paper ledger. The convergence is visible as physical wear — specific corners thinner than others, a crease that deepens session by session. When she deals the convergent hand, she feels the cards arriving in sequence in her fingers before she sees their faces. The trap is mechanical causation, not fate: her hands shaped the deck, the deck records her decisions, reading the record is the decision that shapes it further.

## Novelty claim
"Prophecy that self-fulfills through attempts to prevent it" is standard Oedipus structure; the specific engine here — that the prophecy medium is physically shaped by the act of consulting it, making consultation and fulfillment the same gesture — grounds the familiar trap in a concrete, learnable, almost scientific mechanism. The cartomancer is not undone by ignorance or hubris; she is undone by expertise in a closed system.
