# P07 half-printed-soul

LOGLINE: An orphan whose soul is half machine-printed and half wild-grown becomes the living treaty both factions will burn the city to control — and must decide whether to be a document or a person.

LENS: political

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Senna wants only to live unnoticed — she is seventeen, works a ferry crossing, and has spent her life hiding the seam in herself that others can feel when they touch her.
- anchor: The ferryman Ostric, who pulled her from the river as an infant and has never asked what she is — his safety is what she will not spend.
- escalation: Both factions locate Senna simultaneously; the machine-priesthood wants to complete the print (which will kill the wild-grown half); the wild magic's custodians want to excise the print (which will kill the other half); neither can force the other while Senna is alive and unclaimed.
- trade: Senna survives by becoming the treaty herself — her body as the signed document — but a treaty is not a person; it is property, administered by both sides.
- irony: The only way Senna prevents the war that will kill Ostric and everyone on the crossing is to surrender the personhood that makes her worth protecting.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The machine-priesthood prints souls onto brass wafers using a press that reads the Ledger of Forms — a catalog of approved configurations. A printed soul is stable, transferable, and legible to any priest at a glance. Wild magic grows souls the way fungus grows: slow, from conditions, not from a template — the resulting soul is unique, fragile, and unreadable by the press. The two are categorically incompatible; a body hosting both should be impossible. Senna's body hosts both because her mother interrupted the printing midway and fled into the wild districts, where the unfinished soul pulled in what it could from the ambient growth-magic. The seam is physical: along Senna's left side, from jaw to hip, her skin is slightly warmer than the right, and anyone who grips her arm on that side feels the print through their palm like a struck tuning fork; the right side feels like bark, or rain.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: Senna: legibility shifts from hidden to maximally legible (both sides can read her, neither completely); position moves from peripheral (ferryman's ward) to central (treaty-object); moral-framework stress between self-as-person and self-as-instrument. Ostric: social-tether to Senna becomes the lever both sides use.
- cost_ledger: Senna surfaces to protect the crossing — Ostric is arrested as leverage (immediate, on-page). She negotiates visibility as treaty — she must appear before both councils in public session, ending her anonymous life permanently. She accepts the treaty-body status — she submits to quarterly inspection by both factions, her body read like a text; the inspections are described in full.
- antagonist_pressure: Round 1 — one priest and one wild-custodian each arrive at the crossing independently, each offering safety on their terms; Ostric is caught between them. Round 2 — both factions mobilize city-ward simultaneously; the crossing district is caught between advancing columns; Senna has forty-eight hours before either side decides war is cheaper than negotiation. Round 3 — a faction hardliner on each side argues that a living treaty is too unstable — the cleaner solution is to complete or excise the soul, settling the question permanently, and begins moving on Ostric to force Senna's hand.
- dramatic_shape: Setup — Senna is found on the same morning by both factions; she runs and Ostric covers for her. Escalation — the city mobilizes; the crossing district is blockaded by both sides. Midpoint reversal — Senna realizes neither side can attack while she stands between them, and steps into the no-man's street between the columns. Cost spike — the hardliners move on Ostric; Senna formally accepts treaty status to stop them, signing the document with her own blood and a priest's stamp. Climax — the first quarterly inspection: Senna stands before a combined council and is read aloud like text, her two halves catalogued as clauses. Aftermath — Ostric visits her in the treaty chamber; she is behind glass; she is fine; she is not free.

## Protagonist arc
Senna begins as a person who has learned to make herself invisible because visibility is dangerous. She ends visible to everyone and owned by the visibility — the treaty-body is exactly as safe and exactly as imprisoned as she feared. The single decision: she steps into the street between the two advancing columns, alone, without negotiating terms first, because Ostric is behind her and she will not let him be the cost.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. A priest grips Senna's left arm at the ferry toll and goes still; she pulls free and poles the ferry to the far bank; a wild-custodian is waiting there, hand already extended.
2. Both factions post wardens at the crossing; Ostric is taken to the faction house as a material witness; Senna watches from the water.
3. Senna walks into the street between the advancing columns and stands there until both columns stop; neither side fires because damaging her body damages the legal claim.
4. Negotiators on both sides draft the treaty-body contract; Senna reads every clause; she signs because Ostric is still in the faction house and will stay there until she does.
5. The first inspection: Senna stands on a raised platform in the combined council chamber; a priest reads her left side aloud in the language of Forms; a custodian reads her right side in the language of growth; both readings are entered into separate ledgers.
6. Ostric visits; the glass partition is three inches thick; he presses his hand to it; she presses hers back; the left side is slightly warm through the glass.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
A priest grips Senna's left arm and feels the print vibrate against his palm; he can read its clauses through skin contact the way he reads a brass wafer on the press. A custodian grips her right arm and feels the wild-growth push back against his fingers like roots finding water. Neither reading is complete because neither side holds the whole body. Senna walks between two military columns and both columns stop because each side is looking at the half they need and cannot risk destroying it. She is a document with legs. She knows it. She walks anyway.

## Novelty claim
The treaty-as-body is a known metaphor (diplomacy as bodily sacrifice), but the physical literalism here — her two sides are genuinely different substances readable by different technologies, and the treaty is enacted through a formal inspection regime — makes the metaphor concrete mechanism rather than theme. The novelty is the quarterly inspection as the ongoing cost of the treaty: survival as scheduled reading.
