# P06 clocksmith-rewind

LOGLINE: A clocksmith compelled to rewind any death he witnesses has saved the same stranger forty times, and suspects the stranger is the one who taught him the compulsion in the first life he cannot remember.

LENS: penitential

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: The clocksmith cannot watch a death stand — his hands move before his mind consents, pulling the moment back.
- anchor: The stranger who dies over and over, each time in a different way, each time as if meeting the clocksmith for the first time.
- escalation: The clocksmith finds a notation in his own handwriting inside a clock the stranger carries — instructions for the compulsion, dated before he was born.
- trade: To learn the truth, he must let the stranger die once and watch what the stranger leaves behind; the compulsion tears at him like a wound held open.
- irony: The stranger he has saved forty times taught him the gift specifically so he would never be able to stop — the compulsion is a leash, not a blessing.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
When the clocksmith witnesses a death, his hands move to the nearest mechanism — a watch, a hinge, a gear, a door-latch — and turn it backward. Time in a radius around the dying person stutters and rewinds, typically fifteen to ninety seconds, enough to pull the death back. The mechanism must be physical and within reach; the further the rewind, the longer the mechanism takes to cool, leaving his palms blistered and the metal warped. He cannot rewind deaths he does not personally witness, and he cannot rewind the same moment twice in a row — each save requires a different mechanism, or time to pass. The compulsion is involuntary: he blacks out if he fights it past a threshold of three heartbeats.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability (static — he cannot expand or contract the gift); moral-framework (degrading — each save he performs without consent erodes his sense of agency); legibility (rising toward self — he is learning what he is)
- cost_ledger: each rewind blisters his palms deeper; after forty saves, two fingers have fused at the joint and he cannot hold fine tools; his trade is dying at the same rate as his certainty grows
- antagonist_pressure: the stranger's deaths escalate in proximity — early saves are accidents across a plaza, later ones are deaths in the clocksmith's own workshop; by the final act the stranger is dying at the clocksmith's hand, forcing him to rewind himself
- dramatic_shape: stranger dies in the plaza (setup) → clocksmith finds his own handwriting in the stranger's clock (escalation) → clocksmith lets the stranger die and reads the message left (midpoint reversal) → the message extends the compulsion, not explains it — his hands begin rewinding without a mechanism (cost spike) → clocksmith destroys every mechanism in his shop to force a confrontation (climax) → the stranger survives, the compulsion survives, and the clocksmith's hands are finally still — because they no longer work (aftermath)

## Protagonist arc
Begins: a craftsman who believes the compulsion is a curse he endures. Ends: a man who chose, once, not to save someone — and learned that the choice cost more than the compulsion ever did. The flip: the moment he holds his blistered hands still and watches.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. The stranger is killed by a runaway cart in the market square; the clocksmith's hands find a cartwheel and spin it back; the stranger walks away without looking at him.
2. The clocksmith catalogs the saves in a journal — forty entries, forty mechanisms, forty strangers-who-don't-remember; he notices the stranger's face in all forty.
3. He opens the clock the stranger carries to repair it and finds the notation inside the case, in his own hand, in ink that has not aged.
4. He lets the stranger die in the doorway of his shop, fighting the compulsion until he blacks out; he comes to with his palms flat on the floor, and the stranger's body is still there.
5. The notation in the dead man's hand reads: "You will not remember teaching me either. Turn it back." The clocksmith's fused fingers begin to move.
6. He smashes every clock, every gear, every latch in the shop; his hands find nothing; the stranger's chest rises once — the compulsion has learned to work without him.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
A man's hands move to the nearest turning thing and spin it the wrong direction. Clocks, wheels, hinges — whatever is in reach. The stranger's chest starts beating again. The clocksmith's palms blister and two fingers fuse at the knuckle over forty iterations. When he finally holds his hands down by force, the stranger's body stays cold on his floor until the notation makes his fingers move on their own.

## Novelty claim
The compulsion-as-leash structure is familiar; the recursive loop (he taught the stranger who taught him in a life he cannot access) is the novel wrench — a closed causal loop where the protagonist is both origin and prisoner, and the only exit is to stop being a craftsman entirely.
