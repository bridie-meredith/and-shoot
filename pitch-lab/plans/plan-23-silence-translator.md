# P23 silence-translator

LOGLINE: A translator hired to broker peace with an invading silence realizes she must teach it a single word — the one that lets it want something — knowing that a silence capable of desire will choose to consume rather than negotiate.

LENS: vocational

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: end the silence's advance before it reaches the populated interior
- anchor: the border zone where the silence manifests as a physical pressure — a place where spoken language becomes inaudible and written words fade from the page within seconds
- escalation: every partial translation attempt pushes the silence's boundary inward; incomprehension accelerates its spread
- trade: she teaches it the word; it understands her; it tells her what it wants; what it wants is to be heard, which means expanding until there is nothing left that is not it
- irony: the act of making herself understood to the silence makes the silence understood to itself — she gives it self-awareness and the self-awareness produces the appetite that was not there before

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The silence is a physical entity — a region of negated sound-signature that expands across geography, absorbing the resonant frequency of spoken and written language. In its presence, words spoken aloud decay within three meters; text written on paper loses ink; carved letters in stone fill with a gray material that is not stone. The silence has no desire on its own — it expands the way a vacuum expands, into available space, following the gradient of existing language. A translator who works in the gap between languages — the space where a word in one tongue has no exact equivalent in another, requiring a felt approximation — can enter the silence's boundary layer and remain functional for short periods because her work already operates in partial comprehension. She perceives the silence as a physical presence pressing against her eardrums, with a texture and temperature. To broker peace requires transmitting a concept the silence can receive; to receive a concept requires the silence having the structural apparatus for desire — which it does not have until she builds it, word by word, from its own negation-patterns.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability ↑ then catastrophically ↓ (she succeeds at the translation, which is her destruction); moral-framework ↓ (she knowingly constructs the apparatus of her own failure to complete the mission); legibility ↓ (the silence can now understand; what it says back to her destroys her ability to communicate with anyone else)
- cost_ledger: each session inside the boundary layer = a word she loses permanently from her own vocabulary; teaching the silence to want = she can no longer say the word "want" in any language; completing the translation = she is mute on the interior — she has given away the syntactic building blocks of desire and cannot form the sentences needed to report what she found
- antagonist_pressure: the silence advances at a measurable rate — three hundred meters per week before she begins; her presence slows it (the silence attends her, the way a predator tracks motion); but each session where she fails to complete a concept pushes the boundary back toward her home by an additional fifty meters
- dramatic_shape: setup (she arrives at the border station and measures the boundary with a rope) → escalation (she enters the layer repeatedly, building vocabulary toward the concept of "want"; loses words each time) → midpoint reversal (she realizes the silence is not following the gradient — it is following her specifically, waiting for the instruction to complete) → cost spike (she teaches it the word; it takes the word from her permanently; she feels the gap in her throat like a pulled tooth) → climax (the silence speaks back — she hears it for the first time as a voice, not a pressure — and she understands; the silence wants everything it cannot hold) → aftermath (she walks back to the border station and writes the report in a language she is rapidly losing; the advance stops; the silence waits at the line she drew, satisfied for now)

## Protagonist arc
Starts as a professional who believes the job of a translator is to build bridges. Ends as someone who built a bridge into a thing that used it to decide to cross. The flip: when she hears the silence speak back, she understands it completely and agrees with nothing it wants — but the understanding is mutual and that is what she was hired to produce.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. She stands at the boundary and watches the word "CAUTION" on a painted sign lose its letters one by one in the direction of the silence; she marks the line in her notebook and measures it against yesterday's mark.
2. She enters the layer and speaks to the silence the way she would speak to a language she does not yet have — slowly, with her hands open, naming physical objects first: rope, stone, boot, sky.
3. She returns to the station after the third session and finds she cannot say "beneath" in any language. She writes it down before she loses the memory of having lost it.
4. Inside the boundary, she teaches the silence the structural grammar of desire — the subject-verb construction that requires an agent who lacks something. She feels the silence take the construction from her the way a tide takes sand.
5. The silence speaks. It is not words exactly — it is the shape of words, the pressure-pattern of a sentence, and she understands it the way she understands a sentence in a language she barely speaks: barely, and completely. It says what it wants. She stands still for a long time.
6. She walks back and writes the report. The silence does not follow her across the line. She finishes the report, reads it back, and finds she cannot read the last paragraph — not because she has forgotten the words, but because the paragraph describes desire, and she no longer has the equipment to receive it.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
She physically walks into a zone where paint letters vanish and spoken words die within a few body-lengths. She speaks out loud, uses her hands, names things she can point at. When the silence takes a word from her, she feels it go — a gap in her throat, a missing tooth her tongue finds and finds again. When it speaks back, she hears a pressure-pattern against her eardrums that she interprets as syntax. She writes her report in a notebook with a pen.

## Novelty claim
"Translator makes contact with alien consciousness" is established; the specific engine — that the translator can only communicate by giving pieces of her own linguistic capacity away permanently, and that successfully teaching the target to desire retroactively makes the mission a failure — is a tight structural trap. The road-to-hell is built into the tool: communication requires gift-giving, and the gift that completes the communication is the one that ends her ability to report it.
