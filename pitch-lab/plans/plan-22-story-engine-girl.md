# P22 story-engine-girl

LOGLINE: A girl raised inside a machine that physically writes the kingdom's official history rewrites a door into its output and walks through it — only to find that everyone outside is a character someone else has already drafted.

LENS: escape

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Ink wants out of the story-engine before it writes her death scene, which she has watched it compose for the last six months.
- anchor: She learns the engine works by consuming what happens and printing it as continuous vellum; anything printed exists as permanent record and shapes what comes next.
- escalation: Ink rewrites her own scene on the vellum before the engine can finalize it — inserting a door — and steps through into the city beyond the archive walls.
- trade: She agrees to let a resistance cell use her engine-knowledge to rewrite key figures in the official history, inserting false biographical details that neutralize their power.
- irony: Every person she meets outside is running from a story that was written for them; she is now the person who can rewrite them, which makes her the new engine.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The story-engine is a room-sized mechanism of gears, type-blocks, and ink-rollers that produces the kingdom's Official Register — a continuous feed of vellum printed in real time, narrating official history as it is determined by the Register's editors. The vellum is binding: once an event is printed, the kingdom's legal system treats it as having happened. Unprinted events have no legal standing. The engine does not fabricate events; it selects among possible framings and suppresses alternatives. Ink, raised in the archive as a compositor's apprentice, learned to feed replacement type-blocks into the mechanism mid-run — a one-second window between the setting of type and the roller's pass. She inserts a door into her own scene by physically swapping a type-block that reads "remained at her station" with one she carved herself reading "passed through the east door." The east door does not exist until the vellum says it does. Then it does.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: legibility (Ink's — she becomes visible to the kingdom's reality layer the moment she is printed as "escaped"); position (invisible apprentice → fugitive → re-inscriber); capability (grows as she learns to forge type-blocks for others)
- cost_ledger: Carving the door block earns her exit — cost: she is now printed as a person who escaped the archive, which means the engine's next editor can write what happens to her. Each type-block she swaps for the resistance neutralizes one official — cost: she must return physically to the archive room, risking re-capture, and the swaps leave microscopic misalignments a skilled compositor can read. The final rewrite that could protect her permanently — cost: it requires printing herself as dead, and she does not know if the vellum makes that real too.
- antagonist_pressure: R1 — the Head Editor reviews the vellum and flags the misalignment in her escape scene; an investigation begins. R2 — the Editor sends compositors into the city with her physical description and a mandate to "correct the record" by returning her to the archive. R3 — the Editor prints a new scene in which Ink confesses and returns voluntarily; the kingdom's courts begin treating her as having confessed.
- dramatic_shape: Setup — Ink watches her death scene composited in type six months ahead of its scheduled print-run / Escalation — she carves the replacement block over four nights and inserts it on a Tuesday, then walks through a door that solidifies around her hand as she pushes / Midpoint reversal — she realizes the resistance cell she joined is itself a story the Editor is allowing to run, because it produces useful enemies / Cost spike — the Editor prints her confession; she is legally treated as having surrendered / Climax — Ink breaks back into the archive and swaps the confession block mid-run, mid-roller, cutting her arm on the mechanism; the vellum prints something neither she nor the Editor wrote / Aftermath — she is free and dangerous; she cannot stop rewriting things; she does not know who is left that the engine hasn't already drafted.

## Protagonist arc
Starts as a girl who knows how the machine works and believes knowing is enough to escape it. Ends as a girl who has become what she escaped from — the single decision being whether to print herself dead to close the loop permanently, and her refusal to do it.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Ink finds her name in the type-case for a scene dated six months forward; she reads the scene and it ends with the phrase "and was not seen again," which the engine uses for deaths it is not authorized to print directly.
2. She carves a type-block over four nights, shaving the letter-face down and re-etching "passed through the east door" in the archive's regulation font; she tests it against the type-case grid and it fits.
3. She swaps the block in the three-second window between type-set and roller-pass; she hears the mechanism hesitate, then roll; she walks to the east wall and pushes and a door opens in stone that was solid that morning.
4. The resistance cell finds her within a day — they have been watching the vellum for print anomalies and her exit was visible as a six-word insertion; they tell her they need her hands.
5. The Editor prints her confession; Ink learns about it when a shopkeeper refuses to sell to her because she is legally "in custody pending hearing."
6. Ink enters the archive at night, reaches the mechanism mid-run, swaps the confession block for a blank, cuts her forearm on the roller-housing; the vellum prints a blank gap in the official record — a legal wound no one knows how to interpret.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Ink holds the type-block between two fingers, waits for the type-setter's bell, and slides the block into the chase in the gap between the F and the period. The roller comes down. The vellum feeds through. She walks to the east wall and pushes with her shoulder; the stone gives, hinged now on something the Register says is a door. Outside, the city air hits her face and she sees the street printed on the vellum she walked past every day, exactly as described — cobbles, gutter, baker's sign — real because it was written, written because it was real.

## Novelty claim
The escape mechanism and the prison mechanism are the same physical object — she does not break out, she edits out, and the edit is permanent and legal; the road-to-hell is that editorial power over others' stories is structurally identical to the power she fled.
