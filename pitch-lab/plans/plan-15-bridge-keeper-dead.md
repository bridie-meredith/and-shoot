# P15 bridge-keeper-dead

LOGLINE: A bridge-keeper between the living city above and the archived dead below must ration who crosses when both halves of the world begin sinking toward each other.

LENS: structural

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Nieve keeps the bridge because the crossing is the only thing that cannot be bureaucratized: she is the rule, and the rule is hers to hold.
- anchor: The bridge itself — a three-hundred-step descent carved into the cliff face between the warm city of Vel and the cold archive-city of the recorded dead, where the dead are preserved in stone and can still speak if addressed correctly.
- escalation: The two cities begin rising and falling toward each other by measurable inches each week; at some convergence point the bridge becomes redundant and both populations occupy the same space.
- trade: Nieve can slow the convergence by restricting crossings — each crossing she permits draws the two cities fractionally closer; rationing saves time but traps the living above with their grief and the dead below with their silence.
- irony: She keeps the bridge open to keep the worlds apart; the bridge is the mechanism of collapse.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The archive-city below is not an underworld — it is an engineered preservation layer, cold and dry, where the dead are pressed into stone tablets within hours of death and can answer spoken questions for approximately forty years before the answers thin and stop. The bridge is the only physical connection; weight on the bridge steps compresses the geological stratum between the two cities. Each crossing adds a measurable fraction of pressure. The convergence is geological — slow, irreversible, and accelerated by crossings. Nieve has a mechanical gauge on the bridge's keystone that measures the gap. The living cross to speak to their dead; the dead have no volition but their tablets can call out to specific living persons by name when those persons are within range — which increases as the gap narrows.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: position ↓ (her authority erodes as both cities demand crossings); moral-framework → crisis (she cannot justify the rationing criteria she has been using); legibility ↓ (the tablets' calls grow louder as the gap narrows — she can no longer distinguish who is being called)
- cost_ledger: First crossing permitted → gauge drops 0.3mm / Mass crossing event → gap halves in a week / Nieve's own dead (her mother's tablet) begins calling → she has not crossed once herself / Final permit decision → she grants or denies the one crossing that will bring the floor to the ceiling
- antagonist_pressure: The city council above passes a referendum granting unlimited crossing rights; each round Nieve enforces her rationing, a crowd grows at the bridge-head; by act three the crowd is large enough that a single surge would compress the stratum faster than any controlled crossing
- dramatic_shape: Nieve measures the gap and finds it has closed overnight → she institutes rationing → the dead begin calling the living by name through the stone → the council referendum passes → her mother's tablet calls her name → she must choose to cross or close the bridge permanently

## Protagonist arc
Nieve begins as pure function: the rule that holds the worlds apart. She ends having crossed the bridge herself for the first time, to close her mother's tablet before the voice drives the crowd through the gate. The single decision: she descends the three hundred steps alone and presses her palm to the tablet to silence it, knowing the weight of her crossing is in the gauge.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Nieve checks the keystone gauge at dawn and finds the gap has closed by two centimeters in the night — more than the previous month combined; she institutes a twenty-crossing-per-day cap and posts it on the gate.
2. A man named Curl has crossed every day for six years to speak to his daughter's tablet; he is the first one turned away; he sits at the gate and does not move.
3. The tablets below begin calling names — audible at the bridge-foot as a low harmonic; three living people report hearing their names in their sleep, a block from the bridge entrance.
4. The council referendum passes; Nieve locks the gate; Curl and forty others push against it; the gauge drops 0.8mm from the weight of bodies on the threshold.
5. Nieve hears her own name from below — her mother's tablet, forty-one years old, nearly out of answers. She unlocks the gate, sends the crowd back, and descends alone. She presses her palm to the stone until the voice stops. The gauge reads the crossing. She climbs back up and locks the gate behind her.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Nieve puts her hand flat on the keystone gauge — a brass dial set into the mortar — and reads the needle. It has moved left. She writes the number in her ledger, walks to the gate, slides the iron bolt, and turns the first person away by name. Below her, pressed into stone, forty thousand dead wait in columns. Between them and the living: three hundred steps, a brass needle, and her.

## Novelty claim
The crossing mechanism is the antagonist — not a villain but a physical law that the keeper's own function accelerates, making the protagonist's professional competence the direct engine of the catastrophe she is preventing.
