# P24 dusk-ambassador

LOGLINE: A child born on the seam between a kingdom that runs on sunlight and one that runs on its absence can survive in neither, and is courted by both as the only ambassador who can stand in the dusk where the war is fought.

LENS: political

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: The child stands in the dusk because it is the only place that does not kill her — not because she wants to negotiate anything.
- anchor: A soldier from the light-kingdom who is slowly dying in the dusk-zone while waiting for the terms to come through; the child has been keeping him alive by standing between him and the dark.
- escalation: Both courts discover that her presence in the dusk is not neutral — her body absorbs whichever side she stands closer to, making her a weapon of alignment with every step she takes toward one border.
- trade: She can end the war by absorbing enough of one kingdom's nature to become leverage the other cannot ignore — but absorption is permanent; each step toward one side makes the other side lethal to her.
- irony: She accepts the ambassadorship to preserve her freedom of movement, and it is the act of negotiating that fixes her in place — the more she speaks for both, the more she becomes neither, and the dusk narrows around her.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The light-kingdom runs on solar tithe: every citizen carries a measured allotment of captured sunlight that fuels their body, their tools, and their agriculture. Too much dark kills them in hours — their cells cannot metabolize without the light. The dark-kingdom runs on accumulated absence: their bodies are tuned to dim, and direct sunlight burns them from the skin inward. The dusk-zone is the strip of land between them, perpetually twilight, where neither population can survive more than a day. The child born there metabolizes both spectrums in balance; she cannot absorb more of one without the balance tipping. When she stands within three feet of a light-citizen for more than an hour, she brightens — visibly, measurably — and the same period near a dark-citizen dims her. Prolonged tipping toward either side makes the opposite lethal.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: legibility (rising — both courts are actively studying her, reading her body as a barometer); position (deteriorating — the more politically central she becomes, the smaller her physical territory grows); moral-framework (fracturing — she begins with no allegiance and is pressured into performing both simultaneously)
- cost_ledger: each formal negotiation session requires her to sit equidistant from both delegations — the physical cost is exact positioning that exhausts her by the hour; each treaty draft she carries into the dusk zone, her range contracts ten meters; the soldier she is keeping alive is absorbing her light-balance by proximity
- antagonist_pressure: both courts send advisors who argue over her body's position in the negotiating chamber; by midpoint they are measuring the distance from her seat to each delegation to the inch; by the final act one court moves their chair three feet closer and her skin begins to blister on the far side
- dramatic_shape: she is found by both courts' scouts on the same afternoon in the dusk-zone (setup) → she negotiates her own terms of engagement and agrees to carry messages (escalation) → the courts discover her body-alignment mechanism and begin using it as a treaty instrument (midpoint reversal) → one court deliberately over-brightens her to make the dark-side treaty unacceptable (cost spike) → she walks to the center of the dusk-zone and refuses to move toward either border (climax) → both courts must send representatives into the dusk to continue talks — where they cannot survive without her standing between them and whichever side is lethal (aftermath)

## Protagonist arc
Begins: a child with nowhere to live, valuable only as a body that can stand where no one else can. Ends: a child who has turned her liability into permanent leverage — not by becoming an ambassador but by refusing to move, forcing the war to come to her territory. The flip: the moment she stops walking toward either court and plants her feet.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Two scouts from opposite kingdoms find her at the same stream in the dusk-zone; they each try to pull her toward their side; she sits down and will not stand until they both let go.
2. She carries the first formal message from the light-court to the dark-court, standing in the dusk while they read it through the window — she notices she has drifted two feet toward the light.
3. In the negotiating chamber she watches the light-court move their chair forward by a ruler's length; she watches her left hand brighten; she moves her chair back; they move theirs forward again.
4. The soldier at the dusk-border is too dim to stand; she has been standing beside him daily; she realizes he is consuming her balance by existing near her and she cannot stop without leaving him to die.
5. One court over-brightens her during a session; she walks out of the chamber into the dusk and stands there blistering on her dark side while both delegations argue in the doorway.
6. She walks to the center of the dusk-zone and announces she will not cross either border again; both courts send junior representatives into the strip; she stands between them and keeps them both alive by being exactly where she is.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
A girl stands in a strip of permanent twilight between two armies. When she stands too long near the bright soldiers her skin brightens and the dark soldiers cannot look at her. When she stands near the dark soldiers she dims and the bright soldiers' skin begins to mottle. She plants her feet in the middle of the strip and both sets of soldiers stand a careful distance from her and shout their terms across the gap she occupies, because the alternative is dying in the dusk without her there to absorb whichever side they cannot survive.

## Novelty claim
Child-as-mediator and dual-nature-body are familiar; the novelty is that her negotiating power is purely positional and purely physical — she has no diplomatic training, no speech, no strategy except where she stands. The road-to-hell turn is that ambassadorship fixes her position permanently: she wins the war by becoming a landmark.
