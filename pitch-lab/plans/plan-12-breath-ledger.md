# P12 breath-ledger

LOGLINE: A man who cannot stop counting marks every breath the city takes, and when his tally tells him exactly how many the coming famine will kill, he finds the marks themselves have been selecting who dies.

LENS: structural

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: He marks because silence is unbearable — the counting is the only thing that holds the noise of the city to a shape he can bear.
- anchor: A woman in the tenement below him who breathes with a distinctive catch, audible through the floor, whom he has marked for eleven years.
- escalation: He discovers that people whose breath-marks he stops recording — through error, illness, distraction — stop appearing in the city within a week.
- trade: To save the famine's victims, he must mark them perpetually and personally, which means he can never sleep, never stop, never let his hand falter.
- irony: He has already made the selections without knowing it — the gaps in the ledger across eleven years map perfectly onto everyone who has already disappeared.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The man hears breath: not metaphorically, not diagnostically, but as discrete percussive events he cannot not register. In a room of twenty people he tracks twenty simultaneous counts with no effort and no choice. When he marks a breath in his ledger — any medium, any surface, a scratch on wood counts — that person's presence in the city is anchored. The anchor is not magical protection; it is ontological persistence. An unmarked person does not die at the ledger's hand — they simply become unselected by the city's continuation, the way a stone falls out of a wall and no one remembers it was there. The ledger does not choose whom to erase; it reflects what he has chosen to record.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: moral-framework (collapsing — he is retroactively guilty of every gap); legibility (inverting — he thought he was observing the city, learns he has been authoring it); social-tether (degrading — the woman below is the last mark he trusts himself to keep)
- cost_ledger: each night he extends coverage, he loses a night's sleep and a hand's steadiness; by midpoint his script is unreadable by anyone but himself; by climax he is marking in blood because ink runs out faster than he can source it
- antagonist_pressure: the famine's administrators discover his ledger and want the selection list — first they ask, then they offer money, then they station a man outside his door; by the final act they break in and begin erasing marks themselves, testing whether deletion works on command
- dramatic_shape: he notices a gap in a page that corresponds to a disappeared neighbor (setup) → he tests the gap deliberately by stopping a stranger's mark — the stranger vanishes in four days (escalation) → he realizes the full eleven-year catalog is a retrospective kill list (midpoint reversal) → the administrators erase thirty marks in one night and thirty people vanish from the census by morning (cost spike) → he burns the ledger and begins marking every surface in the city by hand (climax) → the city holds, barely; he marks until his hands stop working; the woman below him breathes on (aftermath)

## Protagonist arc
Begins: a man who counts because he cannot stop, who believes he is harmless and peripheral. Ends: a man who has burned the thing that defined him in order to become too large to weaponize — and who marks now with no ledger, no system, no record, only the motion of his hand on every wall he passes. The flip: the moment he stops trying to contain the count and instead floods it outward.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. He sits at his window and marks; the woman's breath-catch comes through the floor on schedule; he marks it without looking down.
2. He finds a page with a five-day gap from three years ago and goes to the address — the building is there but no one who lived there is; neighbors cannot recall them.
3. He stops marking a beggar at the east gate for one week as a test; on the eighth day he walks to the gate and the beggar's corner is occupied by someone who has been there, everyone agrees, for years.
4. The famine administrator sits across from him, polite, and asks to see the pages for the granary district; the man closes the ledger on the table between them and does not open it.
5. He wakes to find two administrators at the desk, erasing; he watches the marks disappear and counts the screams starting in the street below, one by one.
6. He burns the ledger in the courtyard and walks to the nearest wall and begins scratching — every breath he can hear, every surface he can reach, no page that can be stolen or erased.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
A man sits at a window and scratches marks onto paper, one per breath he hears below. He scratches thirty thousand marks a night. When he stops scratching a particular person's count, that person walks out of the city's memory like a stone falling from a wall — their neighbors cannot find the shape they left. The administrators sit at his desk and rub out marks with a cloth while people stop appearing in the street outside. He scratches the wall instead, moving through the city block by block, his fingers bleeding onto brick.

## Novelty claim
The ledger-as-ontological-anchor is the familiar half; the novelty is that it does not protect or kill — it selects for persistence, making every gap retroactively an act of erasure the protagonist committed without knowing. The road-to-hell is not that he becomes a weapon but that he already was one, for eleven years, in the harmless act of keeping himself sane.
