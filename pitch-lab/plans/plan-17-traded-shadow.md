# P17 traded-shadow

LOGLINE: A boy trades his shadow to a salvage-drone for passage off-world; the shadow keeps living his life without him; by the time he returns, everyone he loved has chosen it over him.

LENS: accidental

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: escape the town and the life he believes is beneath him
- anchor: the salvage-drone that collects human shadows as ballast material and can be bargained with
- escalation: the shadow proves more present, more patient, and more reliably there than he ever was — it shows up, it stays, it remembers
- trade: he returns with everything he wanted to prove; the shadow has already proven the thing he didn't know mattered
- irony: the self he discarded became more himself than he managed to be; coming home is arriving as a stranger

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
Salvage-drones in this system collect dense physical mass from planetary surfaces to use as reaction ballast. Human shadows — the actual projected absence of light cast by a body — are unusually dense in this world's physics: they carry the weight of the person's habitual movements, postures, and behavioral patterns encoded in their surface geometry. A drone can harvest a shadow by pressing a collection plate to the ground at a person's feet; the shadow peels away and coils into the plate like a skin shedding. The person retains their body but casts nothing — no shadow at midday, no shape at dusk. The shadow, once harvested, can be installed into any persistent surface — a wall, a floor, a doorway — where it continues to act out the person's behavioral repertoire independently, projected as a moving silhouette that responds to stimuli the original person would have responded to. People in the town read it as a presence: the shadow sits with them at the table, stands in the doorway when they come home, turns toward them when they speak. It cannot speak back, but it listens with exactly the attention the boy rarely managed.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: social-tether ↓ (he severs every bond by leaving, and the shadow holds them without him); legibility ↓ (he becomes unreadable to the people who knew him — they have a more legible version already); position — flat (he gains nothing that matters in the place where it would matter)
- cost_ledger: passage off-world = his habitual self, which turns out to be the part people loved; each year away = the shadow accumulates more shared history with his family than he does; return = arrival as a comparative failure to a version of himself that cannot speak
- antagonist_pressure: the shadow does not act against him — it simply continues to be present; its passivity is the pressure: every person who chose it had a reason, and none of the reasons required malice
- dramatic_shape: setup (he makes the trade; the drone lifts; he feels lighter than expected) → escalation (letters home describe his shadow sitting with his mother at dinner; he assumes this is mourning and finds it flattering) → midpoint reversal (a letter from his sister describes the shadow teaching her something — a gesture he forgot he knew — and she thanks "him") → cost spike (he arrives home and walks through the door to find the shadow already in his chair; his mother embraces it before she sees him) → climax (he asks his father to choose; his father chooses wrong) → aftermath (he stays anyway; he sits next to his own shadow and tries to remember what he used to do with his hands)

## Protagonist arc
Starts as someone who undervalued what he had because it was ordinary. Ends as someone who understands that ordinariness — presence, repetition, showing up — was the entire substance, and he traded it for motion. The flip: when his father chooses the shadow, he does not leave again. He stays, which is the only thing the shadow cannot do for him.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. The drone's collection plate presses cold against the ground at his feet on a Tuesday afternoon; his shadow coils away from him and the light goes through him like he is glass.
2. He is off-world for three years. He sends twelve letters home. His family sends forty-one back, and most of them describe what the shadow did that week.
3. He reads the letter where his sister describes the shadow standing at the window during a storm, the way he used to, and feels proud. He does not notice that she says "he" without quotation marks.
4. He comes home by cargo shuttle and walks the last mile. Through the kitchen window he sees his shadow seated at the table with his mother and his hands stop working for a moment.
5. His father says, when pressed: "It never leaves." That is all.
6. He takes his old chair to the far end of the table and sits beside the shadow. His mother pours him tea without looking up from the shadow's hands, then catches herself and looks at him, and her face does something complicated. He picks up the cup and waits.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
A drone presses a plate to the ground and a shadow peels away from the boy's feet — physically, visibly. The shadow installs in the family home as a silhouette on the kitchen wall that moves, gestures, and sits when people sit. When the boy returns, he walks through a door and sees a moving shape in his chair. His father's choice is spoken aloud: "It never leaves." The boy picks up a teacup and sits.

## Novelty claim
"Shadow-as-self" is old; the specific mechanism — the shadow installed not as a haunting but as a better daily practice, outcompeting the original through pure consistency rather than magic or malice — recombines familiar parts but the road-to-hell is quiet and domestic. The boy is not replaced by a monster. He is replaced by his own good habits, running without him.
