# P03 healing-wasps

LOGLINE: A field-surgeon who amputated a plague-arm one hour too late now carries a hive of healing wasps in his chest; every life he saves costs a villager the memory that proves the disease was ever real.

LENS: penitential

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Davan graft the hive into his sternum to cure what his blade could not; he wants to undo the death he caused by inches.
- anchor: The village of Solt, where the original plague still moves through the water table and where Davan's first patient — the man he failed — has a surviving son.
- escalation: The hive heals faster and cleaner than any salve, but each cure pulls a specific memory of illness from someone nearby; the village loses the proof of what sickened it.
- trade: To stop a second outbreak, Davan must either cure so many people that the village forgets the plague entirely and repeats every fatal mistake — or withhold the wasps and watch people die cleanly remembering.
- irony: The surgeon who wanted to erase his failure succeeds: the village forgets the death he caused, and forgets it was ever his fault.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The wasps nest in a cavity behind Davan's sternum, kept warm by his body and fed on plague-antibodies his blood produces. When he presses his palm to a patient's wound and opens the cavity latch under his collarbone, three to five wasps exit through a brass port, sting the infection site, and return. The patient heals within hours — fever breaks, necrosis reverses, sepsis clears. The cost is not Davan's to pay: within half a day, one person within a two-mile radius loses the clearest memory they hold of being sick or watching someone be sick. The loss is precise and permanent. The victim does not feel it go. The hive grows stronger with each cure; by act three, a single treatment can blank a whole household's memory of a season.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: moral-framework ↓ (each cure re-argues the same wrong trade); legibility ↓ (village loses its record of what happened); social-tether ↑ then ↓ (Davan bonds with the son, then erases the son's memory of his father's death)
- cost_ledger: Cure 1 → innkeeper forgets her husband's fever week / Cure 5 → the failed patient's son forgets his father was ever ill / Final cure → the whole village forgets the original amputation patient existed
- antagonist_pressure: The plague itself re-emerges in the water; each round of cures produces a village less able to recognize symptoms, so the outbreak accelerates exactly as the hive grows more potent
- dramatic_shape: Davan arrives healing freely → cures compound, forgetting spreads → the son can no longer name what killed his father → Davan realizes the hive will eventually erase the death entirely → he must choose to let the next patient die or erase the last witness → he closes the latch and the patient dies remembering

## Protagonist arc
Davan begins convinced that healing is unconditional good. He ends holding a man's hand while the man dies of a curable infection, because Davan has finally understood that the cure does not undo — it only buries. The single decision: he closes the chest-port while his patient is still conscious and still afraid.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Davan rides into Solt and heals a child with three wasp-stings; the child's mother wakes the next morning unable to describe the symptoms she spent two weeks cataloguing.
2. The failed patient's son, Rem, recognizes Davan and demands to know if the wasps could have saved his father; Davan lies and says no.
3. Davan discovers the plague is back in the south well; he cures six people in one night and the village elder loses the entire memory of last year's quarantine.
4. Rem forgets his father was ill; he asks Davan to describe the death; Davan tells him the truth and Rem does not believe it — there is no grief left to anchor the account.
5. A new patient presents with early-stage plague — curable, fatal if untreated; Davan opens his coat, touches the port, and closes it again. He sits with the patient and does not open it.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Davan unlatches a brass port beneath his left collarbone; wasps crawl out across his fingers and onto the patient's skin. Somewhere in the village, a person pauses mid-sentence and loses the thread — not confusion, just a gap where a particular winter used to sit. The port latches again; the wasps return; Davan wipes his hand on his coat and counts what he has taken.

## Novelty claim
The memory-cost is stripped from the healer and levied on bystanders in a targeted, medically specific way — the loss mirrors the cure's subject matter — creating a collective-amnesia antagonist that the protagonist actively manufactures with every moral act.
