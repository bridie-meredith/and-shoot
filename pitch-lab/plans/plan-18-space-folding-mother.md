# P18 space-folding-mother

LOGLINE: A mother who can fold space carries her son across a galaxy one step at a time, but each fold ages her a decade and him one year less, so the closer they get the more he grows into a man raising a woman he barely recognizes.

LENS: relational

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Her son is stranded in a quarantine system after a collapse; she is the only person who can reach him without a ship, and she is already old enough that ten more years each step is a finite count.
- anchor: The son at twelve, the last image she has of him — the distance between that image and what she finds grows with every fold.
- escalation: By the journey's midpoint she is seventy and he is eight years younger than she needs him to be to recognize her authority; he starts making decisions she would have made for him.
- trade: She can fold them home faster by taking larger jumps — each larger jump costs twenty years instead of ten, closing the distance but opening the gap between them faster.
- irony: She crosses the galaxy to bring him home and arrives to find that "home" has become her leaning on him at the door while he holds the key.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
She reaches into the skin of space with both hands and pulls two points together until they touch — a fold. Walking through the fold costs her a decade from the far end of her life: her hair whitens, her joints stiffen, her vision narrows one degree. The same transit costs her son one year from the near end of his youth: he comes out of each fold slightly larger, slightly more formed, slightly less the child she saved up for. The folds are not distance-proportional — a step across a room costs the same as a step across a solar system. She chooses the step size strategically, chaining through waypoints, but every waypoint is another decade off her back and another year onto his. She cannot fold without him present; she cannot fold herself alone and return.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability (static — the mechanism does not change, only its cumulative toll); social-tether (the mother-son axis inverts across the journey — she begins as protector and arrives as dependent); position (improving — they are physically closer to home with each step, but the position gain is offset by relational loss)
- cost_ledger: fold 1 — she is sixty, he is thirteen, she can still carry him; fold 4 — she is ninety, he is sixteen, he is carrying her pack; fold 7 — she is a hundred and ten and needs his arm to stand; every fold she gains a waypoint and loses a role
- antagonist_pressure: the quarantine authority pursues them across the waypoints, cutting off forward stations — they must take longer jumps to bypass checkpoints, which means bigger age-costs; by the final leg the only path home is a single fold that costs her thirty years
- dramatic_shape: she reaches him in the quarantine zone and makes the first fold (setup) → he notices she has aged overnight and begins reading the navigation himself (escalation) → at the midpoint waypoint he tells her she should let him fold them home alone, that he has watched long enough to try (midpoint reversal) → the authority closes the forward stations and the last fold will cost her everything she has left (cost spike) → she makes the fold anyway, both hands in the skin of space, him holding her upright (climax) → they arrive; she sits down and cannot stand without him; he is seventeen and she is the oldest person either of them has ever met (aftermath)

## Protagonist arc
Begins: a mother crossing the galaxy to retrieve her child, in full possession of the power that makes retrieval possible. Ends: a woman who used that power to destruction and arrived at the other end held up by the boy she came to rescue, who is no longer a boy. The flip: the moment she lets him hold the navigation tablet and reads it over his shoulder instead of from his hands.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. She finds him in the quarantine facility, twelve years old and healthy; she takes his hand and folds them two waypoints toward home; she is suddenly sixty and he is suddenly a year taller.
2. He wakes in the waystation and measures her height against his own; he is almost as tall; he asks how many more folds; she does not answer.
3. At the fourth waypoint she sits down on the dock and cannot immediately rise; he puts his hand under her elbow without being asked, and she feels the muscle in his arm.
4. The authority closes the route ahead; the only path is a single fold across six stations' worth of distance; she calculates thirty years; he asks if she can survive it; she does not answer.
5. He tries to fold without her — puts his hands into the space-skin and pushes — nothing happens; the power is not heritable, only hers; she sees his face when he understands.
6. She makes the fold with his arms around her waist keeping her upright; they arrive in the home corridor and she slides down the wall to sit; he crouches beside her and she does not know, for a moment, whether she is looking at her son or at a stranger who rescued her.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
A woman puts her hands into empty air and pulls until the far wall touches the near wall and they step through. She comes out the other side with white in her hair that was not there before. Her son comes out an inch taller. She picks up her pack. He picks up his pack. By the fourth passage he picks up her pack too, and she lets him, and neither of them says anything about it.

## Novelty claim
Age-swap journeys exist; the novelty here is that the cost is not symmetrical — she ages toward death while he ages toward capability, so the journey's success produces his competence to care for her at the exact moment she stops being able to care for herself. The road-to-hell is arrival: she wins by delivering him home as a young man strong enough to hold the door open for the old woman leaning on his arm.
