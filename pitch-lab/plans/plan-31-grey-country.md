# P31 grey-country

LOGLINE: A conscript who defects into the no-man's-land between two color-kingdoms finds a third country built by deserters — invisible to both armies, thriving, and doomed the moment either side learns the grey is winning.

LENS: escape

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Dov defects not from ideology but from exhaustion — he has been in the red trench for six years, the war has no visible purpose, and he walks into the grey on a night when staying feels more certain to kill him than leaving.
- anchor: Liret, the woman who finds him in the grey and doesn't kill him — who has been here for four years and built something real, and whose investment in the grey's survival is what Dov will eventually endanger.
- escalation: The grey is not neutral territory — it is a functioning country that has developed its own economy, its own architecture, its own political structure, built below the armies' perceptual threshold; Dov's arrival is the first defection from red in two years, which means red is looking harder at the grey than usual.
- trade: Dov gains safety, community, and something to live for — and his presence tightens red's surveillance on the grey, eventually forcing the grey into a conflict it cannot win militarily, only strategically.
- irony: The grey survives by being invisible to both armies; its invisibility depends on its smallness; Dov's defection is itself evidence the grey is large enough to be worth defecting to, and that evidence will end the grey.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The two color-kingdoms perceive reality through their kingdom's pigment: red-kingdom soldiers see the world in spectra anchored to red; blue-kingdom soldiers see in spectra anchored to blue. The grey no-man's-land between the trenches exists in a visual frequency neither army's perception is tuned to — it appears as flat, featureless, potentially dangerous terrain. Deserters who stay in the grey long enough begin to lose their original color-perception and gain grey-sight: a perception mode that renders the grey in full detail — buildings, gardens, faces, firelight — while rendering both armies as flat monochrome silhouettes. Grey-sight is permanent and means a returned deserter cannot fully perceive their original kingdom again. The grey country is physically real and fully developed: three streets, a market, a council house, 340 people. Neither army can see it because neither army can perceive grey. A cartographer working from elevation or an aerial survey would see it. Both armies have begun investing in aerial capability.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: Dov: legibility shifts from red-kingdom conscript to grey-resident (irreversible once grey-sight sets in); social-tether to red dissolves while new tether to the grey forms; moral-framework moves from survival-only to investment in a community. Liret: position moves from grey's de facto architect to the person responsible for the grey's defense strategy, a role she built the grey specifically to avoid.
- cost_ledger: Dov's first week in the grey — he loses red-sight incrementally; by day five he cannot distinguish red uniforms from grey rock; he is permanently separated from any return. His arrival triggers a red surveillance sweep — two grey scouts are killed on the perimeter. The grey council votes to restrict new arrivals; Dov must argue for the policy he himself just benefited from; he loses the vote; the restriction passes. Red deploys an aerial survey balloon; Liret burns it down with materials the grey doesn't have to spare; the grey's chemical stores are now critical.
- antagonist_pressure: Round 1 — red notices Dov's defection and increases perimeter patrols; grey perimeter scouts encounter a patrol and two are taken. Round 2 — a blue officer independently suspects the grey's existence from absence-of-fire patterns (the grey does not shoot; the no-man's-land is statistically too quiet); blue files an aerial-survey request. Round 3 — red and blue simultaneously deploy surveillance; neither knows the other is doing it; neither has confirmed the grey; but the grey is now inside two converging perimeters that are getting closer from opposite directions.
- dramatic_shape: Setup — Dov walks into the grey and Liret decides not to shoot him; he sees the country for the first time as grey-sight starts to develop. Escalation — the surveillance tightens from both sides; the grey council debates options and rejects all of them except the one Liret has been refusing to propose. Midpoint reversal — Dov, looking at the converging perimeter maps, realizes the grey cannot stay invisible; it has to become legible to one side on its own terms before both sides make it legible on theirs. Cost spike — Liret burns the red balloon; the grey is now visible as a fire source; both armies note the location; they have perhaps two weeks. Climax — Liret walks to the blue perimeter under a grey flag — a color blue cannot see — and speaks to the blue officer directly; she proposes recognition rather than erasure; she is the only person the grey has who can do this because she speaks blue-kingdom dialect and has not been listed as a deserter (she crossed into the grey voluntarily before formal enlistment). Aftermath — the blue officer reports upward; the grey has three days before the bureaucratic response; Liret comes home and tells Dov they may have done it; neither of them says the other half of the sentence.

## Protagonist arc
Dov begins as a man who wants only to stop being a soldier — he walks into the grey to escape, not to build. He ends as a resident of something he had no hand in building, whose survival he has accidentally endangered, and whose defense strategy he cannot execute but helped force. He is not the hero of the grey's survival; he is the catalyst of its crisis. The single decision: he presents the perimeter maps to the council and says out loud what Liret has been refusing to say.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Dov climbs out of the red trench at 0300 and walks into the grey; after twenty minutes he sees a light that should not be there; after another ten, Liret steps out of the dark with a hand on her knife and says "new?"
2. Dov's grey-sight develops over five days; on the fifth morning he stands in the grey's market square and can see the stalls, the faces, the smoke from cooking fires, and looks back toward the red trench and sees only a flat brown smear.
3. The council meets after the perimeter scouts are taken; twelve people in a council house that Dov could not have imagined existed two weeks ago vote on whether to close the border that saved him; he argues against the restriction; the restriction passes 9-3.
4. Liret spreads the aerial-survey maps on the council table — two sets, one from each army, sourced through the grey's intelligence contacts — and places them side by side; the converging perimeters are visible; nobody in the room speaks for a while.
5. Liret burns the red balloon at 0400 with a chemical charge; Dov watches from the grey's east wall; the balloon comes down in a slow orange arc; Liret comes back through the gate with her hands smelling of accelerant; she says "we have two weeks, maybe."
6. Liret walks to the blue perimeter in daylight, carrying nothing, under a grey cloth she's tied to a stick — the cloth is invisible to blue eyes as a color-object but legible as a shape; the blue officer stops her thirty meters out; she says, in blue-kingdom dialect, "I am from the grey, I am here to negotiate, and you should know that red is running aerial surveys from the east."

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Three hundred and forty people live on three streets between two armies that cannot see grey. They have a market, a council house, and a perimeter watch. When a red conscript walks in, he starts losing his ability to see red within five days — it is not a decision or a metaphor, it is a perceptual change that happens to his eyes. Liret walks to the blue trench carrying a grey flag the blue soldiers cannot identify by color, states her name and her purpose, and waits to see if the officer will shoot her or listen. He listens. She walks back. The grey has three days.

## Novelty claim
Deserter-communities in no-man's-land have precedent (WWI fiction, Catch-22 adjacent). The specific mechanism here — the perceptual-color system that makes the grey literally imperceptible, the grey-sight as permanent biological cost of defection, and the resolution through diplomatic legibility rather than military victory — is a fresh configuration. The irony that the grey wins by becoming visible on its own terms, after the entire premise is invisibility, is the load-bearing structural turn.
