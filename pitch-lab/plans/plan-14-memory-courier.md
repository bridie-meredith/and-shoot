# P14 memory-courier

LOGLINE: A courier flees across the rooftops of a vertical world by trading memories to the wind for speed, and arrives at sanctuary having forgotten why she ran, what she carried, and the face of the child waiting there.

LENS: escape

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: She carries something the people pursuing her will kill for, and speed is the only advantage she has.
- anchor: The child at the sanctuary — someone she promised to reach, someone whose survival depends on what she carries arriving intact.
- escalation: The pursuit closes faster than she can run on endurance alone; each speed-trade requires a larger memory; she buys time in fragments of herself.
- trade: Each trade to the wind gives her a burst of rooftop speed she could not otherwise sustain — at the cost of a specific memory, gone entirely, not blurred but absent.
- irony: She runs to save the child, who she has promised to remember; the faster she runs, the less she can remember making that promise, or the child's face, or the reason the promise matters.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
In this vertical city — buildings stacked on buildings, streets running at five different altitudes connected by rope-bridges and stairwell-markets — the wind is a persistent upward current that moves faster at the higher tiers. A trained courier can make a trade: she names a memory aloud into the wind, the wind takes it (it disperses into the upper current, unrecoverable), and she is pulled forward at the wind's speed for as long as the memory had mass — which correlates roughly with emotional weight, not duration. Trivial memories buy seconds. Anchoring memories — a face, a name, a felt obligation — buy minutes of open-rooftop sprint. The memory does not blur or fade: it ceases. She does not experience its loss as loss; she simply no longer has it. The package she carries is a physical object, and it does not vanish — only her memory of its contents and its recipient.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability spikes then falls (sprint speed → erosion of navigational memory → she loses route-knowledge she needs to finish the run) / social-tether dissolves (the child becomes a stranger; the promise becomes an obligation she can feel but not explain) / moral-framework erodes (she cannot remember why she said yes; can only feel that she did)
- cost_ledger: First trade (childhood memory) → ten seconds of sprint → safe crossing of Tier 3 bridge; mid-run trade (the child's name) → forty seconds of sprint → clears the second pursuit cluster; final trade (the child's face and the reason she agreed to run) → two minutes of full-speed rooftop flight → arrives at the sanctuary door
- antagonist_pressure: Round 1 — four trackers, standard pursuit pace, she has a lead; Round 2 — a wind-rider tracker who also knows the trade-system closes the gap from above; Round 3 — wind-rider offers to return one memory if she hands over the package, naming the child specifically, which she can no longer picture
- dramatic_shape: setup (she takes the job, is told the risk, runs) → escalation (first trade works; pursuit tightens; she trades heavier) → midpoint reversal (she loses the child's name and realizes she cannot verify who she is delivering to) → cost spike (she trades the child's face to outrun the wind-rider; arrives at the door with no image of who is inside) → climax (she hands the package to whoever opens the door based on a felt certainty she cannot articulate) → aftermath (the child is there; she does not recognize them)

## Protagonist arc
Start: competent, motivated, clear on her obligation. End: she has completed the mission by the erosion of every personal reason she had for caring about it. She is a courier who delivered a package to a stranger. The flip decision: the final trade — she gives the wind the child's face to get the last sprint, knowing she has already lost the name and will arrive unable to recognize who she came for.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. She takes the job, is handed the package, is shown the child's face by the parent hiring her; she repeats the face back aloud as she goes over the rooftop lip to prove she has it.
2. The first pursuit cluster closes on Tier 3; she names a summer afternoon from when she was eight — not important, she thinks — and the wind pulls her across a rope-bridge at a dead run before the trackers reach the near end.
3. Midway through Tier 5 she realizes she cannot remember the child's name and stops on a water-tower platform to check her own pockets for any written record; there is none; she named it to the wind during the second sprint without registering she was doing it.
4. The wind-rider tracker drops to her level and offers her the child's name back in exchange for the package; she cannot verify whether the tracker actually has it; she runs.
5. She trades the face — a deliberate, conscious trade, watching the child's image go out of her like a candle — and sprints the final tier in two minutes flat, arriving at the sanctuary door winded and blank.
6. She hands the package through the door to whoever opens it and stands on the threshold while a child she does not recognize starts to cry, and she feels, without being able to name why, that she did the right thing.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
She speaks a memory aloud into the wind — not a description of it, the memory itself, as a felt thing named outward — and the wind physically accelerates around her, lifting her stride, pulling her forward across rooftop spans she could not otherwise cross at speed. She does not feel the memory leave. She simply no longer has it when she reaches the other side. The package she carries is a hard object in her hands throughout; it does not change. Only she does.

## Novelty claim
Memory-as-currency runs through the genre (Eternal Sunshine, countless others). The specific mechanic here — memories traded *in real time during flight*, each trade felt as nothing (no grief, no awareness of loss), with the accumulating blank only visible structurally as she begins losing the thing she ran to reach — is the engine. The tragedy is not that she forgets; it is that forgetting feels like nothing.
