# P26 wife-as-weather

LOGLINE: A man keeps his drowned wife alive as weather, and the small kindnesses he does to keep her gentle — warm rains, soft winters — are starving the coast that needed her storms.

LENS: relational

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: He lost her and has found a way she can persist; he manages the weather-form to protect her from dispersing, because dispersal is a second death he refuses.
- anchor: Her — the specific weather-pattern that carries her presence, which he can feel when he tends it.
- escalation: Keeping her gentle costs the coast its hard-weather ecology; each soft season the fisheries thin, the soil blows, the cliff-towns lose their water-clearing storms; the coast begins to fail in visible, deniable increments.
- trade: Every act of tenderness toward her weather-form (a warm front held instead of released, a winter kept mild) sustains her coherence — and removes a storm the coast depended on.
- irony: His love for her is expressed as care for the coast, which she spent her life caring about; by keeping her alive, he destroys the coast she would have protected had she been alive to protect it.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
When a person with sufficient weather-bond drowns at sea, their dispersing attention can be caught by a dedicated keeper — someone who holds a specific ritual focus (a piece of their clothing kept wet, a daily spoken word at high-tide line) that gives the dissolving self a surface to cohere around. The resulting weather-form is not a ghost: it is the dead person's attention organized into atmospheric behavior. It has preferences: it thickens and calms when tended, roughens and fragments when neglected. The keeper manages the form by choosing which weather it expresses — he can let her build into a proper storm, or hold the front gentle. Storms disperse her attention outward; she loses coherence. So he keeps her soft, and soft means the coast never gets the gales that drive fish to the surface, clear the harbor sediment, and break the summer droughts with real rain.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: social-tether with her strengthens (he knows her weather-form better than he knew her living) / social-tether with the coast severs (fishers, farmers, cliff-town residents begin to connect their losses to the changed weather) / moral-framework under pressure (he knows what he is doing; he does it anyway; each season he reviews the numbers and decides again)
- cost_ledger: Year 1 (mild winter) → spring fish-run thins by a third → three fishing families sell; Year 2 (warm rain held) → summer drought unbroken → grain harvest fails; Year 3 (full storm suppressed) → harbor sediment blocks the channel → the last commercial trawler cannot make the bar → he is recognized as the keeper when the trawler captain comes to the cliff asking why the weather changed
- antagonist_pressure: Round 1 — the coast's losses are ambient, not attributed; Round 2 — a coastal ecologist identifies the pattern and begins asking who is managing the weather-bond; Round 3 — the trawler captain and ecologist bring a formal complaint to the regional authority with a name, which is his
- dramatic_shape: setup (he keeps her, coast begins to suffer, he notices and adjusts nothing) → escalation (losses aggregate; coast community begins piecing together causation) → midpoint reversal (ecologist names the mechanism publicly; he is no longer anonymous) → cost spike (authority gives him a deadline: release the weather-form or face forced dissolution by a storm-caller) → climax (he lets her build into a full storm, which will disperse her; she breaks over the coast for three days) → aftermath (coast begins recovery; he has nothing; he keeps the wet cloth in his pocket)

## Protagonist arc
Start: grieving, self-deceiving, certain that small adjustments to the coast's weather are a private harm manageable in increments. End: he lets her go into a storm that disperses her entirely, because she cannot be kept without cost and he cannot keep paying on behalf of people she would have died to protect. The flip: the authority's deadline is the mechanism, but the actual turn is when the ecologist shows him the fish-run numbers and he recognizes her handwriting in the data — she kept those records herself, before she drowned.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. He stands at the tide-line each morning with a strip of her oilskin coat held in the incoming wave; the weather-form thickens around the headland visibly, and he can feel her attention in the direction of the wind.
2. He watches the fish-run numbers in the harbor-master's weekly post and recognizes the decline but tells himself it is the season — he adjusts nothing that autumn, holds the winter mild.
3. The ecologist arrives in the cliff-town asking about weather records, shows the assembled residents a ten-year chart of gale frequency and fish-run correlation; he sits in the back and says nothing.
4. The ecologist visits him directly, shows him the older records — his wife's handwriting on the log-sheets — and tells him the coast has eighteen months before the sediment bar closes the harbor permanently.
5. The regional authority offers the deadline: voluntary release, or a storm-caller will dissolve the form by force, which would scatter her without the shape of a proper storm.
6. On the day of the deadline he goes to the tide-line before dawn and stops holding the front gentle; she builds over the headland through the morning and breaks at noon, three days of real gales, and the harbor sediment moves.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
He holds a strip of wet oilskin in the incoming tide and speaks her name; the cloud-cover thickens around the headland within an hour, visibly, the way fog thickens when a warm surface is held below cold air. He lets the front sit soft; the coast gets drizzle instead of gales; the fish don't surface; the harbor fills with silt. When he stops holding the cloth and steps back from the tide-line, the pressure builds and the weather-form moves as it needs to move — which is hard and cold and necessary.

## Novelty claim
Weather-ghost-as-grief-object is not new. The specific load-bearing inversion — that the act of love (keeping her gentle) is the exact mechanism of harm (removing the storms the coast needed), and that her own recorded work (the fish-run logs) becomes the evidence against him — is the engine. The road-to-hell turn works because his love is indistinguishable from her care for the coast, until it isn't.
