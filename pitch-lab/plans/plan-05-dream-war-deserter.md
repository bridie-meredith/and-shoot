# P05 dream-war-deserter

LOGLINE: A soldier deserts the dream-war by hiding inside a sleeping god's nightmare, traveling toward the one country no army can enter — not knowing the god wakes the moment its last dreamer abandons it.

LENS: escape

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: survive the dream-war by finding sanctuary no army can follow into
- anchor: the sleeping god's nightmare as a traversable landscape with exits that open onto the waking world
- escalation: other deserters are already inside the nightmare, burning through the dreamers who sustain it
- trade: the protagonist keeps other deserters from leaving to maintain her cover, trapping them
- irony: by holding the last dreamers in place to preserve her hiding place, she becomes the thing that shatters it — she is the last dreamer left when the god stirs

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The dream-war is fought in the shared sleep of gods — armies enter through the eyelids of sleeping divine bodies and hold territory inside their dreaming minds. A god's nightmare can only sustain itself while at least one human dreamer inhabits it; dreamers are soldiers, deserters, or civilians who slipped in. When the human count inside a god's dream drops to zero, the god surfaces toward waking. The protagonist moves through the nightmare's physical corridors — she can touch walls, open doors, smell smoke, hear other deserters' footsteps — because the nightmare is as solid as the god believes it is. Exits appear as literal seams of daylight in the nightmare's architecture, opening onto the waking geography above the god's sleeping body. Every deserter who slips through a seam reduces the headcount. The protagonist can physically block seams with her body or drag other deserters back from them.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability ↑ then ↓ (gains the nightmare as cover, loses it through own action); moral-framework ↓ (traps others to protect herself); social-tether ↓ (severs every connection to allies and war-kin)
- cost_ledger: each blocked escape = one more deserter who cannot leave = one person's waking life forfeited by her hand; holding the last seam closed = she cannot use it herself
- antagonist_pressure: the god's nightmare grows more hostile as waking approaches — corridors collapse, false exits multiply, the protagonist's senses invert (walls feel like open air, silence sounds like crowds); each deserter who escapes increases pressure on the remaining headcount
- dramatic_shape: setup (she enters the nightmare and discovers the exit seams) → escalation (other deserters find the seams; she starts blocking them) → midpoint reversal (she learns the headcount; realizes she is accelerating the god's waking) → cost spike (she physically prevents the last deserter from leaving) → climax (she is alone; the god's eye opens; the nightmare detonates outward) → aftermath (she surfaces into the sanctuary country, the only survivor, wanted by both armies)

## Protagonist arc
Starts as a soldier willing to break ranks to survive. Ends as someone who survived by becoming the thing she fled from — a commander who decides who stays and who goes, only her command is a nightmare and her orders are grip marks on other people's arms. The flip: the moment she holds the last deserter back, she stops deserting and starts ruling.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. She crawls through the god's left ear canal — the physical entry point — into a burning city that is not burning, where the flames are cold and the ash smells like copper.
2. She finds a seam of daylight in a cellar wall; two deserters are already prying it open. She watches the headcount flicker on the nightmare's sky like a clock hand and pulls them back by their collars.
3. She strikes a deal with the remaining deserters: they pool and take turns at the seams in rotation. They believe her. She does not rotate.
4. The god's nightmare architecture begins closing — ceilings lower, corridors loop — and three more deserters find a seam in the cathedral roof. She climbs after them and locks the roof hatch from inside.
5. One deserter is left. She holds the last seam shut with her shoulder for six hours while he hammers at it, then feels the nightmare go still, sees the god's pupil dilate through the nightmare's sky, and releases.
6. She falls out of a seam onto solid ground in the sanctuary country. Behind her the nightmare collapses into the god's waking howl. The country's border guards find her face-down in a field, covered in someone else's handprints.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
The protagonist physically moves through a landscape — she walks corridors, touches walls, wedges her body against doors. Every time she prevents an exit, she grabs a person's arm or blocks an opening with her weight. The god waking is not a metaphor: the nightmare architecture physically crumbles, the sky brightens, and she feels the ground beneath her feet soften as belief evacuates the space.

## Novelty claim
The "sanctuary inside a sleeping god" is familiar; the specific mechanism — a headcount threshold that the protagonist actively manipulates by physically restraining other people, thereby becoming the cause of the catastrophe she is hiding from — recombines known parts in a way that makes the protagonist's survival instinct structurally identical to the soldier's obedience she deserted to escape.
