# P27 divine-forger

LOGLINE: The realm's finest forger of divine signatures is commissioned to fake a god's blessing on a war, succeeds so perfectly that the god begins to believe it, and must now forge the deity's change of heart before the belief becomes true.

LENS: accidental

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Iven takes the commission because forging divine signatures is the one craft that cannot be mass-produced — each god has a signature whose complexity scales with the god's actual power, and the war-god Saur's is the hardest work of Iven's life.
- anchor: The sanctum in the capital where Saur's priests maintain a direct-attention channel — a bronze basin filled with oil that ripples when Saur is watching.
- escalation: Saur's attention-basin in the sanctum begins rippling without stimulus; the god has become aware of the blessing, cannot distinguish it from something he might have actually granted, and is reshaping his will to match it.
- trade: To forge the change-of-heart, Iven must produce a document more complex than the blessing — a negation that must feel authentic to a god who is now actively paying attention and partially believes the original.
- irony: The only person skilled enough to convince a god of his own change of heart is the person who accidentally gave him a false belief in the first place.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
Gods in this world do not watch constantly; they have attention, finite and directed. A divine signature is a physical mark — pressed in the god's specific medium, structured in a syntax that matches the god's characteristic will-patterns, sealed with a resonance tone that vibrates at the god's personal frequency. Iven works in a soundproofed workshop and uses a tuned-fork array to hit each god's frequency during the sealing stage. A good forgery sits outside the god's attention, indistinguishable from something the god might have done. A perfect forgery enters the god's attention retroactively — the god finds it in his own memory as if he chose it. Saur's forgery was perfect enough to do this. The god now has a false memory of choosing the war. The negation document must be perfect enough to replace that false memory with a false second memory: that Saur changed his mind. Each successive forgery must exceed the previous in verisimilitude, or the god will detect the seam.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability ↑ (each forgery stretches Iven's technique to its limit); moral-framework ↓ (the original commission was mercenary; the negation is still mercenary but now she is also saving lives); position → crisis (the priests who hired her want the war; the negation will destroy them)
- cost_ledger: Blessing forgery → Saur's attention activates / First negation draft → Saur's basin ripples continuously, priests notice / Second draft → Saur sends a direct query through the basin that Iven intercepts in person / Final seal → Iven hits her own resonance frequency during sealing and Saur briefly perceives her
- antagonist_pressure: The priests hired Iven under a sealed contract with a kill-clause; each step toward the negation brings them closer to activating it; by act three they have a forger-hunter in the city whose job is to find where the sanctum's basin is rippling from
- dramatic_shape: Iven seals the blessing and is paid → the basin begins rippling → she is hired again to produce the negation → the priest faction fractures over the war → she attempts the negation and Saur queries back → she seals the final document and Saur perceives her for one second → she walks out of the city

## Protagonist arc
Iven begins as a craftsperson who does not believe her work has stakes — she has forged a dozen gods and none of them noticed. She ends having been perceived by a god directly, for one second, which is the only moment of her career that was not forgery. The single decision: she seals the negation document knowing Saur will look at her through the seal, and she does not drop the fork.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Iven delivers the blessing to the commissioning priest, Ord; the basin ripples twice that evening; Ord tells her it is routine; she knows it is not because she has never made a forgery that drew the basin.
2. She is summoned back: the basin has not stopped in three days; Ord wants a negation; Iven names a price three times the blessing and Ord pays without negotiating, which tells her how afraid he is.
3. Iven's first negation attempt produces a document she burns herself — it is technically correct but metrically thin, and Saur will feel the seam. She starts again.
4. During the second attempt, a response comes through the basin — a ripple in a pattern she recognizes as a question-structure; Saur is asking who made the blessing. She intercepts it by sealing the negation draft over the basin, buying herself one day.
5. She seals the final negation with the full fork array. At the sealing tone, the basin goes flat and still — the god has the new false memory. But for one second before the seal closes, the oil forms a shape: an eye. It looks at her. Then it flattens. She picks up her tools and leaves through the back.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Iven strikes the fork against the tuned-metal plate and holds it over the document until the paper takes the vibration — she watches the ink move in the resonance pattern that is Saur's and no other god's. When the ink stops moving, the seal is set. She lifts the fork away, checks the corners of the document for bleed, and rolls it flat. Across the city, the bronze basin fills and then goes still. She does not go to look at it.

## Novelty claim
The forgery-as-belief-installation mechanism — where sufficiently perfect mimicry backfills into the god's actual memory rather than sitting beside it — creates a situation where craftsmanship itself is the act of violence, and greater skill produces greater theological consequence rather than greater safety.
