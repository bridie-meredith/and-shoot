# P33 demolitions-mage

LOGLINE: A demolitions-mage who brings down the towers of the old magic to make room for the new is the only one who can still read the warnings carved in their foundations, and the towers she has left to fell are the ones that say what falls next.

LENS: vocational

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Reva takes the demolition contracts because she helped write the new magic's framework and felling the old towers is the last stage of a project she believes in.
- anchor: The foundational archive — a chamber beneath the first tower she ever brought down, now sealed by rubble she created, where the complete old-language translation key is buried.
- escalation: Each standing tower's foundation warning is a continuation of a single structured message; she has been reading fragments without context, and the towers she has already felled were the early clauses; the remaining towers are the object of the sentence.
- trade: To read the full warning she must stop the demolition schedule, which will cost her the contracts that fund the translation work she is using to read the warnings.
- irony: She has already demolished the towers that explained what the warning was about; what remains tells her only the consequence, not the cause, and the cause is under the rubble of her own work.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
Old-magic towers were living structures — the stone was cast with intent-binding, which means the builders could inscribe meaning directly into the material such that the meaning persists as long as the stone holds. The inscriptions are not decorative; they are structural — the warning language is woven into the load-bearing foundation courses, readable only by a mage trained in old-magic's grammatical syntax. Reva is one of three people alive who can read it. She demolishes towers using a resonance method: she locates the tower's fundamental frequency, sings it to harmonic overload, and the structure comes down cleanly from the inside out. The rubble retains intent-bound content but fractures it — a demolished tower's warning becomes unrecoverable fragments. The translation key she needs to parse the warnings she has already found is under the rubble of the first tower she fell. The new magic has no such persistence — it is efficient, fungible, and written nowhere in stone.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: legibility ↓ (each fell destroys the context for the remaining warnings); moral-framework → reversal (she believed in the project; she begins to believe she was used to make the warnings unreadable); capability locked (she can read the warnings but cannot act without the key she buried)
- cost_ledger: Tower 1 fell → translation key buried / Towers 2–7 fell → partial warning clauses lost / Tower 8 warning → first complete readable clause, no prior context / Final tower → the only remaining clause that names what falls next
- antagonist_pressure: The new-magic council has a completion deadline tied to a construction contract; each week Reva delays costs them money; by act three they send a second demolitions-mage who cannot read the inscriptions but can bring down towers faster
- dramatic_shape: Reva reads tower 8's foundation and sees structured language → she cross-references and realizes she has been felling a sentence → she requests a delay and is refused → the second mage arrives → she breaks into her own rubble-field to find the translation key → she reads the final warning one hour before the last tower comes down

## Protagonist arc
Reva begins as an expert technician who trusted the council because the project made sense to her. She ends reading a completed warning that tells her the new magic's central conduit is built on a fault the old towers were containing — a finding she cannot prove without the towers and cannot unbury without equipment she does not have. The single decision: she stands in front of the final tower and refuses to sing the frequency while the second mage watches.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Reva fells tower 9 and finds foundation inscription she cannot parse — the grammar structure is conditional, which requires prior clauses she no longer has; she photographs the stone before the rubble settles.
2. She traces back through her demolition logs and maps the grammar structure across towers 1–9; the sentence begins in tower 1's foundation, under sixty tons of her own rubble.
3. She requests a three-week delay from the council to excavate tower 1; they refuse; she shows them the photographs; they tell her old-magic warnings are structurally non-binding superstition and a condition of her contract is that she does not translate on company time.
4. She goes to the rubble of tower 1 at night with a pickaxe and two lanterns; she finds six foundation stones with readable inscription; without the translation key she can only parse two words — "conduit" and "next."
5. The second mage, Bret, arrives at dawn with council authorization; Reva stands at tower 10's base and does not sing; Bret looks at her and sings it himself; the tower comes down around them both and Reva catches a falling foundation stone with the last readable clause still intact. She cannot translate it without the key. She carries it out of the rubble anyway.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Reva presses her palm flat against the foundation stone and reads with her fingers as much as her eyes — the intent-binding gives the letters a slight warmth, the old mages having pressed meaning into the material until it holds heat. She mouths the words slowly. The stone is telling her something. She does not yet know what. She picks up the pickaxe and chips the stone free of the mortar before Bret rounds the corner.

## Novelty claim
The demolition method destroys the warning by the same mechanism that enables her to read it — resonance brings down the tower and shatters the inscription — making the protagonist's expertise both the only tool for understanding the warning and the direct cause of its destruction.
