# P02 star-cartographer

LOGLINE: The empire's last star-cartographer discovers that the constellations she charts are a language, that reading them aloud rewrites the sky, and that her life's masterwork is slowly erasing the stars sailors steer by.

LENS: vocational

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: She charts the sky to preserve navigation for an empire whose sailors are dying on unmapped coasts.
- anchor: Her apprentice — a boy she trained, who sails the northern route and depends on her star-tables.
- escalation: Each chapter she reads aloud, one cluster of stars goes dark; the navy's losses climb; the empire orders her to stop reading — and then orders her to read faster, to erase stars that mark enemy ports.
- trade: Every reading grants her mastery over a new region of sky — and permanently removes those stars from the visible firmament.
- irony: Her dedication to the most complete star-atlas ever made is the instrument of its own destruction; the masterwork erases its own subject matter.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The constellations are a sentence-structure written in stellar positions by a dead civilization. When a trained reader vocalizes a constellation's full syllabic sequence, the stars forming it shift — each one moves along the sentence-grammar's logic toward a new configuration. The shift is permanent: the photons still travel, but the star's position in the sky never returns. Navigation charts drawn before a reading become wrong; ships using them miss headlands and reefs. The more complete the cartographer's atlas, the more sentences she can read, and the faster the sky empties. She cannot partial-read: a constellation read halfway collapses unpredictably, scattering its stars into noise.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability rises (she can read farther, control more sky) / legibility falls (the mapped sky she built her identity on ceases to exist) / social-tether frays (her apprentice's route goes dark; the sailors who trusted her charts die)
- cost_ledger: Reading one constellation → one cluster of navigational stars gone permanently → one sea-lane becomes uncharted → sailors die on that route; each successful weaponized reading buys the empire a tactical win and costs her a portion of the only sky she knows
- antagonist_pressure: Round 1 — the empire discovers the weapon and reassigns her from preservation to erasure; Round 2 — she complies once, then the navy produces a list of enemy port-stars they want erased, each politically justified; Round 3 — her apprentice's ship is listed as "acceptable loss" if she finishes the northern erasure sequence
- dramatic_shape: setup (she charts dutifully, notices readings change the sky) → escalation (empire weaponizes her, she erases enemy-route stars) → midpoint reversal (her apprentice's route is next on the erasure list) → cost spike (she reads the sequence anyway to stop a war; his ship goes down) → climax (she reads the final sequence that would erase the empire's own port-stars, the only thing she has left to bargain with) → aftermath (blank sky; she teaches the apprentice's successor to navigate by dead reckoning)

## Protagonist arc
Start: precise, mission-driven, believes preservation is apolitical. End: she has destroyed what she preserved, by hand, in service of people who never cared about the sky itself. The single decision that flips her: she reads the sequence that erases the northern route, knowing her apprentice is on it, because the empire has given her no third option.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. She reads a small test-constellation aloud to verify her translation; twenty stars move and three vanish; she checks her atlas and finds the discrepancy, then checks the harbor master's accident logs and finds the wreck.
2. An imperial admiral arrives with a list of enemy port-stars and orders her to read them out of the sky; she refuses; he produces evidence that her earlier reading, unintentional, already killed nine sailors — making her complicit whether she cooperates or not.
3. She reads the first weaponized sequence; a cluster over the straits dissolves; the enemy fleet loses the bearing and scatters; she goes home and crosses the stars off her atlas with one line of red ink per star.
4. She finds her apprentice's sailing manifest — his ship is on the northern route the empire now wants erased — and goes to the admiral to negotiate; he offers to reroute the ship if she reads the remaining sequences without delay.
5. She reads the northern sequence on the admiral's schedule; the stars go; the reroute order arrives three hours late; the ship is already past the headland; wreck confirmed by dawn.
6. She reads the final sequence — the empire's own home-port stars — and hands the blind sky to both sides as the only peace she has left to offer.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
She stands at the observatory window, speaks a syllable-string aloud from her atlas, and watches specific named stars move across the dome and stop in a new position — physically visible, verifiable with a sextant. When sailors take their next bearing on those stars, the bearing is wrong by the exact number of degrees the stars moved, and they strike the reef. The chain is: her voice → stars move → sailors die on wrong charts.

## Novelty claim
The constellation-as-language mechanism is known (cf. Le Guin's True Speech genre-adjacents), but the specific inversion — that *completing* the masterwork *is* the act of destruction, not corrupting it — is the engine here. The protagonist is not tempted off her mission; her mission is the catastrophe.
