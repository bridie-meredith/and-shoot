# P16 scent-engineer-fleet

LOGLINE: A scent-engineer who designs the perfumes that keep a refugee fleet calm discovers her newest blend is being used to make the passengers forget which planet they're owed, and that she has been breathing it longest.

LENS: vocational

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Petra synthesizes the fleet's emotional-management blends because she believes calm passengers survive; it is engineering work she is proud of.
- anchor: She creates a new blend commissioned by fleet Command — longer-acting, deeper calm — and disperses it through the ventilation of three habitat rings.
- escalation: Passengers in those rings stop filing resettlement claims; they stop naming the home planet in conversation; they stop knowing why they left.
- trade: Petra destroys her own formula archive to prevent Command from synthesizing the next generation of the compound — and inhales enough of the remaining stock to put herself under for seventy-two hours.
- irony: She wakes unable to remember which molecular pathway she destroyed, or whether she destroyed it at all.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
The fleet's life-support runs a continuous-diffusion aromatics layer — a low-concentration background blend that has maintained passenger composure across eleven years of transit. Scent-engineers design these blends by combining active compounds that bind to stress-response receptors in the limbic system; effects are dose-dependent and cumulative. Short-exposure blends produce calm that fades within hours. Long-exposure blends, sustained over weeks, alter the salience weighting of stored memories — not erasing them, but deprioritizing their emotional charge until they stop surfacing unprompted. The physical signature is subtle: passengers become quieter, stop returning to certain conversation topics, sleep better. Petra identifies the mechanism when she reviews the filter-capture logs and finds her compound binding to memory-salience receptors in a pattern she did not design for. She has been breathing a lower but continuous concentration of every blend she has ever synthesized, by virtue of working in the lab without sealed respiration.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: legibility (Petra's understanding of her own memory, degrading); moral-framework (professional neutrality → complicity → sabotage); position (trusted engineer → internal threat)
- cost_ledger: Each successful blend Petra has made extended the fleet's emotional stability — cost: she has been the longest-exposed person on the ship, her own history deprioritized compound by compound. Destroying the archive stops Command from the next synthesis — cost: Petra takes a therapeutic overdose to force her own consolidation event before the compound clears, and she loses seventy-two hours. Waking — cost: she cannot reconstruct whether the archive is gone or whether she is misremembering destroying it.
- antagonist_pressure: R1 — Command insists the resettlement-amnesia is a documented stress-adaptation, not a compound effect; they have a report that says so. R2 — Petra's lab assistant files a safety concern about her behavior, and her lab access is suspended. R3 — Command presents Petra with her own prior synthesis notes arguing the compound is safe, in her handwriting, which she does not remember writing.
- dramatic_shape: Setup — Petra reviews filter-capture data and isolates the anomalous receptor-binding signature / Escalation — she maps the effect across the three habitat rings and finds the amnesia is spreading in a concentration gradient / Midpoint reversal — she pulls her own medical record and finds her stress-biomarkers have been flatlined for eight years, not three / Cost spike — she enters the lab after hours and burns the archive physically, then breaks the sealed canister / Climax — she wakes in medical, seventy-two hours gone / Aftermath — she sits with the question of whether the notes Command showed her are real or manufactured; she cannot tell.

## Protagonist arc
Starts as a competent professional who trusts her own instruments and her own record. Ends unable to distinguish her memories from the compound's editing of them — the single decision being whether to destroy the archive when she knows the act will also destroy her own ability to verify it happened.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Petra pulls the filter-capture logs to troubleshoot a ventilation complaint and finds the receptor-binding signature that does not match her formula sheets — the compound is doing something she did not design.
2. She walks through the habitat rings on a Tuesday afternoon and asks passengers about the resettlement lottery; three in a row cannot name the planet; a fourth gives her the name of a planet she is fairly sure is not theirs.
3. Petra pulls her own lab-exposure records and calculates her cumulative dose; the number is larger than any passenger's by a factor of six.
4. Command's liaison meets with her in person, sets down the safety report, and tells her the binding pattern is within approved parameters; she watches him breathe the corridor air and realizes he is on the blend too.
5. Petra enters the lab at 0300, physically removes the archive drives, puts them in the incinerator, breaks the sealed canister of synthesized stock over the lab floor, and inhales.
6. She wakes in medical. A nurse tells her she was found on the lab floor with no external injury. Command's liaison brings her notes — in her handwriting — arguing the compound is safe. She reads them. She cannot remember writing them. She cannot remember if she can trust that she cannot remember.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Petra leans over the filter-capture printout and circles the binding-curve anomaly with a pen. She walks the habitat ring and asks a woman directly: "What planet are we going to?" The woman smiles and says she can't quite place it. Petra breaks the canister on the lab floor with her fist, glass cutting her palm, and breathes through her mouth while the compound pools around her shoes.

## Novelty claim
The protagonist's tool destroys her ability to verify her own resistance to it — the sabotage she commits is also the act that makes it impossible to know whether she succeeded; the irony is self-sealing rather than merely reversal-shaped.
