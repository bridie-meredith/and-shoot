# P25 locksmith-tenant

LOGLINE: A locksmith picks a door that was never meant to open and releases the previous tenant of the world, who is polite, grateful, and quietly resuming a life that everyone else has been living in the meantime.

LENS: accidental

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Marta picks locks the way other people read: for pleasure, for the satisfaction of a mechanism yielding to understanding; the door in the sub-basement is the only lock she has not been able to open in four years of trying.
- anchor: The tenant himself — Thresh — who is neither threatening nor magical-feeling, just a man who has been somewhere else for a very long time and needs help navigating what has changed; Marta becomes responsible for him the moment she opens the door.
- escalation: The world has continued without Thresh in ways that are not safe for him to discover gradually — there are people, institutions, and arrangements that were built on his absence, and his return is not a legal event anyone has a procedure for.
- trade: Marta helps Thresh reintegrate — finds him a room, explains the decades, introduces him to the changed city — and in return she is the only person who knows what the door was, which means she is the only person the people who locked it will come to.
- irony: Marta opened the door because it was the only lock she couldn't open; by opening it she becomes the person with the most dangerous key in the city, held responsible for a release she cannot undo.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
Some doors are not locks — they are arrangements between parties that the door itself enforces. The sub-basement door holds Thresh not by chains or seals but by a consensus-lock: as long as every person who knows the arrangement believes it is still in effect, the door holds. Marta's picking bypasses the belief-mechanism through pure mechanism — she works the physical pins without knowing the door is a consensus object, which is the only way it can be opened from outside. Thresh is not imprisoned: he is paused, held in a kind of administrative amber by an agreement made by parties who are mostly dead. He emerges without drama — he steps out, thanks her, and asks if she knows where a particular address is. The address is now a park. He absorbs this. The danger is not Thresh; it is the surviving institutions that locked him, which still function, whose current administrators don't know what they're administering, and who will discover the door is open when Thresh tries to access an account, sign a document, or simply appears in a record that should be closed.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: Marta: position moves from anonymous craftsperson to person of interest (to institutions she didn't know existed); social-tether to Thresh grows from obligation into something more complicated as she learns what he is. Thresh: legibility crisis — he is legally absent, administratively dead, and physically present; every system that touches him throws an error.
- cost_ledger: Marta gives Thresh her spare room — she is now housing an unlicensed administrative presence, which she doesn't know is a crime. She takes him to a records office — her name goes into a file; two administrators she's never met are notified. She helps him access a locked archive — she picks that lock too; this one she knows is wrong; she does it anyway. By the end she is a material witness in an administrative tribunal she didn't know could exist.
- antagonist_pressure: Round 1 — a letter arrives at Marta's shop addressed to "the mechanician who opened the sub-basement door," polite, requesting a meeting; she recognizes the institutional letterhead without knowing the institution. Round 2 — Thresh recognizes the letterhead; his face does something careful; he explains, without drama, that the people who wrote the letter are the ones who locked the door, and they are not angry, they are procedural, and procedure in this case means they will return Thresh to the door. Round 3 — the institution files an administrative claim: the door-opening was an unauthorized interference with a registered consensus-arrangement; Marta is personally liable for the cost of re-locking, which is denominated in years of administrative service — that is, her time, not her money.
- dramatic_shape: Setup — Marta opens the door; Thresh steps out; they have tea; he asks about the park. Escalation — Thresh begins navigating the world and each navigation raises a flag in a system Marta cannot see. Midpoint reversal — the letter arrives; Thresh explains what he is and what the door was; Marta learns she has not helped a man but released an administrative event. Cost spike — the institution files its claim; Marta is personally liable. Climax — Marta appears at the administrative tribunal and picks the procedural lock: she finds the founding document of the consensus-arrangement, which has an expiry clause no current administrator knew about, which means the arrangement lapsed seven years ago and the door should already have been open. Aftermath — Thresh is free, legally. Marta is free, legally. The sub-basement door hangs open and nobody knows what to put in the room.

## Protagonist arc
Marta begins as a craftsperson who defines herself by her skill at understanding mechanisms — she opens things because understanding them is pleasurable and she is good at it. She ends having used the same skill on a procedural document in a tribunal — not a lock at all, but the same act — and having found the expiry clause nobody read. She is unchanged in method and completely changed in consequence. The single decision: she submits the founding document to the tribunal rather than accept the liability claim, knowing it will work or it won't, and either way the door stays open.

## Beat outline (4-6 beats)
1. Marta picks the sub-basement door after her fourth year of visits; it opens inward; a man in a neat but extremely dated coat steps out, thanks her, and asks for directions to a street address; the address is a park; he nods and says he had thought it might be something like that.
2. Marta installs Thresh in her spare room; he is an easy tenant; he reads constantly; he occasionally asks what a word means now; she answers; she starts bringing two cups of tea without being asked.
3. Thresh goes to the bank to access an account; the account exists but is flagged; the teller calls a supervisor; the supervisor calls someone else; Thresh comes home at closing time and says he'll try the archive instead.
4. The letter arrives: formal, polite, institutional letterhead; Thresh reads it over Marta's shoulder and says "yes, that's them" in the tone of someone who has received difficult news before and has learned to receive it quietly.
5. The administrative tribunal: Marta sits across from three administrators who are genuinely sorry but firm; she asks to see the founding document of the consensus-arrangement; one of them produces it; she reads it for four minutes and points to page seven, clause 11(c).
6. Marta walks home through the park; Thresh is on a bench with a book; he has been there since the tribunal; he asks how it went; she tells him; he is quiet for a moment, then says that when they locked the door, clause 11(c) was considered a formality; he had not expected anyone to read it.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Marta inserts her picks into a lock, feels for the pins, and works them in sequence until the cylinder turns and the door swings inward. A man in a dated coat steps out. He is real, he is quiet, and he asks for directions. The danger is not that he is monstrous but that he is present in a city that has bureaucratic arrangements predicated on his absence, and each time he touches one of those arrangements — a bank account, an archive, a record — a file opens somewhere and a letter eventually arrives. Marta reads clause 11(c) of a founding document and points at it across a table. Three administrators look at the page.

## Novelty claim
The returning-prisoner and unlocking-something-dangerous are thoroughly worn genres. The novelty here is the mechanism: the antagonist is administrative procedure, not a monster or a villain; the climax is a contract-reading, not a fight; and the "previous tenant" is not threatening — his ordinariness is load-bearing, because the horror of the situation is entirely procedural and the resolution is entirely procedural, and both are rendered as concrete human action (picking locks, reading documents, sitting on park benches) rather than as magic or violence.
