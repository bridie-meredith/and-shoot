## Full plan

# P01 bonewright-sister

LOGLINE: A bonewright who grows living prosthetics from her own marrow keeps her dying sister walking by replacing her piece by piece, until the sister who finally stands is no longer the person she was saving.

LENS: relational

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: Kael refuses to let her younger sister Daria die of a degenerative bone-wasting disease that is consuming her skeleton from within.
- anchor: Daria — specific, beloved, irreplaceable to Kael — whose survival is the only thing Kael is organizing her life around.
- escalation: Each replacement limb or vertebra requires Kael to harvest from deeper marrow, shortening her own life while Daria's disease spreads faster than the replacements can hold.
- trade: Kael keeps Daria alive and mobile but surrenders her own physical substance piece by piece — and the living bone she grows carries her own cellular memory, subtly overwriting Daria's.
- irony: The more completely Kael saves Daria, the more Daria becomes Kael — the love that drives the saving is the mechanism of the erasure.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
A bonewright draws marrow up through her palms and sculpts it into living tissue outside the body: the marrow firms into cortical bone, sprouts its own blood supply, and will graft to a host's nervous system within hours. The prosthetic is alive — it grows, heals, and carries the bonewright's epigenetic signature. A recipient's body adapts to the new bone, but the adaptation runs both ways: the living graft slowly repatterns adjacent nerve and muscle tissue toward its donor's blueprint. Small replacements (a finger, a rib) leave the recipient largely themselves. Replacing a spine, both femurs, the pelvis — the body's structural core — means the recipient's movement, reflexes, and eventually proprioceptive identity reorganize around a different skeleton. Kael has known this. She has chosen not to say it.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: Kael: capability degrades (marrow loss, fatigue, shortening horizon); moral-framework fractures (from protector to author of erasure); social-tether to Daria inverts as Daria becomes less Daria. Daria: legibility to others shifts — she moves like Kael, reacts like Kael, stops knowing which memories are hers.
- cost_ledger: First replacement (hand) — Kael loses grip strength permanently. Second (left leg) — Kael develops a limp she cannot bonewright away, marrow too thin to regrow for herself. Third (spine) — Kael's own skeleton begins to weaken systemically; she is two years from her own collapse. Each cost is on-page: Kael's body visibly failing as Daria's visibly rises.
- antagonist_pressure: Round 1 — the disease advances faster than projected, forcing a second major replacement before Kael has recovered. Round 2 — a bonewright's guild examiner notices Kael's epigenetic signature in Daria's gait and threatens to report the grafting as identity violation under guild law. Round 3 — Daria herself begins to grieve the memories she cannot locate, then starts using Kael's childhood name for their mother.
- dramatic_shape: Setup — Kael performs a small graft, functional and clean, believing she controls the cost. Escalation — disease acceleration demands the spine; Kael does it knowing the risk, telling herself Daria will stay Daria. Midpoint reversal — Daria passes the guild examiner's bench test perfectly, moving exactly as Kael moves; the examiner congratulates Kael on her "student." Cost spike — Kael's own marrow fails to regenerate; she learns she has given too much to reverse. Climax — Daria, standing strong and wrong, asks Kael why she looks so sad; it is not a question Daria would have asked. Aftermath — Kael must decide whether the person standing is worth grieving or worth meeting.

## Protagonist arc
Kael begins as a technician of love — precise, certain, in control of her gift. She ends hollowed: her sister is alive and walking and gone, and Kael is medically terminal, having spent her marrow to build a stranger who wears her sister's face and her own bones. The single decision that flips her is choosing to replace the spine — the point of no return she crosses without saying aloud what she knows it means.

## Beat outline
1. Kael grafts a new hand onto Daria in their kitchen; Daria flexes the fingers and laughs; Kael quietly notes the harvest site on her palm is not healing the way it should.
2. Guild examiner visits the workshop, watches Daria walk across the room, and tells Kael the graft-signature is unusually strong — asks if she used primary marrow. Kael says no.
3. Daria collapses; the disease has reached the lumbar vertebrae; Kael harvests from her own iliac crest for six hours to build a replacement spine, and installs it over two nights.
4. Daria stands and walks — fully, without pain, for the first time in two years; she moves in Kael's characteristic slight-forward lean; she does not notice; Kael cannot stop watching.
5. Kael visits her own physician and learns her marrow density is now below functional threshold; she cannot replace what she has given. She walks home the long way.
6. Daria calls across the room asking where they keep the tea — using the cabinet name from Kael's childhood home, a place Daria never lived. Kael tells her. Neither of them marks it.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
Kael presses her palms to her own hip bone, draws marrow out through the skin in a slow bright thread, and shapes it against her workbench into the curve of a lumbar vertebra. She carries it to Daria's room and seats it against the exposed surgical site, where it knits to living tissue over the next two hours. Every piece of Daria's original skeleton that Kael removes goes into a steel dish. By the end there are eleven pieces in the dish, and Daria is walking, and Daria's walk is Kael's walk.

## Novelty claim
The recombination is body-horror-as-devotion (known) + identity-replacement-through-medical-gift (known) but the mechanism is genuinely new: the cost of the gift is not the donor's life but the recipient's selfhood, and the donor knows this going in and does it anyway out of love. The tragedy is not accident — it is chosen, incrementally, in full knowledge, from love.
