## Why it won

- **The hagiographic archetype is inverted, not worn.** The generic-attack has real teeth here (the self-consuming saint is a deep tradition). It doesn't kill the plan because this is **parasitism, not martyrdom**: the hunger she absorbs *metabolizes and grows*, borrowing energy from her fat, then muscle, then bone, to sustain its own accumulation. A martyr is consumed by the miracle; she is consumed by an appetite that has become autonomous — "the hungriest thing inside the walls."
- **Unavoidability is airtight at the level of character-as-rule.** The trap isn't only the guards who hold her hands to the flour — it's that she *cannot watch hunger without absorbing it*; refusal is structurally impossible from her first act. The physical confinement merely ratifies her own law.
- **The most dramatically concrete deterioration arc** of the three: week-stamped, visceral (eats double → cannot sleep → hands shake → face caved, ribs showing).
- **The most uncanny image in the field:** a woman whose own body is consuming itself, holding a loaf and not understanding she is waiting for it to get smaller. The cognitive dissociation is the knife.

## Full plan

# P30 famine-saint

LOGLINE: A famine-saint who multiplies bread by taking the hunger into herself is asked to feed an entire besieged city, and the hunger she absorbs begins to multiply too.

LENS: penitential

## Premise trajectory (5-component path)
- motivation: She multiplies bread because watching someone starve is the one thing she cannot endure — the hunger inside her is quieter than watching it outside.
- anchor: A baker in the besieged city who has been rationing his family's flour for six weeks and has reached the last handful; she can save them; the question is whether she can stop at them.
- escalation: By the third week of feeding the city, the hunger inside her has stopped being hers — it has become a second appetite that does not belong to her body and does not diminish when she eats.
- trade: She can multiply enough bread to end the siege's starvation by absorbing every citizen's hunger at once — a single act, not a daily trickle — but the absorption at that scale may not be reversible.
- irony: She came to spare the city from hunger and arrives at the decision point carrying more hunger than the city ever held — she has saved everyone and is the hungriest thing inside the walls.

## World rules (the magic-tech that makes the engine work)
When she touches bread and speaks the multiplication, the loaf divides — one becomes four, four becomes sixteen — and the hunger that would have gone unsatisfied transfers to her stomach. She feels it as a physical cavity behind her ribs: the aggregate of every empty stomach she has relieved. Under normal circumstances it dissipates over two to three days as her own body metabolizes; she eats more than an ordinary person and sleeps poorly, but she recovers. At city scale, the hunger volume exceeds what her body can metabolize — it accumulates. Accumulated hunger takes on the character of the hungriest among those she relieved: by the fourth week it is not dissipating but growing, borrowing energy from her fat, then her muscle, then her bone density, to sustain its own presence.

## Substance signature
- state_axes: capability (degrading — the multiplication still works but costs more each cycle as her body is consumed from within); moral-framework (fracturing — she begins as selfless instrument and arrives at a decision that cannot be called selfless); social-tether (the baker and his family are the tether she holds onto as she decides)
- cost_ledger: week 1 — she eats double and sleeps four hours; week 2 — she eats constantly and cannot sleep; week 3 — her hands shake during multiplication and she drops bread; week 4 — her face is visibly caved, her ribs show through her clothes, the hunger is eating her body to sustain itself
- antagonist_pressure: the city's commanders want her to multiply faster and larger batches, not understanding the mechanism's cost; they station guards to ensure she does not leave; by week four they are bringing her the flour while she is too weak to stand and holding her hands to the bread
- dramatic_shape: she arrives at the city and begins the daily multiplication cycle (setup) → the commanders realize the scale required and double her quota (escalation) → she notices the hunger is growing instead of fading and she cannot account for the caloric gap in her body (midpoint reversal) → she is too weak to stand and the commanders hold her hands to the bread while she multiplies (cost spike) → she performs the single mass-absorption and multiplication — everything the city held — and collapses (climax) → the bread is everywhere; she is alive, barely; the baker brings her the first loaf she has touched in three weeks and she cannot make herself eat it (aftermath)

## Protagonist arc
Begins: a saint who absorbs hunger willingly because the alternative — watching it — is worse than bearing it. Ends: a woman who has absorbed more hunger than her body can survive, who performs the final act not because she can endure it but because she cannot stop. The flip: the moment she lets the commanders hold her hands to the bread because she cannot raise her arms herself, and she does not tell them to stop.

## Beat outline
1. She arrives at the city gate and the baker meets her with his last handful of flour; she makes it into four loaves while he watches; she sits down against the wall and eats a full meal while the family eats their share.
2. The commanders show her the granary — empty — and the population count — twelve thousand — and the multiplication tables; she does the arithmetic and does not say what she learns from it.
3. She wakes at the third week's start unable to feel the hunger behind her ribs as a distinct thing — it has spread into her chest, her throat, the backs of her hands.
4. The baker brings her breakfast and she eats it and is hungry again before she stands up; she asks him how his children are; she does not tell him she cannot account for where the food is going.
5. Two guards hold her hands flat on a sack of flour while she multiplies; her hands shake so hard the bread tears; she multiplies it anyway and the guards catch her when her knees go.
6. She performs the mass absorption in the city square — every citizen's hunger, all at once — and falls; the bread is stacked to the rooflines; the baker puts a loaf in her hands and she holds it for a long time before she understands that she is waiting for it to get smaller.

## Concrete engine (DEC-0115 fence)
A woman puts her hands on bread and speaks, and the loaf splits into four. She feels the splitting as a physical cavity opening behind her ribs — the weight of all the stomachs she has just emptied. She eats a meal to fill it. By the fourth week she eats constantly and the cavity grows larger anyway; her clothes hang off her collarbones. Two guards hold her hands to the flour sack because she cannot lift her arms. The bread multiplies. She falls. The bread is stacked in the street and she is lying in it and the baker has to carry her inside.

## Novelty claim
Self-consumption saints are familiar; the novelty is that the hunger does not simply deplete her — it metabolizes, grows, and begins consuming her from inside to sustain its own accumulation. The road-to-hell is not martyrdom but parasitism: she becomes the host of the city's aggregate starvation, and the city is fed while the saint is eaten.
