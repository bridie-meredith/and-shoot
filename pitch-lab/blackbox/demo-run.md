## Iteration 1

### Summary going in

LOGLINE: A retired vault-architect, blackmailed into robbing the floating sky-treasury she built, must outrun the apprentice who has been re-arming her own traps to kill her inside it.

ENGINE: The treasury is a stone disc the size of a plaza, kept aloft by chained lift-stones in its belly; you climb in through the underside while it drifts. Eddra knows the safe gaps in the trap-grid — pressure plates, the swinging counterweight axes, the flood-sluices — but as she crawls each corridor she finds her old marks scratched out and re-cut a hand's width off, because Sera has physically moved the mechanisms overnight. Eddra advances by ripping panels open and rewiring the triggers with her own hands, racing a girl who relocks them behind her.

PROTAGONIST ARC: Eddra starts cautious, hoarding her secret, trusting no one and least of all the granddaughter-aged apprentice. She moves from solo creeping to actively dismantling her own life's work, then to cutting a deal with Sera mid-vault, ending as a thief who chooses to detonate the lift-stones and ground the whole treasury rather than hand it to either master.

ANTAGONIST (with a WILL): Sera, the apprentice who finished the traps after Eddra was pushed out, wants the architect dead inside the vault so the design dies with her and Sera becomes the realm's sole keysmith. She loses that monopoly — and her patron's reward — if Eddra walks out alive, so she escalates from re-cutting plates to flooding sluices to cornering Eddra at the lift-chamber.

STAKES: Externally, the treasury holds the conscription-ledgers that decide who gets pressed into the sky-fleet; whoever empties it controls the draft. Personally, Eddra's blackmailer holds her exiled brother. The vault is over open water — fail the grid and you fall through the underside into the sea, so retreat is as lethal as advance.

COST PAID: Eddra burns her hands raw rewiring live triggers; she sacrifices her decoy partner to a counterweight to buy a corridor; at the reversal she destroys the one design she was proud of, the thing that made her name.

PLOT STRUCTURE: (1) Eddra breaks in below the disc, finds her marks moved — Sera is ahead of her. (2) She fights inward, losing her partner to a swinging axis. (3) REVERSAL: cornered, she learns Sera doesn't serve the blackmailer but means to murder her freelance — so Eddra stops fleeing and recruits Sera against both their masters. (4) Second gear: together they reach the ledger-vault and find it already emptied — the patron played them both. (5) Eddra cuts the lift-stone chains and grounds the treasury on the patron's own harbor, trading the prize for leverage. (6) Open ending: brother freed, monopoly broken, Eddra and Sera loose in a realm that now wants them both.

HOOK IMAGE (staged at Beat 1): Eddra's hand closes on a corridor wall and finds her own twenty-year-old chisel-mark scratched out and recut a finger's width left — fresh stone-dust still under her nail.

RANGE/TONE: Propulsive heist-adventure with a wary mentor-rival two-hander; brisk, tactile, occasionally funny, and genuinely winnable — not elegiac.

MARKETABILITY: Adult fantasy/heist readers; comps Six of Crows (Bardugo) and Inversions/Gentleman Bastard caper energy. Sells on a closed-room heist where the building is alive and the rival is rewriting the maze in real time.

NOVELTY: The maze fights back through a human hand re-cutting it overnight, not magic — architect vs. her own student inside her own design. Recombines heist + mentor-rival, but the live, hand-altered trap-grid is the fresh turn.

**Weaknesses:**
- UNAVOIDABILITY/self-sabotaging stakes: the pitch BRAGS the ending is 'genuinely winnable,' and Beat 5 proves it — Eddra cuts the lift-stone chains and grounds the treasury on the patron's harbor, a clean third-option escape that voids the much-touted no-exit ('retreat is as lethal as advance'). - ANTAGONIST-AS-FUNCTION on the decoy partner + STOCK COST: the 'decoy partner' exists only to be sacrificed to a counterweight axis in Beat 2 — a cipher with no name, want, or prior page-time, introduced solely so a death can be 'paid.' - FORMULA + self-flagged GENERIC: the overall shape is heist + mentor-rival + mid-vault double-cross + the empty-vault reveal ('the patron played them both' — Beat 4 is the oldest caper twist there is). The pitch names Six of Crows and Gentleman Bastard as comps, an open admission the shape is recombinant. - SUSTAINABILITY: the core engine is one move — 'find my mark moved, rip the panel, rewire, advance, she relocks behind me' — repeated corridor by corridor. A brilliant five-page sequence and an unproven novel. Nothing shows how the maze-vs-counter-maze loop varies enough to carry length without becoming a metronome.

**Fixes handed to the reviser:**
- Close the no-exit you broke. The grounding-the-treasury finale is a third option that makes the lethal-retreat premise a lie — either remove the clean escape or make grounding it cost something irreversible and personal (grounding it kills people in the harbor below, or destroys the only ledger copy that could free the brother), forcing a real either/or. The ending should cost what the premise threatened, not route around it.
- Give the decoy partner a name, a want, and three beats before the counterweight kills them, so the Beat 2 sacrifice reads as loss not token. Better: make the sacrificed ally someone Eddra recruited under false pretenses, so the death is also a guilt she carries into the Sera alliance — converting stock cost into character debt.
- Dramatize the Beat 3 turn instead of asserting it. Show the specific concrete thing Sera does (a recut mark that SPARES Eddra; a trap re-aimed at the patron's men) that flips a murder attempt into alliance. The hardest emotional pivot in the book is currently one clause; it must be earned on-page or the two-hander collapses.
- Vary the maze engine across the novel. Add a structural escalation in HOW the maze fights — Sera runs out of overnight time and alters traps WHILE Eddra watches; the disc loses altitude so the whole grid tilts; Eddra must rewire traps to be lethal to pursuers — so the loop changes shape at least twice.
- Cut or recast the empty-vault reveal (Beat 4). 'The prize was never there' is the most worn twist in heist. If kept, make WHAT was taken worse (the patron emptied the ledgers to start the draft early, so conscription is already underway) rather than a flat 'you were played.'
- Prove one beat of prose + interiority next iteration. Write the 200-word passage where Eddra's hand finds the recut mark — let the voice, the architect's specific grief, and the tactile register actually appear, so 'brisk, tactile, occasionally funny' stops being a promise.

**Feedback to the generator:** The process produced a strong CONCRETE ENGINE and a tight HOOK — the recut-mark-with-dust-under-the-nail is the best thing here and should be protected. Each is a competence-signal that simultaneously caps originality. ## Iteration 2

### Summary going in

LOGLINE: A disgraced vault-architect, blackmailed into robbing the floating sky-treasury she built, discovers her own apprentice has spent a year re-cutting the death-traps to kill her inside it — and the only way out for either of them runs through the other.

ENGINE: The treasury is a stone disc the size of a plaza, held up by chained lift-stones in its belly; you climb in through the underside while it drifts over open sea. Eddra memorized the safe gaps in the trap-grid — the pressure plates, the swinging counterweight axes, the flood-sluices — but every mark she scratched twenty years ago has been chiseled out and re-cut a hand's width off, because Sera physically walks the corridors at night and moves the mechanisms. Eddra advances by prying panels open and rewiring live triggers with her bare hands; Sera re-locks them behind her, until the night runs out and Sera starts altering the grid while Eddra watches, ten feet away.

PROTAGONIST ARC: Eddra starts cautious, hoarding the one secret that keeps her useful, trusting no one. She moves from solo creeping, to rewiring traps to kill her pursuers, to recognizing the girl ahead of her isn't the blackmailer's tool but her own abandoned apprentice — and ends choosing to stay inside the falling vault to hold a door open for Sera, paying with the body she can't get out in time.

ANTAGONIST (with a WILL): Sera, the apprentice Eddra trained then abandoned when she fled the patron's service, finished the traps alone and was told the architect's return means her own execution — the patron keeps one keysmith, never two. Sera hunts Eddra to live; she loses her life the moment Eddra walks out, so she escalates from re-cutting plates to flooding sluices to tilting the whole disc by venting a lift-stone.

STAKES: The treasury holds the conscription-ledgers — the master copy that names every child already pressed into the sky-fleet, including the leverage to free Eddra's brother. Destroy the disc and the ledger burns with it; leave it intact and the draft proceeds. There is no third door: the patron rigged the lift-chamber to vent on entry, so reaching the prize is what starts the fall, and the sea is under everything.

COST PAID: Eddra burns her hands to ruin on live triggers; she rewires a corridor to kill the patron's men and watches it work; at the end, having gotten Sera to the underside hatch, she stays behind to hold the jammed counterweight by hand so it won't crush the gap, and rides the grounding disc down.

PLOT STRUCTURE: (1) Eddra breaks in below, finds her marks moved — someone is ahead of her. (2) She fights inward, rewiring traps lethal against the patron's enforcers, certain the re-cutter serves the blackmailer. (3) REVERSAL: cornered at a flood-sluice, she sees the new cut SPARES her and re-aims at the men behind — Sera isn't the patron's hand, she's been condemned by him, and is signaling, not killing. The hunt inverts into a two-person escape. (4) SECOND GEAR: together they reach the ledger-chamber and find the patron has already torn the master copy out and begun the draft early — the prize they're risking everything for is the proof a crime is already underway, not treasure. (5) Eddra vents the lift-stones to ground the disc on open water rather than the patron's harbor, denying him the wreck — and the venting starts the irreversible fall. (6) She holds the hatch-counterweight by hand so Sera drops clear; the disc takes Eddra into the sea with the burning ledger. Ambiguous close: Sera surfaces, the draft exposed, the architect gone under her own design.

HOOK IMAGE (staged at Beat 1): Eddra's palm closes on a corridor wall and finds her own twenty-year-old chisel-mark scratched out and recut a finger's width left — the stone-dust still soft, still under someone's nail but not hers.

RANGE/TONE: Propulsive, tactile heist-thriller with a wary two-hander at its spine — brisk, occasionally dry-funny, real velocity — that turns the screw into earned sacrifice at the end rather than tragic-irony decoration. Winnable for Sera; costly for Eddra.

MARKETABILITY: Adult fantasy/heist readers; comps Six of Crows (Bardugo) for crew-betrayal texture and Tana French for the interiority-under-pressure prose. Sells on a closed-room heist where the building is a living maze a human rival re-cuts overnight, and the rival is the one person who can't be killed or escaped — only joined.

NOVELTY: The maze fights back through a human hand re-carving it in the dark, not magic — and the antagonist isn't beaten but recruited, with the architect, not the apprentice, paying the final price. Recombines heist + mentor-rival, but the live hand-altered grid and the inverted who-survives ending are the fresh turns.

## Iteration 3

### Summary going in

LOGLINE: A disgraced vault-architect is blackmailed into robbing the sky-treasury she built, only to find her abandoned apprentice running its trap-grid on a rotating clock from the inside — and the two of them must out-time a patron who has already swapped the prize they came for with a forgery.

ENGINE: The treasury is a stone disc held aloft by chained lift-stones, boarded from beneath as it drifts over open sea. The trap-grid — pressure plates, counterweight axes, flood-sluices — was re-cut so no fixed position is safe; instead, each plate's safe gap opens and closes on a counterweight cycle, and the only safe path is a moving window you must walk in rhythm. Eddra's twenty-year-old memory is useless for positions but holds the cycle's tempo; Sera, walking the corridors ahead, drops the counterweights early or late by hand to break Eddra's timing, then to save it.

PROTAGONIST ARC: Eddra starts hoarding the tempo as her one edge, trusting no one; she advances by counting beats aloud and diving the gaps. When she reads Sera's interference as an attack, she rigs a sluice to drown her pursuer — then sees the abort-mark and reverses. She ends having out-argued, not out-died, Sera: choosing to ground the disc and walk off it.

ANTAGONIST (with a WILL): Sera, the apprentice Eddra trained and abandoned, was promised her life only if Eddra dies inside — the patron keeps one keysmith, never two. After the alliance her will still competes: Sera wants the disc burned with the patron's name aboard; Eddra needs it grounded intact to trade for her conscripted brother. They fight over the lift-stone controls with incompatible win conditions.

STAKES: The disc holds the conscription-ledger naming pressed children, including the leverage to free Eddra's brother. But the patron tore out the master copy and planted a forgery, so the women carry false proof unless they find the real ledger elsewhere aboard before the disc grounds. Burn it and the leverage dies; carry the forgery out and the brother stays pressed.

COST PAID: Eddra ruins her hands diving live gaps; she drowns the patron's foreman in a sluice and watches it work. At the climax she lets Sera vent the patron's name overboard — surrendering her clean grounding — and trades her own freedom-from-the-patron back for the real ledger, walking off owing Sera a debt she names.

PLOT STRUCTURE: (1) Eddra boards below, counts the tempo, finds someone breaking her rhythm ahead. (2) She rigs a sluice to kill the interferer. (3) REVERSAL: at the kill-moment she sees a deliberately botched cut in her own old apprentice-shorthand — Sera is signaling an abort, condemned, not hunting. (4) SECOND GEAR: the ledger-chamber holds a forgery; the women split to hunt the real copy while the patron tilts the disc against them. (5) They clash over the controls — burn versus ground. (6) Compromise: Sera gets the patron's name sunk, Eddra gets the ledger; both walk off the grounding disc, brother's freedom bought, debt open.

HOOK IMAGE (staged at Beat 3): under the rising sluice-water, Eddra's lamp catches a chisel-mark cut backward — the private apprentice-shorthand for STOP that only she ever taught one person.

RANGE/TONE: Propulsive, tactile heist-thriller with a sharp, dry two-hander at its spine; a rhythm-puzzle the reader can count along to. Winnable for both — costly, argued-out, not tragic.

MARKETABILITY: Adult fantasy/heist readers; comps Six of Crows (Bardugo) for crew-betrayal texture and The Lies of Locke Lamora (Lynch) for con-architecture wit. Sells on a maze whose only safe path is a beat you keep, re-clocked live by the one rival you can't kill — only out-time and out-talk.

NOVELTY: The trap is a tempo, not a map; the mentor-rival becomes a co-conspirator whose win condition stays opposed to the hero's after the alliance; and nobody is killed by their own design — they argue their way off it. Recombines heist + mentor-rival, but the rhythm-grid and the post-alliance incompatible-goals back half are the fresh turns.
