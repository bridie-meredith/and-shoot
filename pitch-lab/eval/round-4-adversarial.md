P01 bonewright-sister — Round 4 (Tier 4 BESTEST, adversarial)
  Attack 1 GENERIC: The case: body-horror devotion + identity-erasure through gift is the engine of dozens of recognized works (Cronenberg's body-horror-as-love, the "cure worse than disease" arc, donor who becomes the disease); specifically the "saving someone by remaking them into yourself" beat maps directly onto familiar SF/horror — the recipient loses personhood through the donor's love, which is recognizable enough that a pitch-room reader will name the template inside a sentence. → SURVIVES — the mechanism-level defense holds: the novel wrinkle is that the *structural unit* is the skeleton's specific epigenetic repatterning, so the selfhood replacement is incremental, anatomically staged, and Kael knows each unit represents a choice; the cost lands on the recipient's identity (not the donor's life), which flips the expected direction of sacrifice in a way the generic attack cannot wholly absorb. The examiner-midpoint (congratulating Kael on her "student") is a genuinely unforgettable misdirection that the generic form does not contain.
  Attack 2 NAIVE-FOLLOW: A surgeon named Kael grows living bone from her own body and grafts pieces of herself into her dying sister Daria to keep her alive. Each graft carries Kael's biological signature, so Daria's movements and then memories start to drift toward Kael's. A guild examiner notices that Daria moves exactly like Kael. Kael loses grip in one hand, gets a permanent limp, then her spine begins failing. She replaces Daria's spine anyway — the point of no return — knowing it will rewrite who Daria is. At the end Daria stands healthy and strong, but asks a question the real Daria would never ask, and Kael must decide whether to grieve her sister or meet the stranger. → SURVIVES
  Attack 3 UNAVOIDABILITY: Could Kael stop at the first graft, let the sister deteriorate more slowly, and buy time to find a non-epigenetic solution? The plan's internal rule is that disease outpaces replacements — the spine graft is forced by escalating medical necessity, not by impatience. Could Kael seek another bonewright? The guild is the only institution with oversight, and detecting Kael's signature in Daria's graft is itself a guild function — there is no outside source. The trap is structurally airtight: the disease forces each replacement before a non-identity-overwriting alternative can be invented, and Kael's love prevents stopping before the spine. → SURVIVES — airtight.
  Attack 4 ONE-IMAGE: Eleven pieces of Daria's original skeleton in a steel dish, and Daria walking with Kael's slight-forward lean. → SURVIVES — genuinely unforgettable; the steel dish of displaced original bone is concrete, sensory, and carries the whole tragedy in one frame.
  Scores: C:5 D:5 S:5 O:4 A:5 TOTAL:24/25
  VERDICT: SURVIVES-ALL

P10 sisters-alternating-days — Round 4 (Tier 4 BESTEST, adversarial)
  Attack 1 GENERIC: The case: shared-body narratives (Vicious, various split-personality horror) are a established genre; one half sabotaging the other half's relationship is a recognized variant; the jealous sister who destroys her twin's love life is a standard domestic-melodrama engine. The handwriting-as-only-vector is clever packaging but the underlying drive — possessive sibling torpedoes romantic rival — is a trope the reader identifies immediately. → SURVIVES — but narrowly. The defense: the sabotage is anatomically undetectable (shared hand, shared face, shared voice), so there is no possible resolution by exposure — the usual exit from the jealous-sibling engine is absent. The tragedy is sealed not by malice-discovered but by Renne inheriting the grief she manufactured. The mechanism (forgery that cannot be forged) is what lifts this above the generic; the attack partially lands on O but does not kill it.
  Attack 2 NAIVE-FOLLOW: Two sisters share one body — Calla has odd days, Renne has even. They communicate through a shared notebook. Calla falls in love with a man named Dov. Renne, fearing she will wake up every day to someone else's emotional residue, starts sabotaging: she meets Dov coldly, returns his gift with a note in what is (anatomically) Calla's handwriting, then goes to his door and — using Calla's face and voice — tells him Calla ended it. Calla figures out it was Renne and stops writing to her. Renne stops writing back. Both women wake each day to a body that has been crying, with no one to explain why. → SURVIVES
  Attack 3 UNAVOIDABILITY: Could Renne simply tell Calla she finds Dov's presence unbearable and negotiate a shared-body agreement? The plan's internal rules establish that the notebook is voluntary — either sister can leave a page blank. The relationship IS the governing structure; there is no external arbitration. But: could Calla establish a formal shared-body contract through some institution? The plan does not indicate such a mechanism exists, and the scenario is deliberately domestic, not legal-systemic. However — the tragedy depends on Renne choosing sabotage over negotiation. A smarter Renne could write "I cannot bear Dov in our life" and force a conversation. The plan does not foreclose negotiation; Renne chooses not to attempt it. This is a character choice, not a structural trap. The premise's OWN RULES do not make the bad outcome unavoidable — Renne has agency to negotiate, and the plan does not give her a structural reason she cannot. → FALLS — the unavoidability attack lands. A smarter (or less frightened) Renne negotiates through the notebook and the tragedy is sidestepped. The plan depends on a character psychology that is plausible but not premise-forced.
  Attack 4 ONE-IMAGE: The shared notebook open on the nightstand, both sides finally going blank. → SURVIVES — vivid and precise.
  Scores: C:5 D:4 S:3 O:4 A:5 TOTAL:21/25
  VERDICT: FALLS(Attack 3 — unavoidability; S below floor)

P19 life-vote-senate — Round 4 (Tier 4 BESTEST, adversarial)
  Attack 1 GENERIC: The case: life-as-currency voting is a known SF conceit (variants appear across parliamentary-dystopia fiction); the "principled politician who spends herself to the bone for a cause she believes in" arc is a recognizable martyrdom story; the procedural-attrition enemy (forcing revotes to drain her) is a real-world political tactic transposed to a fantastical currency. The story is fundamentally a political martyrdom tale dressed in body-horror costuming, and the martyrdom structure — idealist consumes herself for a bill that passes as she dies — is old. → SURVIVES — the defense: the novel element is that the ENEMY'S weapon IS her virtue. The opposition does not need to defeat her argument; they need only to force her to keep winning, because winning costs her life. This is not martyrdom-as-sacrifice; it is martyrdom-as-the-only-available-political-move, and the mechanism (procedural drain that works because she is the most willing to spend) is genuinely novel in execution even if the shape looks familiar. The generic attack partially lands but does not kill because the mechanism-level wrinkle holds.
  Attack 2 NAIVE-FOLLOW: A 24-year-old senator in a legislature where votes are paid in years of one's own life signs away five years on a famine bill, visibly aging to look 29 by morning. Her opponent cannot beat her on the merits — so he forces procedural revote after procedural revote, draining her regardless of outcome. She spends fourteen years over six weeks of forced votes. Her physician's ledger is bribed to show fewer years remaining than she has. She litigates for a year. The ledger is restored; she has six months left. She signs them all on the final motion. The bill passes. She hears the applause from a wheelchair. → SURVIVES
  Attack 3 UNAVOIDABILITY: Could Elia simply not vote on the procedural motions? The plan's rules state votes are cast by signing a withdrawal slip — presumably signing is voluntary. Could she refuse to vote on procedural drain motions and husband her remaining life for the final bill? This is the key vulnerability. If she can strategically abstain on procedural traps, the attrition strategy fails. The plan does not establish that senators are compelled to vote. Her willingness to spend IS presented as her stated choice ("spends anyway") — but that makes it a character choice, not a structural trap. A politically savvy Elia abstains on procedural motions and forces the opposition to schedule a final vote. → FALLS — the unavoidability attack lands. The tragedy depends on Elia's willingness to spend on every procedural motion, but the plan does not establish that abstention is structurally prohibited. A smarter protagonist survives by refusing to be attritioned on motions that do not advance the bill.
  Attack 4 ONE-IMAGE: A 24-year-old studying a face that ages a decade in a bathroom mirror over forty seconds, then walking back to her seat. → SURVIVES — specific and startling.
  Scores: C:5 D:4 S:3 O:4 A:5 TOTAL:21/25
  VERDICT: FALLS(Attack 3 — unavoidability; S below floor)

P22 story-engine-girl — Round 4 (Tier 4 BESTEST, adversarial)
  Attack 1 GENERIC: The case: the "protagonist discovers the narrative is controlled / reality is written by a machine" story is thoroughly colonized — metafictional escape-from-the-story-engine is a genre unto itself (from The Neverending Story onward); the apprentice who discovers she lives inside a controlled text and learns to subvert it is recognizable; the "escape replicates the prison's logic" turn is the standard irony of the metafictional form. The story-engine conceit is not fresh; it is the dominant metaphor of a whole strand of literary-fantasy fiction. → SURVIVES — but narrowly. The defense: the mechanism is literal and physical (a compositor's type-block, a one-second window, a roller-pass that makes law). The escape is not metaphorical — she pushes on a wall and it gives because the vellum says a door is there. The legal-ontological dimension (courts treat unprinted events as non-existent) is specific enough that the attack does not fully land; the conceit is literalized in a way that most metafictional predecessors avoid.
  Attack 2 NAIVE-FOLLOW: A girl named Ink is an apprentice inside a room-sized machine that prints the official legal history of a kingdom. Whatever the machine prints is treated as having legally happened; whatever it doesn't print has no legal standing. She carves a type-block that says "passed through the east door" and swaps it into the typeset one second before the roller passes — and when the vellum prints that she walked through the east wall, the stone gives way into a door. Outside, she finds the resistance is itself a story the Editor is allowing to run. The Editor then prints her confession and voluntary return; the courts treat her as having confessed. She breaks back into the archive, swaps the confession block mid-roller, cuts her arm so the blank prints in blood, and escapes — but now she can't stop rewriting others. → SURVIVES
  Attack 3 UNAVOIDABILITY: Could Ink simply not swap any more blocks for the resistance and live quietly outside the archive with her door-block edit? The plan says the Editor prints her confession and the courts treat her as having confessed — so merely staying outside is already a legal trap she cannot escape without a counter-edit. She must re-enter or accept the legal fiction of her surrender. This forecloses the sidestep. The trap is structurally airtight: she cannot simply stop editing because the Editor edits her without her participation. → SURVIVES — airtight.
  Attack 4 ONE-IMAGE: Pushing a shoulder against solid stone that gives way into a door, because the vellum that just printed said one is there. → SURVIVES — concrete, strange, unforgettable; the literalized metaphor lands as a physical sensation.

P30 famine-saint — Round 4 (Tier 4 BESTEST, adversarial)
  Attack 1 GENERIC: The case: the self-consuming saint / miracle-worker who takes suffering into her own body is a hagiographic archetype present in Catholic martyr tradition, folktale, and fantasy (from Dostoevsky's suffering-as-sanctity to contemporary grimdark sacrifice arcs); the "gift that destroys the giver" engine is one of the most traveled roads in fantasy; a saint multiplying bread while her body fails reads as direct Saint-of-the-Poor iconography in a fantastical frame. The generic attack has real teeth here. → SURVIVES — but requires a precise defense. The defense is the plan's specific inversion: this is NOT martyrdom (sacrifice with awareness of cost) but parasitism (the hunger metabolizes inside her and grows, consuming her from within to sustain its own accumulation). The key line: "she became the hungriest thing inside the walls." A martyr is consumed by the miracle; this character is consumed BY the hunger ITSELF, which has taken on a kind of autonomous appetite. The self-amplifying loop (more cures → more hunger she absorbs → hunger eats her body faster → more cures demanded) is genuinely distinct from the martyr form. The attack partially lands but does not kill.
  Attack 2 NAIVE-FOLLOW: A woman with the power to multiply bread by absorbing the hunger of those she feeds arrives at a besieged city of twelve thousand people. Every time she multiplies food, she feels the weight of the hunger she just emptied — a cavity opening behind her ribs. At city scale, the cavity grows instead of filling. By week two she cannot sleep; by week three her hands shake; by week four her face has caved in and her ribs show. Guards hold her hands to the flour sack because she can no longer lift her arms. In the final scene, soldiers hold her up so she can touch a mountain of loaves, bread rises to the rooflines, and she collapses. She waits for the loaf to get smaller. It doesn't. → SURVIVES
  Attack 3 UNAVOIDABILITY: Could she simply leave, or ration her multiplications to prevent the hunger from accumulating past her body's capacity? The plan's rules establish that commanders station guards to prevent her leaving and hold her hands to the flour when she is too weak to act voluntarily. This is structural confinement. Could she have negotiated an exit before week two? In week one she is still eating double and sleeping four hours — she has agency. A smarter saint refuses the commanders' demands in week one, before confinement becomes physical. However — the plan establishes the trap as moral, not just physical: "she came to spare the city hunger" and "absorbing it willingly because watching it is worse." Her own nature prevents the sidestep. The catastrophe is built into what she is — a person who cannot watch hunger without absorbing it. The plan's S axis states this explicitly: "performing the final act because she cannot stop." → SURVIVES — the trap is the protagonist's own nature making refusal structurally impossible from her first act; the physical confinement ratifies what was already her own law.
  Attack 4 ONE-IMAGE: Holding a loaf afterward, not understanding she is waiting for it to shrink. → SURVIVES — the image of a woman whose body is consuming itself waiting for a loaf to get smaller is genuinely unforgettable; the cognitive dissociation (not understanding what she is waiting for) is the knife in the image.
  Scores: C:5 D:5 S:5 O:4 A:5 TOTAL:24/25
  VERDICT: SURVIVES-ALL

P03 healing-wasps (REWORKED) — Round 4 (Tier 4 BESTEST, adversarial)
  Attack 1 GENERIC: The case: the healer whose gift harms others is a recognized engine (the physician whose treatment creates dependence, the magical healer whose price is paid by bystanders — Ursula K. Le Guin's "The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas" is the ur-text of exactly this trade); the "cure erases the disease's reality" idea appears in trauma-and-forgetting fiction; a field surgeon with a supernatural healing mechanism is standard fantasy. The rework's addition (the surgeon wanted to erase his own failure, and the cure accomplishes exactly that) tightens the penitential angle but does not change the mechanism's genre address. → SURVIVES — the defense: the specific mechanism (memory of sickness transferred to a bystander in a medically mirrored way, the hive growing more potent per cure, the self-amplifying outbreak loop) is a genuine mechanical novelty. The Le Guin comparison lands on ethics, not on plot engine. The attack partially lands — the penitential-healer frame is recognizable — but the self-amplifying loop (each cure makes the village less able to recognize the next outbreak, accelerating the disease) is not the standard form.
  Attack 2 NAIVE-FOLLOW: A field-surgeon carries a hive of healing wasps in a brass-ported compartment in his chest. When he opens the port, wasps emerge, sting a patient's infection, and return; the patient heals within hours. But within half a day, one person within two miles permanently loses their clearest memory of being sick or seeing sickness — a gap they do not feel go. As he cures more people, the village loses its collective memory of the plague: first an innkeeper forgets her husband's fever week, then a son reaches mid-sentence for his dead father's name and finds nothing. A cured woman, having forgotten the danger, walks to the tainted well and drinks. Davan sees this happen. He closes the brass port and holds a frightened, curable man's hand while he dies, still remembering. → SURVIVES
  Attack 3 UNAVOIDABILITY: Could Davan simply leave the village, taking the hive with him, and stop curing? The plan establishes that the plague re-emerges in the water table — the village faces a genuine outbreak. Could he cure selectively, at a rate low enough that memory loss stays manageable? The hive "strengthens per cure" — but the plan does not establish a floor below which he could cure without causing village-wide amnesia. In the early rounds (cure 1, cure 2), the damage is local and specific; a smarter Davan stops at cure 2, identifies the memory-loss mechanism, and leaves. The plan does not establish structural confinement. The road-to-hell depends on Davan continuing to cure freely despite accumulating evidence of memory-loss cost. This is, like P10 and P19, a character choice (healing is unconditional good) rather than a structural trap that forecloses the sidestep. A smarter protagonist caps his cures early and departs. → FALLS — the unavoidability attack lands. The self-amplifying loop requires Davan to keep curing past the point where the pattern is visible; a cautious protagonist survives by stopping earlier.
  Attack 4 ONE-IMAGE: Rem reaching mid-sentence for his father's name, hand closing on nothing, the table going still as he gropes and finishes with a different word. → SURVIVES — precise, physical, genuinely terrible.
  Scores: C:5 D:4 S:3 O:4 A:5 TOTAL:21/25
  VERDICT: FALLS(Attack 3 — unavoidability; S below floor)

P32 weather-priest-invoice (REWORKED) — Round 4 (Tier 4 BESTEST, adversarial)
  Attack 1 GENERIC: The case: commodified sacred gifts vs. institutional exploitation is thoroughly mapped territory (Pratchett, Le Guin, Pullman's Magisterium arc); the "church/guild is actually a commercial operation" reveal is a standard institutional-disillusionment beat; a priest who discovers his faith was manufactured by the institution that benefits from it maps onto recognizable anti-clerical fiction. The rework's specific wrinkle (his ordination oath is printed verbatim as commercial IP in a charter predating the seminary) is a sharpening of the mechanism, but the genre address remains clear. → SURVIVES — the defense: the novel load-bearing element is not "guild vs. priest" but "faith-was-guild-property-at-origin" — the moment he reads his own ordination oath in a founding charter dated eighteen years before the first seminary is not the standard reveal (he discovers the institution is corrupt); it is the revelation that the sacred was commercial before he existed, that there was no prior state of purity he is being deprived of. His final act — calling rain as a revoked sublicensee, consciously committing the "theft" the guild always named — is genuinely novel because his deed and the guild's description of it exactly agree for the first time. The generic attack partially lands on the surface but not on the mechanism.
  Attack 2 NAIVE-FOLLOW: A weather-priest calls rain by speaking a frequency trained into him at ordination; the rain falls, and by morning a guild clerk delivers an invoice charging the village by the barometric measure for the water that fell. Each unpaid invoice converts to a land-transfer document. The priest refuses a lucrative city post and calls rain again; another field transfers. He goes to the registry to research the guild's charter and finds his own ordination oath — the exact phrasing, the chrism formula, the frequency — printed as Article 1, Clause 2 of a commercial document dated eighteen years before his seminary existed. He copies it by hand onto parish paper. He is deordained. On transfer day — when the village's fields are no longer theirs — he calls rain anyway, without license, in front of the guild factor, on land that no longer belongs to the people he serves. The rain falls. → SURVIVES
  Attack 3 UNAVOIDABILITY: Could the priest simply stop calling rain? The village is in drought; not calling rain means the village starves. Could he call rain on behalf of a wealthy patron who can pay the invoice, funneling income to the village? The plan does not foreclose this workaround — a smarter priest finds a paying client and cross-subsidizes the drought relief. However: the plan's structure rests on the theological trap. After discovering his oath is commercial IP, calling rain for a paying client would be accepting the guild's framing entirely — becoming the thing the revelation proved he already was. The plan establishes this as the moral irreversibility point: his fourth call is made knowing he is a revoked sublicensee committing the guild's named theft, and that is precisely what makes it the only authentic act available to him. The structural trap is theological-epistemic: once he knows, he cannot unknow, and acting within the guild's framework (the cross-subsidy workaround) is not available as an authentic choice. → SURVIVES — the trap is airtight at the level of the protagonist's own theology; the cross-subsidy workaround requires accepting the commercial framing the revelation makes impossible.
  Attack 4 ONE-IMAGE: At a standing desk in the registry hall, reading his own ordination oath — phrasing, chrism formula, frequency in the margin — dated eighteen years before the first seminary, and copying it by hand onto parish paper. → SURVIVES — this image is specific, physical, and devastating; the act of handwriting (copying by hand) performs the internalization in a concrete gesture.
  Scores: C:5 D:5 S:5 O:4 A:5 TOTAL:24/25
  VERDICT: SURVIVES-ALL

## BEST 3

Three plans survive all four attacks: **P01 bonewright-sister**, **P30 famine-saint**, and **P32 weather-priest-invoice (REWORKED)**.

All three score 24/25 with three axes at 5 and C = 5. The ranking is on tie-breaking criteria: O-axis sharpness, unavoidability airtightness, and the specific quality of the central image.

**#1 — P01 bonewright-sister**
The steel dish of Daria's displaced original bones is the single most viscerally unforgettable image of the seven; the epigenetic repatterning mechanism is the most genuinely original engine (no named predecessor); and the tragedy is structurally airtight in the most rigorously mechanical way — disease forces each replacement, guild oversight forecloses alternatives, Kael's love forecloses stopping. O is the one axis that could plausibly reach 5 where P30 and P32 cannot; it is the plan that most thoroughly defeats the generic attack rather than surviving it narrowly.

**#2 — P30 famine-saint**
The parasitic-hunger engine (hunger that metabolizes and grows rather than depleting the giver in a fixed-cost martyrdom) is genuinely inverted from its hagiographic predecessors, and the physical deterioration arc is the most dramatically concrete of the three. The image — a woman waiting for a loaf to get smaller, not understanding she is waiting — is more uncanny and surprising than P32's registry scene. Ranked below P01 because the generic attack lands harder (the martyr form is closer) and the O score is equivalent while the originality defense requires more work to articulate.

**#3 — P32 weather-priest-invoice (REWORKED)**
The registry discovery (ordination oath as Article 1, Clause 2, predating the seminary by eighteen years) is a perfectly staged dramatic revelation with a concrete physical action (copying by hand). The theological trap is the most intellectually rigorous of the three: the protagonist cannot use the cross-subsidy workaround once he knows what he knows. Ranked third because the genre address is the most recognizable of the three survivors — the attack on O lands harder than for P01 or P30, and the plan survives by mechanism-specificity rather than genuine unfamiliarity. The image is excellent but the strongest competitor image in this field (the steel dish, the loaf that won't shrink) edges it.
