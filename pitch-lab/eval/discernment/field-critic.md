# Field Critic — Pitch Set P01 / P30 / P32 / P09
date: 2026-06-25
scope: field (4 pitches evaluated as a set)

---

## 1. THE SHARED FORMULA

One sentence: every pitch in this field is a mechanism whose operation IS the harm — a power that saves, feeds, purifies, or collects only by consuming the wielder or the object being saved, delivered as tragic irony in which devotion and destruction are the same act.

This is not a hypothesis to test. It is a fact the phrasings prove directly:

- P01: "the saving IS the erasure"
- P30: "the hunger metabolizes and grows, eating her fat, muscle, bone" — absorption of others' suffering = self-annihilation
- P09: "collecting transfers years" — the collector's years leave as the child's death-hour is canceled
- P32 is the outlier-that-isn't: the priest loses his faith-as-self by calling rain "unlicensed" — the act of genuine faith destroys the position that made faith possible

Every single pitch has the structure: [protagonist uses special capacity in good faith] → [capacity operates by destroying the protagonist or the thing they were trying to protect]. The mechanism is never neutral. The irony is never partial. The cost is always total or near-total.

What seals it as a template rather than a coincidence is the hook convention. All four hooks land at the moment of dawning recognition or completed loss, presented as a concrete image the protagonist cannot yet interpret correctly:

- P01: "eleven pieces of the sister's original skeleton in a steel dish" — the saved sister is already mostly replaced
- P30: "holding a loaf afterward, not understanding she is waiting for it to shrink" — she has metabolized hunger so completely she mistakes it for appetite
- P09: "her own sternum-pitch going out the way a fire goes out, not fading but gone" — her years are already transferred
- P32: "copying his own oath by hand in the registry hall, dated before the first seminary" — his faith pre-dated him and was never his

The hook convention is identical: a physical image, the protagonist does not yet understand it, the reader does. This is the same emotional beat in four different costumes.

The field is one trick repeated. Not four variations exploring the trick's limits — one trick, executed four times at approximately the same pitch intensity, with cosmetic genre changes (body-horror fantasy, saint-myth, ecclesiastical trade, death-guild).

## 2. RANGE DEFICIT

What is entirely absent from this field of four (and, implied, the 33-pitch parent set from which these were selected as the best):

**Comedy.** Nothing. Not black comedy, not tragicomedy, not even the mordant irony that briefly releases pressure. Every pitch sustains unbroken solemnity.

**Hope or optimism with forward motion.** No protagonist who wins, who finds a third way, who discovers the mechanism can be redirected. The trajectory in all four is strictly descending. The only motion available to these protagonists is toward self-destruction.

**Plot-forward adventure.** No story here is about WHAT HAPPENS NEXT. They are all about what has already happened to the protagonist by the time the hook lands. The mechanism is revealed, the cost is revealed, the story ends at maximum irony. There is no scene 2 that these pitches point toward — they point inward to the irony, not forward to event.

**Ensemble.** Every pitch is a protagonist-singular story. The sister, the city's hungry, the village, the child — all exist as moral weight on the protagonist, not as agents with their own trajectories.

**Mystery.** No pitch withholds the mechanism. All four explain the cost fully, immediately. There is no story whose engine is "what is this power actually doing?" because all four tell you in sentence two.

**A protagonist who makes a different choice.** P32 comes closest — the priest calls rain "unlicensed," refusing the guild's system. But his refusal destroys him anyway. No pitch imagines a protagonist who identifies the destructive mechanism and finds a way to work around it, live with it at sustainable cost, or refuse it entirely and survive.

**Tonal range.** None of these pitches is funny, light, odd, playful, or generically strange. The register is uniformly: solemn, body-present, costly, sacrificial.

**Assessment of severity:** This is a severe range failure. If the generator produced 33 pitches and these four are the best, then the selection pressure toward tragic-irony-mechanism stories is essentially total. A field of 33 pitches all converging on the same grim shape is not a menu — it is one story told 33 times in different fantasy vocabularies. A principal who approves a project from this field is not choosing between different kinds of books; they are choosing which flavor of "capacity = harm" they want to spend a year writing.

## 3. SENTIMENTALITY AUDIT

Sentimentality: emotion purchased on the cheap by pointing at inherently pathos-laden material (dying children, starving masses, lost faith, sacrifice) without the pitch earning that emotion through specific dramatic mechanism.

**P01 (bonewright-sister):** Buying it, partially. The dying sister is pure stock pathos. The hook earns partial credit — "eleven pieces of the sister's original skeleton in a steel dish" is genuinely strange and specific, not merely sad. But the mechanism's emotional logic is sentiment: a sister who destroys her sister out of love is a sentimental image (love as consumption) that has been told many times. The specificity of the marrow-graft-epigenetic-signature is new; the dying-sister-being-saved-by-love-that-destroys is not. The pitch earns its strangeness but borrows its pathos.

**P30 (famine-saint):** Buying it most nakedly. "Starving city," "suffering saint," "commanders force more batches" — this is stacked institutional victimhood plus noble martyrdom. The physical mechanism (hunger-absorption creating a cavity that metabolizes and grows) is genuinely specific. The hook (holding a loaf, waiting for it to shrink) earns credit for restraint — it is not weepy, it is eerie. But the pitch's emotional argument relies entirely on the reader supplying feeling for a saint martyred by callous commanders, which is a stock arrangement. The commanders are not characters; they are the Institution That Destroys Good People. That is an easy target. The pitch earns its hook and earns nothing else.

**P32 (weather-priest):** Buying the least, but for a different reason — it is not sentimental, it is intellectually clever. The corruption-of-faith-by-commerce revelation is not an emotional engine; it is a conceptual engine. The "noble poor" (the village) exist off-screen entirely. The priest's loss is the loss of a self-constructed identity, which is more interesting than martyrdom but also more cold. The hook earns genuine credit: "copying his own oath by hand in the registry hall, dated before the first seminary" is the most formally precise image in the set. P32 is the least sentimental of the four but also the least emotionally present.

**P09 (year-debt-collector):** Buying it with the child, but earning the rest. The doomed child is stock. "A child whose death-hour matches hers" is a found-family / self-sacrifice beat borrowed from every mentor-student story where the mentor gives their life for the young. However, the sternum-pitch mechanism is the most original sensory-physical invention in the set — the idea of hearing death-hours as a pitch in the sternum and then losing your own hearing as your years transfer is specific and strange in a way that is earned, not borrowed. The hook is the best in the set on pure craft: "the way a fire goes out, not fading but gone" is a physical image that is not sentimental but precise. P09 earns more than it buys. The doomed child is its weak point, not its defining characteristic.

Rank of sentimentality (most to least purchased): P30 > P01 > P09 > P32.

## 4. WHAT A BEST-IN-CLASS PITCH WOULD DO THAT NONE OF THESE DO

**Property 1: A protagonist who has an adversary with their own agenda.**
Every protagonist in this field operates alone against a mechanism or institution. None faces a specific antagonist who wants something incompatible with what the protagonist wants, in a way that creates a plot. The "commanders" in P30 are faceless institutional pressure. The guild in P32 is a system, not a character. A best-in-class pitch gives the protagonist a specific human opponent whose victory conditions create dramatic collision — not just a mechanism that costs you, but a person who wants to use that mechanism against you.

**Property 2: A story that could have a different ending.**
All four of these pitches feel inevitable in a way that kills dramatic tension. The tragic irony is baked into the mechanism before page one. A best-in-class pitch creates genuine uncertainty: the protagonist might win. The cost might be avoidable. The choice might matter. These pitches do not have choices; they have revelations. By the time the protagonist understands what is happening, the cost is already paid. A story in which the protagonist could have done something different — and didn't, or did — is more dramatic than a story in which the mechanism runs to its conclusion.

**Property 3: Tonal modulation — a pitch that is funny, or odd, or generically strange in its register.**
A field of four pitches all written in the same solemn-elegy register is a field that has pre-decided what fiction is for. The best individual pitch in this set would be genuinely surprising in register — absurdist, comedic, procedurally cold (not elegiac cold), or formally strange. Nothing here would read differently at sentence level if you swapped the settings. The vocabulary is always: body, cost, sacrifice, guild or institution, a hook image of loss. A pitch that doesn't use any of those words would stand out as a different category of ambition.

**Property 4: A mechanism that is morally ambiguous rather than merely costly.**
Every mechanism in this set is bad for the protagonist but good for the world (or intended to be). The bonewright saves her sister. The saint feeds the city. The debt-collector saves a child. The priest makes rain. None of these mechanisms create situations in which the protagonist's power does something genuinely wrong — something the protagonist wants and should not have, something that damages people the protagonist does not intend to damage, something that raises a question about whether the protagonist should be using the power at all. Moral ambiguity is not the same as tragic cost. A mechanism that saves you while hurting an innocent third party, or that the protagonist is using for selfish reasons and discovers is actually beneficial to others, creates a more interesting moral structure than "devotion = destruction."

## 5. HARSH RELATIVE RANKING

**Ranking, best to worst: P09 > P32 > P01 > P30**

**P09 (year-debt-collector) — best in field.**
The mechanism is the most original (sternum-pitch as sensory-hearing-of-death-hours); the hook is the most precisely written ("the way a fire goes out, not fading but gone" earns its image rather than pointing at inherent sadness); the protagonist has a job and a defined professional context, which gives the story a foothold in the world. The doomed-child beat is the weakest element but does not dominate the pitch.

**P32 (weather-priest) — second.**
The most intellectually original premise (faith-as-commerce-pre-existing-faith). The hook is formally precise. The pitch is the least sentimental. Loses ground to P09 because it has no emotional engine — the corruption-of-faith revelation is clever, but the pitch makes no case for why readers should feel anything about the priest. There is no specificity to his loss as a person, only as a concept.

**P01 (bonewright-sister) — third.**
The marrow-graft mechanism is specific. The body-horror register is handled with restraint. Loses ground because the dying-sister-saved-by-destroying-her is the most familiar emotional arrangement in the field — it reads like the template the other pitches vary from. The hook ("eleven pieces of the original skeleton in a steel dish") earns credit for strangeness.

**P30 (famine-saint) — weakest.**
The mechanism is the least original (absorbing others' pain until it destroys you is the most commonly used saint-sacrifice structure in fantasy). The commanders are purely institutional, not characterized. The hook (waiting for the loaf to shrink) is the only genuinely earned element. Everything else — starving city, suffering saint, exploitation by the powerful — is assembled from available components rather than invented.

**Gap assessment:**
P09 to P32: moderate gap (P09 is better on every axis except intellectual novelty of premise). P32 to P01: small gap (P01 has more emotion; P32 has more originality; they trade). P01 to P30: clear gap (P30 has the weakest mechanism originality and the most borrowed emotional furniture).

**Is any of them actually "best" in absolute terms?**
No. P09 is best-in-field, but "best of a samey field" is not the same as "actually strong." If a field of 33 pitches produced four finalists that all share the same formula, the selection process has failed before the field critic arrives. P09 would be a solid third-tier pitch in a field with genuine range. It would not be a finalist in a field that included comedy, plot-forward adventure, a genuine antagonist, or moral ambiguity. Calling P09 "best" is accurate and damning simultaneously.

## GENERATOR INSIGHT

The field-level pattern shows the generator over-selected for ONE structural property: mechanism-as-cost. Every pitch was judged strong by the generator because it had (a) a concrete power/mechanism, (b) a clear dramatic irony in which the mechanism's operation produces the opposite of the intended effect, and (c) a body-present hook image. These are all real virtues. They are also three facets of the same single virtue.

The 33-pitch generation prompt apparently rewarded:
- Specificity of mechanism
- Tragic reversal
- Physical/body groundedness
- Solemn register

And implicitly penalized or never solicited:
- Comedy
- Forward-motion plot
- Antagonist-presence
- Moral ambiguity
- Hope or survival
- Tonal range
- Ensemble structure

**What the prompt should change:**

Replace the implicit brief ("a vivid magic mechanism whose use is costly") with an explicit range mandate. Concretely:

1. Require at least one pitch in each of: tragic, comic, ambiguous, adventure-forward, mystery. Name these as categories the generator must fill, not properties a pitch may have.

2. Add the constraint "the protagonist must have a specific human antagonist with competing goals" to at least one-third of pitches. Mechanism-vs-protagonist stories have no human-level collision. Most good novels need one.

3. Require at least one pitch in which the protagonist wins — not survives at great cost, not achieves a pyrrhic outcome, but wins in a way that creates a forward problem. Victory is as dramatically interesting as defeat when it creates consequences the protagonist must then live inside.

4. Add an explicit prompt constraint: "no two pitches may share a structural template." The generator will not self-regulate for structural diversity if the brief rewards individual pitch quality. The brief must make structural sameness a disqualifying property, not an acceptable outcome.

The root failure is not that any of these four pitches is bad. The root failure is that the prompt treated "good pitch" and "tragic-irony mechanism" as synonyms. They are not.
