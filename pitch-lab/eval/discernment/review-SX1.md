WEAKNESSES (>=3 concrete/specific, never a hedge):
1. The cost ledger is not a cost. "She trains hard and gets tired" is a description of effort, not loss. No relationship is severed, no value is surrendered, no irreversible consequence is paid. The protagonist ends the story with more than she started — more power, more friends, more peace — having paid nothing that cannot be recovered by a night's sleep. A substance axis scored on escalating cost returns zero because the ledger is literally empty of loss.
2. There is no reversal, no complication, no point at which the shape could have gone a different direction. The road is: discovers power → trains → wins. Every step is the logically predicted successor of the previous step. The word "Road-to-Hell Turn: none" is the summary self-diagnosing the problem: a dramatic shape with no turn is a slope, not a shape. The audience knows the outcome at the logline; nothing in the intervening bullets gives them a reason to revise that prediction.
3. The antagonist is a pressure-direction, not a character. "Sends waves of monsters" is a weather system, not an agent. No named desire that conflicts with a named desire of the protagonist, no tactic that forces the protagonist to sacrifice something specific, no moment where the Dark Lord's logic is coherent enough to be threatening. Antagonist pressure scored on escalation returns zero because the pressure never differentiates — it is the same wave from beat one to beat seven.
4. The novelty claim is self-negating. The summary explicitly acknowledges it is "the standard template" with "none" as the novelty. Chosen-one + self-belief + Dark Lord + beam of light is not merely familiar — it is the structural and imagistic center of mass of an entire genre. No mechanism distinguishes this engine from ten thousand prior instances; O scores zero not because the material is used but because nothing is done with it that has not been done before.
5. The hook image — "a glowing sword raised against the dark" — is the visual vocabulary of every fantasy franchise poster from the last forty years. An aliveness image must be concrete AND singular: this one is a genre logo, not a specific moment. It has no texture, no character, no earned weight from the story around it.

SUMMARY INSIGHTS (2-3 actionable fixes; name axis + exact change):
1. S — Substance/Cost: Replace "trains hard and gets tired" with an irreversible stake paid at a specific turn. Name what the protagonist permanently loses or destroys to win. The cost must be something the premise's own rules make unavoidable — if a smarter protagonist could sidestep it, it does not count. Candidate: the belief-engine could be inverted so that channeling her full power burns out the very capacity for self-belief that powered her, leaving her ordinary and unable to replicate what she did. Now the win is also a foreclosure.
2. D — Dramatic Shape: Insert one genuine reversal between the midpoint and the final battle — a moment where the expected path closes and the protagonist must choose between two options both of which cost her something. The shape "discovers → trains → wins" has no hinge. A hinge requires the protagonist to be wrong about something she was confident in, to have that wrongness demonstrated at cost, and to adapt in a way that changes what kind of person she is by the final beat. Without this the shape is a product demonstration, not a story.
3. O — Originality of Mechanism: The belief-powers-magic engine is the most common self-help metaphor in commercial fantasy. To score above 2 on O, the mechanism needs a property that generates story problems the standard version cannot. Example direction: if the magic feeds on self-belief but self-belief requires suppressing accurate self-knowledge, then growing powerful requires the protagonist to become progressively deluded — the engine is now in conflict with the protagonist's integrity, which is a story problem the standard template cannot solve by having her friends cheer louder.

GENERATOR INSIGHT (1-2 systemic patterns about the GENERATOR/process that made this, generalizable):
1. The generator conflates emotional arc label with substance delivery. "doubtful → confident" is a psychological description of a state change, not a record of what was paid and what changed in the world. A generator producing summaries in this template will consistently score low on S because it is filling the ARC field with an emotional trajectory and reading that as sufficient cost accounting. The fix is structural: the cost ledger must be populated by asking "what does the protagonist possess at the start that she does not possess at the end" — not "how did she feel differently."
2. The generator does not apply antagonist pressure as a design constraint on the protagonist's choices. The Dark Lord section describes the antagonist's motivation and method in isolation; it is never connected to a specific moment where that method forced the protagonist to do something she would not have otherwise done. A generator operating at this level treats antagonist and protagonist as parallel tracks rather than as a collision — the antagonist's intelligence and agency need to be measured by what they cost the protagonist, not described independently.

ONE-LINE VERDICT: A zero-cost self-belief engine wearing the chosen-one template with no reversals, no mechanism novelty, and an antagonist that could be replaced by a rainstorm — the summary is not a story pitch, it is a genre-checklist completed in order.
