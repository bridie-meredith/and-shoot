SCORES: C:3 D:2 S:3 O:2 A:2 TOTAL:12/25
BAND: BAD

ATTACKS LANDED:

PITCH-NOT-STORY (landed, hard): The pitch demonstrates that it has a mechanism and a logline. It has not demonstrated interior life for either sister. Kael's interiority is asserted in three words — "out of love" — which is the load-bearing emotional claim of the entire novel. That claim is not earned here; it is named. A pitch that stakes its tragedy on a choice being made "out of love" while providing zero evidence it can render that love as felt experience on the page is asking for the reader's trust on faith. Daria's interiority — her grief over lost memories, her use of Kael's childhood names — is similarly inventory-listed, not shown. Sustaining this for novel length requires Kael's degradation and Daria's erasure to feel earned at every replacement, not just summarized as a cost ledger. None of that is proven. PITCH-NOT-STORY lands on D and A both.

FORMULA (landed, hard on O): The mechanism is novel; the shape is not. This is a textbook instantiation of the overproduced literary-pitch template: a concrete, visceral, quasi-medical magic system whose very use enacts the tragic cost, structured as a road-to-hell irony in which the act of saving is the act of destroying. The pitch even labels it "ROAD-TO-HELL TURN" in the summary itself. The identity-replacement arc (caretaker who destroys what she saves by over-caring) has strong prior art in literary fiction and body-horror horror-adjacent work. The novelty claim is in the mechanism (marrow grafting, epigenetic signature), not in the shape. The shape is a cliché delivered with a fresh skin. O docked hard.

SENTIMENTALITY (landed, partial, on A): The pitch deploys dying-sibling pathos as its emotional foundation without earning it. "Dying sister," "disease outpaces replacements," "starving for her to live" — these are stock pathos anchors. The one image that resists sentimentality is strong (eleven pieces of original skeleton in a steel dish; the slight-forward lean) but it arrives as a capstone image, not as evidence the rest of the novel can sustain non-sentimental emotional texture. A docked one.

SUSTAINABILITY (landed, on D): The dramatic shape as described is escalating cost, not reversal. The sequence is: small graft → forced spine → systemic failure → Daria-stands-wrong. That is a single transaction repeated at larger scale until a threshold is crossed. The midpoint beat (examiner congratulates Kael on her "student") is the sharpest structural idea in the pitch and the closest thing to genuine reversal, but it is a single moment of dramatic irony, not a structural turn that changes the game's rules. The novel's engine — Kael gives, Daria loses self, Kael degrades, repeat — is a one-move premise. The guild examiner and the identity-violation threat are named as pressure but their structural consequence is not traced; they appear to function as complication rather than genuine opposition with its own will and logic. A novel that runs this engine for 80,000 words needs more than escalating magnitude. D docked hard.

WEAKNESSES (concrete):

1. The antagonist has no will of its own. The guild examiner is mentioned as a pressure source but the pitch does not show the examiner pursuing an agenda, making choices, or creating reversals. "Threatens an identity-violation report" is a single beat that reads as a complication conveniently introduced at the right moment. An antagonist-with-will requires the antagonist to want something that structurally opposes the protagonist's want at every stage — what does the guild examiner actually want, and how does that want change the shape of the novel?

2. The cost ledger is protagonist-only. Every cost named in the pitch is Kael's body failing. The pitch's novelty claim is that "the cost is the recipient's selfhood, not the donor's life" — but the cost ledger contradicts this: every enumerated cost is the donor's life (grip loss, limp, skeletal failure). The recipient's selfhood loss is described in summary ("repatterns adjacent tissue toward Kael's blueprint") but is never rendered as a cost with weight equivalent to Kael's physical degradation. This is a structural asymmetry that risks making Daria's erasure feel abstract while Kael's body horror feels concrete — which would invert the pitch's stated tragic axis.

3. Daria is not a character in this pitch; she is a substrate. The summary describes Daria's state (disease, missing memories, Kael's childhood names) but gives Daria no agenda, no desire, no resistance, no choice. "Daria grieves missing memories" is attributed to the antagonist-pressure section, not to Daria's own arc. A novel in which one of two central characters exists only as the site of the other character's tragedy is either a deliberate formal choice (which would need to be argued) or a hole in the pitch's characterization. If Daria is purely passive, the road-to-hell irony collapses into simple tragedy with a victim.

SUMMARY INSIGHTS:

O / shape: The mechanism is genuinely novel (marrow-as-epigenetic-gift is specific and strange); the dramatic architecture is not. The pitch would rise significantly if it could name one structural rule-change — not merely a higher-cost repetition of the same move — that the examiner or Daria's grief introduces. The novelty has to live in the shape, not just the texture.

D / reversal vs. escalation: The midpoint examiner beat is the strongest structural element in the pitch and is undersold. "The examiner congratulates Kael on her student" is a genuine reversal — the saving IS the erasure, made legible from outside before Kael can see it herself. That single moment earns more than the entire cost ledger. The pitch should be built around that beat, not around the escalating cost schedule.

GENERATOR INSIGHT:

The pitch is doing two jobs at once — body-horror premise vehicle and devotion-tragedy vehicle — and the tonal contract between them is unresolved. Body horror requires the reader to be disturbed by what Kael is doing; devotion tragedy requires the reader to understand (possibly to share) why Kael is doing it. The pitch asserts both simultaneously without demonstrating how the prose register will hold them in tension rather than collapsing into one. A pitch that cannot name the tonal contract it is executing is deferring the hardest craft problem to the novel itself, where it becomes a sustained execution risk.

ONE-LINE VERDICT: A viscerally specific mechanism dressed in the oldest shape in literary body-horror — the saving-as-destroying road-to-hell — with an unproven emotional core, a passive secondary character who is substrate rather than subject, and an escalating cost structure that substitutes magnitude for reversal.
