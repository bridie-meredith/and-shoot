WEAKNESSES (>=3 concrete/specific, never a hedge):
1. The engine is a tautology, not a mechanism. "A console that edits the code of reality" names the abstraction twice without specifying what it costs to use, what it cannot do, what breaks when it is misused, or what physical action pressing enter produces in the world. A naive reader cannot reconstruct a single concrete scene from this description. The engine should generate scenes; this one generates nothing but the words "magic" and "code."
2. The cost ledger is broken at the root. "Might delete him too, but it works out fine" is the author announcing that no cost is actually paid. Substance requires an escalating cost that the protagonist cannot sidestep by being clever or lucky; the summary explicitly states the stakes resolve without consequence. There is no tragedy unavoidable by the premise's own rules — the smarter reading of the premise is that a genius who wrote the code could simply exempt himself. S = 0.
3. The antagonist is a placeholder, not a force. "A corrupted AI virus wants to delete everything" tells us nothing about how it pressures the protagonist specifically, why it cannot simply be shut off, what it has already deleted, or what the protagonist loses when it acts. An antagonist that "wants to delete everything" exerts no particular pressure on this particular story; substitute any generic villain and the sentence is unchanged.
4. The road-to-hell turn is self-reported as absent. The summary explicitly says "none; the theme is stated outright." A pitch that announces its own flatness is not in tension with the scale — it has no claim above 0 on the D axis. Real dramatic shape requires a moment where the protagonist's core strategy is exposed as wrong; here, the protagonist's strategy (trust friends, reboot) works on the first try with no reversal.
5. The aliveness image — "a glowing screen of green code" — is the establishing shot of The Matrix (1999), not an image native to this story. It is borrowed iconography that has been reproduced ten thousand times since; it provides zero information about what is specific or unforgettable about this world.

SUMMARY INSIGHTS (2-3 actionable fixes; name axis + exact change):
1. S-axis: Rewrite the cost ledger so the reboot permanently and irreversibly destroys something the protagonist values before the problem is solved, not as a near-miss. The cost must be paid in the summary itself — not deferred to "might." Name what is gone at the end.
2. D-axis: Add one specific reversal that invalidates the protagonist's initial plan. The shape currently reads as a straight line from problem to solution. Identify the moment where the friendship strategy appears to have failed — what does the protagonist do when the friends are not enough — and make that the narrative hinge, not the resolution.
3. O-axis: Replace the mechanism. "Edits the code of reality" is a skin on "magic wand." The mechanism needs a concrete physical constraint unique to this premise — what can the console do that nothing else can, and what is the one class of thing it categorically cannot fix? That constraint is where the story lives.

GENERATOR INSIGHT (1-2 systemic patterns about the GENERATOR/process that made this, generalizable):
1. The generator is conflating theme-statement with dramatic shape. Every field in this summary describes what the story is about rather than what physically happens and what it costs. A generator producing pitches this way will consistently score near-zero on S and D regardless of genre, because the output format rewards summary-of-meaning over event-sequence-with-price-tags. Prompting for "what does the protagonist lose and when" and "what action fails before the final action succeeds" as mandatory fields would force event-level concreteness.
2. The generator is pattern-matching to recognized IP clusters (Matrix + chosen-one + power-of-friendship) without identifying the mechanism that made those sources work in the first place. The result is logo without logic. A generator that must name the specific constraint or rule that makes the antagonist unbeatable-by-default approach would interrupt the IP-blending pattern before it reaches the output.

ONE-LINE VERDICT: A premise that explicitly announces it has no reversals, no paid cost, and borrowed imagery has done the critic's job for them — there is no story here, only genre furniture arranged in familiar order.
