WEAKNESSES (>=3 concrete/specific, never a hedge):
1. The aliveness image undershoots the engine's capacity. "Eleven pieces of Daria's original skeleton in a steel dish" is strong inventory but it is static — a still-life, not an action. The pitch's own engine is kinetic (marrow drawn through skin in a bright thread, a graft knitting to nerve over hours) yet the designated hook image is a tray of discards. The most memorable image a naive reader will take away should be the process itself mid-act, or the sister walking with the exact wrong lean at the worst possible moment — the dish is aftermath, not witness. A scores 3 because the image is apt but not unforgettable; a better image is clearly nameable.

2. The antagonist pressure does not escalate proportionally with the body-cost ledger. The guild examiner threatens an identity-violation report at what the shape implies is the midpoint, then disappears from the listed beats. The disease is a pressure source but it is mechanical (it outpaces; it forces). Neither antagonist force tightens in the third act alongside the spine decision. The road-to-hell turn — Kael chooses erasure — is driven entirely by interior moral collapse, with no external force that makes refusal equally impossible. A well-constructed pressure system would have the examiner's threat become a reason Kael CANNOT stop (e.g., stopping mid-graft at the spine stage would leave Daria in a worse state than completing it, or Kael's own skeletal failure means if she stops now she can never restart). As written, the antagonist track and the cost track run parallel rather than braiding. This weakens D because the reversal feels elected rather than forced.

3. The identity-replacement mechanism is stated as a biological fact (epigenetic signature repatterns adjacent tissue) but the summary does not show it in conflict with itself at the dramatic level. The pitch tells us Daria "loses track of which memories are hers" and "asks a question Daria wouldn't ask" — but these are symptomatic reports, not scenes where the mechanism produces a genuinely surprising result that the premise's own rules made inevitable. For S to score 5, the tragedy must be unavoidable by the premise's own logic AND the smarter protagonist cannot sidestep it. Here, a smarter Kael could hypothetically have grafted non-identity-bearing tissue (a non-epigenetic substitute) — the summary does not close that escape hatch. The guild examination subplot actually hints that other bonewright approaches exist, which softens the inevitability. The summary needs one sentence closing the "but couldn't she have used someone else's marrow, or a synthetic?" hole, or building into the shape why that was tried and failed before the spine.

SUMMARY INSIGHTS (2-3 actionable fixes; name axis + exact change):
1. A-axis: Replace the designated hook image with an action-image that fuses process + consequence in a single physical moment. The current image (dish of bones) is a result. A candidate: Kael shapes a vertebra on the bench and realizes she is shaping it with her own habitual slight-forward tilt — the same lean Daria now walks with — and the tool in her hand is one she can no longer grip cleanly because the hand graft already took the grip. One image, three costs, the mechanism caught in the act.

2. D-axis + S-axis: Braid the examiner's threat into the spine decision so that stopping is not an option. If the examiner tells Kael that a half-replaced spine is legally an identity violation already — the damage is done, only completion makes Daria a legal person rather than an evidence file — then Kael's choice to continue is not an act of love overriding ethics but an act of love with no ethical exit, which is categorically more devastating. This also closes the "smarter protagonist sidetracks" escape route the summary currently leaves open.

3. S-axis: Add one clause to the mechanism statement that eliminates the synthetic-or-other-donor escape. The summary must explain (in one sentence) why Kael's own marrow is the only viable source for Daria specifically — rejection risk, blood-signature compatibility, the guild's licensing structure, anything that the world's rules make true. Without that sentence, the tragedy reads as a choice rather than a trap, and S cannot reach 5.

GENERATOR INSIGHT (1-2 systemic patterns about the GENERATOR/process that made this, generalizable):
1. The generator front-loads mechanism clarity at the expense of mechanism-in-crisis. The ENGINE section is the strongest part of this summary: it renders the process physically and concretely. But downstream sections (SHAPE, HOOK) revert to inventory and symptom report rather than the mechanism caught failing or contradicting itself. This is a systemic pattern: the generator explains the engine well but does not put the engine under stress in the summary's own language. The fix: require the SHAPE and HOOK sections to demonstrate the mechanism failing or reversing, not just producing outputs.

2. The antagonist-pressure section is structurally isolated from the cost-ledger section. Each is internally coherent but they do not reference each other's escalation. This is a generator-level architecture problem: antagonist pressure and protagonist cost are authored in separate passes with no forcing function that requires them to synchronize. The result is parallel tracks that escalate independently. The generator should be required to show, for each major cost entry, which antagonist force made that cost unavoidable at that moment — and for each antagonist escalation, which protagonist cost it was responding to.

ONE-LINE VERDICT: A mechanism this good deserves a trap this airtight — the pitch earns its GOOD band on engine and moral shape but leaves one escape hatch open and buries its best image in a dish of discards.
