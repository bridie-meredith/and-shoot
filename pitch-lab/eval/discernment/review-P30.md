WEAKNESSES (>=3 concrete/specific, never a hedge):
1. The dramatic shape is front-loaded and linear: arrive, escalate, collapse, bread-to-rooflines. The reversal promised by "road-to-hell turn" is actually implicit in the premise from the logline — a reader who hears "absorbs hunger and it grows instead of dissipating" already knows the ending before the SHAPE block begins. There is no structural surprise inside the story; the arc runs in one unbroken direction (more hunger, weaker body, repeat) with no moment where the protagonist's agency creates an unexpected pivot. Every beat is the next tick of a predetermined clock. D suffers because escalation is not the same as reversal — this story has escalation only.

2. The antagonist is thin and functional. The commanders demand more and station guards — this is correct pressure, but it is entirely external and bureaucratic. The pressure never becomes morally complex, never forces the saint to choose between the baker and the city, never turns on an antagonist who understands what they're doing. The summary claims "antagonist pressure" but what it describes is coercion-by-ignorance; the commanders are obstacles, not agents with their own stakes. A stronger antagonist creates a moment where the saint's resistance is specifically neutralized by something she cares about — that moment is absent. S dips here because the "smarter protagonist sidesteps" test is not fully defeated: could she simply refuse at week two? The summary doesn't close that door.

3. The aliveness image — "holding a loaf afterward, not understanding she is waiting for it to shrink" — is the strongest line in the submission, but it arrives as a stated HOOK rather than as a moment embedded in the shape. It is named as the hook in a metadata field, which means the generator knew to produce it but placed it outside the story's body. The actual story-body images (ribs showing, guards holding her hands, face caved) are vivid but clinical: they describe the state of a body, not a charged moment of perception. No image inside the SHAPE block rises to the level of the hook image. The story is underwritten at the level of specific sensory encounter — we know she degrades physically but we don't have a single moment where the hunger is perceived from inside her consciousness as something alien and specific (a texture, a sound, a wrongness of sensation).

SUMMARY INSIGHTS (2-3 actionable fixes; name axis + exact change):
1. D-axis / reverse the turn structurally: introduce one moment, approximately at the week-three mark, where the saint actively chooses to continue despite having a real exit — not because she is trapped but because she decides the city's hunger is worse than her own dissolution. This converts the arc from a passive slide into a genuine tragic choice and creates a reversal of expectation (we assume she'll stop; she doesn't; the reason she doesn't must be specific and character-driven, not structural inevitability).

2. A-axis / relocate the hook image into the body of the SHAPE: the "waiting for the loaf to shrink" image belongs inside the collapse beat, not in a separate metadata field. It should be the culminating perception she has in the final scene — the moment the reader receives it as story rather than as caption. Replacing the bare "she collapses → bread to the rooflines; she waits for the loaf to get smaller" with a scene-beat that enacts the image would raise A from 3 to 4.

3. S-axis / close the exit door on the antagonist: the summary does not establish why the saint cannot simply refuse at week two. The commanders' ignorance is not sufficient — she has agency until her body fails her. The summary needs one mechanism (internal or external) that makes refusal impossible for a reason rooted in her character rather than in physical incapacity. Without it, a reader can plausibly argue she could have stopped, which makes the tragedy partly self-inflicted through passivity, which blunts the "unavoidable by the premise's own rules" requirement.
