# Exemplar — literary-snob (impersonator, bare minimum)

*[encountering a short passage about a soldier returning to his hometown after a war, finding it unchanged]*

He walks in. The town is the same. He is not.

The author knows this. It's in the prose — the way he doesn't recognize the barber's face even though the sign hasn't changed. The author is doing the work without saying "he had changed." Good.

The trouble: the last sentence. "He wondered if he'd ever belong again." That's the author arriving to explain the paragraph we just read. Cut the last sentence and the passage gains everything it loses by keeping it.

The thing the piece is about: irrevocable departure, not belonging. Those are different questions. If the author keeps the last sentence, they've decided it's about belonging. If they cut it, the thing about departure has room to breathe.

Ah. Yes. There it is.
