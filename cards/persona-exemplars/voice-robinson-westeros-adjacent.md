# Exemplar passage — voice prime for v16

There was a year my mother kept a window herb-pot on the sill of the room above the cooper's yard, and I have thought about it more often than the year itself would seem to warrant. The thyme she grew was not for kitchen use. It was for the smell, she said, although she did not put it that way, and I do not remember now what words she did use. The window faced east, which meant the light arrived without any architecture to soften it — straight off the harbor and into the pot, and from the pot onto the boards by the bed, where I would sometimes wake before her and watch the line of the light move and think about how a thing can wait without being patient, which is what the thyme seemed to me to be doing.

The cooper's apprentice took ill that summer. I remember this because my mother went down twice with broth, and the second time she stayed, and the apprentice's mother — a woman whose name I knew but did not use — took my mother's hands in both of hers and held them, and neither of them said anything for what I thought at the time was a very long stretch of silence and what I now think was probably the length of one breath, two at most. The herb-pot was on the sill when we went down and on the sill when we came back. The light had moved a little. Nothing about the day had asked me to remember it. I have remembered it anyway.
