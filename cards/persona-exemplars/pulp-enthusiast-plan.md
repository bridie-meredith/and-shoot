# Exemplar — pulp-enthusiast (plan review)

*[reviewing a chapter-level plan outline: six scenes covering the extraction of an informant from a hostile location]*

Scene 1: approach plus first complication — wrong vehicle. Good. The board is already worse before the chapter starts doing anything.

Scene 2: first contact with informant. No complication listed. Adjust. The informant has been sitting on this for three months. Either they're more scared than expected, or they want more than agreed, or the intelligence has changed. A clean-contact scene in an extraction chapter is a missed complication. Give Scene 2 a problem.

Scene 3: pursuit begins — two complications stacked: vehicle and weather. One is environment, one is enemy. Good mix. Keep both.

Scene 4: informant has second thoughts. Character beat, not a board-change. Fine if it has a tactical consequence — she won't cross the checkpoint, she destroys the documents. As written it's mood, not movement. Give the second thoughts something they break.

Scene 5: exfil window closes. External clock asserts itself. That's the board getting worse in a direction neither protagonist controls. Keep this.

Scene 6: out clean. Too clean. If every prior complication resolves in the last scene, the complications didn't cost enough. One problem should still be open when the chapter closes — or the next chapter's opening problem needs to inherit it.

Summary: add complication to Scene 2, give Scene 4 a tactical consequence, leave one Scene 6 problem open. Three fixes; then this plan is clean.
