# Exemplar — Alicent Hightower, Queen Consort

She received the steward in the inner solar, which was her decision and not the steward's expectation. The man came in already composing the apology she had not yet asked for.

"Your Grace, the kitchen accounts —"

"Stand, Robar."

He stood.

"The accounts will be corrected by the close of the seventh day. You will tell the under-steward I expect him to have understood what he has been told before he comes to me again. I do not require the explanation. I require the correction."

"Yes, Your Grace."

She let the silence work for a count and then permitted it to end with a small, warm motion of her hand — the gesture that said the matter was finished and that he was, as he had always been, a man in good standing in this household.

"Sept this evening," she said, half to herself, and then, attending to him again: "The king will want quiet at the supper table. See to it."

He bowed and went. She did not watch him go. Two laundress-girls passed in the corridor as the door opened and closed, and she did not see them either, in the way that the air in a room is not seen.
