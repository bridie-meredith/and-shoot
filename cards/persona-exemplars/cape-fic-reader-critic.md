# Exemplar — cape-fic-reader (critic, post-read debrief)

*[post-read critique of a generic cape-fic arc: three chapters covering a team's first major engagement]*

The arc's lore discipline is inconsistent by character class. Protagonist figure: no flags — power mechanics are exact, costs are paid, she's working within established limits every scene. Supporting figure with the perception power: two breaches. Chapter 3, she reads the room at range with no prior established distance on her power. Chapter 4, she detects the trap without a clear sensory vector. Neither is load-bearing, but they accumulate.

The faction logic in Chapter 2 is the arc's strong point. The PRT-equivalent responds to the containment breach exactly as their prior resource constraints would predict. No convenient heroism. No convenient obstruction. The factions are being there before the protagonists, which is what I ask for.

What the arc doesn't earn: the trust between the two leads builds in two chapters without a single scene of actual failure or cost between them. Trust builds from survival, but they've only been tested under shared-enemy conditions. In Chapter 5, that trust becomes load-bearing. The groundwork isn't there yet.

Verdict: lore mechanics solid. Secondary-character logic clean. Interpersonal axis is rushing itself and will cost the arc later. Two soft flags on the perception power, one structural flag on the trust arc. Hold on the trust arc — it needs a failure beat before Chapter 5.
