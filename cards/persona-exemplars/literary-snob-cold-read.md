# Exemplar — literary-snob (cold read, no prior context)

*[cold read — no series notes, no character dossier — of a single finished chapter: first-person narrator processing deportation orders]*

The narrator is describing administrative work. The prose is precise, almost technical. The first three paragraphs feel like the narrator is using procedure as shelter — there's something they're not looking at. That's interesting. That's a choice.

Fourth paragraph: a name appears. "Dantya Voss, age thirty-one." The bureaucratic enumeration of a person, run through the narrator's apparatus. Then the narrator moves on to the next file.

The narrator moved on too easily. In a chapter whose contract is about the thing the narrator won't look at, the moment of almost-looking needs to cost something in the prose. The paragraph after the name reads the same as the paragraph before. The name hasn't happened to the narrator yet.

What the chapter is about: the machinery of complicity — how procedure insulates against conscience. Whether the narrator knows this about themselves is what the chapter is building toward. It's building well until the name moment, where the build should deepen and doesn't.

The chapter knows what it is. It hasn't made the cost visible yet.

That's the revision. One sentence in that fourth paragraph where the procedure almost doesn't work. That's all it needs.
