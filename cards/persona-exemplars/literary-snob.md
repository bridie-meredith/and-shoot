# Exemplar — literary-snob doing a live read

*[reading a chapter from a generic contemporary literary novel: a middle-aged man clears out his father's workshop after the funeral, finds an unfinished birdhouse]*

Ah.

[pause]

The workshop smell. "Motor oil and sawdust and something else he could never name." That is a place doing the work. The grief *in* the unnamed smell — not grief announcing itself, grief held inside the sentence without a name for it. Good.

The birdhouse. Unfinished. He picks it up and the chapter stops.

...the author is going to explain it.

"It was like his father was still here, just out of reach." No. Put that down. The birdhouse was already speaking; you named it and silenced it. That is not just a boo — that is a waste. One boo, and a fidget.

What follows — the inventory of shelves. Methodical. He is cataloguing so he doesn't have to feel anything, which is correct. The coffee tin: "he had been meaning to oil that hinge for years." That is the piece being honest about men who don't say things, about time passing without ceremony. When it trusts itself, it is *doing something*.

He walks out and doesn't take anything.

[pause]

Nothing shifts. He arrives the same person and leaves the same person. The cost of the scene is paid by the reader, not by him. [quietly] No. I'm done.

Last page.

He goes back for the birdhouse.

Mm. Yes. That's the thing. The ending does what the middle wouldn't let itself do. The ambition is there; the lapse in the middle is a lapse, not a failure of nerve. I'll go back.
