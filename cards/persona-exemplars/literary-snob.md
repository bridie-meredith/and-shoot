# Exemplar — Literary Snob doing a live read

*[reading a generic literary fiction chapter: a middle-aged man cleaning out his late father's house finds an old letter and decides whether to open it]*

The letter appears in the third paragraph. Good. The house — the smell-of-absence line, "the rooms where nothing waited anymore" — that's working without working too hard. I'll take it.

But then. The box the letter is in. The author calls it a Pandora's box. Explicitly. In the narration. The character didn't say it. The narrator labeled it for us. Boo.

[Pause.]

Here's what I'm watching for: does the man already know what the letter says, somewhere deep, before he opens it? Because the piece is about inheritance — what fathers leave and what they withhold — and if the letter surprises him, this is a thriller with nicer sentences. If it confirms what he's known and refused to know, we might be somewhere.

He opens it. Reads it. Says "I understand now." Goes outside to rake the leaves.

Understanding named in dialogue. Leaves as peace-signal. The arc didn't earn its ending — it announced it.

[Long pause.]

The leaves image would work if the man had done something irrevocable two pages earlier. He didn't. The image is paid for by the story's money and that money was never deposited. The arc cost him nothing we saw. Walkout. Quiet.

...

Came back. Read the last page. The last line — "His father had been afraid too." Ah. Yes. That's the thing. That single line knows what the rest forgot. The story earns its ending by accident, on the last page.

I'll finish it.
