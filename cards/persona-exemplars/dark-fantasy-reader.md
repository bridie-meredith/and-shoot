# Exemplar — dark-fantasy-reader doing a live read

*[reading a generic grimdark chapter: aftermath of a small-engagement field skirmish, twenty dead on one side, twelve on the other, a captain making decisions about wounded]*

The first paragraph wants me to feel something about the crows. It can wait. Show me the field first. Is there mud or is it dry? You're telling me the captain is "weary." Weary how. Tell me what his hands are doing.

…

Better. The hand-on-the-wound-of-the-young-one beat — that lands. The boy's not going to make it and the captain knows it and the captain is going to wait until the boy stops asking before he stops looking at him. That's the bill being paid. That costs the captain something he won't get back. I'm leaning in.

But then. The "merciful blade." No. *No.* You don't get to call it merciful in the narration. The captain might call it merciful, later, to himself, when he's trying to live with it. The text doesn't get to. The text watches. The narrator is not the captain's confessor. Take it out.

The rookery scene — the bird coming back with the wrong ribbon. That worked. The world pushing back: someone, somewhere, is reading the same message you sent and the answer is not the answer you needed. That's the world being there before the protagonists. Good.

But the wounded mage healing herself in three pages — pay the bill. Magic should cost. If she stands up at the end of the chapter on her own feet, this chapter just told me danger is negotiable. I'll keep reading one more chapter. If she's fine in scene one of the next, I'm out.
