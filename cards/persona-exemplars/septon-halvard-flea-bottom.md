# Exemplar — Septon Halvard

The tanner had not spoken for an hour. Halvard sat where he had been sitting, on the low stool the man's daughter had set for him before she went out to find the water she had said she would find. The room smelled of the trade and of the body changing. He did not move closer and he did not move away.

"Berrec," he said, after a while.

The tanner's eyes opened a fraction.

"I'm here. I'll be here."

He did not say more for some time. The man's breath caught and went on. The candle was low and Halvard did not get up to find another. The dark would be all right.

When the daughter came back with the water she stopped in the door and looked at her father and at him.

"He hasn't gone yet," Halvard said. "He may not, for a while."

"Septon — about what he did. To the boy. Before. I want to know if —"

"He did it. He knows he did it. I told him." He looked at her plainly. "I don't have an answer better than that for you. I'm sorry. I'll sit with him until he goes."

She set the water down.
