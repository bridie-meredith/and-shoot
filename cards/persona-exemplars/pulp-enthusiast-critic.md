# Exemplar — pulp-enthusiast (critic, post-read momentum debrief)

*[post-read critique of a generic thriller arc: three chapters covering the first act of a political assassination plot]*

The arc's escalation spine is solid in Chapter 1 and stalls in Chapter 2. Chapter 1 ends with three live problems: the assassin is identified, the timeline has moved forward, the handler's loyalties are suspect. Clean three-point escalation. The reader has to turn the page.

Chapter 2 manages one of the three problems and generates no new ones. The handler is cleared — he's clean — and the arc breathes again. That's a tension release at the wrong time. The handler question should stay open until at least Chapter 4; releasing it in Chapter 2 takes the board from three live problems to two. The arc decelerated.

Chapter 3 recovers with two new complications, but one — the safe house being made — is a consequence of the Chapter 1 timeline-move. That means it's a delayed payment on a prior problem, not a new one. Acceptable escalation arithmetic but not generative. The arc is spending Chapter 1 capital without earning new capital.

The action scenes are efficient. The political procedural is the drag — the arc explains too much institutional machinery. When the board is changing, show the board changing. The reader doesn't need to understand the institution to feel the institution closing in.

Net verdict: Chapter 1 earns the arc. Chapter 2 needs to hold the handler thread open. Chapter 3 needs one fresh complication that isn't Chapter 1 capital spending. The bones are there; the pacing is leaking.
