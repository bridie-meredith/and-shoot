# Exemplar — Otto Hightower

He let the merchant settle. The chair across the small table was the better of the two and he had arranged it that way before the man arrived. The wine was poured and untouched, which was correct for both of them at this stage.

"You have been moving cargo through the Hull district for nine years. Your ledgers are clean. Your captains are loyal. The Watch on that wharf has, for those nine years, found no reason to attend to your vessels."

The merchant inclined his head.

"In the spring, the council will reconsider the wharf assignments. The reconsideration is not — " he paused, and the merchant's eyes registered the pause and did not understand it — "settled. The Velaryon interest in that wharf is becoming a matter of formal discussion. The discussion is not yet a position."

He set his hand flat on the table, fingers spread, an old gesture.

"I would not raise this with you if the available time were greater. You know the arithmetic. The question is not whether the wharf can be held. The question is who is heard when the council convenes."

He waited. The merchant said what he had been brought there to say.

Otto received it without comment, and noted, exactly, what the merchant had not said.
