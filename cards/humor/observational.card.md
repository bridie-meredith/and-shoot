# Observational Humor

Mechanism card for humor derived from the recognition of shared experience — the "it's funny because it's true" move. This is the sixth card class (humor/reference), not a story-facing card. Consult the dialogue-writer fork; do not cast.

---

## Mechanism

The setup names a specific, concrete observation about something the audience already experiences but has never had articulated. The turn is recognition — the audience thinks *yes, exactly*. The payoff is laughter from the mild shock of being seen, combined with the pleasure of the truth being said plainly for the first time. Secondary engine: mild **superiority** — the teller demonstrates sharper-than-average attention to the shared world, but the target is the situation or the universal human condition rather than a person, which keeps the warmth intact.

What distinguishes observational from satire: satire attacks; observational merely notices. The observation can be dark (this bad thing is universal) without being an attack. The mechanism misfires when it tips into lecture or complaint, which converts the recognition-pleasure into the discomfort of being harangued.

---

## Facet axes

- **setting** — era + place + social stratum. Observational humor is highly setting-sensitive: the shared experience must genuinely be shared by the audience in the scene, not just the author.
- **teller** — social position, relationship to the shared experience. The mechanism is strongest when the teller is inside the experience (not commenting from outside). An outsider observing a community's experience reads as satire or condescension, not recognition.
- **target** — `the-situation` / `power` / `self` / `universal-condition`. Very rarely `other` — targeting a specific person collapses observational into mockery. The default target is an impersonal force or pattern.
- **delivery** — `spoken-aside` / `retort` / `bit` / `interior`. Interior delivery is particularly strong for observational: a character's internal noticing of a shared truth reads as characterization, not as stand-up.
- **scope** — card-specific axis. Rates how wide the "shared" is: `universal` (across all humans), `class-specific` (this social group only), `period-specific` (this era only), `setting-specific` (this secondary world only). Wider scope = more portable; narrower scope = more precise but less reusable.

## Exemplars

> EX-01 [setting: ancient-rome-market | teller: freedman | target: the-situation | delivery: spoken-aside]
> "The rich man says he cannot understand why his tenants are always behind on the rent. He has never been poor, but he is very clear on what poverty looks like from above."
> — Why it lands: articulates a structural truth — power's incomprehension of the conditions it creates — with no anger; the observation is precise and the tone is flat.
> — Deploy: identify a power relationship in the setting; name what the powerful party cannot understand about the powerless party, and why their failure to understand is structural (not individual). The flatness of tone is load-bearing; any heat converts this to satire.

> EX-02 [setting: medieval-village-westeros-smallfolk | teller: smallfolk-elder | target: universal-condition | delivery: spoken-aside]
> "Every man thinks his own roof leaks worse than his neighbor's. Walk past their door in winter and you'll find them thinking the same of yours."
> — Why it lands: names the universal tendency to believe one's own hardship is uniquely bad; the physical specificity (the roof, the door, the winter walk) grounds the abstraction.
> — Deploy: take the universal human tendency to overestimate one's own suffering; place it in a physical, concrete activity native to the setting. The movement of "walk past their door" is the vehicle — it makes the observation an action, not a lecture.

> EX-03 [setting: 17thc-london-coffeehouse | teller: minor-merchant | target: power | delivery: spoken-aside]
> "The king's men always announce they come in peace. You can tell it's true — they only take half the grain."
> — Why it lands: "taking half" as the evidence of peace; the observation is recognizable to anyone who has experienced extraction dressed as restraint.
> — Deploy: identify an institution in the setting whose official claim (peace, mercy, fair dealing) is routinely contradicted by its specific behavior; name the behavior precisely, without the claim. The gap between claim and evidence is the joke.

> EX-04 [setting: 18thc-england-country-house | teller: servant | target: the-situation | delivery: interior]
> She had learned, over twenty years, that what the mistress called "a small supper for close friends" meant twelve guests, three courses, and a sleepless night.
> — Why it lands: names the systematic gap between how the employer describes work and what the work actually requires; delivered as accumulated wisdom rather than complaint.
> — Deploy: the "what they call it / what it actually is" shape. Find a recurring category in the setting (a task, an event, a quality) where the naming systematically understates the reality. The delivery as long experience rather than fresh outrage is what gives it warmth.

> EX-05 [setting: 19thc-american-frontier | teller: homesteader | target: universal-condition | delivery: spoken-aside]
> "The hardest part of starting fresh isn't the work. It's that you bring yourself along."
> — Why it lands: names the irreducibility of character — the thing people believe travel or a new start will cure — with the driest possible phrasing. The "along" does the work; it makes the self sound like an inconvenient piece of luggage.
> — Deploy: the "you thought you left X behind / you brought it" shape. Identify what the character's situation promises to cure; name what cannot be cured by situation. The humor is in the precise naming of the limit, not in exaggerating it.

> EX-06 [setting: secondary-world-court-fantasy | teller: court-steward | target: the-situation | delivery: spoken-aside]
> "A lord who holds three titles from three different kings manages, without effort, to disappoint all three. It is a form of consistency, I suppose."
> — Why it lands: names the structural bind of competing obligations with dry resignation; "consistency" reframes the failure as an achievement, which is the turn.
> — Deploy: any secondary-world setting with overlapping power claims (feudal, magical, ecclesiastical, familial) produces this bind. Find the overlap; name the systematic failure it produces; offer a flat re-labeling of the failure as a perverse virtue.

> EX-07 [setting: wwii-britain-civilian | teller: working-class-woman | target: the-situation | delivery: spoken-aside]
> "They keep telling us morale is high. I reckon they're counting heads and calling it faces."
> — Why it lands: distinguishes quantity (bodies present) from quality (actual spirit); the agricultural idiom "counting heads" makes it register-appropriate.
> — Deploy: the "counting X / calling it Y" shape. Find a situation where quantity is being measured and quality is what matters; name the substitution. The idiom used for the counting should be native to the teller's register.

> EX-08 [setting: modern-hospital-waiting-room | teller: long-term-patient | target: the-situation | delivery: interior]
> He had learned that "shortly" in this context was a unit of time defined entirely by the institution, with no fixed conversion rate to minutes.
> — Why it lands: names the institutional capture of civilian language — words that carry promises in ordinary speech are redefined as institutional estimates. The clinical phrasing ("fixed conversion rate") mirrors the institutional language being satirized.
> — Deploy: the "word that means one thing outside / something different inside the institution" shape. Find a word the setting's institution uses that has been emptied of its ordinary promise. Works especially well when delivered in the institution's own register.

> EX-09 [setting: ancient-greece-household | teller: domestic-slave | target: the-situation | delivery: spoken-aside]
> "My master keeps saying he trusts me completely. I notice he also keeps the keys himself."
> — Why it lands: the gap between verbal expression and actual behavior is a cross-cultural constant; the specificity of the keys makes it concrete. The teller does not comment on the gap — they name it and stop; the audience supplies the judgment.
> — Deploy: "they say X / they do Y" shape — do not editorialize. The teller names the contradiction and stops. Any additional comment converts it from observation to complaint.

> EX-10 [setting: 20thc-eastern-europe-under-occupation | teller: ordinary-citizen | target: power | delivery: spoken-aside]
> "The authorities have announced that food production is up thirty percent this year. They say we should eat the announcements."
> — Why it lands: the official claim versus visible scarcity is a recognized pattern; "eat the announcements" literalizes the substitution — turning propaganda into the meal it replaces.
> — Deploy: wherever official language exists in contradiction to lived experience, the literalization move works — take the abstract official claim and ask what it would mean to consume it physically. The more precise the number in the official claim, the sharper the contrast with the physical want.

## When it lands / When it fails

### Lands when:
- The teller is inside the shared experience — observing from within, not commenting from outside.
- The audience (in-fiction: the characters present; in-fiction/reader: the reader) recognizes the specific thing named. Too abstract and recognition fails; too narrow (specific to one person) and it stops being shared.
- The observation is delivered flatly or dryly — no sales pitch, no waiting for the laugh. The teller states it and moves on; the recognition fires in the listener.
- The target is a situation, power, or the universal condition — not a specific person in the room. Targeting a person converts this to mockery and the warmth evaporates.
- The scene is at a register that tolerates a beat of sideways noticing — conversations, not confrontations; transitions, not climaxes.

### Fails / misfires when:
- The observation is too abstract to anchor recognition (no concrete detail).
- The teller is outside the community whose experience is being described — reads as condescension or tourism.
- The observation tips into complaint or grievance — the mechanism requires detachment; heat converts recognition into lecture.
- The observation is aimed at a specific individual rather than a pattern — converts to mockery.
- The same observation has already been stated in this scene. Repeating an observational beat reads as the author over-explaining; one is the rule.
- In a high-emotional-stakes scene (imminent death, active grief, crisis), observational humor reads as tonal mismatch unless the character is specifically established as someone who observes their way through extremity (in which case it can be devastating — but this is a characterization decision, not a default).

The mechanism is primarily observational when recognition drives the laugh; primarily wordplay when the click of the double meaning does.

## Anti-patterns / fences

**Surface-convention fence (non-negotiable).** Only the comedic mechanism and structure transfer into a dialogue-writer's output. The exemplar content — the specific observations above — does not enter the prose. A dialogue-writer fork that pulls this card learns the shape (inside-experience + concrete detail + flat naming + stop) and constructs a fresh observation in the speaker's voice, setting, and community. Importing an exemplar observation verbatim or with thin reskinning is a hard violation the auditor flags.

**Insider fence.** The teller must be inside the shared experience. An upper-class character making a sharp observation about the poverty of their servants is satire or condescension, not observational humor. If the behavior card establishes the character as an outsider to the experience being named, the mechanism does not apply.

**Lecture fence.** The observation is stated once, flatly, without invitation to agree. Any follow-up that argues for, explains, or repeats the observation breaks the mechanism. The dialogue-writer fork must stop after the observation.

**Anachronism fence.** The specific experience must be genuinely shared in the setting. Modern institutional language (EX-08) does not port to a medieval setting without rebuilding around the equivalent institution. A lord's steward observing the way feast "preparation" quietly expands is the same structure; "fixed conversion rate" does not survive the translation.

**Targeting fence.** The target must be a situation, pattern, or condition — not a specific named individual in the room. The moment the observation names a specific person present, it becomes a put-down and the mechanism changes.

## Dialogue-generation hooks

**Retrieval keys.** Pull this card when: `family:observational`, `target:the-situation`, `delivery:spoken-aside` + `teller:insider`, `register:warm` + `setting:*domestic*`, `register:wry` + `target:power`, `delivery:interior` + `teller:servant | laborer | soldier`, `scope:universal` + any setting, `scope:class-specific` + `setting:*smallfolk* | *working-class*`.

**Applicability test.** An observational beat fits the anchor when: (1) the scene is below confrontation-register; (2) the teller's behavior card does not fence self-expression or candor; (3) the teller is inside the shared experience being named (not a visitor); (4) no prior observational beat has fired in this scene; (5) the setting provides a specific, concrete, recognizable pattern that the teller can name.

**Adaptation rule.** Identify the concrete, specific pattern in the scene's setting that the teller has lived. Name it in one or two plain sentences — no subordination, no hedging, no invitation to agree. Ensure the target is the pattern, not a person. Deliver and stop. Re-skin all content to the speaker's voice and period. Obey the speaker's behavior-card fences (e.g. smallfolk do not observe upward about a lord currently present; a character fenced against candor will not name the thing — they will think it in interior mode instead).
