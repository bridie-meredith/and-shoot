# Contact's Counting-House Premises — Dragon Gate Block (126–129 AC)

## Geography

A ground-floor and partial-below-grade counting-house space in a side courtyard off the inner alley, Dragon Gate block. The courtyard is reached through a narrow passage between two permanent commercial buildings — not visible from the main alley, accessible to anyone who knows the passage exists, discoverable by anyone who follows the correct foot-traffic. Daveth's premises occupy the courtyard's northern two rooms: a front counting-house for client meetings and record presentation, and a rear storage and operations room.

The positioning is practical and readable: a foreign-trade merchant with cross-Narrow-Sea connections and a Black-adjacent movement pattern benefits from premises that are accessible without being conspicuous. The courtyard is not hidden — it is simply one step removed from the main alley's sight-line. Guild inspectors know it exists; the guild-registration records list it as the business address. What is not listed in the guild records is the full range of what passes through it.

The premises are within Taylor's range cap when she is positioned in the inner alley. She has had ambient awareness of the space's occupants for the full three years of her block residency.

## Layout

**Courtyard approach:** The narrow passage from the inner alley is approximately four meters long and one person wide. The passage is flagstone, damp from the adjacent buildings' runoff, and covered by a timber overhang that keeps the passage in permanent shade. The passage opens into the courtyard proper — a small open space, roughly eight meters square, with Daveth's premises on the north side and a storage wall on the south.

**Counting-house front room:** The business-facing space. A meeting table, record storage along one wall (guild-registration documents, transaction ledgers organized by the business-partner's hand), shelves with sample goods — small representative parcels of the import goods the business handles. Natural light from a window looking onto the courtyard. The room presents as a clean, guild-compliant merchant operation, which is what it is.

**Operations rear room:** Behind the counting-house. Larger, lower-ceilinged, below-grade by half a step. Storage for actual goods in transit — the dye-barrels, the spice-parcels, the rope-rigging hardware that moves through on specific trade schedules. Also the communications materials: the sealed correspondence channels, the contact-names in the Braavosi network, the documents that represent the grey-edge business. Not obviously criminal — readable as standard foreign-trade documentation if inspected by someone who did not know to look for faction-access patterns.

**Courtyard itself:** Used for goods movement when a transaction requires it — carts can be backed into the courtyard from a secondary access point on the street behind the block. This secondary access is the border-crossing-pattern that Taylor's Rung-6 framing targets: Daveth's regular use of the secondary access for after-hours goods movement creates a persons-risk-indicator pattern in Watch records.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** The import goods dominate: dye-smell (sharp, mineral, chemical) and spice-smell (warm, complex, specific to the Essosi trade goods) are the foundational notes. The counting-house front room smells of paper and sealing wax overlaid on the goods-smell from the rear. The courtyard itself smells of flagstone and the passage's damp-stone runoff, with the goods-smell drifting from the rear room's secondary access.

**Sound:** The courtyard absorbs street noise — the inner alley's market sounds reach it as muffled ambient rather than direct. The passage itself creates a sound-funnel: footsteps in the passage are audible from the counting-house before the person arrives. Daveth's business runs at a moderate volume; client meetings in the front room are the primary sound event.

**Light:** The courtyard receives overhead light that the narrow passage and surrounding buildings filter. The counting-house front room has reasonable natural light; the rear operations room is deliberately dim — the goods don't need display light, and Daveth prefers his operations room to not be well-lit from outside.

**Temperature:** The courtyard's enclosure means it holds heat in summer and cold in winter, less moderated than the open alley. The passage is always cooler than either.

## Fixed Props

- Meeting table (counting-house front)
- Guild-registration document storage (counting-house front)
- Sample goods shelf (counting-house front)
- Record ledgers (business-partner's organization, counting-house front)
- Storage infrastructure, rear room (shelving, barrel racks)
- Goods in transit, rear room (variable — dye-barrels, spice-parcels, hardware)
- Secondary courtyard access from street behind block

## Exits

- **Inner alley passage (north)** — the primary entrance, through the narrow passage to the inner alley. The normal business entrance.
- **Secondary street access (south/rear)** — the wider vehicle access behind the block, used for goods movement. This access is what the border-crossing-pattern records track. The Watch's persons-risk indicators include this access's usage pattern.

## Hazards

- **The secondary access as persons-risk indicator:** Daveth's regular use of the secondary access for after-hours goods movement is the pattern Taylor's Rung-6 report frames as persons-risk. The pattern is real; the framing is accurate; the Watch protocol runs on the framing, not on Daveth's intent.
- **The goods' documentation:** The rear room's grey-edge documentation is not obviously criminal, but it is readable as faction-access intelligence if the Watch decides to read it that way. During the persons-risk sweep, the rear room is inspected.
- **The passage as chokepoint:** The narrow passage from the inner alley is a natural chokepoint. The Watch can block the passage from the alley end and prevent exit. This is how the persons-risk sweep takes Daveth: the passage blocked from the alley before he is aware the sweep is running.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- Goods delivery: when a cart is backed into the secondary access, the courtyard becomes active with movement and sound for thirty minutes or so; Taylor registers this through insect-ambient before it becomes audible from the alley
- Client meeting in progress: the front room's occupied-and-working state is readable from the insect-ambient (Daveth, the client, the business-partner if present)
- Business-partner absent: at Rung 6, the insect-ambient register of the counting-house shows Daveth present, the business-partner absent — Taylor notes the absence before the sweep arrives
- The passage sound-tell: footsteps in the passage are the earliest warning of approach; the Watch officers' gait is different from merchant customers; Taylor would know the difference if she were in range at the critical moment; she is not
