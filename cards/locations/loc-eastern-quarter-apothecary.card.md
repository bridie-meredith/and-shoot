# Eastern Quarter Apothecary — Broken Maester's Residence

## Geography

An apothecary shop occupying the ground floor of a stone building at the eastern edge of Flea Bottom's transition into the eastern quarter proper — the slightly less dense, slightly more trade-facing neighborhood where medical and pharmaceutical trades cluster. The apothecary serves the surrounding district with standard remedies: feverfew, wound-wash, common tonics. It is not a prestigious establishment; it is a functional one.

The broken maester lives in the upper room — two rooms, actually, connected by a narrow internal stair, with the apothecary owner's family occupying the ground floor's back section. The maester's arrangement with the owner is long-standing: he provides consultation on complex cases the apothecary cannot handle; the apothecary provides materials at cost and asks no questions about what the upper rooms contain.

**Distance from loc-flea-bottom-base:** approximately 250 meters by direct line. The alleys between the two locations add walking distance, but Taylor's insect network does not follow alleys — it saturates through walls and across open spaces within her operational radius. The upper room is within her 300m sphere from story open.

## Layout

**Ground floor (apothecary):** The shop floor is accessible from the street — a door that stands open during trading hours. The interior is the organized density of a working apothecary: shelves, labeled jars (many labels in the maester's handwriting, added as he organized the stock after arriving), a counter where the owner consults with customers, and a back section separated by a curtain where the owner's family lives and where the maester's stair descends.

**The maester's stair:** A narrow wooden stair behind the curtain, steep, rising from the apothecary's back section to the upper level. Not accessible from the street; accessible only through the apothecary's back section or via an exterior door on the building's side alley.

**Upper room (primary):** The larger of the two rooms, which the maester uses as his working space. A large table occupying most of the room's center — dense with books, notes, and the organized clutter of someone who knows where everything is and whose system is not legible to anyone else. A narrow window faces the alley between the building and its neighbor to the east; a second window faces south toward the Flea Bottom district.

**Through-wall observation points (load-bearing for this project):** The south-facing window is approximately 250m from Taylor's lodging by direct line. The walls of this room contain, at any time:
- A beetle colony in the gap between the south wall's inner plaster and outer stone (Taylor identified this in her first week at loc-flea-bottom-base and has maintained a relay through it)
- A seasonal fly population in the window frame's wood-rot (active spring through autumn; thinner in winter, but not absent)
- A mouse-run in the wall cavity connecting to the upper stair (the mice do not enter the working room but their passage through the wall gives positional data)
- Spider presence in the ceiling corners (consistent; Taylor does not direct them, simply monitors them)

These observation points give Taylor: the ambient sound of the room (filtered through arthropod sensory arrays, giving her partial audio of anything said clearly near the south or window walls), the maester's movement patterns (through vibration and positional data from the wall fauna), and the presence or absence of visitors (additional weight, different movement patterns, changed vocal register if conversation occurs).

**Upper room (secondary):** Smaller, used for sleeping and for the maester's personal archive — the thirty-year record, kept in order, in bound bundles labeled by year. This room faces the interior courtyard of the building's block and is less accessible to Taylor's observation network; she has a thinner presence here, primarily through the shared wall with the primary room.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Ground floor:** Medicinal smell — herbs dried and stored, tinctures in sealed jars, the specific astringency of wound-wash. Warm when the owner is present (a small brazier in winter), cool at night.

**Maester's upper room:** Books. The specific smell of old paper and binding material is the dominant register, overlaid with the smoke of the oil lamp he uses for night work. The room has the quality of a space that is always occupied by someone who thinks constantly and moves rarely. The air feels compressed by use.

**Sound profile from outside (observable by Taylor's insect network):** Pen-scratch, the specific rhythm of Citadel-trained handwriting, is audible to the beetles in the south wall when the maester works at his table. Page turns. Occasionally, spoken words — he reads aloud key phrases. The fragments Taylor's insect network catches are partial, acoustic-filtered, but she has been building a vocabulary of his working phrases across s1 and s2. She does not hear everything he says. She hears enough to form hypotheses.

**Night:** The oil lamp creates a glow at the south window after dark that is visible from Taylor's alley as a dim amber square. When the lamp goes out, the maester has stopped working. He does not go out at night; the lamp extinguishes and the room's ambient insect activity drops to sleep-mode patterns. This is a baseline Taylor monitors without directing attention toward it.

## Fixed Props

- The large working table (fixed)
- The book and note collection (extensive; runtime-state for specific volumes present)
- The thirty-year record archive (fixed in the secondary room; bundle-by-year organization)
- The oil lamp (fixed; functional state: lit/unlit runtime state)

## Exits

- **Interior stair to apothecary:** The primary exit from the upper rooms. Requires passing through the apothecary owner's back section.
- **Side alley door (upper):** An exterior door on the side alley, accessible from the landing between the two upper rooms. Used for deliveries and by the maester when he wishes to avoid the apothecary's customer traffic.
- **Window (south-facing, upper room):** Not a practical exit; a ventilation and observation point. Taylor's insect network uses this window's frame as primary acoustic access.

## Hazards

- **The archive:** Thirty years of records. If the maester's archive is destroyed or taken, the witness function's primary output is lost. Taylor does not know the archive exists at story open; she knows a man in the upper rooms works constantly, keeps records, and has visitors occasionally. The archive's existence and content become relevant as the maester's witness function activates in s2.
- **Visitor access:** The apothecary arrangement allows certain visitors to ascend without the maester knowing who sent them. A patient in medical extremis, a messenger with a note, an apothecary referral — these are all plausible. Taylor would detect a visitor entering through either exit; she would not necessarily know the visitor's purpose.
- **Winter thinning of insect presence:** The wall colony is reduced in winter months. Taylor's audio access to the upper room is thinner from late autumn to early spring. The maester does not reduce his work pace seasonally; the observation gap is Taylor's, not his.
- **The maester leaving:** He rarely leaves the upper rooms. When he does — for the apothecary consultations, for occasional walks that appear in Taylor's passive network as a warm-bodied presence in the alley she recognizes from gait — she loses the through-wall observation and has only the ambient network of the street level until he returns.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- The maester reading aloud a phrase Taylor's beetle-relay catches clearly — a fragment from his records or from one of his books that is directly relevant to her hypothesis
- A visitor arriving at the side alley door at an unusual hour — not the regular patient referrals; someone who has been sent by someone
- The lamp extinguishing at an unusually early hour — the maester has stopped working; the deviation from his baseline is a data point
- The maester spending time at the south window — closer to Taylor's primary observation point; his words, if he speaks near the window, are clearer to her relay
- The apothecary owner ascending to the upper rooms — an event that happens rarely and has a specific sound signature (heavier footfall, different rhythm from the maester's movement)
- The side alley door being used at a time Taylor has not observed it used before — new variable in the access pattern
