# The Ashford Dye-Workshop and Dwelling (Craftsman Lane, Fairstead)

## Geography

A combined workshop-and-dwelling on Fairstead's craftsman lane, approximately midway between the market square end and the lane's dead-end at the common hall backing wall. The building is of wattle-and-daub with stone foundation and a thatched roof — slightly better quality than the lane's average, reflecting a decade of modest trade success. The workshop occupies the front two-thirds of the structure; the dwelling occupies the rear third plus a loft sleeping space above the workshop area. An external dye-yard extends behind the structure to the lane's back boundary.

The building is a craftsman's building: it is designed to do work, and doing work leaves marks. The floor of the workshop area is stained with mordant and plant-dye, permanently and in all colors. The walls hold the smell of the work whether work is in progress or not.

## Layout

**Front/Workshop area:**
- Two large dye vats (clay-lined wood, sunk slightly into the ground for stability). These dominate the workspace. When in use they are steaming; when not in use they are cold and smell powerfully of their last batch.
- A mordant station — shelves holding alum, iron vitriol, tannin (from oak galls), and the plant-based mordants the trade uses. Small clay pots, stoppered with wax or cloth. All labeled in Edwyn's hand.
- The ledger bench — a workbench with a writing surface at the front-window side, where Edwyn keeps his accounts. Daylight from the front shutter falls here. This is where Taylor reads when she has cover for it.
- Finished cloth hanging space — wooden dowels above the vat area for dyed cloth to air before it is packaged. The cloth gives the workshop its visual signature: the colors stacked from the last batch.
- Tool storage — on the west wall, the dye tools: stirring paddles, skeining racks, measuring rods. All purpose-built for the trade.

**Rear/Dwelling area:**
- The hearth — central to the dwelling space, slightly off-center toward the north wall. The fire is always going when the household is functional; the fire going out is a household emergency.
- The sleeping area — a box-bed (relative luxury; it contains heat) on the east wall for Elara and Edwyn. A pallet near the hearth for Taylor, which she prefers because it gives her a sightline to the workshop without being in it.
- The kitchen corner — a small preparation area near the hearth with a pot hook, a shelf for dried herbs and stored grain, and a water bucket that is refilled from the market well daily.
- A wooden chest on the south wall — household valuables (the equivalent of a month's trade revenue in coin, the deed to the workshop building in Edwyn's name, Elara's good headcloth for feast days). Taylor knows the contents; she has never indicated that she knows the contents.

**Loft:**
- Sleeping space above the workshop, accessible by a wooden ladder. Used for storage when no one is sleeping there; used by visitors if they are trusted; used by Taylor when she needs privacy, which she claims by going up and which her parents have learned to respect.
- The loft has a gap at the apex of the roof — not a window, but a ventilation space — that gives partial line of sight over the dye-yard and the lane. Taylor has been using this gap as an observation point since she could climb the ladder.

**Dye-yard (external):**
- Behind the structure, bounded by a low wattle fence. Skeining racks for drying and airing finished cloth. A fire-pit for heating vat water in large batches. A drainage channel that runs toward the lane's water-channel. Ash pile from the wood-fire (ash is a mordant component; nothing is wasted in a dye-yard).
- The dye-yard is the richest insect ecology in the building. Damp organic material, standing water in the heating channel when the vat is in use, ash pile warmth in winter — the insect population of the dye-yard is substantially higher than the surrounding lane and is Taylor's primary close-range surveillance terrain.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Mordant is the dominant note — specifically alum, which has a faint astringent quality. Beneath it: the plant dye residue (weld yellow is sweet; woad blue is faintly herbaceous; madder red is earthy). In the dwelling area: peat smoke (from the hearth), and the layered domestic smell of a working household — dried herbs, grain, wool clothing, the specific smell of two adults and a child who wash but not daily.

**Sound:** The vats when in use: a slow bubble or a vigorous boil, depending on the stage. The paddle against the vat wall when stirring. From outside: the lane's ambient noise (craftsmen in adjacent workshops, cart traffic from the market square, children at the lane's dead end). At night: the fire's settle and pop, the sounds from the lane through the wattle-and-daub walls (thinner than stone; Fairstead is not a stone-dwelling town for this stratum).

**Touch:** The workshop floor is uneven — stained, worn in the paths of heaviest use, slightly tacky near the vat stations from mordant splash. The ledger bench has a worn smooth spot where Edwyn's arm rests. The dye on the wall at vat height has dried in ridges. In the dwelling area: the roughspun wool of the bedclothes, the cold packed earth under the rushes at the room's edge, the heat differential between the hearth-near and the hearth-far.

**Light:** The workshop runs on daylight from the front-window shutter and the gap above the door when open. When the shutter is closed (cold weather, or privacy), the workshop is dim enough that ledger work is difficult. The dwelling area runs on fire-glow supplemented by rush-dips; Taylor reads at the ledger bench by day and cannot read by firelight at night without appearing to read too fast. The loft is dim by day and very dark by night.

**Insect ecology (Taylor-relevant):** Richest of any Taylor-accessible space. The dye-yard: flies from the organic material and standing water (dense in summer, lighter in winter), ground beetles in the ash pile, spiders in the yard's corner structures. The workshop interior: spiders in the rafters (substantial colony; the mordant smell drives away some competitors but spiders tolerate it), flies around the vat stations in season, beetles in the tool-storage wall cracks. The dwelling area: fewer — the hearth keeps the temperature high and fluctuating in ways that disrupt insect colony formation. Taylor has approximately 80 individuals under consistent ambient control within the structure without active direction effort. This is her home base.

## Fixed Props

- Two large dye vats (clay-lined, wooden exterior)
- Mordant shelf with labeled clay pots
- Ledger bench (Edwyn's accounts; Taylor's reading station by daylight)
- Dye tools: stirring paddles, skeining racks, measuring rods
- Cloth airing dowels above the workshop area
- Household chest (south wall of dwelling area)
- Box-bed (east wall)
- Taylor's pallet (near hearth)
- Dye-yard skeining racks
- Dye-yard fire-pit

## Exits

- **Front door** — opens onto the craftsman lane; the public face of the building. Default workshop entrance.
- **Dye-yard gate** — a wattle gate at the back of the yard connecting to the lane's rear alley. Used for materials delivery and dye-waste drainage. Less observed than the front door.
- **Loft gap** — not an exit in the standard sense; a sightline for Taylor's passive surveillance of the lane approach.

## Hazards

- **Visibility from the lane:** The workshop shutter faces the lane. When open, the front workspace is visible to anyone passing. Edwyn works at the ledger bench in full view. Taylor reads at the ledger bench in partial view.
- **Neighbor proximity:** The adjacent workshops share walls with the Ashford building. Sound carries. The wattle-and-daub transmits conversation at low confidence. Neighbors know the Ashford household's rhythm and would notice a significant departure from it.
- **Institutional access:** The steward can enter the workshop on trade-inspection grounds — a legitimate institutional access that requires no warrant. Edwyn's ledger is available for inspection. Taylor's reading skill is legible from the ledger bench.
- **The vat chemistry:** The dye vats are chemical environments. Steam from active vats is hot and acrid. Prolonged exposure is not harmful but is unpleasant. Mordant solutions are caustic. Taylor navigates this daily and has integrated it into her passive sense calibration.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A neighbor calls through the workshop wall — not an emergency; a trade question. The shared wall makes this a regular social form. Taylor tracks the conversation from the loft or the sleeping pallet.
- A factor arrives at the front door during working hours. He has come to inspect a completed batch or to place an order. Edwyn conducts the transaction at the ledger bench. Taylor is in the workshop unless Edwyn has sent her elsewhere.
- The dye vat needs immediate attention — it has reached the wrong temperature, or the batch timing is critical. Elara or Edwyn moves fast; Taylor is recruited. The urgency of the work gives Taylor legitimate reason to be physically active in the workshop during a visit.
- The loft gap catches a conversation from the lane — two craftsmen discussing the market square incident, or the reeve, or the steward's recent circuit. Taylor, in the loft, receives this.
- The dye-yard spider colony produces an anomalous cluster — not the normal distribution pattern. Taylor notices the shift before looking; her passive sense registered it first. She has to decide whether to investigate visibly or defer.

## Interaction Notes

**For Taylor:** The workshop-home is her richest operational space and her least-controlled social space. The insects give her the best ambient map she has. Her family's presence makes full solitary operation impossible. The ledger bench gives her reading access with legitimate cover (she is the dyer's daughter; she has been taught to use the ledger). The loft gives her the only privacy she consistently has.

**Privacy gradient:** Dye-yard > loft > dwelling area > workshop front. Her most private planning happens in the dye-yard or the loft. Her most observed moments are at the ledger bench during working hours.
