# Fairstead — Riverlands Market Town (Blue Fork Tributary)

## Geography

Fairstead sits on a navigable tributary of the Blue Fork, approximately three days' upriver from where the tributary meets the Blue Fork proper. It is not a large town — population approximately 250–350, with an additional 100–150 in the immediate farmsteads surrounding it — but it is a market town in the specific Westerosi sense: it exists at a trade node. The river tributary makes it accessible from the south; a road junction makes it accessible from the east (toward the Riverroads) and west (toward Pinkmaiden and Tully territory). Goods moving between the small inland holdings and the Blue Fork trade circuit pass through Fairstead. The town exists because of this passage, not despite it.

The lord is House Ryger of Willow Wood, a minor Tully bannerman. Their seat is Willow Wood, approximately two days' ride south-southwest. In Fairstead, Ryger presence is represented by a steward (Aldric Pryor) and a small garrison outpost at the edge of the market quarter.

Era context: Jaehaerys I's long peace. Approximately 84–101 AC. Fairstead has been at peace for the living memory of its oldest residents. This is not prosperity — it is the specific condition of smallfolk under Jaehaerys's consolidated order: taxes collected reliably, trade functioning predictably, the lord's authority present without being violent. The stability has a ceiling: what is stable can be destabilized by an anomaly. Fairstead has been anomaly-free until approximately 86 AC.

## Layout

Fairstead is organized around three poles: the market square (commercial center), the sept (religious and informational center), and the river dock (trade and transit center). Between these poles the town extends in irregular clusters — craftsman lane (dyers, weavers, leather workers), the reeve's house adjacent to the lord's outpost, the ferryman's cottage near the dock, several dozen dwelling-structures of varying size and quality.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** River mud when the wind is from the dock. Dyestuff from the craftsman lane — mordant and plant-dye smells (weld, woad, madder), acrid and complex. Animal heat from the market square pens. Peat smoke from the common hall. Tallow from the sept's candles when the wind is right. In season: wood smoke in autumn, river-green in spring, dust in summer, wet stone in winter.

**Sound:** The market square on market day — livestock, trade calls, children, the flat ring of the weighing scale. The river tributary's current, audible from anywhere in the lower town. Septon Rowan's liturgy on holy-day mornings, carrying slightly past the sept walls. The garrison outpost's irregular bell (summoning, not announcing time). The ferry horn when the flat-bottom needs the near-shore crew.

**Touch:** Cobble-stone or packed earth depending on which lane you're in. The market square is packed earth. The road from the dock to the market is surfaced with river gravel. The craftsman lane is dirt with occasional board-sections over the worst mud patches.

**Light:** Fairstead is a low-light settlement by modern standards. In summer, long day-work hours; in winter, work by firelight and rush-dip. The market square gets the most light; the craftsman lane is shadowed in the mornings when the larger structures block the east. The sept tower catches the afternoon sun through the seven narrow windows.

**Insect ecology (Taylor-relevant):** The craftsman lane is richest. Dye-vat flies, damp-wool beetles, cellar spiders in the workshop foundations. The market square runs second: animal-pen flies from the livestock pens, stable-adjacent wasps in summer, ground beetles in the wheel-ruts. The dock is third: water beetles and waterbugs in the shallows, mosquitoes in the marshy near-bank, horseflies at the livestock transit pen. The sept is lowest density: stone building with little food-source, but cellar spiders under the altar platform and candle-moths at the windows. Taylor's passive sense covers all of Fairstead from approximately the center of the craftsman lane.

## Exits

From Fairstead the exits are:
- **River south by ferry** — to the Blue Fork trade circuit; three days' travel; the legitimate commercial corridor
- **River north by boat** — to the upper tributary villages; smaller and slower than south; informal
- **East road** — toward the Riverroads and wider Riverlands transit; the road the lord's men use
- **West road** — toward Pinkmaiden territory; smaller road, less trafficked
- **Farmstead paths** — irregular tracks to the surrounding agricultural holdings; passable in good weather, difficult in wet

## Hazards

- **The steward's monitoring network** — Aldric Pryor's man is in the outpost, and Pryor's circuit brings him through regularly. The garrison men note unusual behavior at the market square. Information about Fairstead's residents reaches the lord's chain efficiently.
- **The ferry chokepoint** — anyone leaving or entering Fairstead by the river passes through the dock, where the ferryman knows everyone and where the garrison occasionally posts a man during census or collection periods.
- **The market square as full-exposure space** — on market day, in the square, there is no position that is not observed by at least a dozen people. Taylor's anomalous behavior (tracking insect movement, pre-cognition of events) is most visible here.
- **Fairstead's information density** — it is a small town. Approximately 300 people. Everyone knows approximately everyone else's business. Information suppression is active effort; information spread is the passive default.
- **Septon Rowan's theological output** — he preaches once a week in the sept. His theology about Taylor is building. What he preaches in the sept travels through the community faster than any official channel.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A traveling factor arrives from the Blue Fork circuit with news of trade changes upstream. The market square reorganizes around his arrival; the wool factor is immediately visible in how he responds. Taylor's passive sense gives her the room before she enters it.
- The garrison bell rings at an irregular time. The town contracts slightly — people move toward structures, away from open spaces. A signal of official business. Taylor tracks where Pryor goes after the bell.
- Market day rain. The square empties with practiced smallfolk efficiency. The factors shelter their goods first; the livestock handlers shelter the animals second; the garrison men shelter last because they are paid to be in the rain. Taylor has two minutes before the square is empty of witnesses.
- A community gathering in the common hall. Septon Rowan is conducting something — not a holy-day office, but a gathering requested by the reeve. Taylor's passive sense cannot penetrate the common hall walls as effectively as open spaces; she is working with reduced information.
- The ferry horn at an unusual time. A boat from south of the usual schedule. The ferryman knows if it is a lord's man or a traveling factor or something else. Mira Stonefield knows before the market wives' meeting point does, because she has a node closer to the dock than anyone else in her network.

## Interaction Notes

**For Taylor:** Fairstead is her home terrain — the space she knows best, where her insect map is most accurate. The craftsman lane gives her rich passive surveillance of her immediate neighborhood. The market square is her primary exposure risk. The sept is her literacy-origin and her access to Rowan's theological construction. The dock is her information node about who enters and leaves.

**The town as constraint:** Fairstead is small enough that Taylor's organizing visibility is maximized. There is no anonymity here. Every move she makes is observable by someone. The stability of the Jaehaerys peace means that anomalies stand out against a stable background more than they would in a contested era.
