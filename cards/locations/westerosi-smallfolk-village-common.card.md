# Westerosi Smallfolk Village Common

## Description

The central outdoor space of a Westerosi village — not a named place but a function: the ground in front of the common hall or the open green between the dwellings where the village gathers. The space is defined less by what it is than by what it does: it is where collective decisions are announced, where the lord's man collects the harvest tax, where the flowering-year girl will stand when the knight comes for the naming-right census. It is the space that makes the smallfolk legible to the authority above them.

The common is not a comfortable place. It is not designed for comfort. It is a place of managed exposure — everyone can see everyone; everything that happens here is, technically, witnessed. This is not the same as being protected. The witnesses are also smallfolk. The lord's word runs here.

Scale: two or three dozen paces across, depending on the village. Large enough for the whole village to gather; small enough that no one can pretend not to be present.

## Sensory Hooks

**Smell:** Peat smoke from the common hall chimney. Animal heat — the penned animals are never far from the common. Damp wool. Green hay if it is spring or summer. Kitchen-garden rosemary from whatever grows against the nearest wall. Midden-smell from downwind. The specific iron smell of a rain-wet fence post.

**Sound:** Chickens. Always chickens. The low creak of the common hall door. Mud underfoot if it has rained — the sucking sound of boots pulling free. Children stopped mid-game when a stranger enters the village, then the silence, then the resumption. The flat clink of a horse's bridle when the knight's man rides in.

**Touch:** Cold — the common catches wind. The ground is clay-packed, not paved; it holds boot-prints. In winter: frost-hard and slick. In autumn: mud. In summer: dust and sun-cracked earth, firm except in the wheel-ruts.

**Sight:** The common hall: a long low building, thatched, with a smoke-grimed exterior and a double door wide enough for a cart. A central fire-pit or a chimney-stack (varies by village, era, region). Benches or trestles stacked against the wall when not in use. The well — if the village has one, it anchors one end of the common. The stocks, if the village has them: present as social fact even when empty. Lord's marker: a boundary stone or a fencepost with the lord's colors (faded, usually), indicating the land is sworn to House Somebody.

**Movement pattern:** On ordinary days: dispersed. A woman carrying washing, two men cutting timber at the side, children in and out of the hall. On authority-day (tax collection, knight's visit, naming-census): the village contracts. People gather but maintain two body-lengths from whoever is conducting the authority business.

## Exits

- **Village gate / road entrance** — the direction the knight's man comes from; the direction strangers come from; the direction Tanya will eventually need to leave through.
- **Common hall interior** — double doors; access conditional on weather and social occasion; default is that the hall is used communally, not privately.
- **Dwelling cluster** — irregular paths between the dwellings; no straight lines; a familiar can navigate by habit, a stranger cannot navigate by sight.
- **Kitchen garden / field border** — at the edge of the village green, where the managed land gives way to the worked fields. Marked by a hedge or a low stone wall.
- **Water source** — well (central) or stream (if the village is positioned on one), a short walk from the main common.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- The village elder makes a pronouncement. Everyone's body orients toward him without explicit command. Tanya's does not, or does a half-beat late. This is noticed.
- The knight's man rides in. The village common contracts. The children disappear. The adults do not run — they simply relocate themselves to positions that are not directly in the path.
- A woman shouts from across the common at a child. The child does not answer. The woman shouts again. This is the ambient authority that makes the lord's authority legible — everyone is governed here, down to the smallest scale.
- Rain starts. The common empties with practiced efficiency. Every smallfolk person in Westeros knows exactly when to come in. The stranger or newcomer does not.
- The flowering-year girl is called into the common for the naming-census. She comes. The village watches with the specific watchfulness of people who have already done their arithmetic.

## Interaction Notes

**For Tanya:** The common is where her social illegibility is maximally visible. Her body is nine years old. The village reads her as a nine-year-old. Her gait, posture, and response timing are wrong in a way that registers as odd but not dangerous — a strange child, not an adult in a child's body. On the common, under full village observation, every misalignment costs social capital she has not accumulated. Operating here requires suppression of her natural analytical stillness (which reads as insolence in a child) and substitution of something more like watchful-obedience.

**Pairs with `westerosi-customary-authority-ambient`:** When the authority-day beat fires (knight's man, census, tax collection), the common is where it surfaces. The ambient condition carries the cultural logic; this location card carries the physical staging.

**Pairs with `hedge-knight-road-ambient` (`cards/conditions/dunk-and-egg/hedge-knight-road-ambient.card.md`):** The road-ambient carries the texture of being outside the village. When the knight arrives and the road-ambient resolves to village-common, the contrast between road (no witnesses) and common (all witnesses) is structurally load-bearing.

**Does not pair well with:** Any HotD aristocratic register card (Dragonstone, Red Keep, Small Council). The smallfolk common and the aristocratic location are categorically different spaces and should not load simultaneously.

## Quality Notes

**Generic Westerosi scope:** This card is set in the generic Dance-era Westerosi smallfolk stratum, not tied to a specific named village, lordship, or region. It can serve any Westerosi-peasant-frame board. Region-specific texture (Reach villages vs. Riverlands villages vs. North villages) should be added via a project-scope override or a variant card if the project commits to a named region.

**Canon source:** The smallfolk texture across ASoIaF — specifically *A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms* (GRRM, 2015) for road-and-village-level ground truth, supplemented by Fire & Blood for Dance-era social-structure reference. No direct scene-lift; extracted ambient register.

**Era note:** Dance of the Dragons (~130 AC). The social structure governing smallfolk obligations (including customary rights, knight's census, maester's visit protocols) is materially unchanged between the Dunk-and-egg era (209 AC) and the Dance era — a century apart, but the feudal smallfolk stratum is one of the more stable strata in Westerosi canon. Cross-era use is warranted.
