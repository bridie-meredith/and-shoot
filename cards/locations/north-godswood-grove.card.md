# Northern Godswood Grove

## Geography

A godswood of the type common to Northern keeps and great holdfasts: three or more acres of old forest enclosed within a castle's inner walls or preserved in open land as a sacred precinct. Untouched by axe or fire for thousands of years — the charter forbidding the cutting predates the lords who now hold the land. The ground within shows no sign of management: fallen limbs rot where they land, saplings fight for light in the gaps, and the undergrowth has the density of something that has never been cleared.

At the center: a heart tree. The weirwood is bone-white from root to high canopy, its five-pointed leaves blood red even in summer. The face carved into its trunk — eyes, nose, open mouth — is ancient enough that the bark has grown around the original cuts, softening the lines into something worse than clean carving. Sap pools in the eye sockets and bleeds down the face in dark ribbons. The tree stands over a pool of still water, cold enough to be black even in full daylight.

The grove is alive with animal presence but profoundly quiet. Nothing nests in the weirwood itself.

## Layout

- **The outer wood:** Oak, elm, ash, yew. Dense canopy. Even on bright days the light is green-grey at ground level; the undergrowth of fern and bracken grows to waist height in cleared patches, ankle height where the canopy closes overhead.
- **The approaches:** No formal path, but the ground has been walked for long enough that the feet find the way without conscious decision. The route from the door of the keep or the gap in the stone enclosure leads inward and downward — the godswood sits in a slight depression, as if the land itself has yielded.
- **The heart tree clearing:** The weirwood's canopy overhangs a space perhaps thirty feet across. The pool at its base is roughly circular, three feet deep at center, fed by a spring no one has ever located. The roots of the weirwood break the surface around the pool's edge.
- **The pool:** Still to the point of appearing solid. No current, no ripple except those caused by a leaf falling or a person crouching to touch the water. Whatever lives below the surface is not visible from above.

## Sensory Vocabulary

- **Sound:** The godswood is quiet in a way that requires a moment to register. Birds call in the outer wood; inside the grove boundary, they fall silent. The weirwood's leaves move in a wind that doesn't seem to be present. The pool is absolutely still and absolutely soundless. A person who speaks in a normal voice is startled by how the sound behaves — not echoing, exactly, but not absorbed either.
- **Smell:** Moist earth, centuries of leaf decay, cold bark. The iron-cold smell of still water. Something underneath that is not quite any of these — the smell of old stone left wet for a very long time, or of a kind of wood no longer common.
- **Light:** Dim even by day. The red weirwood leaves filter what light they admit into something close to the color of dusk at noon. At night: the carved face on the heart tree collects whatever light there is — moonlight, starlight — and seems, briefly, to hold it.
- **Temperature:** Noticeably colder within the godswood boundary than outside it. The pool is cold enough to numb the hand within seconds at any season.
- **Touch:** The bark of the weirwood is smooth and dry to the touch in a way that other white-barked trees are not. The sap in the face's eye sockets is tacky, slow, and red. The pool's water is immediately, completely cold.

## Fixed Props

- The heart tree (ancient weirwood, bone-white, blood-red leaves, carved face with sap-weeping eyes)
- The pool beneath the heart tree (still, cold, fed by unknown spring)
- The fallen limbs and dead wood of the outer grove (decades of accumulated rot; no one removes them)

## Exits

- **Toward the keep:** The path that finds you rather than the other way around. Leads to the godswood door or the enclosure gap.
- **Deeper into the wood:** Where the canopy closes and the undergrowth thickens; the sense of being watched increases rather than decreases the further in you go.

## Hazards

- The ground near the pool is soft and will not hold armor weight without sinking; approach slowly on foot.
- The silence is disproportionate to the space; people who have not been in a Northern godswood before sometimes find it difficult to remain.
- The weirwood's carved face is oriented to watch a person who stands at the pool's edge regardless of what angle they approach from. This is a property of the carving and not evidence of anything. It is also not evidence of nothing.
- A person who speaks or prays at the heart tree in some traditions is understood to be heard. The theological implications are contested; the behavioral ones are not — people behave differently when they believe they are being watched.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A raven landing in the weirwood canopy — the only bird that will nest or perch there
- A patrol from the keep checking the godswood wall, visible through the tree line
- The pool's surface breaking without visible cause — something rising from below or a spring surge
- Snow falling into the grove: it accumulates on the red weirwood leaves and falls in clumps; the effect is specific
- The sense that the carved face's expression has changed since you last looked directly at it (no mechanism for this; nevertheless it is a persistent observation by people who spend time in the space)
