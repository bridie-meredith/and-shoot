# Fairstead Market Square — Ignition Site

## Geography

The commercial center of Fairstead, approximately 40 paces across (east-west) by 30 paces (north-south). Open to the sky. Bounded on the west by the common hall, on the east by the craftsman lane's market-facing end, on the north by the road from the dock, and on the south by a terrace of trade structures (factors' offices, a potter's stall that is a permanent structure, the weighing station). The garrison outpost is visible at the square's northeast corner, set back slightly from the main square boundary.

On market days (once per week, and on special collection days), the square fills: stalls erected by traveling factors and local traders, animal pens at the east end where the market gives way to the craftsman lane, the weighing station's table deployed, the lord's collection apparatus set up if it is a tax day.

On non-market days: mostly clear except for the permanent structures and the weighing station. The market square is used for community gatherings called by the reeve or Septon Rowan, for official announcements, and as a transit point.

## Layout

**Permanent structures and features:**
- **Weighing station** — on the north edge, a stone platform with iron hooks for hanging the balance. The scale itself is stored in the garrison outpost; the ferryman or the lord's man brings it out on collection and market days. The weighing stone (a large dressed block with the Ryger mark) is fixed to the station platform.
- **Common hall doors** — the double doors of the common hall face the square's west edge. On ordinary days these are closed; on gathering days they stand open.
- **Potter's stall** — a permanent semi-structure on the south edge; the potter has a shop there and a firing kiln behind it. His setup provides cover (visual obstruction) from the south approach.
- **The lord's marker** — a post with faded Ryger heraldry at the square's center, indicating the square itself is lord's property and that transactions here are subject to the lord's tariff.

**Market-day additions (relevant to ignition scene):**
- **Livestock pens** — east end of the square, adjacent to the craftsman lane mouth. Temporary wooden rails; six to twelve animals on an average market day (sheep for the wool trade, occasional cattle, goats). Animal-pen flies cluster here heavily. Taylor's richest short-range offensive resource during market days.
- **Traveling factors' stalls** — center of the square, arranged in rough rows. Cloth, grain, metal goods, spices from the south. Stalls provide partial cover from direct observation.
- **Collection table** — on tax collection days, the garrison men set up a trestle table on the square's north side, adjacent to the weighing station. The collection agent sits here. The smallfolk queue. The lord's men stand beside the table.

**Witness positions (load-bearing for ignition scene):**
- Common hall doors — if open, any adult inside can see the full square
- Potter's stall — the potter himself, and anyone sheltering behind the structure's corner
- Craftsman lane mouth — adults and children from the lane who are not yet fully in the square; partial sightline
- Garrison outpost — the outpost's front window faces the square's northeast corner; anyone inside can see the collection table area clearly
- Market stall coverage — factors and their assistants, intermittently, depending on where the confrontation occurs relative to stall positions
- The well — a woman drawing water from the well at the square's edge has a clear sightline to the collection table from approximately 15 paces

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Animal-pen dominates on market days — sheep and cattle, their dung, the specific wet-wool smell of penned animals in close quarters. Factor's goods add: spice from the south (pepper, cinnamon, carried in open sacks), cloth-bolt mordant from the wool trade, grain-dust from the grain stalls. Common hall smoke when the hall is open. Under all of it: the packed-earth smell of a surface that has been trafficked for decades.

**Sound:** On market day: constant. Livestock noise from the pens. Factor calls. Bargaining. Children running. The specific sound of coin on the collection table when a payment is made. The weighing scale's creak and the balance arm's ring when it settles. The lord's man's voice carrying the collection formula ("in the name of Lord Ryger of Willow Wood, the autumn quarter is owing"). Off market days: quiet. Wind through the common hall roof. Distant cart noise from the dock road. Septon Rowan's bell in the sept tower (audible from the square on still days).

**Touch:** Packed earth, hard-worn. Boot-churned mud when wet; cracked and dusty when dry. The animal pens' bedding (straw, replaced imperfectly) gives way underfoot near the east end. The weighing stone is cold to the touch year-round; children dare each other to hold their hand to it in winter.

**Light:** Open to the sky; the best-lit space in Fairstead. On market days, full sun plus the reflected light from the common hall's whitewashed interior wall (if the doors are open). In winter: grey and cold, with the limited winter sun not reaching the square's north section until midday.

**Insect ecology (Taylor-relevant):** The animal pens are the critical resource. Market-day fly density around the livestock pens: very high. These are Taylor's best offensive resource in the ignition scene — she can raise the flies from the pens to crowd-scale without having to deploy from a distance. The flies are already there. They are already disturbed by market noise. Her active direction can escalate what is already present noise to an overwhelming event in approximately thirty seconds of active control.

Secondary resources: ground beetles in the packed-earth cracks (mobile but slow to rally), wasps in the potters' stall eaves in summer (high sting potential, low controllability due to colony-defense instinct in unfamiliar handler), spiders along the common hall exterior wall (limited). The animal-pen flies are the resource she uses at the ignition.

## Fixed Props

- Weighing stone (fixed, stone, Ryger-marked)
- Lord's marker post (fixed)
- Seasonal: weighing scale balance (stored in garrison), collection trestle table (stored in garrison), livestock pen rails (stored by the market reeve's assistant)

## Exits

- **North road** — to the dock and the ferry; the direction most traffic comes from
- **East lane** — to the craftsman lane; the residential direction
- **South path** — through the factor structures to the road east (toward the Riverroads)
- **West through the common hall** — only when the hall is open; provides exit through the hall to the back of the common hall structure and the west-facing lane beyond it

**Escape analysis for the ignition scene:** When Taylor's swarm rises, the collection agent's horses are tethered at the weighing station's north post. The horses bolt north (toward the dock road) when stung — they cannot bolt east (too many stalls) or west (common hall wall). The agent and his men retreat north with the horses or south through the factor stalls. Taylor can move east through the craftsman lane (her home direction) or west through the common hall if it is open. The witnesses who are observing from static positions (potter, garrison outpost) cannot easily intercept her if she moves fast and she has Elara or Edwyn ahead of her.

## Hazards

- **Full visibility** — the square's open plan means a confrontation in the center is visible from all four boundary positions simultaneously. There is no private quadrant.
- **Garrison outpost sightline** — the outpost window faces the northeast corner. The collection table is typically set up in the north section. Pryor's man in the outpost has a clear view of any confrontation at the collection point.
- **High social density on market day** — approximately 150–200 people in or around the square on a busy market day. Any action in the square on market day is witnessed. The witnesses will talk. The talk will travel.
- **Animal instability** — the livestock pens' animals are already reactive to market noise. Taylor's swarm from the pens will read as a market disturbance even before it becomes organized. The horses' reaction (bolting) will be the most visible outcome and the one the collection agent's report focuses on.
- **The collection table's institutional frame** — on tax day, the table converts the square from trade space to authority space. The smallfolk queue. The lord's men stand. The social geometry of the space changes. Any action that disrupts this geometry is immediately legible as a disruption of the lord's authority, not just a market incident.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- The weighing scale produces a disputed reading. A factor and a smallfolk farmer argue the balance; the argument draws a crowd. Taylor can observe the argument through the insect ambient or from the craftsman lane mouth.
- A traveling factor arrives during the market with goods from south of the Blue Fork. His arrival shifts the market's center of attention temporarily. The garrison man at the outpost window watches the new arrival; his attention is off the square's interior.
- A child runs through the livestock pens, scattering the animals. The brief disruption looks like the beginning of Taylor's ignition event if you were not watching closely. Taylor notes this; it gives her a cover model.
- The collection agent's retinue moves through the square before setting up the table, conducting a preliminary census of who is present. This is the moment before the ignition event proper — the men moving, the smallfolk contracting, Taylor tracking the retinue through her passive sense.
- Rain starts. The market square empties rapidly. The collection table is the last thing to be taken in; the lord's men do not move for weather.

## Ignition Scene Notes

**Spatial setup:** The collection table is at the north end of the square, adjacent to the weighing station. The retinue horses are tethered to the weighing station's north post. The smallfolk queue extends south from the table into the center of the square. The coercion incident (the woman being physically pressured at the table) occurs at or immediately beside the table — in full view of the garrison outpost, in full view of the factor stalls, in full view of anyone in the square.

**Taylor's position at ignition:** She is in the queue with her mother, or near the queue, or in the craftsman lane mouth watching the square. Her passive sense is already covering the livestock pens (standard ambient for any market day). The coercion of the smallfolk woman triggers the involuntary swarm response. The animal-pen flies rise in quantity and coherence before Taylor has made a conscious decision.

**The thirty-second sequence:** Flies rise from the pens. The horses respond before the men do — equine sensitivity to the insect density. The horses bolt north (dock road direction). The men scatter. Two men are stung substantially (the two closest to Taylor's position; the swarm focused before she redirected it). The collection table is overturned or abandoned. The woman is no longer being coerced. Taylor is standing in the square. The garrison outpost man is at the window. The reeve's son Clem is near the craftsman lane mouth with a clear sightline to Taylor's face.

**The steward's note:** Pryor arrives in the square within twenty minutes (he is at the garrison outpost on a circuit visit that day). He interviews the collection agent. He walks the square. His eyes move to Taylor once, hold for one second, move away. He writes the note that evening.
