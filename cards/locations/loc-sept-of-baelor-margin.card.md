# Great Sept of Baelor — Approach and Outer Courts (Margin Space)

## Geography

The Great Sept of Baelor stands on Visenya's Hill, the southwestern prominence within King's Landing's walls — the hill opposite and across the city from Rhaenys's Hill and the Dragonpit. The Sept commands its hill with the deliberate authority of institutional religion: its placement, the approach road's geometry, and the cleared outer courts around it are all structured to produce a specific effect on the arriving person. The effect is size and permanence, communicated at the scale of the city's largest enclosed structure.

This card covers the margin space: the approach streets and the outer courts that ring the Sept's base. It does not cover the Sept's interior; the margin is the location of the scene this card supports — a crossing of Taylor and the opposite-number in a space that is neutral by virtue of being Faith-territory, public by virtue of being the approach to the city's primary religious site.

The margin is one of the few genuinely public spaces in King's Landing where high-status and low-status figures share ground without the hierarchy being enforced by architecture. The outer courts are Faith-territory; the Faith administers them, not any lord. A Flea Bottom woman in the outer courts is unusual but not illegal; a Targaryen household member in the outer courts is pious rather than political, and piety is a legitimate reason for anyone to be anywhere Faith-territory includes. This is the neutral ground's structural logic.

Distance references: from Flea Bottom, the approach to Visenya's Hill is a twenty-five to thirty minute walk westward through the city's middle tier, passing through increasingly ordered streets as elevation rises. The opposite-number's route from Targaryen household premises (somewhere in the noble quarter or Red Keep adjacent) is shorter — the noble quarter to Visenya's Hill is an organized traverse, not a district crossing.

## Layout

**The approach streets (lower margin):** The streets that ascend Visenya's Hill toward the Sept's outer courts are wider than Flea Bottom alleys but narrower than the Dragonpit approach road — the width of a street designed for civic movement, not for large carts or military transit. They are swept more regularly than the streets below; the Faith maintains the approach streets as an extension of the Sept's presented face. Street vendors permitted in this approach zone are Faith-adjacent: candle-sellers, bead-sellers, the distributors of written prayers for the illiterate to purchase and have read by a septon. The goods being sold here are signals about what kind of space this is.

**The outer courts (primary scene space):** The courts that ring the Sept's base are paved — flagstone, maintained, with drains at intervals. The courts are not enclosed; they are open to the sky and bounded on one side by the Sept's outer wall and on the other three sides by low stone balustrades that mark the Faith's claimed territory from the approach streets. The balustrades are waist-height; they demarcate without blocking. People move through the courts continuously during daylight: those attending services entering and exiting the Sept's outer doors, those who have come to light a candle at the court shrines without entering the main building, those simply crossing the hill on foot using the court as a shortcut.

**Shrine stations in the outer courts:** At intervals along the Sept's outer wall, small alcove shrines to the Seven's individual aspects hold candle-tiers and offering bowls. The candle light at each station creates warm pockets of brightness against the Sept's stone; the attending septon or lay servant managing each station is a fixed human presence at the court's perimeter. These shrine stations are the natural pause-point for anyone in the court who is present on religious business — and the plausible reason for two people to slow or stop in proximity.

**The outer court crossing (the meeting point):** The court has two main foot-traffic flows: those arriving at the Sept's entrance (moving toward the central doors), and those crossing the court on a diagonal from the approach streets to the steps at the opposite corner. The diagonal crossing cuts across both flows. The opposite-number's route and Taylor's route intersect on this diagonal — briefly, in passing, near one of the shrine stations on the outer wall. The encounter is semi-accidental in the sense that neither person came here to find the other, but the patron's use of the Sept margin as a discrete passing-ground for occasional faction contacts means that both people being present here at the same time is not purely coincidental.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Incense is the distinctive note, arriving before the Sept's outer courts come into view — the specific compound of whatever the Faith's suppliers distribute to KL's primary religious site, which is better quality than the provincial sept smell and therefore more identifiable. This incense smell functions as a spatial warning system for Taylor: the incense means Faith territory, which means a different set of social rules than either Flea Bottom or the Velaryon townhouse. Underneath the incense, the outer courts have a clean-stone smell (maintained, not weathered) and a candle-wax note from the shrine stations.

**Sound:** The outer courts are quieter than the streets below despite being more open — the Sept's stone mass absorbs echo and the court's open sky vents it upward. The sounds present are human-voice at the volume of people observing a social quiet that nobody has enforced but everyone maintains in proximity to the Faith's primary building. Candles lit at shrine stations make no sound; the attending septors do. The sound register is the specific kind of restrained public space where people lower their voices without being asked.

**Visual:** The Sept's facade dominates the visual field from any point in the outer courts; there is no position in the court where the Sept itself is not the primary object. The courts are lit in daylight by open sky; in overcast conditions the stone takes on a cool grey tone that the candle stations punctuate with warm pockets. The people in the court are dressed across the social spectrum: pilgrims from outside the city in road clothes, KL residents in daily dress, the occasional noble or noble household member in more formal attire. The opposite-number is identifiable by clothes in this crowd — they are the figure whose dress does not match the surrounding average.

**Taylor's register in this space (project-specific):** The Sept's margin is specifically uncomfortable for Taylor in a way she does not immediately identify as institutional. The discomfort is not the Faith's aesthetics — it is the septon presence. The Faith has begun, in this period, to develop the witch-category as a formal social-control tool. The septors in the outer courts carry the specific behavioral signature of people who are trained to identify and respond to deviancy — not as individuals with malicious intent, but as institutional agents performing their institutional function. Taylor reads this correctly as a categorization threat and incorrectly assumes it is something she can manage by behavioral suppression. The actual risk is the institutional network: what a septon observes here can reach the Faith's administrative structure via channels that have nothing to do with the individual septon's personal interest.

## Fixed Props

- Shrine stations (architectural, outer wall alcoves) — candle-tier, offering bowl, attending septon
- Balustrade (architectural, court perimeter)

## Exits

- **Approach street descent (primary exit, multiple directions):** The approach streets descend from the court on three sides — north toward the noble quarter, east toward the city's middle tier and Flea Bottom, south toward the lower city and harbor approaches. Taylor's exit route is east; the opposite-number's is north.
- **Sept outer doors (interior access):** Entry to the Great Sept's interior. Not Taylor's destination in this scene; she does not enter the building. She knows the doors are behind her.
- **Court diagonal crossing (transit use):** The foot-traffic diagonal that both Taylor and the opposite-number use to cross the court. Its utility as an exit is that it leads to the step-descent on the opposite corner, which offers a different approach street to the north.

## Hazards

**Septon observation network:** The attending septors at the shrine stations are not individually dangerous; they are individually observant. What they observe enters the Faith's administrative awareness without the observer specifically intending to report anything. Taylor in the outer courts — a woman from Flea Bottom, with the specific presentation of someone who has survived without institutional support — is the kind of person that Faith-adjacent charity work is trained to identify. A septon who has heard the witch-reputation rumors about the Flea Bottom woman who knows things she should not would recognize the match.

**Social legibility of the crossing:** The opposite-number is a Targaryen household member; a recognized person. Taylor is an emerging recognized presence in Flea Bottom's social field. Their proximity in the outer courts — two people who have no stated social reason to be in the same space — is legible to anyone present who knows both identities. At story-open, the number of KL observers who know both identities is small; this number will not stay small.

**Flicker in Faith space:** The outer courts are not dragon-adjacent, but they are emotionally pressured — Taylor's discomfort with the septon presence adds a low background stress load that makes involuntary flicker-events more likely to produce visible reactions. A flicker episode in the outer courts, in front of an attending septon, would be precisely the kind of observable event that generates Faith-category reports.

**Weather exposure:** The outer courts are open to sky. Rain makes the flagstone slick; cold makes the open space unfriendly; heat in summer concentrates the incense smell into something that becomes an irritant rather than an ambient note. The courts offer no shelter beyond the Sept's doorway, which is not neutral territory for Taylor to enter.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A septon at the nearest shrine station interrupting the Taylor-opposite-number crossing to offer a blessing or a candle — the intrusion of institutional presence into the margin encounter
- A Flea Bottom resident crossing the court who recognizes Taylor from the district — the collision of Taylor's two social contexts in the same space, in front of the opposite-number
- The Faith's charity distribution in progress at the court's edge — lay brothers distributing food tokens to the poor, creating a cluster of Flea Bottom-adjacent faces near the crossing route
- Rain arriving during the crossing — the outer courts in rain are uncomfortable enough that people move faster, the encounter is shorter, the flicker-condition changes
- A Green-faction noble and household passing through the outer court on their way into the Sept — the opposite-number recognizing and being recognized, Taylor watching the social mechanics of a Targaryen household member navigating faction politics in a public space
- The candle-tier at the nearest shrine running short — the attending septon calling for replenishment from inside, a functional pause in the septon's observation of the court that creates a brief reduction in institutional witness
- A crying child at the court perimeter, being attended to by someone — the specific human-scale scene that interrupts Taylor's tactical mapping of the space with something that pulls her Gold-Morning response (civilian-casualty assessment, involuntary)
