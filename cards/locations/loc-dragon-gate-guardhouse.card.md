# Dragon Gate Watch Guardhouse — King's Landing (126–129 AC)

## Geography

The Dragon Gate precinct Watch station sits inside the city wall adjacent to the eastern gate arch, built into the wall's mass itself — the structure occupies a carved-out section of the wall's lower courses, with its entrance off the patrol staging yard that separates the gatehouse from the open market space. The wall on this side is thick enough to contain a working Watch station: cells, a log-room, a patrol briefing area, and the captain's office.

The position is deliberate and practical: the Watch station is close enough to the gate arch to respond to arrival problems, set back enough from the market traffic to be institutionally separate from the commercial district. Gate traffic passes in view of the station's exterior. Nothing passes through the gate that cannot be seen from the station's logbook windows.

The guardhouse is Taylor's first location in King's Landing (overnight detention at arrival, late summer 126 AC) and her last location in King's Landing (died in custody, month 36, ~129 AC). The sensory vocabulary of this space is the book's threshold register — the first and final notes of the same key.

## Layout

**Entrance and patrol yard:** An open staging area between the market's edge and the guardhouse door, used for patrol assembly and prisoner transfer. The yard is exposed to gate traffic on one side and Watch-only on the other. The entrance door is iron-banded oak, reinforced against the specific problem of gate-district crowd situations.

**Log-room (front):** The administrative heart of the station. One long table, the watch-log, record ledgers, the captain's administrative materials. The Watch officer on duty at the log-room controls prisoner intake, records review requests, and the daily patrol records. The log-room has a narrow window looking onto the patrol yard. Natural light is supplementary; the room relies on the hanging lamp that is always lit during watch hours.

**Captain's office (side, off log-room):** Small, door with its own latch. The captain reviews records here, meets with administrative contacts here, signs paperwork here. The office has the captain's specific smell: paper dust, the tallow smell of Watch lamp-oil, the faint harbor-salt that moves through the entire eastern wall regardless of season.

**Patrol briefing area (rear of log-room):** Open space where the patrol reports before and after shift. Benches along the walls, a board with the sector map and any standing orders. The space smells of bodies, worn leather, and the damp stone of the wall's interior.

**Cell block (below, via stairs):** Stone stairs descending from the patrol briefing area to the cell level, which is technically below street level though not underground — the cell level is in the wall's lower courses, and the exterior face of those courses is at ground level outside. Three cells: two general (roughly sleeping-three capacity), one isolation. The cells have the acoustic properties of heavy stone: sounds do not travel out easily, but sounds within carry between cells and up the stairwell.

The isolation cell is where Taylor spends her overnight detention at arrival. It is where she dies at close.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** The foundational smell of the guardhouse is stone — the specific mineral smell of old cut stone that has been damp and dry in alternation for a century. Over that: tallow lamp-oil, the particular body-smell of Watch officers in leather and wool who use this space continuously, and the gate-district's ambient — harbor salt, animal traffic from the gate, the faint rendering-fat smell of the market stalls' cooking. In the cell block: the smell is heavier, cooler, the stone smell predominant, the lamp-oil arriving from the upstairs as a thin note through the stairwell.

**Sound:** The gate's traffic — cart wheels, livestock, the gate-keeper's call — is audible from the cell block as a muted but continuous wash. Footsteps on stone above are clearly audible from the cells; the Watch officer's chair scraping on the log-room floor arrives as a specific note. Voices from the log-room travel down the stairwell as muffle rather than words. The cell door's iron latch has a specific sound: a clean mechanical click that carries in the stone.

**Light:** In the log-room: the hanging lamp, supplemented by the narrow logbook window. Good enough for administrative work; not warm. In the cell block: a wall-mounted lamp at the stairwell base provides minimal light across all three cells. At night, the cell block's light level is the minimum that prevents the lamp from being functionally absent. Taylor's first awareness of her first morning in King's Landing is gray light leaking through the stairwell from above before the lamp is re-trimmed.

**Temperature:** The stone holds cold. The cell block is cold at night and cool in the day regardless of season. The patrol briefing area has a brazier but uses it only in winter — the rest of the year, the stone's ambient temperature is the ambient temperature.

**The insect register:** Gate-district insects are present throughout. Flies that follow the market traffic come through the gate and drift toward the guardhouse entrance in the heat. The cell block has beetles in the wall-seams — the old stone's mortar gaps are colonized by small beetles that have been in the wall longer than the current Watch garrison. Taylor knows these beetles. They are the edges of her ambient awareness in both scenes. At arrival they are registering her distress-response. At close they are the last things reporting.

## Fixed Props

- Iron log-book table (log-room)
- Hanging oil lamp, log-room
- Wall-mounted lamp, cell-block stairwell base
- Cell-block door, iron latch, specific click
- Isolation cell door, iron-bar slot at eye-level
- Captain's seal and stamp apparatus (captain's office)
- Sector map board (patrol briefing area)
- Brazier (patrol briefing area, active winter only)

## Exits

- **Main entrance** — patrol yard, then onto the market/gate-adjacent open space. The primary exit under normal conditions. Guarded at all times during watch hours.
- **Gate access** — a Watch-personnel-only passage through the wall connects the guardhouse to the gate arch's upper level (the gate-keeper's watch platform). Not an exit for detainees.
- **The stairs** — the cell block has no exit except the stairs up to the patrol briefing area. The stairwell is the only path.

The isolation cell has one exit: the door. The door has one latch, from the outside. There is no window.

## Hazards

- **The acoustic problem:** Sounds in the cell block travel selectively — up to the log-room via the stairwell, and between cells via the stone. A detainee in the isolation cell can hear the general patrol briefing area but cannot make out words. The log-room can hear a detainee calling from the cell block if they are loud enough. This creates a specific acoustic isolation: close enough to hear that people are present, far enough to be functionally unreachable.
- **The cell block's cold:** Sustained cold in the cell block is a health hazard for extended detention. The Watch does not have a warming provision for the cell block. A person in fragile health — or whose health deteriorates under detention — is at risk.
- **Persons-risk detention protocol:** The Watch persons-risk protocol holds detainees for processing before release or escalation. Processing time is variable; the cell block's conditions during an extended sweep are the conditions of prolonged cold-and-minimal-light confinement. The contact's death in the M27–28 sweep occurs under these conditions. Not torture; the conditions of correct procedure applied to a man whose health the conditions do not account for.
- **The insect-ambient edge:** Taylor's 100–150m range cap means that inside the isolation cell, the edges of her ambient register are the building's walls. She knows what is happening in range. She cannot act on it. The cap is the structural enforcer of her inability to interrupt the sweep once the isolation cell door closes.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- Gate-traffic sound increases sharply when the gate arch is in heavy use (dawn and dusk, market days, caravans); the wave of sound reaches the cell block as texture change
- Watch shift-change: regular, audible via the patrol briefing area's foot traffic and the stairwell
- Other detainees in the general cells: sounds, voices, the general acoustic presence of people in adjacent stone rooms
- The wall's insect ecology: beetles in the mortar gaps, continuous low-level ambient presence regardless of time of day
- Lamp-trimming: the wall lamp is re-trimmed at shift change; the brief period of reduced light before re-trimming is a recurring acoustic/atmospheric event
- Harbor salt in the air: when the eastern gate is open and wind is from the river, the salt-smell becomes more pronounced in the cell block — a weather signal that costs nothing to render
