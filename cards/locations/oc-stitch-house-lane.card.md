## Geography

A narrow service lane off the Hook District's lower grid, Flea Bottom, King's Landing. Runs between the stitch-house (a garment-repair workshop on the east wall) and the angle of two converging tenement walls. The lane terminates at a junction lane-mouth to the north. Width: one cart plus pressed shoulders. Length: roughly forty paces.

## Layout

- **Angle-gap** — the pinch-point where the two tenement walls converge at the lane's south end; drain water channels through the gap at street level. The narrowing is visible from any point in the lane.
- **Lane-mouth** — the north-end opening onto the broader Hook District alley. Constitutes a threshold: anyone at the lane-mouth controls line-of-sight both into the lane and out to the alley.
- **Stitch-house wall** — east side. Ground-floor workshop; tallow lamps burn through working hours; smoke carries into the lane at floor-level.

## Sensory Vocabulary

Tallow smoke from the stitch-house lamp, pooling at lane-floor level in still air. The drain-water trickle at the angle-gap — audible when the lane is quiet. Compressed foot-traffic sound. Cobblestone underfoot, uneven at the angle-wall side.

## Exits

- Lane-mouth (north) → Hook District alley network
- Angle-gap (south) → drain channel; not a traversable exit for persons

## Hazards

Lane width forces single-file or pressed-shoulder movement at moderate crowd density; crowd compression blocks retreat. The angle-wall side loses direct lamplight.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- **Stitch-house door opening mid-scene** — throws a rectangle of lamplight into the lane from the east wall; anyone standing near the wall is briefly illuminated. Door may open for a delivery, a departing customer, or a worker stepping out. Closes in seconds, but the moment of exposure is real.
- **Lamp goes out in the stitch-house** — abrupt darkness on the east side. Could be closing time, an accident, or trouble inside. The lane goes from dim to nearly lightless on the east half.
- **Crowd press at the lane-mouth** — a Watch patrol or a surge in the alley beyond pushes foot-traffic into the lane-mouth. People back up, compressing the already-narrow space. Forward movement stalls; the angle-gap becomes the only alternative direction.
- **Private exchange at the angle-gap** — two people using the gap for a hushed conversation; anyone entering the lane from the north breaks their cover line-of-sight. They may stop, reposition, or react.
- **Drain-water trickle stopping** — the trickle at the angle-gap is a constant ambient note. When it stops, something upstream has blocked it. Small enough to be ignored; notable enough to register as wrong. Historically signals debris accumulation, a body, or deliberate blockage.
- **Delivery to the stitch-house** — a cart or porter with a bale of cloth or hides turning into the lane from the lane-mouth; the cart fills the width, blocking the lane until the delivery completes. Not hostile; just impassable for several minutes.
- **A child posted to wait at the angle-gap** — sent out of earshot while adults conduct nearby business. Sits in the drain-shadow, unmoving. Visible from the center of the lane; easy to overlook against the darkened wall angle.
- **Tallow-smoke plume reversal** — wind direction shift pulls the smoke toward the lane-mouth rather than pooling at floor-level; momentarily fills the lane at nose-height. Signals weather change or a fire upwind; masks smell and slightly stings the eyes of anyone passing through.
