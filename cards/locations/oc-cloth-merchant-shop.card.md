# The Cloth-Merchant's Shop — Hook South End, Flea Bottom

## Geography

A covered shopfront at the Hook's south end — the point where the Hook's curved waterfront alley network meets the overland lane feeding into the broader Flea Bottom grid. The shop sits on a corner position: one face opens onto the Hook lane, the other faces the cross-lane approach that runs inland toward the tallow and rendering wards. Position makes it a natural observation point without being an obvious one — a man standing at the worktable in the back of the shop can hear lane traffic on two sides.

The cloth trade here is genuine: the merchant stocks bolt-cloth and cut-lengths from Hook dockside unloading and from minor wholesale suppliers, and the shop does enough trade to sustain a routine. It is not prosperous. The stock is utilitarian — undyed linen, coarse wool, the practical cloth Flea Bottom buys. The shop's legitimacy is its cover; the cover sustains because the trade is real.

In 122 AC the cloth-merchant has been a node in the Black-faction intelligence structure for at least six months by the time of b01c11. The node function is passive receipt of courier traffic — the merchant does not analyze or route intelligence, only receives sealed packets from off-hour messengers and burns their contents in the iron dish after reading. The standing protocol means the shop's Black-faction function requires no improvisation: the same physical sequence (receive, read, burn) executes each time.

## Layout

**Shopfront (street-facing):** The public face of the shop. Shallow depth — a counter-length display of folded bolt-cloth, a bolt-frame at the wall, and a passage to the back. The counter is the merchant's public workspace: bolt-tickets, cutting board, a small weight-balance for priced-by-weight remnants. The shopfront is visible from the Hook lane and partially visible from the cross-lane.

**Back worktable:** The functional second space behind the shopfront partition — a narrow worktable running along the shop's rear wall. This is where the merchant does ledger work, off-hours receiving, and the burn-protocol when a courier packet arrives. The worktable is separated from the shopfront by a hanging curtain of rough-woven cloth (a functional partition, not decorative). The back worktable space is not visible from the street.

**Rushlight position:** A single rushlight holder is fixed to the wall above the back worktable — the primary light source for the back space. Low-yield, positioned for close work (reading, writing bolt-tickets). The rushlight is lit during off-hours work; during open-shop hours the shopfront's ambient light is sufficient for the counter. When the rushlight is lit in the back, the shop's public appearance does not change.

**Iron dish:** A small iron-cast dish with a flat bottom and shallow sides, kept on the back worktable's left corner. Used for the burn protocol: a sealed courier packet's contents are read and then burned in the dish, reducing to ash before the merchant returns to routine. The dish retains heat after burning — the residual warmth is a physical fact that survives longer than the burn event itself. The dish's position on the worktable puts it within the ambient zone of the back space.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Wool and linen — the flat, pressed-cloth smell of a working bolt-stock. Slightly dusty (cloth accumulates ambient particulate). The back worktable carries a secondary note of rushlight tallow when lit — the same tallow-smoke register that characterizes the adjacent rendering-ward lanes, but here contained and close-range (single light source, enclosed space). After a burn: a brief char and paper-smoke note that clears quickly in a small space with a draft.

**Sound:** The shopfront produces the ambient sound of cloth-trade work: the drag of bolt-cloth across the counter-top, the snap of cut linen, the soft thud of bolt-ticket writing. The Hook lane noise enters through the front; the cross-lane approach noise enters at low register through the corner wall. The back worktable is quieter — the hanging-cloth partition absorbs some of the front-facing sound. A messenger crossing the threshold produces a recognizable door-passage sound (threshold creak or doorway shadow); the off-hour entry is acoustically distinct from open-shop-hours foot traffic.

**Light:** Variable by time and back-worktable state. Shopfront: adequate ambient during working hours (the Hook lane, while narrow, provides diffuse daylight). Back space: low-light default, rushlight-dependent for the close work. When the rushlight is lit in low-rushlight conditions (early morning, overcast, or off-hours), the back space is legible for close work but not for reading at a distance.

**Texture:** Bolt-cloth surface on the counter — the specific texture varies by stock (linen smooth, wool coarse). The iron dish surface: cast iron, slightly rough from use, warm after a burn. The back worktable surface: worn wood, grain visible from years of use, the texture of a working surface that has absorbed its work.

## Fixed Props

- **Iron dish** — the burn-protocol implement; fixed to the back worktable's left corner. Present-at-position throughout. See prop card for full detail if authored separately; for card reference purposes: small, iron-cast, flat-bottomed, 4-5 inches across.
- **Rushlight (back-worktable mount)** — the single fixed light source for the back space. Position: wall above the worktable. State tracked at scene-level (lit / unlit); default is unlit during open-shop daylight hours.

## Exits

- **Hook lane (front-facing)** — primary access from the Hook's main alley traffic. The natural entry point for open-shop customers and for a messenger who wants to appear as ordinary foot traffic.
- **Cross-lane (corner-facing)** — secondary access from the inland cross-lane approach. A messenger arriving from the soap-rendering lane direction would approach this way; less observed than the Hook lane entry.
- **No rear exit.** The shop's rear wall is a shared wall with the adjacent building. Exit from the back worktable requires returning through the shopfront.

## Hazards

- **Burn-protocol smoke signature:** A paper-burn in a small enclosed space produces detectable char and smoke. In the back space this disperses quickly through any gap at the shopfront, but during the burn event the smell is present at close range. An observer in the insect-feed's ambient zone around the back worktable will register the thermal-shift and the smoke-curl before the fire is extinguished. The burn is designed to complete before the smoke becomes visible to the shopfront or the street; the design works under normal conditions.
- **Off-hour messenger visibility:** A messenger crossing the Hook-lane-facing threshold at an hour when no order of cloth is placed is a timing anomaly visible to any consistent observer of the shop's daily pattern. The standing protocol is calibrated to minimize time at the threshold (no loitering, direct approach, direct exit), but the anomaly cannot be entirely eliminated — the threshold is observable.
- **Courier-trace geometry:** The shop sits at a corner where the Hook lane and cross-lane approach provide two vectors of approach and observation. An insect-feed covering the cross-lane has ambient-zone access to the back worktable's thermal signature without requiring physical proximity to the shopfront.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A customer arriving during an off-hour burn sequence — the merchant must manage the shopfront while the back space is still in burn-protocol mode
- A bolt-ticket discrepancy requiring the merchant to produce records going further back than the current day
- The rushlight running low mid-sequence, requiring relighting — a delay in the burn protocol that extends the window of observable smoke
- Weather making the Hook lane temporarily more or less trafficked, changing the observability calculus for an off-hour messenger
- A Watch patrol on the Hook lane passing during the window between messenger-arrival and burn-completion
- The iron dish showing visible residue ash accumulation from prior burns — physical evidence of a pattern, not a single event
