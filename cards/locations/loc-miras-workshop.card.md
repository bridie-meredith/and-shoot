# Mira's Rope Workshop — Dragon Gate Block (126–129 AC)

## Geography

Ground floor of a permanent commercial building in the inner alley, Dragon Gate block — one of the established craft operations that serves the gate-traffic's maritime-adjacent supply needs. The workshop is approximately eight meters wide and twelve deep, with the working space on the ground floor and Mira's personal use of a half-loft above the rear section. The building shares walls with a cloth trader on one side and a metalmonger on the other; the shared-wall sound profile is constant background.

The workshop is Mira's by occupation, not ownership. She has held the space for nine years under a guild-registered lease arrangement. The landlord is a minor merchant whose interest in the space is rental income, not management. The space has been configured for rope-work: the floor, the fixtures, the specific layout of the braiding equipment. Mira did the configuring. The space reflects her work habits.

The workshop is the primary site of Taylor's genuine-attachment relationship with Mira. It is where Taylor comes more than workshop business requires, and where Mira has allowed this without making it a subject.

## Layout

**Working floor:** The bulk of the space. Against the rear wall: the braiding frame, a large fixed wooden structure where the rope is worked, with foot-pedal mechanism and the specific set of hooks and guides that the rope-braiding process requires. Along the north wall: fiber storage — coils of raw fiber in various states of preparation (washed, combed, twisted-to-strand). Center floor: the work-table for smaller rope tasks, cutting, finishing. The floor is flagstone, worn smooth where Mira's work path runs. It is clean in a working-space way: no debris that would catch in the fiber, dust managed.

**Counter/front area:** A narrow counter at the front, facing the alley. This is where customer transactions happen. Finished rope on organized pegs behind the counter: different gauges, different materials, the guild-standard labels on each category. A simple ledger for sales.

**Half-loft (rear, above working floor):** Mira's personal space, accessed by a steep ladder in the workshop's back corner. Not visible from the ground floor except as the overhead structure. Used for sleeping, personal belongings, the kinds of non-work activities a person needs privacy for. The loft is small. The loft is hers.

**The alley door:** The front door to the inner alley, which can be propped open during working hours. When open, the workshop's interior is visible from the alley and the workshop's sound — the foot-pedal rhythm, the braiding frame — is audible. When closed (after working hours), the workshop is dark and quiet.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** The dominant smell is fiber and lanolin — raw rope fiber carries a specific animal-wool-and-oil smell that saturates the space and Mira's clothing and does not fully leave either. Underneath: the wood smell of the braiding frame and the work-table, the specific mineral smell of the flagstone floor. When the front door is open, the market alley's ambient smells mix in (cooking, animal, dust). When closed, the workshop smell is self-contained and thick.

**Sound:** The braiding frame when working: a specific repetitive rhythm of the foot-pedal (a low mechanical click-thud), the drag of rope through the guides (a continuous sibilant friction), the occasional correction-knock when Mira adjusts the tension. This sound is the workshop's primary ambient. It is the sound Taylor has been hearing through the wall from the alley for a long time before she first entered. When the braiding frame is idle, the workshop is quiet — the comparative silence is its own note.

From the shared walls: the cloth trader's fabric-handling sounds on one side (soft, intermittent), the metalmonger's hammer-and-anvil on the other (louder, more variable). The metalmonger's percussion is the acoustic challenge; serious conversation in the workshop happens in the natural pauses.

**Light:** The alley front provides the primary natural light through the door and a narrow high window. The braiding frame is positioned to have the best use of the alley light for detail work. In the afternoons when the wall's shadow falls on the alley, and after working hours, the workshop runs on a hanging lamp. The lamp is positioned over the work-table; the braiding frame is in relative shadow.

**Temperature:** Flagstone floor, shared walls, no direct sun — the workshop runs cool. In summer it is the most comfortable space on the inner alley; in winter it is cold. Mira works in the cold without apparent difficulty. Visitors who are not accustomed to standing on stone for hours become aware of it.

**Touch/texture:** The rope fiber is the tactile vocabulary of this space. The raw fiber before working: rough, slightly oily, springy. The worked rope in progress: smooth developing twist, the specific tension of a strand under correct tension. Finished rope: the specific density and weight of a good braid. Mira's hands hold this vocabulary continuously; Taylor registers it through the insect-ambient and through the specific sound-texture of the working frame.

## Fixed Props

- Braiding frame (large, fixed, rear wall)
- Foot-pedal mechanism (braiding frame)
- Fiber storage coils (north wall)
- Work-table (center floor)
- Counter and rope-peg display (front)
- Hanging lamp (over work-table)
- Sales ledger (counter)
- Half-loft ladder (back corner)

## Exits

- **Alley door (front)** — the primary entrance and exit, onto the inner alley of the Dragon Gate market zone. Propped open during working hours; latched after.
- **Shared-wall pass-through (none)** — no connecting doors to the adjacent buildings. Exit is the alley door only.

Taylor's range cap means the workshop is well within her ambient awareness whenever she is in the inner alley. She knows the occupants of the workshop before she opens the door. She has known for the entire three years whether Mira is present or absent.

## Hazards

- **The loft's single exit:** The half-loft has one access: the ladder. A person in the loft has no alternate exit. This is not normally relevant; it is relevant if the workshop becomes the site of an emergency.
- **Fire hazard:** Raw fiber and lamp-oil in a stone-walled workspace with shared walls. A fire in the cloth trader's space on the shared wall side is the most likely ignition vector. The market alley has fire-response procedures; their speed is not reliable.
- **Guild-inspection exposure:** Mira's lease and guild registration are visible to guild inspectors. If something brings guild attention to the workshop, the lease and registration are auditable.
- **Watch visibility:** The inner alley is within regular Watch patrol coverage. The workshop is not a place where unusual activities would go unobserved by patrol-routine. Mira manages this through nine years of being the unremarkable rope-braider. Taylor's visits are within the range of explainable customer traffic — barely.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- Braiding frame idle versus running: the sound-shift when Mira puts down the work is the acoustic signal that her full attention has shifted
- Customer arrival from the alley: the counter transaction interrupts any other conversation with the specific brevity of commercial exchange
- Metalmonger's hammer-rhythm: the natural pauses in the metalmonger's work create the spaces in which extended conversation is possible; sustained hammering prevents it
- The alley's traffic outside: when the alley is busy (market-day morning), the workshop's ambient is the working sound against the market background; when quiet (late afternoon), the workshop's sound is isolated
- The loft's presence: the ladder at the back of the workshop is always a presence — a reminder of the personal space above, of the privacy Mira maintains within the work-space, of the distinction between work-floor and life
- Evening lamp-lighting: when Mira lights the lamp for after-hours work, the light quality of the workshop shifts entirely — the alley is dark outside, the lamp's amber light is self-contained, the workshop becomes a different register of space
