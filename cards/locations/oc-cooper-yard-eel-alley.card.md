# Cooper's Yard off Eel Alley — Courier Handoff Location

## Geography

A working cooperage yard set two cuts north of the river-side stalls in Flea Bottom, reached by following Eel Alley inland from the Hook and turning at the second lane-junction. The yard is near enough to the Hook that the tidal smell off the Blackwater Rush reaches it in still weather, far enough from the third-bell market that foot traffic does not extend to the lane-mouth once the market closes. It is not on any route a deliberate traveler takes; it is on a route a barrel-carter takes.

The ward here is one tier above the Hook's most marginal housing — small-trade owners with rented yards, not itinerant labor — but the cooperage draws working-class custom only. The yard's owner is not narratively significant. The work is legitimate smallfolk commerce.

Eel Alley itself is a secondary lane, not a through-route. Its traffic is barrel-delivery carts, the cooperage's own workforce, and residents of the surrounding tenements running household errands. After the third bell, foot traffic on Eel Alley drops to near zero; what remains is the cooperage's close-of-day work and the occasional nightsoil run.

## Layout

The yard opens off the lane-mouth of the alley branch north of Eel Alley proper. The lane-mouth is the single approach — anyone entering or leaving the yard must pass through it. From the lane-mouth, a sightline into the yard is broken almost immediately by the angle of the shed's near wall; a watcher at the lane-mouth cannot see the interior of the yard without entering.

**Near shed** — the first structure encountered on entering: a roofed working area where the coopers do the labor-intensive assembly work. Stave-bending equipment, hoops, adzes. Two workers typically occupy this shed during working hours. The shed wall provides cover for anyone standing against it — back to the wall, clear line to the yard-mouth, unobserved from Eel Alley.

**Open yard** — the central space between the near shed and the far end. Stacked finished barrels, hoop rings in organized rows, sawdust and shaving underfoot. The yard is large enough for a dray-cart to turn. It is not large enough for a crowd.

**Far end stacking area** — the northern boundary of the yard, where finished barrels are staged for delivery pickup and empty returns are sorted. A boy typically works here on hoop-stacking and counting during the day. The far end is functionally separated from the near shed by the distance of the open yard — two people at opposite ends cannot hear a low-voiced conversation at the other end.

**Lane-mouth** — the single exit point. Wide enough for a cart; the angle of approach from Eel Alley means that a person emerging from the yard cannot be seen from the main lane until they are already in the lane-mouth. Jarvis Coin uses the lane-mouth position (three steps inside, back exposed to Eel Alley) as his standing position during handoff meetings.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Dominant: tallow-damp. The lane-caulking on Eel Alley two alleys over uses tallow-and-oakum as sealant, and the smell carries on still air — particularly in early morning and at the close of the working day when there is less foot-traffic to displace it. The yard adds fresh-cut wood and sawdust shavings to the baseline. In damp weather, wet oak and the particular sharpness of green stave-wood. The tallow-damp is a consistent anchor — it is present every visit regardless of weather or time of bell, and it functions as a location-marker before the yard is visible.

**Sound:** During working hours: the rhythmic knock of mallet and hoop-driver; low-voiced coordination between the near-shed workers; the scrape of finished barrels being moved across the yard stones. After the third bell: near-silence. The lane settles. Barrel work does not continue after dark. What remains is ambient Flea Bottom — distant alley conversation, the occasional cart on Eel Alley proper.

**Light:** The near shed has a south-facing opening; it is adequately lit during working hours and dim by the late afternoon. The open yard is unroofed — full sky, which in King's Landing's lower district means partial shade from the tenement overhangs on two sides. At first bell in the morning, the yard is in pre-dawn grey; the near-shed interior is darker than the yard.

**Texture:** Sawdust and shavings on the yard floor — compressed but not packed; soft underfoot compared to the cobbled lane outside. The near-shed wall is rough timber, weathered. The hoop-rows at the far end are cold iron, damp in morning.

## Fixed Props

None card-level. The cooperage's equipment (mallet, adze, hoop-driver, stave-clamps) is workshop property and not individually carded. Finished barrels and hoop-stacks are runtime state.

## Exits

- **Lane-mouth (south)** — the single approach and exit. Opens to the Eel Alley branch lane, which connects to Eel Alley proper. No secondary exit from the yard itself; the near shed and far end are bounded by tenement walls on two and three sides respectively.

**Sightline note:** The lane-mouth's angle relative to Eel Alley means there is no clear sightline from Eel Alley into the yard. A watcher on Eel Alley cannot identify persons inside the yard without entering the branch lane. This is the property that made the yard Jarvis Coin's selection for the Otto Hightower proposal handoff.

## Hazards

- **Single-exit constraint** — the lane-mouth is the only way in or out. Two people positioned at the lane-mouth can control egress completely. This makes the yard an excellent private meeting space under normal conditions and a containment trap if the lane-mouth is blocked.
- **Third-bell foot traffic window** — the yard's traffic-quiet window is reliable but not infinite. It depends on the third-bell market dispersal. A market disruption (Watch sweep, incident at the Hook) can change the foot-traffic pattern on Eel Alley unpredictably.
- **Worker presence in working hours** — during the day's working bells, the near-shed workers and the hoop-stacking boy are present. They are not threats, but they are witnesses to who enters the yard. Low-voiced conversation in the open yard at the near-shed wall is not audible to the far-end boy; it may be audible to near-shed workers if they are attentive.
- **Tallow-damp as sensory marker** — the distinctive smell anchors the yard to anyone who has visited it once. A surveillance tail who has visited the yard can identify the approach route by smell.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A delivery cart arriving early to pick up a barrel order — blocking the lane-mouth for the duration of loading
- The near-shed workers finishing early and leaving the yard empty before the usual closing bell, disrupting the expected workforce-present cover
- A neighboring tenement resident cutting through the branch lane to Eel Alley proper — possible if the main Eel Alley route is blocked
- The hoop-stacking boy leaving his position at the far end and approaching the near shed at an unexpected moment
- Rain arriving before the meeting — the near shed provides cover, but the lane-mouth is exposed, changing the standing geometry
- A Watch sweep passing down Eel Alley during the meeting window — audible before visible from inside the yard
