# Forest Clearing, Dusk

## Geography

A natural clearing in temperate Westerosi forest — the kind of gap that opens where a tree came down years ago and the canopy hasn't grown back in. Roughly thirty feet across at the widest. The surrounding forest is dense enough that the light dies fast at the treeline; the clearing itself holds the last of the dusk light for a few minutes longer than the wood around it. Could be anywhere between the Trident and the Neck: oak, elm, bracken, old leaf-rot underfoot.

## Layout

- Fallen log near the clearing's center or edge — old enough to be stable, mossy, flat enough to sit on or use as a work surface.
- Open sky above the clearing, roughly circular. Stars visible in the gap if you look up; trees wall all other sightlines.
- Treeline on all sides within thirty feet. Someone moving in the trees is audible before they're visible.
- The ground is uneven but not hazardous: leaf litter, bracken, occasional exposed root.

Three exits, all informal: the paths game and people have worn through the treeline. None marked. Direction derivable by the sound of water (one exit leads toward a stream), the sound of road-traffic (one opens eventually toward the King's Road or a track), and one that leads deeper into the wood.

## Sensory Vocabulary

- **Sound:** Wind through the canopy — higher, colder than ground level. A wood-pigeon somewhere in the oaks, going quiet as dusk falls. The snap of a twig when something moves in the treeline. A stream in the middle distance, audible when the wind drops.
- **Smell:** Leaf rot. Cold bark. The specific iron-cold smell of evening coming in over standing water. Woodsmoke if anyone has been here recently.
- **Light:** The clearing holds the last grey-gold of dusk for a few minutes. Then: dark fast. The treeline is already black.
- **Temperature:** Cold that arrives with the dark, faster than expected. The clearing loses its daytime warmth within minutes of the sun dropping below the treeline.

## Exits

- **Toward the stream** — eastward through the treeline, following the sound of water. Game trail, barely.
- **Toward the road** — southward, track widens as you go. Twenty minutes to the King's Road or a comparable route.
- **Deeper into the wood** — northward. No clear trail. For people who don't want to be found.

## Hazards

- **Rapid dark.** The clearing loses usable light within minutes of the sun dropping below the treeline. Exits that were visible by landmark become exits navigable only by memory or sound. Anyone who has not memorized their route before dusk will have difficulty leaving without noise.
- **Treeline concealment.** Anyone stationary inside the trees has complete cover while retaining sightlines into the clearing. The clearing's openness is a disadvantage for anyone standing in it; an ambush position is available to anyone who was there first.
- **Exposed to weather.** The gap in the canopy that makes the clearing a clearing also makes it the most exposed point in the wood. Rain hits it first and hardest; wind that the surrounding trees filter reaches the clearing unbroken. In winter, frost accumulates on the clearing floor before the paths do.
- **Stream crossing in dark.** The stream exit requires navigating toward sound rather than sight once dark falls. The crossing itself is shallow but uneven; a missed step at full dark is a wet boot at minimum.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- **The wood-pigeon stops.** A wood-pigeon in the oaks is the clearing's constant sound from late afternoon until full dark. When it stops mid-call, something in that direction moved. Useful: tells direction, not identity.
- **Woodsmoke.** The clearing is clear enough to carry smoke from outside it. Woodsmoke arriving on the wind means a fire somewhere upwind — a camp, a village, a burning. Not necessarily close.
- **A dog on a farm.** A farm within half a mile of this clearing will have dogs. Farm dogs that bark at dusk are barking at something: travelers on the road, a predator on the margin, or nothing at all. Directionality is meaningful.
- **Rustling that doesn't resolve.** Game moves through the treeline constantly at dusk. Most rustling is deer, rabbit, or wind-disturbed bracken. Rustling that continues at a consistent rate without resolving into a visible animal is the sound of something staying still.
- **A second fire-smell.** Woodsmoke is already in the sensory vocabulary. A second, distinct smoke-smell — sharper, closer — is someone lighting a fire within the treeline or just outside it.
- **The stream changes.** The stream in the middle distance has a baseline sound: low, consistent. When that sound shifts — louder from rainfall upstream, quieter from someone upstream disturbing the flow — the shift is audible from the clearing if the wind is right.
