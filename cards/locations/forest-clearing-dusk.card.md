# Forest Clearing, Dusk

## Geography

A natural clearing in temperate Westerosi forest — the kind of gap that opens where a tree came down years ago and the canopy hasn't grown back in. Roughly thirty feet across at the widest. The surrounding forest is dense enough that the light dies fast at the treeline; the clearing itself holds the last of the dusk light for a few minutes longer than the wood around it. Could be anywhere between the Trident and the Neck: oak, elm, bracken, old leaf-rot underfoot.

## Layout

- Fallen log near the clearing's center or edge — old enough to be stable, mossy, flat enough to sit on or use as a work surface.
- Open sky above the clearing, roughly circular. Stars visible in the gap if you look up; trees wall all other sightlines.
- Treeline on all sides within thirty feet. Someone moving in the trees is audible before they're visible.
- The ground is uneven but not hazardous: leaf litter, bracken, occasional exposed root.

Three exits, all informal: the paths game and people have worn through the treeline. None marked. Direction derivable by the sound of water (one exit leads toward a stream), the sound of road-traffic (one opens eventually toward the King's Road or a track), and one that leads deeper into the wood.

## Sensory Vocabulary

- **Sound:** Wind through the canopy — higher, colder than ground level. A wood-pigeon somewhere in the oaks, going quiet as dusk falls. The snap of a twig when something moves in the treeline. A stream in the middle distance, audible when the wind drops.
- **Smell:** Leaf rot. Cold bark. The specific iron-cold smell of evening coming in over standing water. Woodsmoke if anyone has been here recently.
- **Light:** The clearing holds the last grey-gold of dusk for a few minutes. Then: dark fast. The treeline is already black.
- **Temperature:** Cold that arrives with the dark, faster than expected. The clearing loses its daytime warmth within minutes of the sun dropping below the treeline.

## Fixed Props

None catalogued.

## Exits

- **Toward the stream** — eastward through the treeline, following the sound of water. Game trail, barely.
- **Toward the road** — southward, track widens as you go. Twenty minutes to the King's Road or a comparable route.
- **Deeper into the wood** — northward. No clear trail. For people who don't want to be found.

## Hazards

- **Dark arrives fast.** From first-star to full dark is fifteen minutes in the clearing; less at the treeline. Anyone who misjudges the remaining light gets caught in near-total darkness under the canopy.
- **Treeline concealment cuts both ways.** An observer in the trees has a clear sightline into the clearing; someone in the clearing cannot see into the wood beyond five or six feet of depth. Anyone moving in is invisible until they step out.
- **The fallen log is wet and rotten at its underside.** Weight applied to the wrong section fails without warning; anyone using it as a work surface or seat in the dark risks a fall.
- **Wildlife at dusk.** Boar and deer are active at this hour. Neither reliably retreats from a human who isn't moving; a boar that beds in the clearing's bracken before a party arrives is a hazard if startled.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- The wood-pigeon in the oaks goes abruptly quiet — something moving in the treeline.
- The stream sound cuts off or changes register: water level rising upstream, or something large standing in the ford.
- A torch or cookfire visible through the treeline in the wrong direction — someone else has camp in the adjacent wood.
- A rider on the road (audible before visible) slowing at the treeline gap that leads toward this clearing.
- An animal moves through the bracken at clearing-edge without crossing into the open — circling.
