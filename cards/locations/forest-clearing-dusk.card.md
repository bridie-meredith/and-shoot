# Forest Clearing, Dusk

## Geography

A natural clearing in temperate Westerosi forest — the kind of gap that opens where a tree came down years ago and the canopy hasn't grown back in. Roughly thirty feet across at the widest. The surrounding forest is dense enough that the light dies fast at the treeline; the clearing itself holds the last of the dusk light for a few minutes longer than the wood around it. Could be anywhere between the Trident and the Neck: oak, elm, bracken, old leaf-rot underfoot.

## Layout

- Fallen log near the clearing's center or edge — old enough to be stable, mossy, flat enough to sit on or use as a work surface.
- Open sky above the clearing, roughly circular. Stars visible in the gap if you look up; trees wall all other sightlines.
- Treeline on all sides within thirty feet. Someone moving in the trees is audible before they're visible.
- The ground is uneven but not hazardous: leaf litter, bracken, occasional exposed root.

Three exits, all informal: the paths game and people have worn through the treeline. None marked. Direction derivable by the sound of water (one exit leads toward a stream), the sound of road-traffic (one opens eventually toward the King's Road or a track), and one that leads deeper into the wood.

## Sensory Vocabulary

- **Sound:** Wind through the canopy — higher, colder than ground level. A wood-pigeon somewhere in the oaks, going quiet as dusk falls. The snap of a twig when something moves in the treeline. A stream in the middle distance, audible when the wind drops.
- **Smell:** Leaf rot. Cold bark. The specific iron-cold smell of evening coming in over standing water. Woodsmoke if anyone has been here recently.
- **Light:** The clearing holds the last grey-gold of dusk for a few minutes. Then: dark fast. The treeline is already black.
- **Temperature:** Cold that arrives with the dark, faster than expected. The clearing loses its daytime warmth within minutes of the sun dropping below the treeline.

## Fixed Props

None. The clearing is a natural formation. The fallen log is a permanent feature of the layout but is not a prop card — it is part of the geography.

## Exits

- **Toward the stream** — eastward through the treeline, following the sound of water. Game trail, barely.
- **Toward the road** — southward, track widens as you go. Twenty minutes to the King's Road or a comparable route.
- **Deeper into the wood** — northward. No clear trail. For people who don't want to be found.

## Hazards

- **Darkness after dusk.** The clearing loses usable light within minutes of the sun dropping below the treeline. Movement through the treeline in full dark risks twisted ankles, low branches, disorientation. Anyone without a torch is effectively stopped.
- **Treeline concealment.** The surrounding forest is dense enough to hide an observer within ten feet of the clearing edge. A conversation in the clearing carries to anyone standing still in the treeline. Characters cannot safely assume they are unobserved.
- **Animal presence — nighttime escalation.** After dark, the clearing becomes a hunting corridor for owls and bats; larger animals move through at the edges (boar in the bracken, fox, occasionally deer). Nothing immediately dangerous under normal conditions, but a panicked response to an animal in the dark has its own costs. Wolves are a seasonal hazard in lean winters anywhere north of the Reach.
- **Weather exposure.** The open sky that gives the clearing its light is the same opening that lets in rain and wind. In a squall, the clearing is the worst place to be — exposed to both the wind funneled by the treeline and the rain with no canopy overhead.
- **Sound carry.** The clearing is acoustically anomalous: sounds from inside carry to the treeline clearly, but sounds from the treeline or road are muffled and directionally ambiguous. Characters inside frequently misjudge where an approaching sound is coming from.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A wood-pigeon or crow calls from the treeline — may signal a disturbance in the direction it called from, or may be routine.
- Something moves in the bracken at the clearing edge. Pause. Fades. Could be a boar; could be a person.
- The stream sound drops out or changes direction: the wind has shifted, which also means the temperature is about to drop.
- Distant dog-bark: a settlement, closer than expected, upwind.
- Torchlight moving through the treeline at road-distance — not approaching, but visible between the trees. Someone traveling the road with a torch, or a patrol.
- Rain smell arrives before the rain: five minutes, maybe ten, before the clearing becomes untenable.
- Horses on the road, audible but not yet visible. Their pace and number are estimable from sound if someone knows how.
- A fire smell — woodsmoke from a previous occupation of the clearing, or from a fire somewhere deeper in the wood. Old or recent, indeterminate.
