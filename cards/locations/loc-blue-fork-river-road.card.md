# The Blue Fork Tributary Road Network — Fairstead to East

## Geography

The road network running east and northeast of Fairstead, connecting the market town to its tributary villages along the Blue Fork tributary and its feeder streams. The network is not a single road but a branching system: one principal road running east from the Fairstead market junction, then forking at the wool factor's cot approximately two hours' travel out; the northern branch running to the millward's house and a string of smallfolk hamlets; the southern branch curving toward the garrison waypost and the Willow Wood road.

Era context: approximately 88–94 AC, Jaehaerys I's long peace. The road network is old — predating the current lord's family by two generations at least — and maintained by collective smallfolk labor under the reeve's coordination. The surface is packed earth and river gravel where drainage demands it; the rest is compacted track, well-worn but unimproved. In wet season the low sections flood and must be bypassed through field-edge paths.

The network covers approximately twelve miles at its fullest extent from Fairstead's east gate to the most distant regular stop (the garrison waypost), with the primary tributary villages clustered within six miles. House Ryger of Willow Wood holds formal road authority along the southern branch; informal usage of the northern branch is administered through the reeve's circuit. The garrison waypost is the easternmost formal Ryger installation on this road system.

The road is the primary operational surface for two competing purposes: Taylor's expanding coalition work along informal channels east (contacts among tributary smallfolk, the wool factor, the millward's network) and Pryor's monitoring perimeter (surveillance route for the garrison courier, behavioral anomaly logging at altered stops, insect-grid ceiling at extended range).

## Layout

**Fairstead east junction** — where the market road meets the eastern road network. A three-way split: south toward Willow Wood (lord's road, more traveled), east toward the tributary villages (the road this card describes), west back into Fairstead. A Ryger waymarker post stands at the junction (faded heraldry, lord's distance marks scratched into the stone, largely ignored by locals who know the way by landmarks).

**Wool factor's cot** — approximately two hours east of Fairstead on foot; one hour by horse. A working structure: the cot itself (small, one room, a sleeping loft, trade documents stacked near the window where light is best), a yard with a covered weighing area (wool sacks can be assessed here before committing to the Fairstead market), and a small outbuilding for short-term storage. The wool factor is not always resident — he moves a circuit between the tributary villages and Fairstead and the Blue Fork south road — but the cot is his fixed point. A regular stop on Taylor's expanding route; a calibrated intelligence node in her outer coalition.

**Road fork — north branch** — one mile past the wool factor's cot. The branch point has no marker; locals identify it by the lightning-struck oak that has been dying since before anyone living can remember. The north branch is narrower and less maintained; predominantly foot traffic and pack animals. This is the primary Taylor-expansion corridor.

**Millward's house** — two stops east of Fairstead on the north branch; approximately three hours on foot. A substantial smallfolk dwelling by tributary standards: the millward manages a water mill on the feeder stream, which gives him unusual economic stability and a natural social position as information aggregator (everyone who brings grain passes through). His wife is a regular contact in Taylor's expanding network. The house sits on a low rise above the mill itself; the mill is in the creek bottom, audible from the house as a constant wheel-turn rhythm.

**Tributary hamlets (east)** — two to four small settlements along the north branch past the millward's house; population ranges from eight to thirty-odd each. These are the outer-coalition contacts Taylor reaches in B2. The hamlets have no institutional presence — no reeve, no garrison man — but the word networks among them are dense and fast. What happens in one hamlet is known in the next within a day.

**Southern branch — garrison waypost** — approximately three hours south-southeast of Fairstead on the Willow Wood road branch. A stone-built structure: a guardroom, a small stable for two horses, a document box under lock, and a signal beacon (fire-pot on an iron stand, visible for two miles on clear nights). Staffed intermittently: a rotation of one to two garrison men from the Fairstead outpost, cycling roughly every five days. Rymer's courier route passes through here. The waypost is the formal eastern terminus of Pryor's monitoring perimeter; behavioral anomalies logged here are the leading edge of his awareness.

**Ferry crossings (extended network)** — two secondary crossing points on the tributary road's east reach: one is a ford (knee-deep in late summer, impassable in spring flood, the ford at the unnamed hamlet one mile past the millward's house), the second is a small plank bridge at the garrison waypost road's crossing. Neither is controlled; neither is manned. They are transit chokepoints by geography, not by institutional design, but Pryor has logged that anyone moving east without using Fairstead's main ferry dock must use one of these two points.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Road dust in summer (the light silt from the tributary floodplain, lifted by foot traffic); wet earth in spring and autumn (the road surface turns to pale clay-mud in the first rains); woodsmoke at the cot stops; the wool factor's cot carries lanolin and dried-grass smell from the wool sacks. The north branch past the millward's has a creek smell — the feeder stream is close — combining green water, algae, and the faint sour of the mill's wet-wood underworkings. The garrison waypost smells of stone and horse and iron — the specific combination of a military post that has been in continuous use long enough to have acquired a character.

**Sound:** The road itself is quiet by Fairstead standards. Foot traffic, occasional cart wheel, the animal sounds that travel with pack transport. Birds are the dominant sound in the stretches between stops — the specific population changes as the road moves from open field to hedgerow to woodland margin to creek side. The millward's house can be heard (wheel turn, water rush) before it is seen. The garrison waypost: the particular clank of garrison routine — equipment check, horses in the stable, occasional bell test of the signal apparatus.

**Touch:** Packed earth underfoot, varying with season and recent weather. The road surface at the northern branch is softer than the southern; more leaf matter, less gravel. The wool factor's cot yard is well-maintained underfoot — the weighing work requires a stable surface. The ford crossing: cold water, slippery riverbed stone, the sensation of current against the ankle in even moderate flow.

**Light:** The road is predominantly open to the sky on the south branch (agricultural land, few tall trees). The north branch closes over progressively as it moves east — hedgerow, then woodland edge, then occasional full canopy over the track for stretches of a hundred paces. The garrison waypost clears again (it was built with visibility in mind). Taylor's insect grid functions differently in the canopied sections: her aerial-foragers (flies, wasps) lose altitude, and she works primarily with ground-level beetles and crawlers in those stretches.

**Insect ecology (Taylor-relevant):** The road network is Taylor's extended operational surface in S2. Calibration varies by sub-location:
- **Wool factor's cot:** Fly density high (animal traffic, wool smell). Established population she has calibrated. High-confidence passive coverage within 40-pace radius of the cot.
- **North branch (open sections):** Ground beetles, grassland flies, wasp nests in the hedgerow banks. Moderate confidence; she has been extending her mental map along this route across B2 beats.
- **Millward's house vicinity:** Excellent density around the mill (water attracts; the wet wood and grain attract secondary populations). She has calibrated the water-beetle population in the creek as a navigational anchor. High confidence.
- **Tributary hamlets:** Each has a distinct insect micro-ecology depending on kept animals and stored food. Taylor builds individual maps as she visits; at B2 close she has working models for two hamlets and partial models for the others.
- **Garrison waypost:** The stable provides fly density; she can read the waypost from approximately 60 paces using stable-flies and the latrine-adjacent beetle population. This is intelligence she has, but using it operationally requires her to approach within 60 paces of a garrisoned structure, which is a cost she notes.
- **Road ceiling (general):** Taylor's ambient grid on the road network operates at a lower ceiling than in Fairstead — she is not home terrain here. Her passive coverage extends in corridors along routes she has traveled, with gaps at the junctions she has not walked. The insect-grid-at-extended-ceiling note in the aggregate (B4) refers to Pryor's anomaly detection: he has noticed that his courier's behavioral changes (altered stops, hesitations, route deviations) correlate with periods when Taylor has been on the road.

- Weighing area at wool factor's cot (covered; fixed infrastructure)
- Signal beacon at garrison waypost (fire-pot on iron stand; fixed)

## Exits

From any point on the road network:
- **West back to Fairstead** — always available; time variable by sub-location; Fairstead's ferry dock is the next transit node
- **South to Willow Wood road** — garrison waypost connects; this is the lord's formal transit corridor toward Ryger's seat
- **North branch terminus** — the track continues past the outermost hamlet but becomes a footpath; exits onto open agricultural land and eventually to the next watershed; not a named exit
- **Ford crossing (north branch)** — lateral crossing point onto the west bank of the feeder stream; connects to field paths on the far side (no road continues there)
- **Blue Fork south** — by returning to Fairstead and using the ferry; or by using the secondary crossings, accessing the river corridor directly

## Hazards

**Pryor's monitoring perimeter and courier routing:** Rymer's courier uses the southern branch on a documented rotation, passing the garrison waypost on roughly predictable days. His route has changed — behavioral anomalies noted (altered stops; he avoids the millward's stretch on certain days; he takes the ford instead of the waypost road crossing without stated reason). Pryor has logged these deviations. Taylor's insect coverage of his route is creating the changes she has not yet fully accounted for; she shapes behavior without visible mechanism, which is precisely the anomaly Pryor is tracking.

**Extended-ceiling insect grid as exposure:** Operating her grid at extended range on the road network costs Taylor more than in Fairstead. In Fairstead she works passively within established density. On the road she must actively maintain coverage at the edges of familiar territory, which requires the kind of focused attention that makes her appearance and behavior observable to anyone sharing the road. She cannot work her grid and also look like a woman on an errand without cognitive conflict.

**Road isolation:** Between stops, the road network is sparsely traveled. A confrontation between Fairstead and the wool factor's cot might have no witnesses at all. This is a hazard in both directions: Taylor can operate without observation; Taylor can also be approached without observation.

**Seasonal road status:** The low sections north of the fork flood in spring. This is not a narrative set-piece hazard but a logistical one: when the north branch low sections are flooded, the tributary hamlet contacts are unreachable overland without significant detour. A weather event or prolonged rain can sever Taylor's outer-coalition corridor for days.

**Garrison waypost as institutional observation point:** The waypost's signal beacon means a garrison man at the waypost can alert Fairstead within minutes (fire-signal, line of sight where the terrain allows). Taylor's presence on the southern branch in the vicinity of the waypost creates institutional observation risk that her presence on the north branch does not.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- A factor's cart moving east on the main road, loaded goods covered against the weather. The factor does not stop at the wool factor's cot — he is heading further east — but he notes who is on the road. Taylor's passive sense gives her his load profile before he reaches her position.
- The millward's wife is at her door when Taylor passes, sweeping the threshold. The exchange is brief — the kind of routine social contact that does not look like intelligence transfer to any observer — but it carries information in both directions about conditions in the hamlets further east.
- A garrison courier passes on the southern branch, Rymer's man on rotation. He is moving faster than the schedule requires. Pryor has sent an unscheduled message. Taylor can see him coming on her grid 200 paces out; she can be somewhere else before he rounds the bend.
- A tributary hamlet has a sick animal that died three days ago. The body has not been dealt with; the insect density spike is anomalous, visible to Taylor's grid as a bright spot 400 paces before she reaches the hamlet. This tells her something has gone wrong before any person does.
- Rain begins on the north branch. The track surface changes fast — the packed-earth-over-clay holds water poorly. Taylor has forty minutes before the low section floods and the route is compromised. She makes a decision about which she prioritizes: getting to the outer hamlet or getting back to the ford before it becomes impassable.
- The wool factor is unexpectedly at his cot on a day Taylor did not plan for his presence. He is reviewing documents and has not seen her yet. What she knows about the cot's insect population gives her two seconds' advance warning; what she does with those two seconds is a choice.

## Interaction Notes

**For Taylor:** The road network is her S2 operational expansion zone — the terrain she is actively learning and working to cover with her grid. She is not home terrain here; she is extending. The distinction matters for her confidence and for what she can rely on. Every new stop is a calibration exercise and an intelligence-gathering opportunity simultaneously. The outer-coalition contacts she builds here are both the goal and the cover story for her presence on the road.

**For Pryor:** The road network is the perimeter he monitors. His courier route gives him behavioral data at regular intervals; the behavioral anomalies (altered stops, route deviations, the courier's own changed behavior around specific stretches) are the evidence accumulating in his log. He has not connected the anomalies to Taylor directly — that connection requires a further step — but he is building the dataset that makes the connection possible.

**Relationship to loc-river-ferry-dock:** The ferry dock is the western throat of this network. Anyone moving east from Fairstead by road either left from inside the town (no dock transit) or arrived via the dock. Tomm Weyl's observation of arrivals and departures from south of the Blue Fork is the informational complement to Taylor's grid on the road east. What Tomm sees, Pryor may hear; what Taylor covers on the road, Pryor maps through behavioral change. The ferry dock is the chokepoint for external contact; the road network is the chokepoint for internal coalition extension.

**Relationship to loc-river-market-town:** Fairstead is the base and the return. The road network's operational significance depends on Fairstead remaining stable enough to operate from. Pryor's monitoring of the road perimeter is an extension of his monitoring of Fairstead itself; his interest in road anomalies is continuous with his interest in market-square behavior. The two location cards are a single surveillance surface operating at two scales.
