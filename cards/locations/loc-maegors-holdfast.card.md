# Location — Maegor's Holdfast

The fortified inner keep within the Red Keep. Twelve-foot curtain walls. A dry spiked moat. A single drawbridge. This is where the royal family's children are housed — the innermost defensive shell of a castle that is already defensive.

The cage is the attendant, not the door. The architecture provides the *frame* — one monitored exit — but the primary confinement instrument is not the wall, it is the household watch. Anyone living inside Maegor's Holdfast discovers quickly that the drawbridge is rarely the obstacle. The obstacle is who is watching the drawbridge.

**Cross-reference:** `loc-red-keep-interior` covers the wider Red Keep. This card covers the holdfast as a distinct inner fortress.

---

## Geography

Maegor's Holdfast sits at the heart of the Red Keep, accessed from the castle's inner ward. It is a self-contained defensive enclosure: a square curtain wall approximately twelve feet high, faced in dark red stone, with a dry moat at its foot lined with iron spikes. The moat is not water-filled — it is a pit obstacle, intended to prevent scaling and deter siege-engine approach.

The interior contains the royal nursery, family apartments, administrative rooms, and a private sept. The enclosed courtyard between the apartments and the curtain wall is small — enough for a child to run in, not large enough for meaningful exercise.

The single drawbridge spans the moat to the Red Keep's inner ward. When it is up the holdfast is genuinely isolated; when it is down (the default in peacetime) it functions as a monitored crossing. Anyone entering or leaving is seen.

## Layout

### The nursery and family apartments

The nursery is the primary children's space: stone floors, a hearth, windows facing the inner courtyard. Adjacent are the individual chambers for the royal children, which develop in character over the years as their occupants grow. The apartments are comfortable but compact; no room is far from any other.

### Alicent's solar

The administrative center of the holdfast's household. Formal for receiving visitors; the correspondence and management of the household flows through here. A working space that is also a social-intelligence source for anyone paying attention.

### The private chapel/sept

Daily household devotions. The acoustics are specific — sound travels in particular directions and not others; experienced occupants learn the chapel's acoustic map.

### The courtyard

The enclosed space between apartments and curtain wall. Used for supervised outdoor time, guard watch, servant movement. The courtyard's smallness means very little movement during daylight goes unobserved.

### The drawbridge approach

The bridge and the gatepost passage through the curtain wall. The chain-and-counterweight mechanism is mounted in the gatehouse; its sound is audible throughout the holdfast and announces every entry and exit. Two guards at standard household staffing; formal crossings are logged.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Curtain wall and moat:**
Cold stone, radiating chill in winter. The dry moat below: faint iron smell from the spikes, stone dust, the dead-air quality of a pit that receives sun only at specific angles. The spiked bottom visible from moat-facing windows — an architectural statement, not only a functional one.

**The apartments:**
Beeswax candles (maintained throughout; the household distinction from tallow is standard). Cedar and lavender from storage chests. The soap-clean smell of royal-household upkeep.

**The drawbridge chain:**
The most significant acoustic feature of the holdfast. Audible as a chain-load sound that distinguishes up-chain from down-chain. An unscheduled raising announces an interruption before any word has been spoken.

**The courtyard at night:**
Cold-air quality distinct from the day. The distant watch-change sounds in the inner ward, audible on quiet nights. The rectangle of sky above.

## Exits

**The drawbridge:** the only official exit. Crossed on official purpose; the gatepost logs entries and exits. All formally recorded movement passes through this exit.

**The below-stairs equivalents:** there is no secret door out of Maegor's Holdfast. The soft equivalents are timing-based — guard rotation overlaps, supply delivery windows — and depend on the household's habits and attention rather than any architectural feature.

---

## Hazards

**The watcher problem (primary hazard for any occupant operating outside official channels):**
The holdfast's primary containment is observational. The architecture is a frame; the real constraint is who is watching and when. Household staff, the septa, the posted guard — the risk is not the door, it is cross-referenced accounts.

**Unscheduled bridge closure:**
Isolates the holdfast from the rest of the Red Keep without warning. Anything already in motion on the outside becomes inaccessible. The closure may last minutes or hours; the reason is not always communicated to the holdfast's occupants until after the fact.

**The isolation function (double-edged):**
The twelve-foot walls protect occupants from outside intrusion. They also mean that activity within the holdfast is not easily visible from outside the walls. The norm of holdfast privacy, extended over time, creates a zone of reduced external scrutiny for what happens inside.

**The report cascade:**
Any household watcher who files reports can trigger escalation if enough reports arrive in close succession. A single unusual observation is unlikely to produce action. Multiple observations across a short span — especially during an event that draws household attention — can force a response even from a figure of authority who would prefer not to act.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- **The chain going up without ceremony:** the drawbridge being raised without announcement; whatever was planned outside is now inaccessible; whatever was in motion inside is now isolated.
- **A guard posted at an unusual station:** the watch rotation has placed someone with a clear sightline to a place that is normally lightly observed.
- **A summons from the household authority:** immediate and not at the scheduled time; the reason is unspecified; the urgency is legible from the tone.
- **The formal room search:** an official household inventory, ordered from above, with notice measured in hours.
- **A new guard at the gatepost:** replacement of a known face with an unknown one; the new guard's habits, alertness level, and knowledge of prior patterns must be assessed before the next scheduled movement.
- **An overnight household guest:** a visitor lodged inside the holdfast who has no prior pattern to read, who is present in the corridors at unpredictable hours, and who does not know what is normal here.
