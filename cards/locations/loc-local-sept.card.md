# The Fairstead Sept (Septon Rowan's Seat)

## Geography

A small stone sept on the south edge of Fairstead's settled area, set slightly apart from the main craftsman and market cluster. It occupies a plot that was donated to the Faith by a prior Lord Ryger, probably sixty years before story open. The building is local stone — the grey limestone that the Blue Fork quarries produce — laid by craftsmen who were competent but not exceptional. The result is solid and unpretentious.

The sept is Septon Rowan's principal residence, administrative seat, and service space. He is the parish septon of Fairstead — the regular, full-time Faith presence here. He has been in this sept for nineteen years. The sept is where he lives, where he conducts the weekly offices, where the community comes for the Faith's services (marriages, naming ceremonies, funerals, the occasional dispute brought for mediation). The Faith-circuit septons from the regional chapter house (probably Stoney Sept area) pass through annually; Rowan corresponds with the Septon-Superior of the nearest regional seat.

**Septon Rowan's status in this project:** He is the parish septon of Fairstead, full-time. He is not a circuit septon; he does not travel. The Septon-Superior of the nearest city (referenced in the library card) is his institutional superior via correspondence. Rowan's correspondence with his superior is careful — designed to describe what he has observed without triggering an intervention response he does not yet want.

## Layout

**Exterior:**
- A seven-sided tower (the sept's defining form), approximately two stories in height — not tall; the tower is symbolic, not aspirational. The narrow windows (one per side, at the upper level) face each of the seven directional positions associated with the Seven.
- A small forecourt, enclosed by a low stone wall. Three stone benches inside the forecourt for those who wish to sit before entering. The sept entrance faces roughly east.
- A storage room built against the tower's base on the north face — not part of the original structure, added within Rowan's tenure. This is where he keeps the sept's supplies (candles, holy oil, spare robes, a small locked chest with the sept's coin).
- Rowan's living quarters: a separate small structure attached to the south side of the tower, two rooms (a sleeping room and a working room where he writes his correspondence and reads). The working room has the best light in the sept complex.

**Interior:**
- The seven-faced altar occupies the center-floor of the tower's lower level. Seven painted wooden panels, each depicting one of the Seven, arranged in a circle around a raised stone platform. The paint is old and cracked in places; Rowan has never had the funds to have them refaced, and he has not tried.
- Candles: the primary light source for the sept interior. A dozen candle-holders around the altar circumference, plus two large floor-standing iron holders near the door. The sept always smells of tallow and spent wax.
- Seating: stone benches along the tower wall, worn smooth by twenty years of use. Approximately forty people can sit in the sept interior; more can stand. Full capacity is a significant community event.
- The baptismal basin: a stone basin near the entrance, filled on ceremony days. Currently holding an inch of old water and a dead fly (Taylor's passive surveillance unit; she has maintained it at low cost since approximately 85 AC).
- The prayer alcove: a small carved-out section of the north wall where supplicants can kneel in relative privacy. Rowan uses this for his private prayer. Taylor cannot see into it from her surveillance unit without repositioning, which she has not done.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Smell:** Tallow candles — the dominant note. Not pleasant exactly, but distinctive: animal fat burning, slightly waxy, with a faint rancid undertone from the cheaper candles used between ceremony days. Stone: cool and slightly damp in winter, warm and dry in summer. Incense on ceremony days, which Rowan uses sparingly (it is expensive; he uses it for funerals and nameday ceremonies). Old wood from the altar panels, with the specific smell of paint gone dry. Very faint: the smell of the people who use the space — wool clothing, the specific honest-labor smell of a smallfolk congregation.

**Sound:** Silence in a specific register — the sept's stone walls absorb rather than reflect, producing a quiet that is different from outdoor quiet. Rowan's voice during service: liturgical cadence, carrying without effort. The candle flames: nearly inaudible, but their flicker is a sound register Taylor's passive sense picks up through the moth-colony near the window. The forecourt: wind against the stone wall, the birds in the sept's exterior (sparrows in the roof eaves — Taylor's secondary surveillance unit). The tower bell, which Rowan rings at the opening of each service — audible throughout southern Fairstead.

**Touch:** Stone — cold in all seasons, retaining less heat than the dwelling-interiors. The altar platform is worn smooth from decades of hands placed on it. The prayer benches are worn in the same way. Candle wax, occasionally, from a candle guttered by an unexpected draft — hard and slightly waxy, easily scraped.

**Light:** The sept is chronically dim. The seven narrow tower windows let in directional light at specific times of day; at any other time the interior is dependent on candles. On ceremony days with all candles lit, the interior reaches a warm semi-darkness. On weekdays with minimal candles, the interior is genuinely dark beyond the immediate candle halos. Rowan reads by his working-room window, not by the sept interior.

**Insect ecology (Taylor-relevant):** Low density by Fairstead standards, but stable and exploitable. The baptismal basin holds a small fly population (water-associated). The stone walls' interior cracks host spiders — a specific population Taylor has been cataloguing for approximately two years, long enough to know their territories. The roof eaves hold sparrows (not insects, but bird surveillance via passive corvid-adjacent sense — she has one line of communication to the exterior sparrow population). The candle-moth population near the upper windows is Taylor's secondary interior unit. None of these are offensive resources; all are surveillance resources.

## Fixed Props

- Seven-faced altar (painted wood panels, stone platform)
- Candle-holders (twelve around altar circumference, two floor-standing)
- Stone prayer benches (wall-mounted, interior)
- Baptismal basin (stone, near entrance)
- Tower bell (upper level, rope-operated from interior)
- Iron candle-snuffer (Rowan's)
- Supply chest (north storage room, locked — Rowan has the key)

## Exits

- **East-facing entrance** — the main door; heavy wood, iron-hinged; opens outward to the forecourt; requires an adult's weight to open against a wind
- **Storage room access** — north side; not a public exit, but accessible from inside the tower through an internal door Rowan controls
- **Rowan's quarters** — south-side structure; accessed from the sept interior through a passage door, and from the forecourt via a separate exterior door; neither is typically open to the general congregation

## Hazards

- **Septon Rowan as observer:** He watches his congregation and his space closely. He is the person in Fairstead most likely to notice Taylor's anomalous behavior during religious services, because he is paying attention in a way that the social environment of the sept facilitates — people who come here are expected to be observed.
- **The correspondence channel:** Rowan writes to his Septon-Superior regularly. Whatever he observes and decides to share travels through this channel to a larger Faith institutional presence. Taylor cannot intercept these letters; she can observe when he writes them (she can tell from her bird-adjacent surveillance whether the outgoing messenger is carrying correspondence).
- **Theological framing:** The sept is where Rowan's theology about Taylor is constructed and then preached. Taylor cannot prevent the construction; she can observe what is being said and make decisions about how to respond.
- **Congregation as relay:** What happens in the sept is known to all forty families of Fairstead's regular congregation within two days. The sept is an information node, not an information-secure space.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- Rowan conducts the weekly office. The congregation is present. Taylor is in the congregation. The liturgy is happening, and Rowan's eye falls on her with slightly more attention than it falls on any other worshipper. She knows he is watching. He is not sure whether she knows.
- A community member comes to Rowan for pastoral consultation. Taylor's surveillance gives her the room layout but not the words (stone walls absorb; she cannot read lips through a spider). She knows someone is there; she does not know what they are saying.
- Rowan writes a letter at his working-room desk. He is at the desk for approximately an hour. The content of the letter is not known to Taylor. She knows he is writing it. The sparrow on the eave can tell her when the messenger leaves.
- A Faith-circuit visitor arrives — a traveling septon from the regional network. Taylor's passive sense gives her the visitor's arrival before most of Fairstead knows. Rowan's response to the visitor (cautious, slightly formal) tells Taylor something about what the visitor represents institutionally.
- The sept bell rings for an occasion other than the weekly service. An emergency rite — a death, a fever in a household, something requiring immediate Faith response. The community gathers. Taylor's information network is activated by the gathering.

## Interaction Notes

**Taylor's literacy origin:** The sept's books — a small collection, four volumes, including one partial copy of the Book of the Seven and two older texts in a pre-standard script — are where Taylor's reading progressed past the ledger vocabulary her father taught her. Rowan allowed her access because she is a craftsman's child who asks sincere questions about the text, and because a child who can read the Faith's books is a credit to the parish. He does not know how fast she read them or how much she retained.

**Taylor's passive surveillance of the sept:** She has maintained the baptismal-basin fly unit since approximately 85 AC. This is her longest-running continuous surveillance installation. She checks it as background, not actively. The check costs her almost nothing. She knows the interior layout of the sept more precisely than anyone who is not Septon Rowan.

**Septon Rowan's dual role in this project:** He is the parish septon (resident, full-time, Taylor's regular religious institutional contact) and the Faith-witness who builds theology around her power without her consent. These two roles are the same person. The danger is that the pastoral relationship (he taught her to use the books; she attends his services; he knows her name) and the theological project (he is building a framework around what she is) are running in the same person simultaneously. She cannot separate them.
