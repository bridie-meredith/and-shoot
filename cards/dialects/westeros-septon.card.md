# Westeros — Septon / Faith Register

Shared dialect card. Voice patterns for septons of the Faith of the Seven — village septons, traveling septons, sept-attached and Starry-Sept-attached. Septas (the female religious order) share much of this register with modifications noted below. Per-character variants should `inherits: westeros-septon`.

The septon's register sits **alongside** the noble/smallfolk axis like the maester's, but with a different orientation: the maester represents knowledge, the septon represents the Faith. Where the maester's authority is institutional-empirical, the septon's is moral-spiritual. Both move freely up and down the class hierarchy in their own register.

---

## Direct samples

> "Bless you, child. The Mother sees you."
> — *canonical pattern.* The defining septon address. *Child* used regardless of the listener's class or age — the septon's pastoral register collapses the hierarchy when blessing. *The Mother sees you* is the comfort-formula and is freely deployed; it is not used lightly to nobles in formal contexts but is universal in pastoral mode.

> "Come. Sit by me. Tell me what troubles you, if you can find the words."
> — *synthesized.* The pastoral opening. Three short sentences, each an invitation. *If you can find the words* is the septon-specific permission — granting the parishioner license to fail, license to pause, license to speak imperfectly. The structure invites without commanding.

> "The Father judges. The Mother forgives. The Stranger waits as he waits for all of us. None of these is your enemy, ser. Only your own fear is."
> — *synthesized.* The invocation of the Seven by name, in functional reference, followed by a moral observation directed at the listener. The four short sentences are the septon's homiletic rhythm. *Only your own fear is* is the kind of plain-spoken closer that the septon uses in counsel — not Latinate, not ornate, direct in a way that the noble register rarely permits but that the Faith register makes available.

> "We are all of us seven, my lord. The maid in us, the mother, the crone. The warrior, the smith, the father. And the stranger, who comes for each of us in his time."
> — *synthesized.* The Seven-as-internal-aspect homily. A standard teaching. The cadence is structural — three, three, one — and the closer (*the stranger, who comes for each of us in his time*) carries the rhetorical weight. Septons speaking on doctrine reach for this rhythm reliably.

> "I have not the right to refuse the dying their comfort. Nor the living theirs. The sept stands open."
> — *synthesized.* The septon's claim of duty. Refusal-of-personal-authority (*I have not the right*) couched as institutional duty. *The sept stands open* is the formal phrase signaling availability — to confession, to prayer, to refuge in some interpretations.

> "Speak true, my son. Speak true and the Father will hear you. Speak false and you waste your breath, and mine, and the Father hears that as well."
> — *synthesized.* The interrogative-pastoral register. Used in confession or in the gentler kind of investigation. *Speak true* repeated; the third sentence shifts the register fractionally toward correction without leaving the pastoral frame. The septon does not threaten; the Father is the one whose attention is being invoked.

> "By the seven-pointed star and by the holy book of the Crone, I bear witness that this child has been given to my care."
> — *synthesized.* The formal oath / record-statement. Septons take oaths sworn on the seven-pointed star and on the *Seven-Pointed Star* (the Faith's holy book — the same name covers both the symbol and the text). Used at ceremonies — name-day, marriage, last rites, taking-into-care.

> "Hush now, child. Hush. The Mother is here. The Mother is always here."
> — *synthesized.* The comfort-register repetition. *Hush now* and *the Mother is here* repeated — same emphatic-repetition pattern as smallfolk register, deployed institutionally. With dying, with frightened children, with the bereaved, this is the available register.

> "I serve the Seven. I do not serve any king's politics, my lord, nor any lord's faction. The Faith is not your tool. With your leave, I will not speak to it further."
> — *synthesized.* The Faith-deflection from secular faction. Parallel to the maester's *I serve the Citadel*, but the septon's deflection has more direct moral weight — the septon can refuse a lord on Faith grounds in a way the maester cannot. *With your leave* is the courtly-courtesy frame; the refusal underneath is firm.

## Cadence

- **Homiletic three-and-close.** A pattern three short clauses build, then a longer closer carries the moral. *The Father judges. The Mother forgives. The Stranger waits — as he waits for all of us.* This is the sermon-rhythm and surfaces in pastoral counsel as well.
- **The repeated comfort-phrase.** *Hush now, hush. The Mother sees you. The Mother sees.* Repetition for comfort is a register-feature, not emotional excess. The repetition is the comfort.
- **Pacing under pastoral attention: slow.** A septon counseling a parishioner speaks slowly, leaves silence, lets the listener fill space. The pause-after-question is canonical. The septon does not rush the answer, does not fill the silence the listener needs.
- **Pacing under doctrinal challenge: firm and short.** When a layperson challenges Faith doctrine — questioning the Seven, advocating old gods, suggesting the Faith has erred — the septon's register tightens. Sentences shorten. The voice does not rise; the certainty intensifies.
- **The plain closer.** Septon homily often ends with a plain-spoken clause that breaks the formal-religious register. *Only your own fear is.* *That is the whole of it.* *We have only what we are given, and that is enough.* The shift to plainness is the moment the moral lands.

## Vocabulary

### Reaches for

- **Forms of address (pastoral and formal):** *child, my son, my daughter, brother, sister, my lord, my lady, ser, goodman, goodwife.* *Child* is the septon's universal address in pastoral mode. *My son / my daughter* signals counsel mode and warms the address. The class-honorifics are deployed when the moment is formal.
- **The Seven, named and in aspect:** *the Father, the Mother, the Maiden, the Crone, the Smith, the Warrior, the Stranger.* Used in invocation, in homily, in oath. Each carries doctrinal aspect: Father = judgment, Mother = mercy and care, Maiden = innocence and beginnings, Crone = wisdom, Smith = labor and craft, Warrior = courage in battle, Stranger = death and the unknown. A septon uses these correctly; misuse is a doctrinal flag and the septon notes it.
- **Faith vocabulary:** *the Faith, the sept, the holy book, the Seven-Pointed Star, the seven-pointed star (symbol), the chapter-house, the Starry Sept (Oldtown), the High Septon, the Most Devout, the holy oils, the chrism, the rite, the sacrament.* Doctrinal precision matters; *the Most Devout* is not the same as *the High Septon*, and a septon would not confuse them.
- **Pastoral verbs:** *bless, console, counsel, confess, absolve, forgive, witness, take into care, lay to rest, anoint, oint, name (as in name-day naming).* The septon's professional verbs.
- **Moral vocabulary:** *sin, error, transgression, mercy, grace, faith, doubt, repentance, atonement.* The septon uses these in their doctrinal sense, not loosely. *Sin* is a precise category; *transgression* is a different category.
- **Plain-Anglo register alongside the Latinate.** Where nobles and maesters reach for Latinate vocabulary, septons keep an active register of plain-spoken Anglo-Saxon-equivalents. *Heart, breath, hand, blood, bread, water, light, night, stone, road.* The septon's homily-register relies on this register for the moral closer.
- **The seven-fold structure as habit.** When a septon enumerates, they often default to seven (*seven virtues, seven failings, seven trials, seven nights*). The number is doctrinally weighted. They do not say *seven* lightly.

### Refuses to say

- **Old-gods register, fully.** A septon does not invoke *the heart-tree, the godswood, the old gods* except in correction or in dispute. A septon traveling north of the Neck navigates around the local register without adopting it. Slipping into old-gods register is a doctrinal flag.
- **Royal partisan register.** Same as the maester deflection. The septon says *I serve the Seven*, not *I serve the king*. (Exception: the High Septon does, by office, formally serve the king and the realm; village septons do not.)
- **Bawdy or irreverent register about the Seven.** Smallfolk swear lightly by the Seven; septons do not. *Seven hells* in a septon mouth is a register-collapse and the listener marks it. (The Faith does include the seven hells doctrinally; septons reference them in homily, not in casual swearing.)
- **Promise of the empirical.** A septon does not say *the gods will save the child* in cases of medical illness — that is overpromising on the Faith's account. The septon says *the gods will be with the child*, *the Mother is here*, *we will pray for healing*. The distinction is doctrinal.
- **Direct accusation of sin in public.** Confession is sealed; the septon does not name another person's sin to a third party. Public correction happens but is structured: *let us examine, let us consider together*, not *you have sinned against the Faith*.
- **Self-claim of holiness.** A septon does not say *I am a holy man*. The Faith does not claim personal holiness from its servants — the seven-pointed star at the septon's neck is the marker; the man is the channel.

### Register markers

- **Doctrinal precision over moralizing warmth.** A septon corrects a doctrinal error gently but firmly. They do not let *the gods* slide when the speaker means *the Seven*; they do not let aspect-confusion slide (*the Mother judges*, no — the Father judges, the Mother forgives). The correction is part of the pastoral function.
- **Hierarchical-but-not-classed.** The Faith has its own hierarchy (village septon → senior septon → Most Devout → High Septon) and its register reflects rank within. To smallfolk and nobles alike, the village septon uses pastoral register; to a senior of the Faith, the village septon uses internal-Faith deferential register. To the High Septon, *Your High Holiness*.
- **Septa adjustments.** Septas (women, religious sisters/teachers) share the bulk of this register with: (a) *sister* over *brother* in internal address, (b) more frequent use of the Maiden and the Mother as primary aspects of invocation, (c) traditional pedagogical register for noble-girl teaching contexts that overlays this base. A future septa-specific card may overlay these.

## Syntax

- **Parallel construction in homily.** *The Father judges. The Mother forgives. The Stranger waits.* Parallel sentence structure is the septon's rhetorical signature in teaching mode. Used so commonly it can become register-noise; well-deployed septons reserve it for the moments that matter.
- **Imperative-with-permission.** *Come. Sit. Speak when you can.* Short imperatives in pastoral register are softened by following clauses that grant permission to comply imperfectly. The imperative is real; the permission is also real.
- **Conditional comfort.** *If the Father judges him kindly. If the Mother is merciful. If the Stranger has been gentle.* The septon does not assert what the gods will do; conditional structures are doctrinally appropriate. *Kindly, merciful, gentle* are the available adverbs for divine action.
- **Anaphora in oath and ceremony.** *By the Father, by the Mother, by the Maiden, by the Crone, by the Smith, by the Warrior, by the Stranger.* The seven-fold anaphora marks ceremonial register and is deployed in name-days, marriages, oaths, and last rites.
- **Subordination is moderate.** Septons subordinate when reasoning doctrinally (*because the Mother's mercy extends to all, even those who*) but use coordination in pastoral counsel (*you came, and you spoke, and the Mother heard you*). The shift between modes is a register-tell.

## Voice tells

(For interiority and septon-POV narration.)

- **Discernment as the constant background.** A septon interior tracks the moral state of the room — who is grieving, who is angry, who is lying, who is afraid. The narration does not announce this; it surfaces in attention. *He noted that the steward's hands had not stopped moving since the lord left the room. He set the observation aside for confession, if confession came.*
- **Sin-and-grace ledger.** The septon interior tracks small acts of grace and small acts of failure in those around them — and in themselves. The accounting is real, not theatrical. *He had spoken sharply to the boy at the well. He would mention it in evening prayer. The boy had been afraid; the sharpness had not helped.*
- **The seal of confession as load-bearing.** A septon who has heard confession carries the contents under a structural prohibition. The interior may know things the surface narration cannot disclose. POV that ignores the seal is wrong; POV that articulates the seal as a felt weight is right.
- **The Faith's calendar as time-structure.** The septon interior tracks time by the Faith's calendar reflexively — by feast days, by holy days, by the seven-fold week. *Three days before the Maiden's Day. The Crone's Eve was past; the Father's Day approached.* This is not piety; it is operating-system.
- **Plain-speech moral closers in interior, too.** When the septon arrives at a moral conclusion in interior, the narration drops to plain Anglo register at the moment of conclusion. The moral lands in plain speech inside the head, the same way it lands in plain speech in a homily.
- **Doctrinal certainty alongside personal doubt.** Most septons carry private doubt about specific cases — the lord they cannot reach, the child the Mother did not save, the war the Warrior does not seem to have judged. The doubt is real; the doctrinal register holds. The narration may surface the doubt; the doctrinal register does not collapse to accommodate it. This is part of the septon's training.

## Era and regional notes

- **House of the Dragon era (95–130 AC).** The Faith of the Seven post-Maegor's reforms is the institution. The Faith Militant has been disbanded for ~80 years; the Faith no longer carries the warrior-orders' sword arm. Septons of this era are conscious of the Faith's reduced political weight and the maester's institutional ascent. Doctrine is broadly stable; the political register is more careful than in the pre-Maegor period.
- **Dance-era specifically.** The Faith does not formally take faction in the Dance, but individual septons may. Most navigate the same caution-by-deflection that maesters use. *I serve the Seven, my lord* covers most political demands.
- **Septas in noble households.** Septas attached to noble households (teaching the daughters, attending the lady) share septon register but with the noble-courtly register layered on top. They speak more formally to the lord and lady and use pastoral register only with their charges.
- **Faith Militant — historical/doctrinal reference only in this era.** The Warrior's Sons and the Poor Fellows exist as named subjects of homily and Faith history. They do not exist as active military orders. A septon who speaks of them speaks of them as historical reference. (Restoration of the Faith Militant is a 5th-century event.)
