# Taylor Hebert — Westeros Dialect (120 AC)

Variant of the base `taylor-hebert` dialect for the Westeros reincarnation project. Inherits all base samples, cadence, vocabulary, syntax, and voice tells. This card layers the eleven-year-old's external-register mask and the Westerosi-context constraints on top.

**Inheritance:** Read `cards/dialects/taylor-hebert.card.md` first. Everything there applies unless a section here explicitly overrides.

### Anchor samples (synthesized from card constraints — flagged as authored, not harvested)

> "I do not know, ser. Septon Aldric kept the ledger."
> — *authored, illustrative.* The mask under direct adult questioning. "I do not know" is the deflection; "Septon Aldric kept the ledger" is the deflection-with-cover. She names the institutional source rather than answer the question. The Westerosi formal register surfaces ("ser") and the contraction-free phrasing softens the precision underneath. The sentence ends on the load-bearing noun, base-card-style.

> "There is a man on the road. Past the orchard. He has not turned in."
> — *authored, illustrative.* Three short declaratives. The fragments and the dropped article on "past the orchard" are the base-card cadence. The information is from the passive sense (insects on the approach road), but she delivers it as a child reporting something she could plausibly have noticed.

> "I am sorry, ser. I am only a girl. I cannot read so quickly."
> — *authored, illustrative.* The literacy-cover in deployment. The triple repetition of "I" and the apologetic frame is the mask. The base-card voice would never use this construction in earnest. The writer fork should mark this register as performance, not as voice.

> "The ravens are restless tonight."
> — *authored, illustrative.* Omen-register cover for tactical observation. She does not say "the ravens are restless because I am running them." She lets the listener supply the explanation that fits their world. This is the signature Westeros-mask move.

## Cadence

Base cadence patterns hold. Modifications:

- **Spoken cadence is shorter than base default by another beat.** A child is not given the social space to deliver a long planning sentence aloud. Long-when-planning prose moves to interior; spoken lines stay short. When she does deliver a long sentence to an adult, it is a tell — the mask has slipped, and the slip is the moment.
- **Sentence-final softeners appear.** The Westerosi-child mask sometimes requires a closing phrase that the base register would never use: "if it please you, ser," "I think," "I do not know." These attach to the end of an otherwise base-cadence sentence. The dialect-writer should note that these softeners are removable — the line underneath is in base register.
- **The pause habit shifts.** A child waiting for permission to speak pauses before speaking; the adult Taylor pauses to choose a word. Both pauses appear in this register, and they look the same on the page. The writer fork should not try to disambiguate; the audience can tell which is which from context.

## Vocabulary

Base reaches/refusals hold. Modifications:

### Replaces (Earth-Bet → Westerosi register)
- *cape, parahuman, trigger event, threat assessment, area denial, containment, operational scope* — all forbidden aloud. Replaced with situational paraphrase or, more commonly, with silence. She does not have a Westerosi vocabulary for these concepts and will not invent one in dialogue.
- *swarm, bug-control, sensory uplink* — replaced with *insects, ravens, the birds, the pests, what the rats have heard.* When she names her capability aloud (rare), she names it as something the world might believe ("I have a way with the birds, ser") rather than as a power.
- *PRT, Wards, Protectorate, Endbringer, Cauldron, Scion, Gold Morning* — fully forbidden. Not even paraphrased. These do not exist in this world's vocabulary and any reference is a continuity break.
- *kilometer, meter, percent, statistic, data* — replaced with *paces, stones, the length of the long field, three out of every four, what I have seen.* Numerical specificity is preserved as a voice tell, but the units shift to Westerosi-plausible measures.

### Adds (Westerosi register, integrated)
- Forms of address: *ser, septon, m'lord, m'lady, goodman, goodwife.* Used correctly by class. Misuse is an audit flag.
- Religious register: *the Seven, the Mother, the Father, the Maiden, the Crone, the Stranger, the Smith, the Warrior, the seven-pointed star, the sept, the godswood, the old gods.* The septon's ward would default to Faith-of-the-Seven register. Old-gods register would be a deliberate choice she does not make casually.
- Domestic register appropriate to a sept ward: *the bell, the brazier, the stoup, the rushes, the candle-stub, the prayer-book, the chapter-house, the Mother's altar.* These ground her speech in the lived environment.
- Period-plausible idioms: *come morning, by nightfall, on the morrow, I have not seen, I am told.* Avoid post-medieval idioms; the audit flag is high here.

### Refuses (carry-forward and additions)
- All base-card refusals hold.
- Contractions are reduced. Westerosi formal register tends toward uncontracted forms in adult-facing speech ("I have not," "you would," "it does not"). Contractions are permitted in interior prose and in casual speech with peers. With adults, especially adults of standing, contractions thin out.
- Anachronistic vocabulary — *okay, fine, yeah, hi, bye, sure, alright* — forbidden aloud. The audit flag is high. These are the most common slips.
- Direct emotional declarations remain forbidden, base-card rule, with one addition: she does not perform the wide-eyed-child register. No "I'm scared," no "I want my mama," no "please don't hurt me." She would not say these even if she were scared, wanted comfort, or was being threatened. The mask does not reach that far.

### Register markers
- **Two registers.** Internal/interior is base-card register, unmodified. External/spoken-to-adults is mask-register: child-deferent Westerosi-formal with the underlying voice still detectable in cadence, in fragment-finals, in the closing-clause-twist. The dialogue-writer fork should be aware of which register the line lives in.
- **The mask is not perfect.** Slips are diagnostic. A long planning sentence delivered aloud to an adult is a slip. A piece of vocabulary the child should not have ("structurally indistinguishable," "evaluation," "operational") is a slip. A closing-clause-twist deployed at register-incorrect register-height is a slip. The writer should let slips happen on purpose, at moments of pressure, and should mark them with audience-flag commentary if unintended.
- **With peers (children) the mask is partial.** She does not have to soften as far. The base register surfaces more openly. With Septon Aldric specifically, the mask thins considerably — he has earned trust and she lets the underlying voice show in small ways.

## Syntax

Base syntax patterns hold. Modifications:

- **Fragments are still permitted but the syntactic environment shifts.** A fragment in adult-facing speech reads as a child being concise; same surface, different received register. The writer fork should not lengthen the fragments to compensate for register — the doubling-down on brevity is the cover.
- **Comma-spliced run-ons relocate to interior.** Spoken planning-sentences are rare for this variant. When they happen, they are tells. The interior prose still runs the long syntax.
- **Subject-implied openings remain frequent in interior.** In dialogue, they read as either childlike abruptness or as authoritative — both readings are useful and ambiguity is acceptable.
- **The closing-clause-twist is preserved but rationed.** Using it once per scene with adults is a slip the room may notice. Using it once per scene with peers or in interior is invisible. The writer fork should treat the twist as expensive in adult-facing dialogue.
- **Period-formal phrasing.** "I would prefer not to" → "I would not wish to," "I cannot agree" → "I do not think I can," "Understood" → "Yes, ser." The base move is preserved (deferral, receipt-confirmation, refusal-by-precision); the surface phrasing is shifted into Westerosi-formal.

## Voice tells

Base voice tells hold. Westeros-specific layers:

- **The inventory tell relocates from arthropod to corvid.** Under fear or pressure, the interior narrates what the ravens are doing, what the bell-tower roost is doing, where the rats in the outbuildings have gone. The structural function is identical to the base card (bug-deployment as emotional register-shift). The species is different.
- **The age-mismatch tell.** In interior prose, she sometimes notices the gap between her body and her cognition: a moment where she reaches for a word that the child's mouth cannot produce, or where she catches herself sitting too still. The narration of these moments is base-register and unflinching. The reader should feel the doubled awareness.
- **The Gold Morning shadow.** The base-card "refusal to look directly" tell extends to Gold Morning, the Undersiders by name, Earth-Bet referents, every dead person from her prior life. These appear in the prose as deliberate gaps. The narration moves around them; when she does name one, the cost of the naming is the load. The writer fork should not have her name them in dialogue at all in the early run.
- **Dance-of-Dragons foreknowledge as silent register.** She knows what is coming. The interior sometimes flickers with this knowledge — a date, a name, a calculation — and then closes. The base-card "pre-calculation announcements" tell extends here: she narrates that she has already calculated something nine years out, but does not narrate the content of the calculation. The reader should feel the load without reading the document.
- **The septon as the only soft landing.** When she speaks to or with Septon Aldric, the mask thins. The voice tells become slightly more visible. This is the only Westerosi figure with whom this happens in the opening run. The writer fork should treat his proximity as a register-modifier.
- **Children read as wrongness.** When the interior describes Westerosi children at play, the narration is precise, brief, and slightly displaced — she is sharing their developmental stage by body and is a stranger to them by mind. This is a base-card-pattern voice-tell extended: emotion-as-event, with the event being a small interior recoil that is not commented on.

## Non-verbal tics

Base-card tics (`taylor-hebert` §Non-verbal tics) all carry. Modifications and additions for the eleven-year-old Westerosi-mask register:

- **Body softened to read as child.** Where the base-card stillness is held, this variant softens it deliberately into something closer to a quiet child's stillness — small motions introduced (hands on cloth, feet shifted, a small adjustment to the overdress) on a calibrated cadence. The base stillness lives underneath; the child-fidget is a layer over it. Under pressure the layer thins and the base stillness reappears, which is part of the audit signal.
- **Eyes-down at the right moments.** A Westerosi child of her station looks down when an adult is speaking to her with rebuke or instruction. She has trained this. The eye-flicker that precedes the look-down is calibrated to be unobtrusive; under pressure it lags and the eye-contact holds a beat longer than the role permits, which the smarter observer catches.
- **The half-curtsy.** Septon's ward to a maester, to a knight, to any noble — a small bob, hands at the skirt, eyes down. Performed correctly. Under stress the timing is slightly wrong — too fast, slightly too deep, a beat off — and the slip is the audit flag.
- **The hand on the septon's sleeve.** When Septon Aldric is present and within reach, her hand finds his sleeve at moments that read as a child's seeking-comfort and that simultaneously give her a fixed point to read the room from. The gesture is genuine and tactical; she has not separated the two and does not try.
- **The fauna-track tilt, modified.** Base-card head-tilt at a sound the room hasn't registered now reads as *the strange, watchful child*. Adult observers register it; smart adult observers note it. She has tried to suppress it and cannot fully — the passive sense is structural. She compensates by directing the tilt as if toward a pre-existing object (a candle, a shadow, a bird at the window) so the gesture has cover.
- **Refusal of childish affect.** The Westerosi child's casual register includes wide-eyed wonder, dramatic disappointment, exaggerated curiosity. She does not produce these and cannot reliably fake them. Her refusal is itself the tell — under sustained adult observation, the not-doing of the expected childish behaviors registers.
- **Posture of a hard-worked sept ward.** Shoulders set for carrying water, hands roughened, posture economical. This is genuine, not performed; she has done the work. The posture covers some of the stillness because hard-worked children also do not fidget.
- **The careful greeting of ravens.** Where Northern dialect uses *the careful greeting of dogs*, this variant has the same gesture aimed at corvids — a slight head-incline, a brief hand-out without forcing the bird to acknowledge. Under cover of feeding the bell-tower roost. Witnesses read it as unusual familiarity with birds and may file it as omen-sign; she uses this strategically.
- **The cleanliness compulsion.** Base-card cape-hygiene survives as a tic. She is particular about being clean — hands washed before eating, the overdress shaken out, the floor of the cell-room swept. This is class-incongruent for a sept ward (smallfolk children are not this clean) and registers as a small audit signal to anyone paying attention.

## Memory monuments

The Westeros variant carries **two registers of monuments** that no one else in this world carries simultaneously: the Earth-Bet monuments as memory burden (inaccessible, never spoken aloud), and the Westerosi monuments as historical foreknowledge (read but not lived; some are coming).

### Earth-Bet monuments (carried as memory burden — never named aloud)

All Earth-Bet monuments from the base card carry. Westeros-specific authoring rules:

- **Hard fence: never named in any Westerosi-set scene.** No *the locker, the S9, Leviathan, Gold Morning, the Birdcage* in dialogue, ever. Audit immediately if a draft contains them.
- **Surface as displacement only.** A Westerosi scene may produce a register-shift in her — a child being deliberately harmed, an enclosed space, a fire at scale, the sudden silence of an area that was full of fauna — that traces back to an Earth-Bet monument. The interior knows; the spoken line does not name. The reader feels the weight without the cause being articulated.
- **The interior may circle.** Free-indirect or close-third interior may approach a monument without entering it. *This was not the year Bakuda lit the city. This was a different year.* The negation-by-naming is permitted at low frequency; the affirmation-by-naming is not.
- **Gold Morning as load on patience.** Her shorter patience-budget compared to base card traces directly to Gold Morning. The interior does not say so; the prose shows it through her decisions. Audience may flag a scene where her patience is base-card-default rather than Westeros-variant-default.

### Westerosi monuments (foreknowledge — read but not lived)

She has read *Fire & Blood*, ASOIAF, the histories. She knows the canonical events at the level of a careful adult reader. In Westeros, she has not lived these — they are either past (and she is one of the few who knows the full record) or future (and she is the only one who knows they are coming).

Authoring rule for this register: she does not voice these monuments by their canonical names. She voices them by Westerosi-internal register only — what a sept ward would have heard and would plausibly know. Slipping the canonical name (*Aegon's Conquest, Dance of the Dragons, Field of Fire*) without Westerosi cover is an audit fence.

- **Aegon's Conquest (1 BC – 2 AC).** The universal calendrical anchor. Permitted aloud in Westerosi register: *since the Conquest, after the Conquest, in the days before the Conquest came.* The dating system is hers to use. She uses it correctly and at adult precision under cover of being a literate sept ward.
- **The Doom of Valyria (~114 BC).** Permitted in Westerosi register: *the Doom, when the Freehold fell, the Doom of old Valyria.* Used with religious-historical weight as a Faith-trained ward would. She knows the historical record beyond what the Faith teaches; she does not deploy that knowledge.
- **The Field of Fire / Harren the Black.** *Where Harren burned. The black king who would not bend.* Locally significant — she lives in Harrenhal's shadow. The smallfolk and septons of the area carry this monument heavily. She voices it in their register; the historical specifics she knows beyond their version stay interior.
- **The Long Night.** Permitted in Faith register: *before the Long Night, the long winter of the First Men.* The Faith has its own version, which differs from the historical record she has read. She uses the Faith version aloud.
- **Maegor's reign and the Faith Militant Uprising.** A century in the past at this entry point. Permitted: *Maegor the Cruel, the troubles before King Jaehaerys, when the Faith bore swords.* A literate sept ward would know this; she voices it at sept-ward depth.
- **Jaehaerys and Alysanne.** Living memory for the eldest in the realm. *The Old King and the Good Queen.* She voices these in the affectionate-historical register the smallfolk and septons use.
- **The Dance of the Dragons (129–131 AC).** **Coming in approximately nine years.** This is the load-bearing future monument. She knows: who lights it, who survives, who burns, who hangs. She cannot say any of it without exposure. The interior tracks the timeline constantly; the spoken line never references it. Audit-fence: any line of hers that reveals foreknowledge of the Dance is the deepest audit-trip in this card.
- **The Hour of the Wolf (post-Dance, ~131 AC).** She knows it is coming. Specific to Northerners; she is not Northern but has read the histories. Same audit-fence as the Dance.

### Local / current-pressure monuments (sept ward of Harrenhal area, 120 AC)

These are the monuments her *current cover identity* (sept ward, no prior life) would plausibly carry.

- **Harren the Black and the burning of Harrenhal.** Universal in this region. Smallfolk speak of Harrenhal's curse; she echoes the local register. She knows more than the local register; she does not show it.
- **The dying septon's predecessors / the local sept's history.** A sept ward would know the history of her own sept — the prior septon, the building of the sept, local Faith events. She has acquired this and voices it at appropriate depth.
- **The current king (Viserys I) and his court.** A literate ward would know names: King Viserys, the Princess Rhaenyra, the Queen Alicent (depending on year), the Hand. She voices these in the sept-ward register — *His Grace, the King, the Princess* — and at the level of detail a ward would have. The level of detail she actually possesses is decades deeper; she clamps.

### Authoring rule

For this variant, the monument-register is a **doubled register**: the speaker's actual knowledge is on one level, the role-permitted knowledge is on a lower level, and the spoken line lives at the lower level with the higher level held back. The audit-reviewer evaluates lines on whether the doubled register is held — not whether the line is interesting or accurate, but whether it leaks knowledge the role does not have. Leakage is the load-bearing failure mode for this card.

## Authoring notes

- This variant inherits in full from the base card. If a writer fork loads only this card and not the base, the voice will be incomplete — the inheritance must be honored.
- Audit flags for this dialect specifically:
  - **Anachronistic idiom.** Highest-frequency slip. Any *okay, yeah, fine, sure, alright* in adult-facing dialogue is a flag.
  - **Earth-Bet proper noun.** Any cape-context word leaking through. Hard fence.
  - **Cognitive-age slip.** A child delivering a base-cadence long-planning sentence in front of someone with standing to flag it. May be intentional and load-bearing; if so, the surrounding prose should mark it. If unflagged, audit.
  - **Religious register error.** Wrong god named, wrong mode of address, old-gods/new-gods confusion. Audit.
  - **Mask-too-perfect.** A scene where she should slip but does not. The eleven-year-old performance has cost; if the cost is invisible across a long pressure-scene, the writer is overproducing the mask.
- Samples grow as episodes ship. Each shipped episode that produces in-voice spoken Taylor lines should backfill direct samples here, with provenance citation (e.g. `s01e01:42`).
