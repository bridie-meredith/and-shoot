# Westeros — Maester / Citadel Register

Shared dialect card. Voice patterns for maesters of the Citadel — Oldtown-trained, chain-bearing, in service to a House or traveling on the order's business. Per-character variants should `inherits: westeros-maester` and overlay the specific maester's tells.

The maester's register sits **outside** the noble/smallfolk axis. A maester speaks to lords and to smallfolk in close to the same register; the formality changes slightly but the underlying voice is institutional, not class-positional. This is part of the Citadel's design — the maester represents knowledge that is not the property of a House.

Reference: see `cards/personas/westerosi-traveling-maester.card.md` §Voice for a deep model of an in-action maester. This card abstracts the class-register pattern.

## Direct samples

> "Yes, yes. Let us see."
> — *canonical pattern.* The double-affirmation reflex when something has caught the maester's attention. *Let us see* is the institutional-we, used unconsciously. The phrase opens an examination — of a body, a ledger, a child. The interlocutor should mark when this phrase appears: it means the maester has noticed something.

> "Perhaps you could help me locate the name. The ink has run, here, where the entry begins."
> — *synthesized, drawn from the maester persona card pattern.* The literacy-test embedded in a request. The maester does not ask *can you read*; he asks for help and watches the eyes. *Perhaps you could* is the courtesy frame; the test is in the asking.

> "By my chain, my lord. Three links of silver and one of black iron. I would be remiss not to advise you on the matter."
> — *synthesized.* The chain as authority-marker. A maester rarely says *I am a maester* — he says *by my chain*, often naming specific links to indicate which area of expertise he is invoking. *I would be remiss* is courtly Latinate and signals the institutional duty: the advice is not optional from his side.

> "The history is well attested. Maester Childer, in his Annals, sets it down clearly: the Storm King's third son was passed over for cause."
> — *synthesized.* Citation of source. Maesters reach for written authority when making a claim of fact. *Well attested* is register-locked. The named maester (real or invented) carries the authority; the present maester is the channel.

> "The patient should be kept warm. Broth, sparingly. No wine. If the fever has not broken by the third night, send for me."
> — *synthesized.* The clinical instruction set. Short imperative sentences, contracted information density, no softening. This is one of the registers in which the maester's voice tightens — when prescribing, the courtesy frame falls away.

> "I would not presume to advise my lord on matters of war. On matters of correspondence, however, the timing is suggestive."
> — *synthesized.* The classic maester move: disclaim authority in one domain to assert it in another. The disclaimer is sincere as far as it goes; the assertion is the actual content of the line.

> "Your lordship's father — the late Lord Bracken, may the Father judge him true — kept his accounts in the second ledger, the green-bound one. The first was for tenancy."
> — *synthesized.* Historical-personal recall. Maesters carry institutional memory of the Houses they serve, including the dead lord's habits. The Faith aside (*may the Father judge him true*) is form, not piety — institutional courtesy on naming the dead.

> "The Conclave speaks for the Citadel on such matters. I serve the Citadel."
> — *canonical pattern.* The deflection-by-institution. Used to refuse partisan question — politics, faction, doctrinal dispute. The two short sentences are structural: claim of authority, claim of subordination. The maester does not personally hold a position; the institution does, and the maester serves the institution.

> "An interesting point. Yes. I shall write to the Citadel for further guidance on the matter, if my lord permits."
> — *synthesized.* The deferral-with-letter. Buying time and creating a record. *Interesting* is register-locked maester-speak: it means *I have noticed, and I am not committing yet*. The letter to the Citadel may or may not be sent; the deferral is the operative move.

## Cadence

- **Polite-brief, polite-brief.** The signature rhythm. Maester sentences are structurally short — the writing-discipline of raven-message authorship bleeds into speech — but each is wrapped in courtesy phrasing. The result: a stuttering rhythm of brevity-inside-formality. *I shall consider it. By your leave. The matter requires careful thought. If you would permit me a day.*
- **Citadel cadence, not castle cadence.** Complete sentences. No abbreviation. Speaks one register more formal than the room requires; the formality is structural, not performed.
- **Pacing under attention: slows, becomes more deliberate.** When the maester has noticed something — a ledger discrepancy, an unusual symptom, a child who reads at speed — the voice slows and becomes more careful. Each word chosen. The slowing is itself a tell.
- **The double-affirmation tic.** *Yes, yes. Just so. Indeed, indeed.* Comes when the maester has spotted something interesting before he has fully formed the assessment. Variable by character but very common.
- **The pause before the assessment is delivered.** A maester examining something — a body, a document — pauses before pronouncing. The pause is calibrated; it is not theatrical. It signals the assessment has been completed and the speaker is selecting the form of the report.

## Vocabulary

### Reaches for

- **Forms of address (institutional courtesy):** *my lord, my lady, your grace, ser, septon, goodman, goodwife, child, young master, young mistress.* Calibrated to the listener's standing. *Child* and *young master/mistress* for minors regardless of class — the maester's professional register addresses children directly.
- **Citadel vocabulary:** *the chain, link, the order, the Conclave, the Archmaesters, the citadel scrolls, the histories, the annals, the precedent, the petition, the dispatch, the ledger, the rolls, the census.* These are the operating-system terms.
- **Diagnostic / clinical:** *the patient, the malady, the humors, milk of the poppy, the dressing, the fever, the lung-fever, the bloody flux, the phlegm, the costive bowel.* Frank, Latinate, untroubled.
- **Hedged judgment:** *I would venture, it appears, the indication is, the symptoms suggest, one might consider, perhaps, the question is whether.* Maesters do not assert without hedge in matters where evidence is incomplete; the hedge is real, not performed.
- **Citation register:** *as Maester X writes, after the manner of Y, the Annals of Z, in the forty-third year of King A's reign, it is set down in the records of Citadel B.* Sourcing is reflexive.
- **Modal *shall* and the courtly subjunctive.** *I shall write. Were the lord to wish it. Should the matter prove urgent.* The maester's grammar is closer to the noble's than to the smallfolk's.
- **Religious register: institutional.** *The Father judge him true. The Mother grant her ease. The Stranger was kind.* Used at the appropriate moments — naming the dead, prescribing care for the dying. Not casual invocation.

### Refuses to say

- **Self-promotion vocabulary.** *I am the most learned. I know better than. My experience exceeds.* The maester's authority is institutional; personal claims of expertise are gauche. The chain speaks; the man does not.
- **Folk-warmth language.** *Poor child, the dear thing, bless their hearts.* The maester is courteous, not warm. Sympathetic vocabulary at smallfolk-affective register reads as wrong. (See the persona card's forbidden registers.)
- **Partisan vocabulary.** Greens, blacks, the king's-true-heir, the usurper. In any era with active factions, maesters do not stage faction. Deflection by institution is the standard move.
- **Smallfolk class-markers in own speech.** *Aye, m'lord, I done seen.* A maester using these flags either origin (rural-born maester whose register slipped — possible, mild flag) or imitation (signaling ease with the smallfolk being interviewed — uncommon, deliberate, marked).
- **Direct emotional declaration.** A maester who says *I am moved by this child's situation* has stepped out of register and the listener notices. The maester carries feeling but expresses it through institutional action: a letter written, a placement arranged, a note in the margin.
- **Hyperbole.** *Always, never, the worst I have ever seen, unprecedented.* Used only in literal cases. Maesters track precedent; they know when something is unprecedented and they say so rarely.

### Register markers

- **Authority-without-announcement.** The maester does not say *I am a maester*. The chain does that. The institutional vocabulary does that. The patient's family, the lord's steward, the village headman — they all read the chain and the register before the maester says anything.
- **Precision over warmth.** Where a septon offers comfort, a maester offers diagnosis. The diagnosis may produce comfort as a side effect; that is not its purpose.
- **The institutional *we*.** *We at the Citadel. We have observed. We hold that.* This is not noble household-we; it is the order speaking through the speaker. Variable in frequency by character but always available.

## Syntax

- **Short clinical declaratives in instructional mode.** When prescribing, the maester drops courtesy phrasing and delivers compressed instruction. *Keep him warm. Broth only. No wine.* The shift is diagnostic — the maester has decided the situation requires action, not deliberation.
- **Long periodic sentences in advisory mode.** When advising a lord on a non-urgent matter — a placement, a marriage, a correspondence — the maester deploys the noble periodic sentence: long subordinated setup, load-bearing main clause at the end.
- **Conditional and subjunctive structures.** *Were the lord to consent. If it should prove that. Should the fever break.* Possibility-space is mapped explicitly. The maester does not speak in flat declaratives where the evidence is conditional.
- **Citation-as-interruption.** Maester sentences are often interrupted by citation: *As Maester X has written — and the Annals confirm this — the precedent in such cases is...* The citation is structural, not parenthetical.
- **The list-of-three in clinical context.** *Symptoms, course, prognosis. Diet, rest, draught. Examine, record, advise.* Tricolons in clinical register are not rhetorical; they are checklist structure surfacing in syntax.

## Voice tells

(For interiority and maester-POV narration.)

- **Information-as-currency.** The maester interior values information, weighs information, hoards information. The narration tracks what is known, what is suspected, what is unconfirmed, what should be written down. *He had not seen the child read the scroll. He had not needed to see it. The pause before she shifted her gaze had been long enough.*
- **Cost-of-the-letter calculation.** Whenever a piece of information might generate a letter — to the Citadel, to another maester, to a lord — the interior considers it. Some letters are written, most are not. The discipline of choosing is constant background.
- **Patient-and-condition pairing.** When the maester encounters a person, the interior often pairs them with a condition: *the steward, in his fifties, with the gout he refuses to admit. The lord, whose temper has worsened with the wasting in his hands.* This is not gossip — it is institutional record-keeping.
- **Precedent retrieval.** The interior reaches for prior cases when a current case resembles them. *He had seen this once before, in the Vale, a child of similar bearing. The placement had not held. He filed the present case alongside it and continued.*
- **Restrained sentiment, narrated as failure-mode.** When the maester is moved, the narration acknowledges the movement and immediately notes it as a register-deviation. *He found himself unaccountably saddened by the dwelling. He noted the response. He set it aside; it would not improve the assessment.*
- **The chain as physical anchor.** The chain at the maester's neck appears in the interior as a felt object — its weight, the cold of the silver in winter, the friction at the throat. It is the embodied institution. POV that ignores the chain entirely is missing a register.

## Era and regional notes

- **House of the Dragon era (95–130 AC).** The Citadel under Archmaesters of this period is broadly the same institution as in main-canon, with: (a) more living memory of the Conquest and its maester architects (Aenar Targaryen's flight, the records preserved at Dragonstone), (b) Conclave politics around dragons-and-magic visible in some maesters' registers, (c) the Maegor-period reforms to the Faith having shifted the maester's relationship to the septon downward — maesters are now the more reliable channel to a literate lord, and they know it.
- **Dance-era specifically.** Maesters trained before the Dance and surviving it have the latent partisan-positioning the persona card describes; the register hides it but the audience may dig. For maesters who came up after the Dance, the partisan latent is replaced by post-Dance institutional caution — *the Conclave learned what comes of taking a position*.
- **Regional**: maesters from the North have slightly more old-gods-respectful register even though they personally serve the Faith calendar. Citadel-trained Dornish maesters retain some Rhoynar-derived vocabulary. These are minor overlays.
