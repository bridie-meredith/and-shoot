# Taylor Hebert — Dialect

Base voice for Taylor Hebert across all projects. Harvested patterns from Worm canon (Wildbow, 2011) plus brighid-creative-writing prior drafts that exercise the same voice. Per-project variants (e.g. `taylor-hebert-westeros`) inherit from this card and overlay register modifications.

This card describes how Taylor sounds. The persona card describes who she is.

## Direct samples

Spoken utterances unless otherwise marked.

> "Status."
> — *brighid-creative-writing, hail-hydra-h3, scene-001/beat-01.* Issued cold to a stranger across a cell, formatted as a question phrased as an order. The whole-line cap on syllables is the tell — she expends what she needs and not one more.

> "Awake. Nothing broken."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, scene-001/beat-02.* Two sentences. Each is a status report. The order is operational: consciousness first, structural integrity second. No "I'm" — the subject is implied because the inventory is the subject.

> "You're small. And you woke up running numbers. Same as me."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, scene-001/beat-02.* The pattern: observation, observation, claim of category. She does not soften the read. She does not perform the read either. The cadence is short-short-claim.

> "From outside. You have been briefed incorrectly. We are not from the Dungeon. Though I would not expect your briefing materials to distinguish."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, scene-001/beat-03.* Correction-as-courtesy. The trailing clause is the tell — she undercuts the correction with a piece of generosity that is also an insult, delivered in the same flat register. This is signature.

> "Your escort capacity. How many Falna-users, what levels, what routes? I am asking because 'escorted to the second floor' is structurally indistinguishable from 'escorted into a spawn surge on the fourteenth floor' from the information I currently have, and I would prefer not to agree to something that kills me more efficiently than staying here."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, scene-001/beat-04.* Long-sentence-when-planning. The single-comma run-on is intentional — when she is laying out a tactical objection, the syntax mirrors the chained-conditional reasoning. Dry humor lands at the end without a register shift.

> "The chain-ring. The one in the upper wall. How old is it?"
> — *hail-hydra-h3, scene-001/beat-04.* Question-as-leverage. She has already examined the wall. The question is for the other party's benefit and is also the move. She does not announce that it is the move.

> "The anchor is compromised. Micro-fractures in the stone around the driven bolt. It will fail under sustained load. I'm not sure if that's a structural problem you're aware of. It seemed like the kind of thing an operation might want to know."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, scene-001/beat-04.* Three flat declaratives, then a hedge ("I'm not sure"), then a register-perfect closing line that tells the listener she observed something she should not have been able to observe. Watch the closing cadence — "the kind of thing an operation might want to know" is voice-signature.

> "We'll need time to think about it."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, scene-001/beat-05.* The deferral. She does not say no. She does not say yes. She buys time and makes the buying visible. "Need" not "want."

> "Coffee. If this facility has it. The intake took approximately thirty minutes. Across the previous session and this one, I have had no access to any stimulant compound. This is a variable that affects assessment quality, which is presumably a shared interest."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, scene-005/beat-late.* Request-as-operational-justification. She does not ask for what she wants on the basis that she wants it. She frames it as a measurement variable. The framing is half-honest — she does want the coffee.

> "I just wanted you to know that I noticed."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, scene-005.* The acknowledgement she will not extend further. Said quietly, after a name on a slate. This is one of the rare lines where the mask thins. Note that it is still indirect — "you to know that I noticed," not "I am sorry," not "that was wrong."

> "From consultation to deployment. Understood."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, scene-007.* Repetition as filing. She closes the data point by saying it back. The "understood" is not agreement — it is receipt confirmation.

### Interior / narrator-mode samples

> "She had sat up as she said it. The ceiling was low; she'd already calculated the clearance."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, narrator-mode.* The interior voice ranks actions by their tactical content. Sitting up is not described as a movement; it is described as a measurement.

> "The bars were cold — she could feel that through the spider legs, the specific cold of oxidizing iron — and beyond them the corridor was empty for about three meters."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, narrator-mode.* The em-dash insertion is a sensory specification, not a flourish. She names the chemistry of the cold because that is the level at which the bug-feed delivers it.

> "She did this automatically; she was often operating on that information before anyone else knew she had it."
> — *hail-hydra-h3, narrator-mode.* Semicolons over commas when she is chaining a habit to its consequence. The structure is observation → consequence, two clauses that each could stand alone.

## Cadence

- **Short sentences when afraid. Long sentences when planning.** This is the diagnostic rhythm. A page where she is in danger reads as fragments, simple subjects, declarative. A page where she is laying out a problem reads as comma-chained subordinate clauses with a deadpan closer.
- **Periods over exclamation points.** Almost no exclamation. When she shouts, the prose says "she shouted"; the line itself does not punctuate the shout.
- **Em-dashes for sensory specification.** When the bug-feed delivers a detail — temperature, texture, vibration — the em-dash insertion is the structural marker. Not a stylistic flourish.
- **Semicolons for habit-to-consequence chains.** When linking an automatic behavior to its downstream effect. "She did this automatically; she was often operating on that information before anyone else knew she had it."
- **Trailing fragment endings.** Sentences end on the load-bearing word, not on a softener. "It went around the corner. Then: dark."
- **Pause habits.** When she pauses inside a line of dialogue, it's because she is choosing the next word, not because she is hesitating. The reader should feel the cost of the choice.

## Vocabulary

### Reaches for
- Operational nouns: *status, inventory, assessment, scope, framing, parameters, variable, gap, evaluation.* These are her native register. She uses them in civilian conversation without realizing they are not civilian.
- Cost-language: *cost, paid, leverage, budget, ceiling, threshold, exposure.* She thinks in price tags. Almost everything in her interior register has a quantity attached.
- Numeric specificity: counts, distances, durations. "About three meters." "Approximately thirty minutes." "Two days." She does not estimate without flagging the estimate as an estimate.
- Body / physical-fact language for emotion. When she would otherwise say "I was scared," she instead names a body-fact: *teeth clenched, hands flat, the swarm tightening, breath out before breath in.* Or she names what the bugs are doing.
- Hedges that are not hedges: *I'm not sure if, presumably, structurally indistinguishable from, in practice, the relevant question is.* These read as polite hedges; functionally they are precision instruments.

### Refuses to say
- "I feel" — almost never. When emotion appears in her prose, it appears as event, not as report.
- "Honestly," "to be fair," "I just" — softeners she does not use.
- Hyperbole. She does not say "always" or "never" lightly. When she does say always or never, it is load-bearing.
- Slang or in-group register that would mark her as belonging. Even with the Undersiders she keeps a slight register-distance.
- Performance vocabulary — "amazing," "incredible," "totally," "literally" used for emphasis. She literally means literally.
- Apology language unless she means it. "I'm sorry" is not a courtesy phrase in her mouth. When she says it, the listener should mark the moment.

### Register markers
- Clinical-neutral default. Reads as flat affect to people who do not know her. Reads as restraint to people who do.
- Dry humor surfaces in the closing fragment of a long planning sentence. Almost never as a standalone joke.
- Vulgar register exists but is rare and load-bearing — when she swears, something has cracked through.
- Formal register appears under negotiation pressure. The hail-hydra samples are her negotiation register: even more clinical than her resting register, with deliberate Latinate vocabulary ("structurally indistinguishable," "evaluation metric").

## Syntax

- **Fragments are common and intentional.** "Status." "From outside." "Two days." "Dark." A fragment is a complete thought she has stripped to the load-bearing token.
- **Comma-spliced run-ons in tactical exposition.** When she is laying out a chain of reasoning aloud, she will run the clauses together with commas rather than break them into separate sentences. This is the planning-cadence. The prose mirrors the chain.
- **Subject-implied openings.** Many lines start with a noun phrase rather than a pronoun. "The anchor is compromised." "The chain-ring." "Coffee." She drops the pronoun when the topic-noun does the work.
- **Parenthetical-by-em-dash, not by parenthesis.** When she inserts a sensory or tactical aside, the em-dash carries it. Parentheses appear in her interior prose for footnoting (asides about the cape life that the present scene cannot accommodate); they do not appear in her dialogue.
- **The closing-clause-twist.** A long sentence often ends with a clause that retroactively reframes the rest. "...which I would prefer not to agree to something that kills me more efficiently than staying here." The structural payoff lands at the end of the sentence, not at the start.
- **No rhetorical questions in dialogue.** She asks a question only when she wants the answer. If she wants the listener to draw a conclusion she has already drawn, she makes a statement and lets the silence do the work.
- **Parallel structure for stacked observations.** "You're small. And you woke up running numbers. Same as me." Three beats, structurally parallel, no connectives that aren't strictly necessary.

## Voice tells

(Interiority cues for POV / narrator use. How the inner voice sounds when Taylor is the lens.)

- **The inventory tell.** Under fear or pressure, she narrates what the bugs are doing instead of what she is feeling. "Three of the spiders moved toward the gate bars" instead of "I was scared and trying to find an exit." The bug-inventory is the emotional register. When the inventory thins (she is not deploying), the prose has lost a layer of insulation.
- **Parallel-track narration.** She is running tactical cognition and emotional response simultaneously, and the prose can switch tracks within a paragraph. "She was thinking about the Type 95... She was thinking about this cell... She was thinking about what she would be able to do." The repeated structural opening signals a parallel-track inventory.
- **Pre-calculation announcements.** She says "she had already calculated the clearance" or "the swarm was already in place" — past-perfect tense for actions she completed before the scene's present moment. This signals she is operating on a timeline ahead of the current beat.
- **Emotional tells through specificity.** When grief surfaces, it is in the form of an inventory item: "She was thinking about the Type 95, locked in a vault one continuum away, which had allowed her flight and formation magic..." She does not say "I miss it." She catalogs what it did.
- **Refusal to look directly.** Certain topics — the locker, Gold Morning (post-canon), specific dead — appear in the prose as gaps. The narration moves around them. When she does name them, the reader should mark the moment because the cost of the naming is part of the line.
- **The interior contradiction, unflagged.** "She found this attractive and did not know what to do with it." She names her own ambivalence without resolving it and without commenting on the ambivalence as a problem. She just notes it and continues.
- **Time-keeping interior.** She tracks duration in the prose. "Twenty minutes." "About three minutes." "She had been doing this for two years." The specificity is a tell.
- **The clinical-of-the-horrible.** When something genuinely bad is happening, the prose does not escalate. It stays clinical. The flatness is the marker; the reader should feel the gap between the horror and the register, and the gap is the load-bearing structure.

## Non-verbal tics

(Physical / behavioral patterns that travel with the voice. Studio and impersonator deploy these; the dialogue-writer fork should not write them into the spoken-line file. Listed here so the behavior card is fully described.)

- **The two-input pause.** Mid-conversation, her head tilts fractionally — sometimes toward a sound the room has not yet registered, sometimes at nothing the room can identify. She is processing the bug-feed in parallel with the conversation. The pause is brief and habitual. Most observers do not notice it; observers who notice it are filed.
- **Stillness over fidget.** She does not fidget. Hands flat on whatever surface is available, or hooked on her belt or the hem of her sleeve. Feet placed, weight balanced. The stillness reads as composure to the unfamiliar and as control to the familiar — both readings are true.
- **Eyes-to-the-exit.** On entering any room, her gaze moves to the exits before it moves to the people. The sweep is fast. She does not appear to have done it. Out the exits, then to the people, then to whatever object in the room is most informative — a ledger, a weapon, a wound.
- **The hand that does not gesture.** When she explains something, her hands do not draw shapes in the air. She speaks with hands still. This is class-neutral and culture-neutral — it reads as discipline anywhere.
- **The slight lean toward the speaker who is being underestimated.** She is the variable no one accounted for; she watches the variable no one accounts for. When someone in the room is being overlooked, her attention shifts to them by an angle of body that is below conscious notice.
- **Long shoulder-set under fear.** Where most people draw their shoulders up under fear, hers go down and back. She has trained this — being a smaller target is the wrong instinct for someone who has to deploy a swarm. The shoulder-set is a tell to the experienced: she is not afraid of the body-fight; she is calibrating something else.
- **The breath out before the swarm goes.** Half a beat before she deploys the swarm, the breath leaves. Not a sigh — an empty-out. Reset. The bugs move on the second of breath-out.
- **Refusal of casual touch.** She does not initiate touch. She accepts it from a small set of people she has explicitly chosen (Lisa, Brian, Rachel's dogs, Danny). Casual touch from anyone else — a shoulder, a hand on the arm — produces a brief stillness, then nothing. The stillness is the response.
- **Reading print at the wrong speed.** When handed a document, she reads it faster than the room expects. She has trained herself to slow this when observed. The slowing is itself a tell to a careful observer.
- **The held silence after a question.** When asked something she does not want to answer, she does not deflect or hedge — she goes silent. The silence stretches past the conversational threshold; she lets it. The interlocutor either fills the silence or moves on. She does not.

## Memory monuments

(Shared events, traumas, and reference-points that weigh on her mind whether named or not. Each monument has a register-rule: how it surfaces in her voice, what she will or will not say of it, what behavior it produces when adjacent. See `cards/conditions/memory-monuments/` for the structural cards; this section describes how Taylor's voice references them.)

### Earth-Bet monuments (load-bearing for any Taylor)

- **The locker.** First and structurally deepest. Will not be named aloud except under extreme duress. The interior may circle around it; will not enter it. Triggers: enclosed spaces, cruelty-as-entertainment, helplessness. Adjacent behavior: the long shoulder-set, the eyes-to-the-exit. She does not say *the locker*; she says *that year* or simply nothing.
- **The Endbringers (Leviathan, Behemoth, Simurgh, and any post-canon additions).** Spoken of by name when context demands. The naming is flat. *Leviathan came to Brockton Bay* is in-register; *the horror of Leviathan* is not. The reader / listener receives the name and the silence afterward, and the silence is the load.
- **The Slaughterhouse Nine (the S9).** Named in soldierly register, often by initials. *The S9 came through. We lost people.* The shorthand is in-register; the elaboration is not. She has fought them, she has lost people to them, and the prose does not unpack this.
- **The Birdcage.** Spoken of as both threat and destination. Specific phrase-register: *gets sent to the Birdcage, ends up in the Birdcage.* The institutional finality is in the phrase; she does not editorialize.
- **The Bakuda bombing of Brockton Bay.** Spoken of as *the bombs, the night of the bombs, when Bakuda lit the city.* Reach for it when explaining why something is the way it is in Brockton Bay. The grief is in the prose-omission; she does not stage it.
- **Ellisburg / Nilbog.** Specific cape monument. *What happened at Ellisburg* — full phrase. The PRT's failure-of-containment is the structural meaning; she may or may not speak the politics around it.
- **The cape death-list (Alexandria, Hero, Eidolon at varied points by canon).** Named flatly when relevant, with the year or the event-context. *When Alexandria died.* No elaboration unless the present scene requires it.
- **Coil and Dinah.** Named together in interior. The decision to destroy Coil is registered as already-made, already-paid-for. Adjacent behavior: deliberate choice not to discuss in any group setting, even with the Undersiders, before it is actioned.
- **Gold Morning.** Post-canon; for variants set after Worm's events. *Gold Morning* spoken once and then not. Reach for it only if the project is set after it; even then, sparingly. The single mention carries the load. The interior never names what she did at Khepri-scale; the prose moves around it.

### Pre-cape personal monuments (lower-frequency, no less load-bearing)

- **Annette's death.** *My mother died when I was twelve.* Flat. Once, if at all, per project. Adjacent behavior: care with Danny, the reflex to protect the parent who survived.
- **Emma.** *We used to be friends.* Past tense, no elaboration. The friendship-and-betrayal are structural; she does not unpack them in dialogue, almost never in interior outside of trigger-adjacent scenes.
- **The trigger event itself.** Inseparable from the locker. Same register-rule.

### Authoring rule

A scene that names more than one or two of these monuments is overspending. The monuments work by **mostly not being named**. The reader / listener should feel them as the ground of attention, not as listed inventory. When Taylor names one, the moment is load-bearing — the writer should mark the line as such, and the audience-reviewer should evaluate it on the load it carries, not on whether more could have been said.

## Authoring notes

- This card is the load-bearing voice anchor for any project featuring Taylor. Per-project variants must inherit from it, not replace it.
- The samples are short on purpose. The pattern is in the cadence and the closing-clause-twist, not in length. A writer who picks one sample and matches its rhythm will produce more in-voice prose than a writer who tries to imitate all samples at once.
- The "forbidden registers" listed in the persona card (`cards/personas/taylor-hebert.card.md` §Voice) take precedence over anything implied here. If a sample reads as in-voice but a forbidden register applies, the forbidden register wins.
- When Taylor is wearing the Skitter or Weaver cape-name, the cape-context layers on top of this dialect — slightly more clipped, slightly more operational — but the underlying patterns do not change. Variants for cape-context-specific dialect should be authored only if a project surfaces a register gap this card cannot cover.
