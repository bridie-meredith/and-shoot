# Monument — Override-Architecture Prohibition (122 AC)

## Description

Interior monument card for Taylor Hebert at 122 AC. The Khepri-period scale-coordinating-override capability — the once-deployed mass-control apparatus that ended Taylor's prior world — and the vow prohibiting its redeployment that she carried forward into this life. Two-part monument: the architecture (what the capability is and what it can do) and the prohibition (the vow not to use it, which is the load-bearing structure the monument names). The prohibition is not a world-law, not a power-mechanics constraint, and not an absence: it is a choice she has already made, made specifically because of what the architecture did the one time she used it at scale. The monument lights when her pattern-reading reaches the threshold that precedes the architecture — when the coordination logic assembles in the passive sense without authorization, and the vow has to catch it.

Distinct from `cond-override-architecture-residue-122ac` (the mechanism card: world-law that the multi-shard hijack capability is sealed; the structural absence of Shard infrastructure on Planetos). That card covers what the world does not permit. This card covers what Taylor refuses — the interior weight of the prohibition, not the external constraint. Both reference the same capability; they describe different levels: one is auditor-facing world-law, this one is impersonator-facing monument-content.

## Sensory Impact

None at the world level. The monument does not alter the ambient environment. Its presence is entirely interior:

- In Taylor's passive sense: a brief pattern of coordinating awareness assembling at the edge of the insect-feed that has no instruction behind it. The architecture trying to run on its own is the sensory event. It resolves without deployment because the vow catches it.
- In Taylor's interior register: a pause in the pattern-reading that the surface does not explain. Clinical-of-the-horrible register; the interior notes the event and files it as cost paid again.
- In the prose surface: no signal unless the narrator-interest and memory-flags facets fire on the triggering beat. The monument is interior-facing; its expression is in the facet layer, not in the world-state.

## Duration

Permanent across the project. The architecture is sealed by world-law (`cond-override-architecture-residue-122ac`, `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`) and by vow. The monument itself is not temporary — the weight of what the architecture did does not reduce across the project arc. The prohibition does not relax.

**Monument-lighting frequency:** Infrequent by design. The pattern-reading threshold that triggers this monument is high; most of Taylor's coordination work with the insect-network does not reach it. When the monument lights, it lights against a specific pattern: large-scale organizational assessment that the architecture is structurally suited to run, in a situation where running it would produce a consequential outcome. The lighting event is not a temptation-narrative; it is a recognition event followed by the vow catching it. The two-phase structure (read arrives → vow catches) is the monument's load-bearing shape.

## Monument Body

### The architecture

The scale-coordinating-override capability at its deployment-scale produced a single, total outcome: the coordination of thousands of bodies against a target that could not be stopped by any other means. The outcome was the desired outcome. The cost was a civilization. This is the interior fact the monument holds. Not as guilt-framing — the interior does not narrate guilt — but as a calibration datum. At Khepri-scale, the architecture is not a tool with a handle; it is an event. Once deployed, it runs to completion. The operator's intentions past the point of deployment are not load-bearing.

The architecture she carries is not the Khepri-scale version — the Shard infrastructure required to run that does not exist on Planetos, and the residue she has in 122 AC is sealed against that use. But the pattern-recognition precog that degraded from the organizational capacity she had at Khepri-scale retained the structural logic. She reads large-scale coordination problems the way the architecture was designed to read them: identifying the points of application, calculating the cascade. The reading is not the deployment. The reading is what the vow catches.

### The vow

The prohibition is not a formal vow; there is no naming ceremony, no witness. The vow is the shape of a decision already taken and not revisited. She came here having already made the decision — that the architecture she used once would not be used again; that the circumstance that warranted it on Earth-Bet does not warrant it anywhere else, because the warrantability of the one use was inseparable from the finality of the outcome, and no outcome available in this life is structurally equivalent to that finality. The vow's logic: there is no situation here where deploying the architecture produces an outcome worth the cost, because the only outcome worth the cost was the one that already happened, and that one is behind her.

The vow is not maintained by effort; it is maintained by the clarity of the logic. The monument lights when the logic is tested — not by a deliberate argument against the vow, but by the pattern-read arriving before the vow catches it. The architecture reads first; the prohibition catches second. This is the monument's phenomenology. The interior registers the gap between first and second as cost paid again: the gap itself is the load.

### Threshold-of-deployment dynamic

The monument lights specifically at the threshold between reading and deploying — the beat where the architecture has assembled the coordination logic and the decision to run or not-run it is the present moment. Before the threshold: the architecture is pattern-reading, which is permitted, which is inevitable given the residual cognitive structure. After the threshold: the vow has caught it, the architecture is held, and the event is registered as cost paid and filed. The threshold is the beat; the monument fires at the threshold.

For memory-flag authoring: the threshold-of-deployment beat is the monument-trigger. The trigger is not the deployment (which does not happen); it is the arrival of the read that precedes deployment combined with the vow catching it in the same interior beat. The two-phase structure must be present for the monument to fire correctly: read arrives (the architecture-logic assembles) → vow catches (the prohibition names itself, holds the architecture). A read without the catch is not this monument. A catch without a prior read is not this monument. Both phases in one beat, at the threshold, is the monument.
