# Westerosi Witness Vocabulary — How Characters Describe Taylor's Capability

## Description

Governs the vocabulary available to Westerosi characters when they describe, report, or discuss Taylor's insect-control and pattern-recognition capabilities. No Westerosi character has access to parahuman concepts. Their descriptions route through the cultural frames available to them. This card is the negative-space companion to `cond-earth-bet-noun-fence` — that card governs what Taylor does not say; this card governs what Westerosi characters do say instead.

## Sensory Impact

None directly. The expression is in the specific words Westerosi characters reach for when they witness or report Taylor's capability. The vocabulary is culturally specific and class-specific; a Flea Bottom smallfolk person describes what they saw differently from a maester, who describes it differently from an Otto Hightower intelligence report.

## Duration

Persistent throughout the project.

## The Available Frames by Class

**Smallfolk (Flea Bottom):**
The folk vocabulary for Taylor's capability is: *witchery*, *the touch*, *cunning-craft*, *the old woman's knowledge*, *being god-marked* (negative or neutral), *having the devil's ear* (negative, more fearful). At street level, the term that sticks is usually whatever rhymes with the witness's existing relationship to the phenomenon — a fearful witness says "witchery" or "devil's work"; an opportunistic witness says "she knows things" or "she has some cunning"; an older woman who has seen stranger things says "the old ways" or "she's touched by something."

**Specific witness vocabulary for insect events:**
- "The flies went to her hand" / "the flies moved where she looked"
- "The rats came when she stood there — rats don't come to people like that"
- "She stood in a cloud of gnats and they didn't trouble her"
- "Something was with her in the air"
- "The bees moved wrong — they moved together, not like bees"

**Specific witness vocabulary for pattern-recognition (the behavioral precog effect):**
- "She knew before it happened" / "she saw it coming"
- "She asked about [event] before anyone knew it was going to happen"
- "She wasn't there when it went wrong — she left before anyone knew to leave"
- "She says things that turn out true"

**The Watch and minor officials:**
Watch officers have a more institutional vocabulary. They do not usually say "witch" in a report; they use the institutional frame available to them: *person of unusual behavior*, *of unknown provenance with behaviors consistent with use of cunning or craft*, *reported to have knowledge or capability not explained by ordinary means*. The Watch's vocabulary is a laundered version of the street-level vocabulary — the superstition is real but expressed in procedural register.

**Maesters:**
Maesters are institutionally skeptical of supernatural claims. A maester who observes Taylor's capability will reach for natural-philosophy explanations first: *unusual familiarity with vermin*, *trained in some Eastern craft of animal-handling*, *effect of specific pheromone compounds or feeding practices not documented in the Citadel's records*. A maester's report is the most likely to avoid supernatural vocabulary while still flagging Taylor as anomalous. The Citadel's institutional frame is "unexplained natural phenomenon" not "witchcraft."

**The Faith (septons, Most Devout):**
Faith vocabulary is theological: *touched by evil* / *marked by the Stranger* (the death aspect of the Seven) / *devilry* / *foreign sorcery* (for things that cannot be explained within the Seven's framework) / *diabolical craft*. A septon witnessing what a smallfolk person would call witchery has access to a richer theological vocabulary for categorizing it as spiritually threatening rather than merely strange. The Faith's vocabulary is more dangerous to Taylor than the Watch's — the institutional consequences of a Faith determination of "devilry" include the possibility of formal intervention.

**Noble and court characters (when filtered through Otto's network):**
The intelligence-report vocabulary strips the superstition out and focuses on capability and threat-category. Otto Hightower's network does not write "witch" — it writes: *foreign national of unknown provenance, observed to exhibit behavioral patterns consistent with capability of unknown nature in the area of animal behavior and foreknowledge, assessed as potential intelligence asset or intelligence vulnerability*. The intelligence vocabulary is the most legible to Taylor's actual situation and the most dangerous because it sees her correctly as a capability rather than a category.

## Vocabulary Prohibitions

**No Westerosi character uses parahuman vocabulary.** Terms like "power," "ability," "trigger," "shard," "cape," or any Worm-canon terminology are not available to Westerosi witnesses. This is a hard fence. A Westerosi character who describes Taylor's capability using Earth-Bet vocabulary is a fence violation regardless of how the author intends the scene.

**No Westerosi character correctly identifies the mechanism.** The most sophisticated Westerosi observer (a maester, an intelligence-network analyst) may correctly identify that Taylor's capability is real, consistent, and controllable. They do not and cannot identify it as a parahuman fauna-control shard-derived capability. Their explanation is always within their world's available explanatory frameworks, even when those frameworks are wrong.

**Dragon-and-witch conflation is not canon to KL.** In some Westerosi contexts, unusual capabilities near dragons are attributed to Valyrian blood or dragon-connection. Taylor's capability has no dragon connection (the insect dead-zone around dragons is the opposite of connection). Westerosi witnesses near the Dragonpit who observe Taylor's insect-network behavior should not attribute it to dragon-magic; that is a lore violation.

## Scene-Specific Rules

**When Taylor is the POV:** Other characters' witness vocabulary reaches Taylor through dialogue and through her insect-network's observation of their body language and conversation fragments. She hears "she has witchery" and registers it as threat-level and classification — useful intelligence, wrong mechanism.

**When a Westerosi character is a scene partner:** Their description of Taylor to third parties (guards, other characters, letter-writers) follows the vocabulary of their class and their institutional role as described above. A smallfolk person says "witch." A maester writes "of unknown capability." Otto's network writes a threat-assessment.

**The maester-chronicler coda:** Governed by `cond-maester-chronicler-voice`. The coda is not a witness to Taylor's capability in the same mode as in-story Westerosi characters — it is retrospective, archival, and operating after the events have been assessed by history. The coda's vocabulary is the institutional maester-register applied retrospectively.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-earth-bet-noun-fence`:** This card governs what others say; that card governs what Taylor says and thinks. Together they define the complete vocabulary boundary for Taylor's capability in this project.

**With `cond-westerosi-superstition-frame`:** That card covers the general Westerosi folk epistemology for uncanny events. This card is the KL-and-122-AC-specific vocabulary derived from that epistemology, organized by class.

**With `cond-kl-witch-label-formation-122ac`:** That card governs the institutional escalation process by which this vocabulary accumulates into a label and reaches Otto Hightower's network. Load together for any scene where witnesses observe Taylor's insect-network behavior.

**For auditor use:** Flag any line of dialogue or reported speech where a Westerosi character uses parahuman vocabulary. Flag any scene where a Westerosi character correctly names the mechanism of Taylor's capability. Flag any scene where the Watch or Faith character uses street-level superstition vocabulary in a formal context (their institutional vocabulary is different). Flag any attribution of Taylor's capability to dragon-connection or Valyrian blood.
