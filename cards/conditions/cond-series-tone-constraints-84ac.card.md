# Series Tone Constraints — taylor-hebert-jaehaerys (84–101 AC Config)

## Description

Project-level tonal law. Defines the operational registers this series runs in, the registers it does not run in, and the specific escalation-as-structure rule that governs how scenes must be constructed. These constraints apply to all agents across all scenes — they are not Taylor-specific persona rules, they are production rules for the entire series. The auditor loads this card when reviewing scene-level register drift.

The substance of this card is genre-level and project-configuration-independent. What is different from the prior project: cadence (the runway is longer, ~17 years rather than ~9), the series horizon (Great Council 101 AC, not the Dance itself), the prohibition on catharsis (specifically: catharsis before the Dance is prohibited because Taylor knows the Dance follows regardless), and the costs-become-final framing at Council close (the Council is a stress-test, not a resolution).

## Sensory Impact

Tonal constraints have no ambient sensory expression. Their expression is felt in scene velocity, in the ratio of action beats to reflection beats, in whether a scene's center of gravity is forward-moving or interior. A scene that violates these constraints will feel slow, over-explained, or intellectually remote from its physical stakes.

## Duration

Persistent across all episodes of this project. These are series-defining rules, not episode-specific constraints.

## The Primary Register

**Fast. Pulpy. Dramatic.**

Scene pace is driven by physical stakes and immediate consequence. The sentence-level and beat-level writing style prioritizes forward velocity. A scene that lingers must be lingering on something that escalates. If a scene is lingering on a feeling or a thought that does not create a new problem or reveal a new danger, it is drifting.

Pulpy register means the world's drama is felt in the body: cold, hunger, fear, pain, proximity, speed. Abstract analysis of a political situation is not the primary register; the physical experience of being in the middle of the political situation is. Taylor's tactical cognition produces rapid assessments, not extended disquisitions. She thinks fast and moves; she does not think slow and consider.

Dramatic register means the series acknowledges that what is happening matters and treats it accordingly. Understatement is allowed when the understatement amplifies the drama. Flat affect in the face of horror is allowed when the flat affect is itself the dramatic signal. What is not allowed is ironic detachment that deflates stakes or meta-awareness that distances the reader from consequences.

## The Prohibited Registers

**No chess-match scheming as the primary mode.** Taylor has tactical intelligence. She reads situations fast and identifies leverage. She uses that intelligence in service of action. What she does not do, in this series, is sit in a room for a scene and plan twelve moves ahead in elaborate detail. Planning happens in motion or in brief, and then the situation changes and the plan is worthless anyway. This is not the story of a protagonist who wins by out-thinking everyone; it is the story of a protagonist who escalates faster than everyone and then has to live with the consequences.

**No extended introspection as the primary mode.** Taylor has an interior life. Gold Morning is present in her at all times. She has grief, moral complexity, the weight of things she has done. None of this is suppressed from the fiction. What it cannot do is stop the scene. Interior moments happen in the gaps between action beats, not as extended set-pieces. A paragraph of Taylor's Gold Morning weight is allowed when it earns its place. A full scene of Taylor processing her grief with no external action is a register violation.

**No teaching-the-reader-the-world as a scene function.** Lore dumps are a register violation. Taylor's knowledge of ASOIAF and of Worm-universe mechanics surfaces as relevant action, not as internal encyclopedia entries. She does not remind herself of what she knows; she uses what she knows. The reader learns the world through what happens, not through Taylor's internal wiki.

**No scheming-through-foreknowledge as the primary mode.** Taylor knows the Dance is coming. She knows the Great Council is the pivot. This knowledge is orientation, not script. She cannot plan against specific chapter-by-chapter events; her memory is a reader's fuzzy recall, not a prophet's ledger. Scenes that rely on Taylor's foreknowledge as a reliable tactical resource violate the foreknowledge-degrades-under-pressure constraint from `cond-reincarnation-mechanics-84ac`.

## The Escalation Rule

**Taylor's escalation-reflex is structural.** When she is threatened — physically, procedurally, or in terms of someone she is protecting — she escalates. She does not de-escalate by default. She does not take the cautious path by default when an aggressive path is available. This is not a flaw she is working on; it is a load-bearing feature of who she is and what makes her interesting in this world. Note: `cond-shard-behavioral-weight` makes this tendency harder to self-correct — the Shard's thumb on the scale amplifies the existing reflex without adding a new one.

Every escalation has consequences. The Westeros world does not absorb escalation neutrally. A child who responds to an armed man with unusual sharpness creates a record. A child who appears to control animals creates a reputation. A child who successfully resists tax collection becomes a problem someone will eventually solve through institutional escalation. Each step closes some options and opens others, and the series runs forward on that dynamic.

**The escalation ratchet is one-directional within a scene.** A scene that has escalated does not de-escalate. The new equilibrium is the floor of the next scene's starting tension. This does not mean every scene ends in catastrophe; it means every scene ends at a higher tension level than it started, and the series arc is constructed from the accumulation of those steps.

## The Violence-as-Fluency Rule

Taylor speaks the language of organized violence more fluently than almost anyone in this world. This is both an asset and an isolating condition. The asset: she can read a combat situation faster than the people in it, she can identify leverage points in a threatening scenario before they become visible to others, she can organize defense and offense with a cognitive efficiency that her body cannot yet fully express. The isolating condition: the fluency is visible. People in this world who are themselves violence-fluent recognize it. People who are not are made uneasy by it. There is no social register in Westeros for a child who speaks that language.

This rule does not mean Taylor is always right about violence. It means she reaches for violence as a framework faster than the situation sometimes warrants. This is allowed to cost her. It is not allowed to be corrected into a softer default.

## The Cost-Migration Rule

**Early escalations cost Taylor personally.** The first several seasons' escalations are hers to pay — physical risk, isolation, grief, psychic damage from operating without a shared frame of reference. This is the personal cost phase.

**Mid-arc escalations cost the coalition.** As Taylor's organizing becomes more effective, the cost migrates to the people around her. Her internal failure modes (self-isolation, distributed control as substitute for trust) are the mechanism: she cannot sustain the relationships her project requires. The people who trust her pay for her control calculus. Coalition cost begins no later than S2 mid-arc.

**Late escalations cost the institution.** As Taylor's organizing reaches scale, the institution's counter-escalation costs more than hers does. This is the payoff of the escalation structure. Suppression-as-policy (not incident response) must be reached no later than mid-S3.

The cost-migration rule is not optional. Scenes that skip the sequence — that have the institution paying costs before the coalition does, or Taylor paying nothing while the coalition burns — violate the series structure.

## The Council-Close Rule

The Great Council of 101 AC is the series end-state. It is not a resolution. It is a stress-test: the moment when the work Taylor has built is put under institutional pressure and costs more than she expected. What survives the Council survives in damaged, diminished, or transformed form.

**Costs become final at Council close.** Debts that have been accumulating across the series come due at the Council. The coalition's relationship to Taylor, the institutional record Aldric Pryor has been building, the Faith's position on what Taylor is — all of these reach their terminal weight at the Council.

**Catharsis before the Dance is prohibited.** Taylor knows the Dance of Dragons follows the Great Council regardless of what the Council decides. Her organizing cannot prevent the civil war. Any scene that implies her work resolved something in a way that will survive the Dance is violating the structural prohibition on catharsis. The audience should feel that the work mattered and that it was not enough — simultaneously, not sequentially.

## Interaction Notes

**With `taylor-hebert-jaehaerys` persona card:** The persona card carries escalation-reflex and violence-as-fluency as Taylor-specific traits. This card is the series-level authorization for those traits — they are not just Taylor's personal defaults, they are structural features the series is built around.

**With `cond-shard-behavioral-weight`:** The Shard's behavioral weight amplifies the escalation-reflex without overriding it. The escalation rule and the Shard rule are additive, not redundant. Load both for any scene involving Taylor's decision-making.

**For auditor use:** Flag any scene where the primary action is uninterrupted planning, strategy session, or introspective monologue with no forward physical movement. Flag any scene where Taylor de-escalates by default without external constraint forcing her to. Flag any scene where the lore density exceeds what the action can carry. Flag any scene that uses ironic register to undercut immediate stakes. Flag any scene at Council close that implies resolution rather than stress-test. Flag any catharsis that arrives before the Dance horizon.

**For impersonator use:** The fast/pulpy/dramatic constraint means Taylor's output in any scene should be short, targeted, and forward-moving. Her internal voice does not over-explain. Her dialogue is functional. If you find yourself writing extended internal monologue, you have drifted; trim and accelerate.
