# [cost-bearer] — Cost-Bearer Presence and Closing-Image Rule

## Description

Binding structural constraint for [cost-bearer], the cost-bearer role of this project. Governs scene-frequency requirements across acts, the closing-image role, and the ledger-anomaly rule that defines [cost-bearer]'s relationship to Taylor's moral accounting. Load this card at /and-substance chapter phase and /and-write bone-gate for any act-boundary check or cost-bearer coverage verification.

The specific identity filling the [cost-bearer] slot is resolved at /and-cast. Until then, all rules in this card apply to whichever persona is selected for that slot.

## Sensory Impact

None directly. [cost-bearer]'s presence is a structural requirement, not a sensory condition. The impact on scene texture is felt through Taylor's suppressed accounting anomaly — the one item the ledger does not enumerate — and through the reader's accumulating relationship with [cost-bearer] across acts.

## Duration

Persistent and load-bearing across all acts. The frequency rule is enforceable at act boundaries. The closing-image rule fires at book end.

## Frequency Rule

**At least one scene per act in which Taylor and [cost-bearer] share screen time.** This is not satisfiable by off-stage mention, reported dialogue, or Taylor thinking about [cost-bearer]. The requirement is direct shared scene presence — [cost-bearer] physically present in a scene where Taylor is also present, with at least one direct interaction beat.

**Act-boundary check:** At the close of each act, the substance pipeline must be able to locate at least one scene in that act meeting the shared-presence criterion. An act with no qualifying scene is a structural violation requiring revision before proceeding to the next act.

**Why the frequency rule is load-bearing:** The closing-image cost ([cost-bearer]'s death) has earned weight only if the reader has accumulated a relationship with [cost-bearer] across the book. A [cost-bearer] who appears at act one and then vanishes until the coda is not a cost-bearer; she is a prop. The per-act frequency rule is the mechanism through which the cost becomes structural rather than decorative.

## Closing-Image Rule

**[cost-bearer]'s death in Flea Bottom violence of the Dance's opening is the final cost image of the book.** This is not negotiable and not subject to substitution. The closing image is [cost-bearer] dead — not Taylor's fate, not the political consequences, not the counterfactual. The maester-chronicler coda records [cost-bearer]'s death as the final named item (per `cond-maester-chronicler-voice` ordering rules).

**The closing image precedes the coda.** The book's final narrative pages show or imply [cost-bearer]'s death in the Dance's opening violence. The coda then records it in archival register. The narrative closing image and the coda's recording are two separate moments; neither substitutes for the other.

## Ledger-Anomaly Rule

**[cost-bearer] is the one item Taylor does not put in her moral ledger.** Taylor's moral accounting is otherwise explicit and systematic (per `cond-taylor-pov-behavior`). She tracks what each trade costs. She does not track [cost-bearer] — not because she does not register [cost-bearer]'s vulnerability, but because to name [cost-bearer] in the ledger would be to acknowledge her as an acceptable cost, which Taylor cannot do without breaking her own prevention-logic.

**The anomaly is the tell.** Readers who watch Taylor's systematic cost-tracking will notice the gap where [cost-bearer] should appear. The absence is active, not passive — Taylor's attention in scenes with [cost-bearer] is characterized by compression, brevity, or deflection at the moment when the ledger-accounting would normally occur. This is not sentimentality; the absence is legible as an accounting failure, not as affection.

**Violation condition:** Any scene where Taylor explicitly cost-accounts [cost-bearer] in her inner monologue — calculates her as an acceptable loss, places her in the ledger as a named trade — is a ledger-anomaly violation. The anomaly must remain implicit throughout. The coda is the first place [cost-bearer]'s death is named as historical fact; Taylor never names it as a projected cost.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-taylor-pov-behavior`:** That card governs Taylor's moral accounting register. This card declares [cost-bearer] as the anomaly item in that register. Both must be loaded for any scene involving Taylor and [cost-bearer] together.

**With `cond-maester-chronicler-voice`:** That card requires [cost-bearer]'s death be recorded in the coda in archival register. This card declares the narrative requirement that the closing image precede the coda's record. Both must be loaded for coda authoring and for /and-review verdict checking.

## Auditor Flags

Flag any act boundary where no shared Taylor–[cost-bearer] scene exists. Flag any scene where [cost-bearer]'s presence is satisfied only by off-stage mention or reported dialogue. Flag any scene where Taylor explicitly names [cost-bearer] in her moral ledger as a projected cost. Flag any coda that records [cost-bearer]'s death without a preceding narrative closing image establishing the fact.
