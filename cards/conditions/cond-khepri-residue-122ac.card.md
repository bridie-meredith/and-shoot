# Khepri Residue — Power-State Rules at 122 AC King's Landing Open

## Description

Governing constraint for Taylor Hebert's parahuman power-state as she arrives in King's Landing in 122 AC. This is the project-open configuration: what survives of the Queen-Administrator shard after Gold Morning, what does not, and how the KL urban environment interacts with what survives. Load alongside `cond-fauna-control-rules` (base species/range/cost rules) and `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure` (no shard buffering). Where this card conflicts with the base card, this card wins.

## What Survives of Queen-Administrator

**Fauna control: intact, range-limited.** Insect and small-fauna control is operational at story open. The multithreaded parallel awareness survived the Gold Morning transition as a cognitive restructuring, not an active shard connection. It runs on Taylor's own neural architecture, not on shard infrastructure.

**Pattern-recognition precog: surfaces as ordinary cognition.** The organizational precog that made Khepri capable of coordinating thousands of bodies has degraded to a persistent bias toward systems-level pattern detection. Taylor reads political and social structures unusually fast — she identifies the fracture lines, the leverage points, the pre-catastrophe indicators — but she experiences this as her own intelligence, not as a shard capability. She cannot distinguish it from ordinary analytical skill. It is not infallible, and its failure mode is over-confident extrapolation from Gold Morning analogues to situations that are not Gold Morning.

**Multi-shard hijack: gone.** The Khepri-mantle capability — coordinating human bodies at scale — is sealed at story open and permanently. Unlike the flea-bottom-dance config, there is no unlock path in this project. The shard infrastructure required to run that capability does not exist on Planetos. Sealed means sealed: not partially available, not available under stress, not available at any point in the project. Any scene implying Taylor can coordinate or override human nervous systems is a fence violation.

## KL Insect Availability

King's Landing in 122 AC is warm, stone-dense, and refuse-rich. The insect ecology is significantly denser than the Riverlands in autumn:

**High-density zones:** Flea Bottom (the Hook especially) — flies, beetles, roaches, ants in large numbers; the midden-lanes produce constant fauna-traffic. Dragonpit Hill — moths attracted to torch-light, large beetle populations in the stonework. Market districts along the Streets of Steel, Seeds, and Silk — food storage attracts rats, flies, grain-moths.

**Medium-density zones:** The noble quarter between Rhaenys's Hill and Aegon's Hill — lower street-level refuse density but rat populations in walls and undercrofts. Red Keep outer courts — maintained, but refuse accumulates at service entries.

**Low-density zones:** Red Keep inner chambers — manicured; the passive insect feed is quiet here, not absent. Useful for single-insect courier work; not useful for swarm operations.

**Practical result:** Taylor's passive awareness in Flea Bottom at story open is loud — denser than anything she managed in the Riverlands. She must suppress more of it more continuously. The suppression cost is a background drain that does not appear on the cost-curve clock but accumulates across the day. This is both an asset (richer operational palette) and a management burden (harder to be quiet).

## Range at Story Open

**Normal operational range: 200m radius.** As specified in `cond-fauna-control-rules`. No override at this project's open; the base card's 200m normal / 400m extended applies. In KL's dense urban environment, 200m covers significant population — the entirety of many Flea Bottom blocks.

**Range expansion:** Not a project feature. This is a single-book arc. No organic range ceiling expansion is in scope. The base card's cost curve and range parameters are the fixed operational envelope.

## Wrongness Fingerprint — What Observers See in KL

KL has more sophisticated observers than the Riverlands. The wrongness-perception problem is sharper:

**Street-level (Flea Bottom):** The same uncanny-animal-behavior register as the Riverlands — beetles that mass, flies that move against flow, rats that gather and wait. Flea Bottom's cultural frame for this is *witchery* or *the old woman's touch* — a folk-category for women (and occasionally men) who have some influence over vermin and flies. The label is older in KL than in the Riverlands; it is a known category, not a novel event.

**Watch layer:** City Watch patrols who observe unusual animal behavior in a district and then observe it consistently near the same person will report upward. Not immediately — the Watch's informant-reporting latency for non-emergency observations is days to weeks — but the observation accumulates in the Watch's informal intelligence network.

**Noble/court layer:** Anyone in the court who hears "Flea Bottom witch who knows things" is hearing through institutional filters. Otto Hightower's intelligence network specifically captures unusual persons of unknown provenance in KL. A foreign woman with consistent animal-uncanny behavior is exactly the category his network is calibrated to surface. The path from street-level witch-label to Hightower intelligence attention is shorter in KL than anywhere else in the realm.

**Taylor's concealment options in KL:** Same as base card (single animals, edge of range, passive reconnaissance, superficially natural animal behavior) but harder to sustain against KL's observation density. The Watch sweeps more frequently. The informant network is denser. The noble quarter's household servants are practiced watchers.
