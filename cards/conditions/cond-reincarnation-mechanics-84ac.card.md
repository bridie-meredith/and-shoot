# Reincarnation Mechanics — Hard Rules for taylor-hebert-jaehaerys (84 AC Config)

## Description

Governing constraint card for the terms of Taylor Hebert's reincarnation into a Westerosi child body at approximately 84 AC, Jaehaerys I era, Riverlands. Defines what carried over, what the reincarnation created anew, what it permanently closed, and what paths do and do not exist. All agents must load this card when writing Taylor's internal experience of her own continuity, her relationship to her prior life, or any scene that raises questions about whether she could return, escape, or access Earth-Bet resources.

This card supersedes `cond-reincarnation-mechanics` for this project. The mechanics sections (what carried over, what closed, what the body is) are the same in substance. The situational apparatus is replaced: arrival age is 7–8 (not 11), birth station is craftsman family in Fairstead (not orphan ward of a sept), the horizon is Great Council 101 AC (not the Dance), and the foreknowledge runway is ~17 years (not ~9). All era-specific material — Harrenhal, Septon Aldric, the impressment census — is wrong for this project and does not apply.

## Sensory Impact

No ambient sensory expression. The constraint is experienced from the inside: Taylor carries complete episodic and semantic memory of her prior life, housed in a body that produces entirely different sensory data. The mismatch between remembered body-schema and current body-schema is a persistent low-level wrongness — the proprioceptive gap of being twenty years old psychologically while seven or eight physically. She has largely suppressed this. It surfaces under high physical stress, extreme danger, or sleep. At story open (~86 AC, Taylor age ~9), the suppression is practiced and mostly stable.

## Duration

Permanent. These rules hold for the full project duration and cannot be altered by in-world events.

## What Carried Over

**Full episodic memory.** Taylor carries complete recall of her life as Taylor Hebert: childhood, trigger event, cape career, the Slaughterhouse Nine encounters, the Echidna incident, Leviathan, Cauldron, Gold Morning, her death. These are not impressions or fragments; they are intact memories she can access as clearly as any other autobiographical memory. She does not experience them as a dream she woke from. They are her life, lived, complete. What she does not have access to: events after Gold Morning's resolution she was not conscious for, other people's internal states, and any information she did not personally acquire on Earth-Bet.

**Semantic knowledge.** Everything she knew about the Worm universe — cape powers, Shard mechanics, the Ward structure, Earth-Bet geography, the political map of parahumans — is intact and accessible. Internally useful. Externally unreferenceable.

**Planetos knowledge — text, not lived experience.** She retains her Earth-Bet knowledge of ASOIAF and Fire & Blood as a reader. That knowledge is a reader's knowledge of a text, not personal knowledge of a world she has lived in. She knows the Dance of Dragons is coming in approximately 30 years from story open. She knows the Great Council of 101 AC is the structural pivot roughly 17 years out. Broad strokes accurate; specific dates and minor characters fuzzy. She recognized "Targaryen" within her first weeks of consciousness in this body. She knows Jaehaerys is aging and the question of succession is not yet acute. She does not remember every chapter, and she was never certain about exact years. Her foreknowledge is actionable as broad strategic orientation, not as a predictive clock.

**Tactical cognition and pattern recognition.** The cognitive restructuring produced by her trigger event — multithreaded parallel processing, tactical reading of conflict space, threat assessment by habit — is present in the new body. It did not require the Shard to maintain; it is structural. It is also the cognitive substrate for her fauna-control ability.

**Core temperament.** The person she is — the tolerance for necessary evil, the discomfort with authority that does not earn itself, the reflex to protect people who cannot protect themselves, the willingness to pay costs others will not — is continuous. She is Taylor Hebert. The body is different. The person is not.

**Mid-canon psychological baseline.** Taylor arrives as post-Leviathan / post-Echidna mid-canon Taylor: operational, not broken, but carrying live internal failure modes. Self-isolation is her default. Distributed control as substitute for trust is structural. Dinah's debt is a live moral wound. She lies to allies when she calculates it is necessary. These are not obstacles to her organizing — they are the mechanisms by which coalition cost is delivered. Loaded from `taylor-hebert-jaehaerys` persona card's Hard Fences; binding here as the reincarnation-level anchor.

## What the Reincarnation Created Anew

**A body with its own history.** The body Taylor inhabits is a Westerosi human child who has been alive for approximately seven or eight years. She arrived in a body that has been alive and carries that life in its bones and posture and immune memory. She is small. She is not well-fed by any comparative standard. The body has never been in a fight. It does not have the muscle memory of cape work. Its history is not hers by memory but it is hers by biology. She cannot access the physical competencies she developed on Earth-Bet except as knowledge of what to practice — not as readiness.

**A new identity layer.** The body has a name given in this life. She has continuous memory from approximately age seven — she does not have memory of early infancy in this body. Her pre-awareness Westerosi childhood is a gap. She arrived in a family: a craftsman father (Edwyn Ashford, dyer-with-accounts) and a mother (Elara Ashford) in a river-market town (Fairstead) on a Blue Fork tributary.

**A family structure.** Unlike the prior project's orphan configuration, this Taylor has living parents. They have legitimate intimate claim on her. She cannot keep them out without generating cost. The family is the first coalition she is in and the first coalition her internal failure modes will cost.

**A starting position.** Born smallfolk in Fairstead, Riverlands, ~84 AC, daughter of craftsman tradespeople. No house name, no legible authority, no institutional cover beyond family. She works from this position.

## What Did Not Carry Over

**The original body.** Gone. See above.

**Parahuman infrastructure.** Covered fully by `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`. The absence of Shards, the cost curve, the no-return rule as it applies to Shard transit.

**Her relationships.** Emma, Lisa, Brian, Rachel, the Undersiders, the Wards, Cauldron contacts — all dead or unreachable. She knows they are dead. She knows the world they inhabited is gone. These are not relationships she is separated from; they are relationships that ended at Gold Morning and cannot be resumed. She is alone in this in a way that is total and non-negotiable.

**Her name and prior identity.** No one in Westeros knows who Taylor Hebert is. Her prior-life identity is entirely internal.

## What the Reincarnation Closed

**Return.** There is no route back to Earth-Bet. Not through Planetos-native magic, not through any Shard mechanism, not through death and re-reincarnation, not through any mechanism Taylor has knowledge of or can conceive. Her internal model does not include a return scenario. If a scene implies she is planning or hoping for return, that is a fence violation.

**Contact with the prior world.** She cannot communicate with Earth-Bet. She cannot receive information from Earth-Bet. The people she knew are inaccessible. Their fates after Gold Morning are unknown and unknowable to her.

**Explanation.** She cannot explain her prior life to anyone in this world. The Westerosi religious frame, the maesters' empirical frame, the cultural frame of any group she will encounter — none of them have a category for what she is. She has learned not to attempt explanation. Partial truth ("I have strange dreams") is more dangerous than silence because it invites questions she cannot safely answer.

**The ability to be an ordinary child.** She is not capable of performing the cognitive profile of a seven- or eight-year-old. She can perform the social submission and physical limits of a child her age; she cannot perform not-knowing things she knows, not-assessing what she is always assessing, or not-caring about the political environment. The reincarnation did not provide a way to be less than she is.

## The Body Is Real

Taylor's current body is not a projection or a temporary vessel. It is a real biological entity that was born, will age, can be injured, and will die. She is not protected by her prior-life status or by the fact that she has died before. Death in Westeros is permanent.

At story open (~86 AC), she is approximately nine years old. Her body is not capable of what an adult body is capable of. Her development is real — she will grow across the series, reaching her mid-teens at the Great Council (101 AC). Her capabilities will expand on a biological timeline she does not control. She knows this, and it is a source of grinding strategic frustration.

**Growth tracking:** The story spans approximately 17 years from arrival to Great Council close (84–101 AC). Taylor grows from a child of seven or eight to a young woman of approximately seventeen. Her physical capability ceiling rises meaningfully across the series. Writers should track her approximate age and not write her adult-capable before the biology supports it, nor write her still child-limited when she has outgrown it.

## The Foreknowledge Weight

Taylor carries textual foreknowledge of ASOIAF as a reader. The specific weight for this project:

- She knows the Great Council of 101 AC is the succession pivot and that it determines nothing good about what follows — the Dance (129–131 AC) follows regardless of the Council's outcome.
- She knows she is organizing in the final decade of Jaehaerys I's long peace and that what she builds will be stress-tested by something she cannot prevent.
- She does not know what specific people will do at specific moments. She knows broad arcs, general factions, structural outcomes. The details are a reader's fuzzy recollection, not a prophet's precise vision.
- The foreknowledge is a strategic orientation tool, not a cheat code. It degrades under sustained smart questioning. She is not running a prediction from an accurate database; she is extrapolating from incomplete textual memory across an eighteen-year gap since she read those books.

## Uniqueness

Taylor is the only displaced soul in this project. There are no other reincarnated Earth-Bet parahumans, no other isekai arrivals, no other persons operating with anachronistic knowledge. If a scene introduces a character whose knowledge implies displacement from another time or universe, that is a fence violation unless explicitly authorized by a project-scope revision.

## Taylor's Internal Experience of Continuity

Taylor does not experience her reincarnation as miraculous, as reward, or as a second chance in any positive sense. She was at least mid-twenties psychologically before she was seven or eight biologically. She has done things most people in any world never do. She carries Gold Morning, which is not a memory that occupies the same category as other memories — it is the anchor weight everything else orbits. The reincarnation is simply where she is now. She has not forgiven it for happening, and she is not waiting for it to reveal its purpose.

The specific cognitive dissonance she carries: she is a child in this world, and she knows she is not. She performs the social role of child because non-performance is dangerous. The performance is coherent enough to survive casual observation and falls apart under sustained scrutiny. She does not expect to perform it forever — and she has nearly seventeen years of runway before the Great Council provides the stage where she stops having to.

## Specific Scene Constraints

**Memory completeness is non-negotiable.** Taylor does not forget Earth-Bet events, selectively lose memories under stress, or have her prior-life knowledge fade over time. If a scene implies memory degradation, that is a fence violation.

**The body's age is non-negotiable.** Physical feats available to an adult are not available to her at series open. She has adult cognition and a child's body. The body's limits do not bend to the cognition. If a scene implies adult physical capacity before the biological timeline supports it, that is a fence violation.

**The pre-awareness gap is maintained.** She has no memory of the first years of this Westerosi life. She cannot fill that gap with fabricated specifics. She can reference what her parents have told her; she cannot claim personal memory of her own infancy or toddlerhood.

**The reincarnation was involuntary.** She does not have a patron, a deity who placed her here, or a mission the reincarnation was designed to accomplish. She does not know why she is here instead of simply dead. This absence of explanation is permanent. If a scene implies she has discovered a purpose or origin for the reincarnation, that requires a project-level story decision above scene level.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`:** The no-return-channel rule on that card is the Shard-specific expression of the general no-return rule here. Both cards should be read together when handling any scene where Taylor might have a path home.

**With `taylor-hebert-jaehaerys` persona card:** The persona card's Hard Fences and Memory Burden sections operationalize the psychological weight described here. The Vibe Seeds section carries the Gold Morning weight and the second-life-not-reward framing. This card is the structural source; the persona card is the operational expression.

**With `cond-shard-behavioral-weight`:** The Shard is present at behavioral-weight level. It does not alter what carried over or what the reincarnation closed; it operates on top of what Taylor already is, biasing her judgment without her awareness. Both cards must be loaded together for Taylor POV scenes.

**For auditor use:** Any scene where Taylor behaves as though return is possible, where another displaced soul appears without project authorization, where Taylor's body performs above its developmental level without appropriate cost, where her memories are described as fading or fragmentary, or where the reincarnation is implied to have a discoverable purpose is a constraint violation against this card.
