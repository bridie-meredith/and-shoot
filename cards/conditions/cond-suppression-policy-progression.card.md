# Suppression Policy Progression — Institutional Response to Taylor Hebert (84–101 AC)

## Description

The structural stages by which institutional response to Taylor's organizing escalates from incident-response (local lord's steward makes a first note, ~86 AC) to active suppression-as-policy (the lord directs systematic monitoring, reporting, and eventual action against Taylor specifically). Defines what triggers each escalation step, what institutional actors' incentives are at each level, and what "suppression-as-policy must be reached no later than mid-S3" means in terms of scene-level evidence.

This card governs the antagonist clock. The institutional machinery is not a villain; it is a system responding to deviation from expected behavior according to its own logic and incentives. Aldric Pryor (House Ryger's steward) is the primary institutional face across S1–S3. The lord himself becomes a factor at Stage 3. Tully-level involvement is a Stage 4 trigger.

## Sensory Impact

The progression is felt not as ambient sensation but as a change in the quality of official presence in Taylor's environment. At Stage 1, the steward's men ride through Fairstead on the same schedule as always; the difference is that one of them is watching the Ashford workshop with a slightly different kind of attention. At Stage 4, the quality of that attention has become policy: there are men whose assigned task includes Taylor's monitoring.

The shift from ambient to directed is something the smallfolk network detects before Taylor might. The whisper chain notices when official attention becomes personal. By Stage 3, Mira Stonefield-Jaehaerys will have told Taylor that Pryor is asking about her specifically.

## Duration

This condition evolves across the series arc. It does not resolve; it escalates. Stage 1 begins at ~86 AC (ignition). The mid-S3 deadline is the policy-level Stage 3. The Council close (101 AC) is the terminal pressure point where accumulated suppression meets Taylor's accumulated organizing in direct confrontation.

## Escalation Stages

### Stage 1: Incident Response (~86 AC — Taylor age ~9)

**Trigger:** The ignition event. Taylor's swarm rises involuntarily against the tax-collector retinue coercing a smallfolk woman in Fairstead's market square. Two of the collector's men are stung badly enough to need treatment. The horses bolt. The tax collection is abandoned for the day. The collector's retinue leader reports upward. Aldric Pryor, the House Ryger steward, receives the report and makes a note.

**What the note contains:** A girl, approximately nine years old, the Ashford dyer's daughter. Insects behaved abnormally. The horses bolted. The men cannot explain it as natural. No accusation is made; the note is descriptive. It is associated with a name (Ashford, Edwyn's daughter) and a location (Fairstead market square).

**Institutional actor incentives at Stage 1:** Pryor is a good functionary. A single anomalous incident is not worth escalating to the lord's formal attention. It is worth noting. His incentive is to accumulate a minimal record — if this turns out to be nothing, the note costs him nothing. If this turns out to be something, the note establishes he was thorough.

**Scene-level evidence of Stage 1:** The tax collection is abandoned for the day. The collector's men avoid looking at Taylor directly when they regroup. The word "strange" appears in the gossip. Pryor or his deputy asks the reeve, casually, whether there have been other incidents with the Ashford girl. The reeve answers noncommittally. The note exists.

**Taylor's awareness at Stage 1:** She knows she has created a record. She does not know its contents or how seriously it is being taken. She knows the event was witnessed by the right (or wrong) people. She begins the post-ignition management problem: how to be less visible without disappearing.

### Stage 2: Patterned Response (~87–90 AC — Taylor age ~10–13)

**Trigger:** A second anomalous event associated with Taylor, or a pattern that Pryor's record identifies as deliberate rather than accidental. The specific triggers are flexible (the season planning team determines which events cross this threshold), but the threshold is: the steward concludes that Stage 1 was not a one-off. Something is happening. It happens to or around the Ashford girl with a regularity that a coincidence-explanation cannot carry.

**What changes at Stage 2:** Pryor begins actively monitoring Taylor's immediate environment. He or a designated deputy is assigned to observe her during every official visit to Fairstead. He begins asking questions about her more systematically — of the reeve, of Septon Rowan (who will not tell him what he wants to know, for his own reasons), of neighboring wool factors who do business in Fairstead. He requests any available precedents from the Citadel's administrative records for similar anomalies. He is building a file, not an accusation.

**Institutional actor incentives at Stage 2:** Pryor's incentive is still documentation, not action. He is a minor lord's steward; he does not have the authority or the mandate to take direct action against a smallfolk based on insect behavior. His lord would want to know about a genuine threat, not about a steward who overreacted to a strange girl. He must demonstrate the pattern before he can recommend anything.

**Scene-level evidence of Stage 2:** The steward's men visit Fairstead slightly more frequently than before. They do not announce that they are watching Taylor; they are present and their attention is distributed, but it lingers slightly on the Ashford workshop. Pryor himself asks Septon Rowan a direct question about Taylor that Rowan deflects with theological language. The reeve begins telling Taylor (or her parents) that the steward has been asking about the family. The whisper chain carries word that "someone official is curious about Edwyn Ashford's daughter." Pryor's file grows.

**Taylor's awareness at Stage 2:** She has enough information from Mira or the whisper chain to know Pryor is watching. She does not know the full contents of his file. She is actively managing her visibility and calibrating her organizing to stay below the threshold that would trigger Stage 3. This management is its own cost — it eats will and attention she cannot spend on organizing.

### Stage 3: Policy (~91–97 AC, no later than mid-S3 — Taylor age ~14–20)

**Trigger:** Pryor concludes that monitoring is insufficient and that his lord must be informed and given a recommendation. The trigger is either: (a) an event significant enough that Pryor cannot justify keeping it in his personal file without escalating, or (b) Pryor's accumulated file reaching a weight where the pattern is undeniable and his administrative duty requires escalation.

**What changes at Stage 3:** The lord of House Ryger is briefed. He reads Pryor's file. He makes a policy decision: Taylor Hebert (the Ashford girl, now an older child or young woman) is a named subject of institutional attention, not merely an anomaly in Pryor's records. A suppression directive is issued. What that directive contains is determined by the lord's specific character and the accumulated weight of the file, but it includes: systematic monitoring with regular reports; restriction on Taylor's movement (she may not leave Fairstead without the reeve's notification of Pryor); inquiry into the scope of her organizing activities (who is she talking to, what is she doing with the whisper chain, what does Septon Rowan actually know).

**World-notes mandate:** "Suppression-as-policy (not incident response) must be reached no later than mid-S3." This is a hard constraint on the series' antagonist timeline. If Stage 3 has not been reached by mid-S3, the collision at the Great Council in the series finale lands underweight. The institutional machinery must be in active suppression mode before the Council convenes.

**Institutional actor incentives at Stage 3:** The lord's incentive is the same as any minor lord's: protect his territory from instability, protect his relationship with Tully, and not create a problem larger than the one he is solving. He is not going to burn the Ashford workshop in S3; he is going to establish official oversight and restrict Taylor's freedom of movement. The violence is available if she continues to escalate; it is not the first tool.

**Scene-level evidence of Stage 3:** An official summons to appear before the steward — not to appear before the lord (that comes later), but a formal administrative summons, delivered in writing, with the lord's seal. The reeve's behavior changes: he is no longer a membrane between Taylor and authority, he is now under official instruction to report Taylor's activities. Pryor visits Fairstead personally, specifically to interview Taylor's parents. The Faith itinerant arrives in town and spends unusual time with Septon Rowan (Pryor has requested the Faith's institutional read on Rowan's theological position regarding Taylor). The lord has been told.

**Taylor's awareness at Stage 3:** The summons makes it explicit. There is no longer ambiguity about whether she is being watched; she has been officially noticed. The summons is a test: it will tell her what the lord's immediate intention is. Whether she appears, refuses, delays, or attempts to invoke competing authority is one of the critical decision branches of the series.

### Stage 4: Action (~98–101 AC, Council horizon — Taylor age ~21)

**Trigger:** Stage 3 suppression is not effective. Taylor's organizing continues or escalates despite official oversight. The lord concludes that administrative monitoring is insufficient and escalates either to direct intervention (arrest, destruction of organizing infrastructure, economic pressure on her family or coalition members) or to Tully-level reporting (taking the problem to his liege for guidance and institutional backing).

**What changes at Stage 4:** The institutional response gains official violence potential. This does not mean violence is certain; it means it is now on the table in a way it was not at Stage 3. The lord might choose economic strangulation of Taylor's coalition (call in debts, restrict market access, deny guild recognition to her allies) before physical violence. Or he might dispatch men-at-arms to Fairstead with explicit instructions. The specific form depends on the lord's character and Taylor's accumulated threat profile.

**Tully involvement:** If the lord escalates to Tully, the problem moves from minor-lord-with-a-strange-girl to Great-House-institutional-response. Tully's interest is in Riverlands stability, not in the specific Ashford girl. Their tools are different from the minor lord's; they include inter-lord diplomatic pressure, official investigation with Citadel backing, and the possibility of trial-level process.

**The Council horizon:** Stage 4 and the Great Council of 101 AC are designed to converge. The Council is when all of the suppression machinery's accumulated weight meets Taylor's accumulated organizing at the moment of maximum institutional pressure. Whether she is in Fairstead, in Riverrun, or somehow at King's Landing for the Council is a season-planning decision, but the Council is the moment where Stage 4's pressure reaches its peak.

**Scene-level evidence of Stage 4:** Men-at-arms stationed in Fairstead on a permanent basis (not visiting, present). A formal investigation opened under Tully authority, with investigators who are not Pryor and who have no existing relationship with the Fairstead community. Economic pressure: a Tully bannerman instructs market factors to refuse business with known Taylor-associated people. The lord's seal on a warrant (for what charge depends on what Taylor has done — organizing without lord's permission, practicing irregular craft or sorcery, disturbing the lord's peace). The arrest of a coalition member as leverage.

## The Steward as Clock

Aldric Pryor is the series' institutional antagonist across S1–S3. His escalation is not personal; it is professional. He is not cruel; he is thorough. His arc runs parallel to Taylor's: every move she makes that creates a record makes his file more persuasive; every time she manages to stay under the threshold, his file stalls.

The emotional content of his antagonism is the absence of emotion. He does not hate Taylor. He is doing his job correctly. This is more difficult to organize against than personal cruelty, because there is no interpersonal lever for Taylor to pull. The record does not care how she feels about it.

## Incentive Structure at Each Level

| Stage | Primary actor | Primary tool | Primary incentive |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pryor (steward) | Note-taking | Be thorough; create no record of overreaction |
| 2 | Pryor | Active monitoring, file-building | Demonstrate pattern before escalating |
| 3 | Minor lord (via Pryor) | Official directive, official summons | Manage territory without creating a larger problem |
| 4 | Minor lord + Tully (potential) | Direct intervention or warrant | Restore stability; protect institutional position |

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-westerosi-customary-authority-jaehaerys`:** That card describes the behavioral physics; this card describes how deviation from those physics triggers institutional response. Both should be loaded when writing Taylor's encounters with official authority.

**With `cond-riverlands-84ac-state`:** The Jaehaerys long peace creates a stable institutional machinery that can afford to move slowly and methodically. Stage 1 to Stage 3 takes 5–11 years in this project precisely because the administrative apparatus is not under pressure. Under a more contested political environment, the stages might compress.

**With `cond-feudal-hierarchy-law`:** That card defines what the lord is legally permitted to do at each stage. This card defines what his incentives are. Both are needed: the law defines the ceiling; the incentives define what he actually reaches for.

**For auditor use:** If institutional response escalates faster than the trigger criteria above support (e.g., the lord takes direct violent action in S1 without accumulated pattern justification), that is a pacing violation. If institutional response is slower than required (Stage 3 not reached by mid-S3), that is a structural violation of the series' antagonist clock. Track the stage-level on each Pryor appearance.
