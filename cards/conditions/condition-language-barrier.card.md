# Condition: Language Barrier

## Description

Taylor Hebert arrived in the Riverlands with no knowledge of the Common Tongue of Westeros. This condition governs the mechanics of her communication illegibility and tracks its progression from zero across the full acquisition arc.

## Acquisition Timeline

**Stage 0 — Zero comprehension (series open):**
Taylor speaks no Common Tongue and understands none. She can read English. She cannot read any Westerosi text. Her communication is: body language, gesture, demonstrated action, and the universal register of pain, compliance, and threat. She cannot correct misidentifications. She cannot explain herself. She cannot ask questions. She can observe and gather information she cannot act on through speech.

**Stage 1 — Partial reception (weeks to months in):**
She begins to understand words in context — names (proper nouns come first), repeated commands ("stop," "come," "eat"), emotional register (tone before content). She does not yet speak. She understands more than she communicates. This gap is dangerous: people will say things near her assuming she cannot understand.

**Stage 2 — Minimal functional speech (months in):**
She can produce short, heavily accented sentences. Present tense. Nouns and simple verbs. She makes grammatical errors that locate her as foreign in ways she cannot fully mask. She can ask basic questions. She can deny basic accusations. She still cannot argue, explain, or negotiate at the speed that situations require.

**Stage 3 — Functional communication (end of arc 1 / early arc 2):**
Working Common Tongue. Accent is heavy and permanent. Vocabulary gaps remain in register-specific domains (legal, theological, heraldic). She can hold a conversation. She can be misunderstood for reasons of register and context rather than pure vocabulary absence. The accent and vocabulary gaps continue to mark her as foreign-born throughout the series.

## Interaction Rules

**Actors must check this condition before writing Taylor's spoken communication.** At Stage 0, she produces no Common Tongue. At Stage 1, she may demonstrate understanding through behavior before she can produce language. At Stage 2, all dialogue should be flagged for simplified vocabulary and grammatical constraint. At Stage 3, the constraint relaxes but the accent and register gaps remain as persistent texture.

**The condition creates information asymmetry in Taylor's favor at Stage 1.** When characters speak near her assuming she cannot understand, she accumulates information she cannot act on immediately. This is a resource, not merely a cost.

**The condition governs other characters' behavior toward her.** At Stage 0–1, characters will assign identity based on appearance, behavior, and context rather than self-report. What they assign is what they believe. She cannot correct it until Stage 2.

## Sensory Impact

No direct sensory impact. The social and informational texture: a room full of conversation she cannot parse; words that arrive as sound before they arrive as meaning; the specific frustration of wanting to say something and not having the vessel for it.

## Duration

Persistent and staged. This condition does not resolve — it transforms. Stage 0 → Stage 3 is an arc-length progression. The accent and register gaps are permanent.

## Interaction Notes

**With condition-war-of-five-kings-riverlands:** The language barrier makes Taylor's survival odds worse at every stage — she cannot claim protection, cannot de-escalate verbally, cannot explain herself to anyone who finds her threatening. The ambient danger condition intersects with the language barrier to create a threat multiplier at Stage 0–1.

**With swarm capability:** Her power is not language-dependent. She can gather information through her bugs before she can ask for it verbally. This creates a specific dynamic: she often knows more than her interlocutors believe she knows, a gap that compounds over the arc.

**With Thoros of Myr:** His tendency to assign meaning to her before she can correct him is structurally enabled by the language barrier. He will have formed and acted on a theological interpretation before she has the language to contest it.

**With Mira Stonefield:** Information exchange with Mira is initially non-verbal — demonstration, shared task completion, observed behavior. The working relationship that develops before language is a specific kind of trust that is different from verbal trust.
