# Reincarnation Mechanics — Hard Rules for taylor-hebert-westeros

## Description

Governing constraint card for the terms of Taylor Hebert's reincarnation. Defines what carried over, what did not, what the new body is and is not, and what paths do and do not exist. All agents must load this card when writing Taylor's internal experience of her own continuity, her relationship to her prior life, or any scene that raises questions about whether she could return, escape, or access Earth-Bet resources.

## Sensory Impact

No ambient sensory expression. The constraint is experienced from the inside: Taylor has complete episodic and semantic memory of her prior life, and a body that produces different sensory data than the one she died in. The mismatch between remembered body-schema and current body-schema is a persistent low-level wrongness she has largely suppressed — not eliminated. It surfaces in moments of high physical stress, extreme danger, or sleep.

## Duration

Permanent. These rules hold for the full project duration. They cannot be altered by in-world events.

## What Carried Over

**Full episodic memory.** Taylor carries complete recall of her life as Taylor Hebert: childhood, trigger event, cape career, the Slaughterhouse Nine encounters, the Echidna incident, the S-class threat sequence, Cauldron, Gold Morning, her death. These are not impressions or fragments; they are intact memories she can access as clearly as any other autobiographical memory. She does not experience them as a dream she woke from. They are her life, lived, complete.

**Semantic knowledge.** Everything she knew about the Worm universe — cape powers, Shard mechanics, the Ward structure, Earth-Bet geography, the political map of parahumans — is intact and accessible. This knowledge is internally useful and externally unreferenceable. There is no one in Westeros to confirm, contradict, or use it.

**Tactical cognition and pattern recognition.** The cognitive restructuring produced by her trigger event — the multithreaded parallel processing, the tactical reading of conflict space, the instinct for threat assessment — is present in the new body. It did not require the Shard to maintain; it is structural.

**Core temperament.** The person she is — the tolerance for necessary evil, the discomfort with authority that does not earn itself, the reflex to protect people who cannot protect themselves, the willingness to pay costs others will not — is continuous. She is Taylor Hebert. The body is different. The person is not.

## What Did Not Carry Over

**The original body.** The body that died is gone. The body Taylor inhabits now is an eleven-year-old Westerosi human girl, not a reconstruction or copy of Taylor Hebert's body. It has never been in a fight. It does not have the muscle memory of cape work. It has its own developmental arc, its own hunger and cold and capacity for exhaustion. She cannot access the physical competencies she developed on Earth-Bet except as knowledge of what to practice — not as readiness.

**Parahuman infrastructure.** Covered fully by `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`. The absence of Shards, the cost curve, the no-return rule as applies to Shard transit.

**Her relationships.** Emma, Lisa, Brian, Rachel, the Undersiders, the Wards, Cauldron contacts — all dead or unreachable. She knows they are dead. She knows the world they inhabited is gone. These are not relationships she is separated from; they are relationships that ended at Gold Morning and cannot be resumed. She is alone in a way that is total and non-negotiable.

**Her name and identity.** No one in Westeros knows who Taylor Hebert is. She cannot invoke her prior identity as any kind of claim. She has a new name (or no name) in this world; her prior-life identity is entirely internal.

## The Body Is Real

Taylor's current body is not a projection, a temporary vessel, a dream, or a simulation. It is a real biological entity that was born, will age, can be injured, and will die. She is not protected by her prior-life status or by the fact that she has died before. Death in Westeros is permanent. Injury is permanent. Starvation, cold, and disease operate on her exactly as they operate on any Westerosi human child.

She is eleven years old at episode 1 opening. Her body is not capable of what an adult body is capable of. Her development is real — she will grow, her capabilities will expand, but on a biological timeline she does not control. She knows this and it is a source of grinding strategic frustration that she cannot entirely suppress.

## No Return

There is no route back to Earth-Bet. Not through Shard contact (covered by `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`), not through Planetos-native magic, not through death and re-reincarnation, not through any mechanism Taylor has knowledge of or can conceive. This is not a rule she must accept reluctantly; it is a physical fact she has already accepted. Her internal model does not include a return scenario. If a scene implies she is planning or hoping for return, that is a fence violation.

This also means: she cannot send information back to Earth-Bet, cannot warn anyone there, cannot use her foreknowledge of Earth-Bet events to influence Earth-Bet (Earth-Bet is gone as an ongoing timeline from her subjective position). Her foreknowledge is entirely of this world's future — the Dance of Dragons, the succession fracture, the political map of Westeros — and that is the only direction her knowledge is actionable.

## Uniqueness

Taylor is the only displaced soul in this project. There are no other reincarnated Earth-Bet parahumans, no other isekai arrivals, no other persons operating with anachronistic knowledge. If a scene introduces another character with knowledge that implies displacement from another time or universe, that is a fence violation unless explicitly authorized by a project-scope revision.

## Taylor's Internal Experience of Continuity

Taylor does not experience her reincarnation as miraculous, as reward, or as a second chance in any positive sense. She was thirty-two years old psychologically before she was eleven years old biologically. She has done things most people in any world never do. She carries Gold Morning, which is not a memory that occupies the same category as other memories — it is the anchor weight everything else orbits. The reincarnation is simply where she is now. She has not forgiven it for happening, and she is not waiting for it to reveal its purpose.

The specific cognitive dissonance she carries: she is a child in this world, and she knows she is not. She performs the social role of child because non-performance is dangerous. The performance is coherent enough to survive casual observation and falls apart under sustained scrutiny. She does not expect to perform it forever.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`:** The no-return-channel rule on that card is the Shard-specific expression of the general no-return rule here. Both cards should be read together when handling any scene where Taylor might have a path home.

**With `taylor-hebert-westeros` persona card:** The persona card's Memory Burden section operationalizes the psychological weight described in this card. The Vibe Seeds section on the persona card carries the Gold Morning weight and the second-life-not-reward framing. This card is the structural source; the persona card is the operational expression.

**For auditor use:** Any scene where Taylor behaves as though return is possible, where another displaced soul appears without project authorization, where Taylor's body performs above its developmental level without appropriate cost, or where her memories are described as fading, fragmentary, or dream-like is a constraint violation against this card.
