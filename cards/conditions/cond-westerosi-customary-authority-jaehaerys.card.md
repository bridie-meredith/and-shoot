# Westerosi Customary Authority — Riverlands Smallfolk Social Physics at 84–101 AC (Jaehaerys I Era)

## Description

The behavioral constraints governing how Riverlands smallfolk interact with authority figures at 84–101 AC. This card defines the social physics of Taylor's operating stratum — the default postures, the available compliance behaviors, the specific costs of deviation from expected norms, and what various kinds of wrongness look like in a smallfolk child in this environment.

This is the ambient behavioral layer that everything in Taylor's daily life runs against. It is not a special circumstance; it is the water.

**Key era difference from the 120 AC config:** This is Jaehaerys I's long peace. The Riverlands are not actively contested terrain. The lord's men are functionaries, not soldiers in a war footing. The institutional machinery is stable, patient, and thorough rather than aggressive and rapid. Suppression of smallfolk deviation is documentary and procedural before it is physical. This matters for how Taylor's early-arc escalations register: they go into a record, not into immediate violence. The record is the threat.

## Sensory Impact

The social physics of deference are physically embodied. When a man-at-arms arrives in a village common, every adult smallfolk body undergoes a postural shift within seconds: eyes drop, weight shifts back, shoulders relax to signal non-threat. This is not performed; it is automatic for people who grew up in this social world. The absence of these behaviors is visible immediately — a person who does not make the shift is noticed before anyone has consciously decided to notice them.

This physical vocabulary is the tell that Taylor cannot entirely suppress. Her body is Westerosi-born and has some of these reflexes. Her adult cognition pushes back against them. The result is a performance that is close but slightly off — a beat late, a fraction wrong in the direction of the movement, eyes that drop and then return too fast. In a child, this reads as strange. In a child around adult men with authority, it reads as dangerous-strange.

## Duration

Persistent. This is the ambient social structure, not a temporary event. Era-stable for the full 84–101 AC project window.

## Default Postures by Authority Type

**Armed men (men-at-arms, household guard, mounted escorts):**
- Eyes down on approach
- Short answers, nothing volunteered
- Physical positioning: stepped back and to the side, out of direct confrontation with the authority figure's sightline
- No asking questions of the armed man
- Requests or problems directed toward whoever appears to be in charge, briefly and formulaically: "Begging your pardon, ser, we—" before the actual content
- Era note: under Jaehaerys's long peace, men-at-arms in a market town are tax-collection escorts, not soldiers on a war footing. The deference rules are the same; the physical risk of deviation is lower (immediate violence is less likely); the documentary risk is higher (the escort captain notes what he sees and it goes into a record).

**Septons:**
- Different register than armed men: horizontal rather than vertical. You do not look down at a septon; you meet his eyes with appropriate humility but not avoidance.
- The septon's authority is moral and social; it does not require the same physical deference as armed authority.
- Disputes with a septon: not done openly. You comply with the septon's direction or you go around it quietly. You do not argue theology with a septon in public.
- Era note: the Faith Militant was suppressed by Jaehaerys I approximately fifty years before story open. The Faith at 84–99 AC has no organized military arm. A septon's authority is moral only. He cannot call armed enforcement; he can write a letter. The absence of the Faith Militant does not mean septons are powerless — it means their power is soft: reputation, charity access, communication network. Taylor's relationship with Septon Rowan operates entirely in this soft-power register.

**Maesters:**
- Institutional rather than physical authority. The chain is the signal; the deference is toward the institution, not the man.
- A maester conducting a census or record-check is in a different mode than a maester practicing medicine. In administrative mode, the maester has quasi-administrative authority; the physical deference rules apply at reduced intensity.
- Era note: the Citadel is at institutional peak in Jaehaerys I's reign. Septon Barth's legacy (he served as Hand and collaborated extensively with Citadel scholars) has not yet faded. Maesters are the realm's information backbone and are treated with corresponding deference. A maester's anomalous finding travels upward through the chain quickly and efficiently. See `cond-maester-network-behavior`.

**Lords and stewards:**
- Full physical deference. Eyes down, stance receded, do not speak unless spoken to.
- Speaking first to a lord marks you immediately as not-of-this-stratum.
- In practice, smallfolk in a market town never address the lord directly; they address his steward (Aldric Pryor for House Ryger's interests in Fairstead) or his men-at-arms, who relay as necessary.
- Era note: House Ryger of Willow Wood is a minor Tully bannerman. Their lord is not personally present in Fairstead most of the time. His authority is felt through Pryor's administrative presence and periodic tax-collection visits. The lord's hall (not a great castle) is the authority center; Fairstead is a revenue node in his territory, not a place he lives.

## Literacy as Red Flag

Literacy in a smallfolk child is not a positive signal. It is a red flag. The available interpretations:

- **Sept education:** The child has been in a septon's household and received minimal reading instruction. Most charitable and most common interpretation. Plausible for Taylor given Septon Rowan's presence in Fairstead. Rowan will have taught her — this is the cover story her literacy runs on.
- **Unusual origin:** The child is not what she appears. Literate smallfolk children sometimes turn out to be the children of merchants, traveling scholars, or minor ruined lords who have been placed in a smallfolk household for safety. This interpretation makes the child a person-with-a-secret.
- **Trade education:** A craftsman's child in a trade that requires accounts-keeping (a dyer, a wool factor, a scribe-adjacent trade) may plausibly have been taught letters by her father. This is Taylor's primary literacy cover — Edwyn Ashford keeps accounts, taught her from those same books, Rowan supplemented. The cover is plausible and defensible.

**Taylor's specific exposure:** Her literacy exceeds what the cover stories produce. She processes written text with adult-level comprehension speed. She can do accounts-level arithmetic. She can write in a practiced hand. A maester paying attention will notice this before she realizes he's noticed. The cover (father's trade + septon's supplementation) holds for casual inspection; it degrades under sustained scrutiny by someone who knows what extraordinary literacy looks like.

## The Competence Problem

A child who speaks to authority with adult confidence is misread in one of three ways in this social world:

1. **Mentally affected:** The child is touched, cursed, or otherwise cognitively abnormal. This is protective (she's not threatening) and exposing (she's memorable and discussed in the smallfolk network).
2. **Taught:** The child has been coached by someone with an agenda. This makes the child an instrument and draws attention to who might have that agenda.
3. **Dangerous:** The child is something that is pretending to be a child. This interpretation arrives when the strangeness accumulates past a certain threshold.

Taylor can pass as mentally affected (strange but harmless) if she is careful. She cannot pass as a normal child. The choice is between strange-and-dismissed and strange-and-flagged.

## Era-Specific Authority Architecture (84–101 AC)

**Jaehaerys I long peace:** The Riverlands have been largely peaceful for a generation. There are no active cross-house wars in the region; the Bracken-Blackwood feud is a chronic background condition, not an active campaign. Minor lords' men in market towns are administrative presences, not war-footing soldiers. This lowers the immediate physical threat of deviation but raises the documentary threat — a thorough, stable administration records more than a distracted, overextended one.

**Tully suzerainty stable:** House Tully of Riverrun is the Great House over all Riverlands lords. Their authority is not contested in this era. House Ryger answers to Tully, who answers to the Iron Throne. The chain of authority is functional. Taylor's escalation will eventually climb this chain — local lord → Tully bannerman → Tully → Iron Throne — not through some exceptional breakthrough but through normal institutional reporting.

**House Ryger of Willow Wood:** Minor Tully bannerman. Their territory includes Fairstead's tributary area. Their local representative is Aldric Pryor (steward). The lord himself is a minor figure by realm standards — not a villain, not a hero, a man with a territory to administer and a tax revenue to deliver upward. His interest in Taylor is entirely administrative.

**No Faith Militant:** The organized military arm of the Faith was suppressed by Jaehaerys I approximately 50 years before story open, following his political accommodation with the High Septon. At 84–99 AC, the Faith absolutely cannot field armed forces or compel compliance by violence. This is a hard era constraint. Any scene implying Faith-organized violence or armed Faith investigation is a fence violation. The Faith's tools are: letters, charity access, preaching, excommunication, and social pressure through the septon network.

**Citadel at peak:** The maester network is at institutional peak in this era. Septon Barth's legacy (cooperative governance between Crown, Faith, and Citadel) is still fresh. The maesters' soft-surveillance function (literacy detection, anomalous-findings reporting) operates efficiently and is taken seriously by lords who value their maesters' counsel.

## Available Resistance Modes for Smallfolk

**Delay:** Move slowly through a process. Annoying but not threatening. Buys time without creating a record of refusal.

**Disappear (before the authority arrives):** Requires advance notice. Converts the problem into a fugitive-status problem, which may be differently manageable.

**Claim illness:** A person too ill to be processed is a complication the officer prefers not to deal with. Temporary measure; the record notes the claim and expects return.

**Invoke competing authority:** Appeal to a septon's protection, another lord's claim, a trade guild's record. Creates a procedural dispute requiring resolution. Resolution takes time.

**Comply and document correctly:** Provide accurate information, establish status clearly, survive the process cleanly. Best outcome, only available to people with genuinely valid status.

**Direct refusal:** Not viable. Results in force. Not in Taylor's functional toolkit unless she is prepared to escalate to violence, which at age nine in a body with no fighting training is not a realistic path. As she ages, this calculus changes — but direct refusal against the lord's administrative apparatus remains high-cost throughout the series.

## What Deviation Costs

In a stable, patient administrative apparatus (which Jaehaerys's long peace produces), deviation from expected deference behaviors enters the record methodically:

- **First deviation noted:** Aldric Pryor makes a note. The note is associated with Taylor. An open flag exists.
- **Accumulated deviations:** Taylor is treated as a pattern, not an incident. Pattern cases get routed to higher review through normal administrative channels, not emergency escalation.
- **Patterned-response level:** The lord's steward is paying attention on every visit. Taylor's behavior is being measured against the prior record. Each incident adds to the weight.
- **Policy level:** The lord has directed systematic attention. Taylor is a named subject, not a category. This is the mid-S3 threshold.

The path from strange-child → anomaly-on-record → patterned-response → suppression-as-policy is the project's institutional clock. See `cond-suppression-policy-progression`.

He does not enforce; he reports.

He is thorough because he is a good functionary, not because he has a personal animus. This distinction matters: he would be just as thorough about any other anomaly in his administrative territory.
