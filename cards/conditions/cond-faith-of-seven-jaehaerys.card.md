# Faith of the Seven — Institutional Behavior at 84–99 AC (Jaehaerys I Era)

## Description

How the Faith of the Seven operates as an institution at 84–99 AC. Covers: the post-Faith-Militant institutional structure, hierarchy and communication patterns, theological categorization of uncanny phenomena, the Faith's relationship to the smallfolk it serves, and what Faith institutional actors can and cannot do in this era.

The critical era-defining constraint: the Faith Militant was suppressed by Jaehaerys I approximately fifty years before story open. The Faith agreed to this suppression in exchange for political cooperation with Jaehaerys's administrative agenda. This is not ancient history — it is within living memory of older Faith figures. The Faith at 84 AC operates with full awareness that it is not permitted an organized military arm and that this constraint is enforced by a king with dragons. Any scene implying Faith-organized violence or armed Faith enforcement is a hard fence violation for this era.

## Sensory Impact

The Faith's ambient sensory presence in Fairstead: the parish sept's bell marking the canonical hours, the smell of tallow candles and cold stone, the sound of collective prayer on the holy days, the septon's voice at market, the circuit septon's arrival every season or so. The Faith is present as smell and sound and routine — as background to daily life rather than as intermittent event. It is the most consistent institutional presence smallfolk encounter.

## Duration

Persistent across the project window. The Faith's institutional structure does not change significantly within 84–101 AC. Septon Barth's death (~99 AC) does not disrupt the Faith's operations, though it does begin a slow drift in the Citadel-Faith collaborative model that had characterized Jaehaerys's reign.

## Post-Faith-Militant Institutional Character

The Faith Militant — the Warrior's Sons (knights) and the Poor Fellows (footsoldiers) — were the Faith's organized military arm. Jaehaerys I and Alysanne negotiated their suppression approximately fifty years before story open, following the Faith's armed confrontation with the crown over various political matters. The suppression was accepted by the High Septon at the time in exchange for crown cooperation on Faith institutional interests.

**What this means in practice at 84 AC:**
- No organized Faith military. The Faith cannot field soldiers or men-at-arms. It cannot compel compliance by armed force.
- Individual septons may carry a knife for personal protection on travel. This does not constitute an organized military arm.
- The Faith can request that a lord provide armed escort for Faith purposes. The lord may comply or decline. The Faith cannot compel.
- The memory of the Faith Militant is present in institutional Faith culture. Older figures who were trained in the Faith's history know what was given up. Some resent it. Publicly, the Faith is united in its compliance.

**What the Faith does instead:** The Faith's tools without its militant arm are: pastoral authority (moral influence over its parishioners), charitable redistribution (the septon controls who receives alms), communication network (letters through the septon hierarchy, circuit septons as traveling messengers), institutional records (the Faith maintains birth and death records, marriage records, land-donation records), and social pressure (excommunication, denial of ceremony, the weight of community religious opinion).

These are substantial tools. They are not swords. Taylor's organizing and the Faith's response to her are entirely in this soft-power register.

## Hierarchy and Communication

**The Faith hierarchy at 84 AC:**

| Level | Title | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Peak | High Septon (in the Starry Sept, Oldtown) | Doctrinal authority; political relations with the Iron Throne |
| Regional | Septons-Superior | Regional administrative authority; oversee diocese circuits |
| Circuit | Circuit septons (traveling) | Move between village septs on seasonal routes; carry information and authority; the mobile information layer |
| Parish | Local parish septons (resident) | Serve a specific community full-time; Septon Rowan is this level in Fairstead |
| Auxiliary | Lay brothers, septas | Assist in larger establishments; not independent authorities |

**Communication flow:**
- Fast channel: letters, carried by circuit septons or by riders on the road. A letter from Fairstead to the nearest Septon-Superior (probably based in Stoney Sept or a comparable regional center) takes days. A letter to Oldtown takes weeks.
- Normal channel: circuit septons carry verbal reports and letters on their routes. Rowan's circuit visitor arrives in Fairstead approximately twice per year.
- Emergency channel: the Faith does not have a raven network of its own — ravens are the maester-lord network. A septon who needs to communicate urgently sends a letter by the fastest available road messenger, or asks a lord's maester to send a raven on Faith business (which may or may not be granted).

**How information about Taylor travels through this hierarchy:**
Rowan witnesses a swarm event. He categorizes it theologically. He writes a letter to his circuit septon on the next visit, framed in theological terms ("phenomena consistent with the Seven's intercession" or "a child whose relationship to the natural world merits pastoral attention"). The circuit septon takes the letter to the Septon-Superior. The Septon-Superior reads it and decides whether to: file it (most likely first response), send a circuit investigator back through Fairstead on the next pass, or write to a higher authority. This process takes months, not days. The Faith's information about Taylor is systematically slower than Aldric Pryor's because Pryor has more direct access to the lord's raven network.

## Theological Categorization of Uncanny Phenomena

The Faith has an established theological vocabulary for uncanny events. It does not have an investigative apparatus comparable to modern institutions. It categorizes, it does not forensically investigate.

**Available categories for Taylor's swarm events:**

- **Divine intercession / the Seven's hand:** A clear sign from one of the Seven — the Warrior, the Smith, the Maiden, the Mother, the Father, the Crone, the Stranger. Insect behaviors that protect the innocent could be read as the Mother's grace or the Warrior's favor. This is the most favorable interpretation for Taylor, and Septon Rowan will reach for it.
- **Blessing of the Stranger:** Unusual phenomena that are beyond normal human understanding but not evil are often attributed to the Stranger, the seventh of the Seven who represents death and the unknown. Not a comfortable attribution but not an accusatory one — the Stranger blesses as well as takes.
- **Curse or malefic working:** If the interpretation is adversarial — if the uncanny protects a bad person or harms a good one — the Faith has theological vocabulary for curse-work, working through spirits, or the influence of evil beyond the Seven. Taylor's swarm protecting a smallfolk woman against a tax collector is not easily read as malefic; the power was protective of the weak.
- **Old magic / pre-Faith forces:** The Faith acknowledges that the old gods of the north existed before the Andal invasion and that certain pre-Faith magical traditions persist in the margins of Westerosi culture. An event that does not fit cleanly into Seven-theology may be attributed to "old magic" or "the old powers" — particularly in the Riverlands, which sits at the border between old-gods north and Faith south.
- **Witchcraft or diabolism:** The adversarial extreme — someone who has made a compact with evil powers and uses that compact to harm others. This is the Faith's worst-case categorization for Taylor. It is the interpretation Rowan will not reach for; it is the interpretation that a hostile Faith investigator might.

**What the Faith does with its categorization:**

Categorization is a pastoral act, not a judicial one. A septon who decides Taylor's events are "the Stranger's working" writes that down in his notes and continues to observe. A septon who decides it is "divine intercession" may begin to publicly associate Taylor with the Seven's favor, which creates a reputation for Taylor she does not control. A septon who decides it is "witchcraft" reports upward and requests guidance.

The Faith cannot arrest Taylor. It cannot try her. It can refer her to a lord's authority (which has the power to arrest and try) with a letter recommending investigation. The political weight of a septon's letter recommending investigation is not nothing — it is exactly the kind of soft pressure the Faith has instead of swords.

## The Faith's Relationship to Smallfolk

The Faith is the most consistently accessible institution for smallfolk — more than the lord's administrative apparatus, more than the Citadel-connected maester network. The parish septon is the person smallfolk go to for charity, for the recording of births and deaths, for the management of grief, and for the resolution of minor disputes that don't rise to lord-level attention.

**What this means for Taylor's organizing:**
- The Faith's charity network is the existing infrastructure for smallfolk community cohesion. The septon controls it. Taylor working with Rowan, or around him, or eventually in tension with him, is central to her organizing.
- The Faith's record-keeping (births, deaths, marriages) is the official record of smallfolk existence. Rowan holds the official record of people in his parish. His records are the first documentary source for anyone trying to understand the Fairstead community.
- The smallfolk trust the septon in a way they do not trust the lord's men. This trust is Faith capital that can be spent or lost. Rowan spending his community-trust capital in support of Taylor (actively or passively) is a meaningful resource. Rowan spending it against her is a significant obstacle.

## Septon Rowan Specifically (Fairstead Parish)

Rowan is a parish septon at the lowest level of the Faith hierarchy. He has been in Fairstead for nineteen years. He is not a circuit septon (he does not travel) and he is not a Septon-Superior (he has no administrative authority over other septs). He receives circuit septons on their visits; he reports to the circuit septon; he writes letters that travel upward through the circuit.

Rowan's theological position on Taylor is: sincere, genuinely held, and built without Taylor's consent. He has been constructing a theological framework around her swarm-sense since before the ignition — he noticed her strangeness earlier and has been quietly incorporating it into his pastoral practice. The framework he has built is: she is in a special relationship with the Seven, specifically with the aspects of the Seven that deal with the natural order (the Stranger, the Smith-who-shapes-matter). He does not think she is a witch. He thinks she is blessed in a way she has not chosen to be.

This creates a specific dynamic: Rowan is an ally who is building a category around Taylor that she cannot control and may not want. His theological construction protects her from worse interpretations but also makes her visible in ways she would not choose. He is not her enemy, but his friendship is its own kind of constraint.
