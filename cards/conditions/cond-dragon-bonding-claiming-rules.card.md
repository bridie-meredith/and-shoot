# Dragon Bonding and Claiming Rules — ASOIAF Canon Lore (Fire & Blood / Dance Era)

## Description

ASOIAF canon lore governing dragon-bonding: the Targaryen-blood prerequisite, what bonding involves, what happens when a bonded rider dies (the claiming-window mechanics), and what the claiming process requires per the Dragonseeds precedent documented in Fire & Blood. This card is a hard lore constraint — the auditor checks all dragon-claiming scenes against it. No invention of bonding rules is permitted; all mechanics recorded here draw from Fire & Blood and ASOIAF canon.

## Sensory Impact

Dragon bonding has no ambient sensory expression; it is a state, not a weather condition. Its presence is felt in scenes where a bonded rider and dragon interact — the quality of a dragon's attention to its rider (the stillness, the specific quality of recognition), the rider's physical confidence near their dragon versus near another's. An unbonded dragon registers differently: less oriented, more alert to threat from any direction, unpredictable in a way a bonded dragon is not.

## Duration

Persistent as a background rule-set. The claiming-window mechanics become active only on the death of a bonded rider. The claiming mechanics remain operative until the window closes (the dragon accepts a new bonder or becomes irretrievably feral).

## Targaryen-Blood Prerequisite

Dragon-bonding in canon requires Targaryen blood. This is the necessary (not sufficient) condition for approaching a dragon with intent to bond. Without it, no claiming attempt can succeed — the dragon will kill the claimant.

**The blood prerequisite in the Dragonseeds context:** During the Dance of the Dragons, dragonseed claimants — bastards and distant Targaryen-blood descendants — successfully claimed dragons. This establishes that "Targaryen blood" does not require legitimacy or close lineage. Distant blood, bastard blood, acknowledged or unacknowledged blood are all valid. The prerequisite is biological, not genealogical formality.

**What the prerequisite does not mean:** Dragon-blood heritage is a threshold, not a guarantee. Many Targaryens never bonded. Many Dragonseeds were killed in the attempt. Blood permits the approach; it does not compel acceptance.

**For this project:** The wrong rider's successful claim after the opposite-number's death must be consistent with this. The wrong rider has valid Targaryen blood (near or distant) sufficient for the claim attempt. Their success is contingent on the claiming-window mechanics described below, not on their blood alone.

## What Bonding Involves

A dragon bond, per canon, is not a formal ceremony or contractual act. It is a mutual recognition established through:

1. **Initial approach**: The potential bonder approaches the dragon directly. A bonded dragon allows its rider this approach; an unbonded or initially hostile dragon may attack at this stage.
2. **Survival of initial hostility**: The dragon may challenge the approaching person — flame, snapping jaws, aggressive movement. If the person survives this phase (which may involve the dragon pulling back from a kill rather than completing it), the next phase can begin.
3. **Mounting**: The potential bonder mounts the dragon's back. This is the critical threshold. A dragon that has not recognized the bonder may kill them at this point; a dragon that has shifted toward recognition allows the mount.
4. **The moment of recognition**: An ineffable exchange — described variously in Fire & Blood as the dragon going still, the eyes fixing on the rider, the fire not coming. The bond is established when the dragon accepts the rider on its back and does not kill them.

**The bond is not instantaneous in all cases.** The Dragonseeds accounts in Fire & Blood include claimants who spent time near their target dragon before the claiming attempt — presence, familiarity, the dragon learning the person's scent. The bond is initiated in the claiming moment but its texture develops.

## Claiming-Window Mechanics Post-Bonder-Death

When a bonded rider dies, their dragon does not immediately become safely approachable by anyone. The canon pattern:

**The unmoored state**: A dragon whose rider has died becomes unmoored — behaviorally irregular, potentially volatile, not oriented to any particular person. The unmoored state is not the same as feral. The dragon retains its intelligence and its ability to recognize potential bonders. It is in a period of transition.

**The claiming window**: There is a period after the rider's death during which the dragon is approachable for a claim attempt. This window is not precisely defined in canon but is documented to exist — the Dragonseeds claimed dragons in the period after their riders' deaths during the Dance. The window is the gap between the rider's death and the dragon's either accepting a new bonder or becoming reliably hostile to all approaching persons.

**Factors that affect the window**: The existing bond's depth (a more tightly bonded pair may produce a longer-grieving, less approachable dragon post-death), the dragon's individual temperament (older, more dominant dragons are harder to claim), and the availability of valid blood candidates to attempt the claim.

**What closes the window**: Either a successful claiming (new bonder accepted) or the passage of enough time that the dragon's state has shifted from unmoored to feral-hostile. A feral dragon will kill claimants regardless of blood.

## The Wrong Rider Claiming Mechanic (Project-Specific Application)

The structural mechanic: when the opposite-number dies, the dragon they were destined to bond is left in the claiming window. Any Targaryen-blood person who can approach and survive the initial hostility can attempt a claim.

**The opposite-number's death creates the window**: The dragon was in the process of recognizing the opposite-number (or had established early bond stages). The opposite-number's death interrupts that process and leaves the dragon unmoored.

**The wrong rider's entry into the window**: The wrong rider is present, has valid Targaryen blood, and attempts the claim during the window. Their success is not a matter of destiny — it is a matter of who attempts the claim, when, and whether the dragon accepts them. In the absence of the opposite-number (who would have been the bond-partner), the dragon accepts the next viable candidate who can survive the approach.

**Why "wrong"**: The wrong rider is wrong not because the claiming is invalid (it is a genuine bond, canonically consistent) but because this rider is the faction's worst available candidate — the person the opposite-number was actively working to prevent from acquiring a dragon. The dragon does not know this distinction. The dragon knows the blood. The tragedy is that the claiming-window mechanics are neutral: they do not distinguish the best candidate from the worst. The outcome is determined by who presents at the window, not by the dragon's preference for a better rider.

**Binding lore constraint**: No agent should write a scene where the dragon claiming works through a mechanism not documented here (no magical selection, no prophetic designation of riders, no dragon refusing a valid-blood Dragonseed-equivalent claimant once the claiming threshold has been passed). The canon mechanics are the only mechanics available.

## The Dragonseeds Precedent

Fire & Blood documents the Dragonseeds of the Dance period — the bastards, distant kin, and acknowledged bastards who successfully claimed dragons when legitimate riders were unavailable or dead. This precedent establishes:

- Non-dynastic Targaryen blood is sufficient
- The dragons accepted riders who were not "meant" for them in any dynastic sense
- Multiple candidates attempted claims; not all succeeded; survival of initial hostility was the threshold, not genealogical legitimacy
- The claiming process was documented by Mushroom and Maester Munkun as including the hostile-approach phase, the mounting, and the recognition moment

The Dragonseeds precedent is the canon framework for the wrong rider's claim. No invention needed.
