# Condition: Targaryen Claim Disputed

## Description

The ambient political condition that follows any unclaimed dragon in Targaryen-era Westeros. A dragon without a rider is, by Targaryen law and precedent, the property of the Iron Throne. This is not merely a claim — it is the foundational legal basis by which House Targaryen organized its dominance over Westeros after the Conquest. The claim is real, active, and will be asserted.

The disputed element is the practical problem: the crown cannot simply take a dragon. It can send men to attempt to claim one. Whether those men survive or succeed depends on the dragon's response, which the crown's protocol assumes to be manageable by a legitimate Targaryen or a bonded rider. An unclaimed dragon that does not respond to Targaryen approach according to protocol is a scenario that the law does not have a good framework for.

## Sensory Impact

This is a political condition rather than a sensory one. Its effects are procedural and social:

- **Legal:** any location where Taylor has been sighted becomes a site where the crown has a legitimate legal interest. Local lords who harbor or assist an unclaimed dragon are technically in violation of crown law.
- **Social:** every lord, maester, septon, and merchant who sees Taylor is now a witness to an event the crown will eventually want to know about. Information travels via raven network; the report reaches King's Landing eventually.
- **Factional:** in ~110 AC, the Targaryen political factions (the forming Greens and Blacks) both have interest in an unclaimed dragon. An unclaimed dragon that could be bonded by a Targaryen would be a significant political asset. The factions are not yet at open war; both would attempt to claim through legitimate means; both would be competitive about it.

## Duration

Persistent throughout the series. The condition does not resolve unless Taylor is formally claimed (bound to a Targaryen rider, which she will refuse) or formally declared independent (which has no legal category in the current framework).

## Interaction Notes

**With ser-edwyn-celtigar:** He is the direct embodiment of this condition arriving in person. His mission is to administer the claim. His difficulty is that the claim's implementation assumes a cooperative or controllable dragon.

**With rhaenyra-targaryen (horizon):** Rhaenyra is aware of reports. She is also a dragonrider who understands that dragon bonding is not a legal process — it is a relationship. She will eventually recognize that Taylor cannot be bonded by anyone else, which changes her calculus relative to the Small Council's.

**With viserys-i-targaryen:** He is the source of the claim. His response to ongoing non-resolution will be to defer, defer, defer, and then eventually be forced by faction pressure to act. The timeline is long; the pressure accumulates.

**With local Riverlands lords:** Every lord has a different calculation. Some will cooperate with crown agents to avoid political problems. Some will attempt to position themselves as the intermediary who delivers the dragon. Some will attempt to claim the dragon themselves (legally impossible but politically tempting).
