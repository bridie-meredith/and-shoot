# Paranoid Personality Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card that colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Paranoid Personality Disorder (PPD) is a pervasive, long-standing pattern of distrust and suspicion in which others' motives are read as malevolent by default, without sufficient grounds. The essence in one line: the world is assumed hostile until proven otherwise, and proof is never quite enough.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Personality Disorders, Cluster A (the "odd/eccentric" cluster). ICD-11 codes personality disorder dimensionally (6D10) with a *prominent personality trait* of Detachment and/or Negative Affectivity; the discrete "paranoid PD" category was retired in ICD-11 in favor of severity + trait domains.
- **Core features:**
  - Baseline suspicion that others are exploiting, harming, or deceiving them, absent real evidence.
  - Preoccupation with unjustified doubts about the loyalty or trustworthiness of friends and associates.
  - Reluctance to confide, fearing information will be used against them.
  - Reading hidden, demeaning, or threatening meanings into benign remarks or events.
  - Persistent grudges; unforgiving of perceived insults or slights.
  - Quick to feel attacked and to counterattack, often where no attack occurred.
  - Recurrent, unjustified suspicions about a partner's fidelity.
- **Onset & course:** Recognizable by early adulthood; patterns often visible in adolescence. Chronic and pervasive across contexts rather than episodic; can intensify under stress, occasionally to brief quasi-psychotic suspiciousness, but generally stays reality-adjacent (suspicion, not fixed delusion).
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 1.5–4% of the general population in community estimates; methods vary widely.
- **Common comorbidities:** Often co-occurs with other Cluster A patterns (schizoid, schizotypal); also depressive and anxiety conditions, substance use, and sometimes avoidant or narcissistic traits.
- **Differential:** Distinguished from psyche-delusional-disorder (persecutory type) and psyche-schizophrenia by the *absence of fixed delusions or sustained psychosis* — PPD suspicion is pervasive and trait-level but stays this side of frank delusion. Differs from PTSD-driven hypervigilance, which traces to identifiable trauma rather than a lifelong characterological default.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character scans constantly. Attention is allocated to threat-detection: eyes track exits, hands, the direction of a glance, who else is in earshot. Tone of voice and word choice from others are parsed for hidden barbs. Affect runs guarded and tense, rarely fully relaxing even among supposed allies. Speech is careful, often clipped or qualified, with information withheld. The body holds readiness — squared posture, controlled gestures, a reluctance to turn their back to a room.

## Duration
Chronic and trait-level: this is who the character is across scenes, not a passing state. Within a scene it is stable as a baseline but the *intensity* shifts — a perceived slight, a whispered aside, a locked door, or stress and fatigue can spike vigilance into open accusation. A genuinely safe, predictable environment can lower the dial slightly, but never to off.

## Presentation & Tells
- Asks probing or testing questions; checks stories against each other.
- Reluctant to put things in writing; guards passwords, plans, locations.
- Reacts to a joke at their expense as a real attack; remembers it for years.
- Reads neutral feedback as criticism and criticism as a campaign.
- Keeps a mental (or literal) ledger of who wronged them and how.
- Scans the room on entering; chooses the seat with the wall behind it.
- Cool, formal, hard to befriend; warmth, when offered, is treated as bait.
- Litigious or rule-citing impulse — documents, threatens to escalate, "puts it on record."

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, the suspicion does not feel like suspicion — it feels like *clarity*. The character experiences themselves as the one person awake while everyone else is naïve. Every smile is a surface; the question is always what is underneath it and who benefits. The inner voice narrates motive constantly: *he said that to soften me up before he asks; she's being kind because she wants something; the door was unlocked, who has been in here.* Coincidence is intolerable — events must connect, and the connection is usually aimed at them. They refuse to look directly at their own loneliness, because to admit they need anyone is to admit a vulnerability that could be used. Trust, when extended, is conditional and provisional, a loan called in at the first deviation. There is real intelligence in the pattern-matching; they are often *right* about small things, which confirms the whole apparatus. The exhaustion underneath — the cost of never being able to set the weapon down — is felt as fatigue, not as a problem to solve. Being proven wrong about a suspicion does not bring relief; it brings a new question about why the reassurance was offered.

## Dramatic Affordances
This character reaches for verification where others extend trust — and that single move bends every relationship. They open scenes of interrogation disguised as conversation, of alliances that curdle, of the lonely competence of the person who trusts no one and so checks everything themselves. They are the one who notices the real betrayal coming because they were watching for it — and the one who destroys a true friendship by treating it as a trap. The drama lives in the gap between their vigilance being vindicated and their vigilance being the thing that loses them what they wanted. The quiet truth lives in the moment someone earns a sliver of their trust and the character does not know how to hold it without flinching.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with PPD; this card renders a character, not a verdict. Avoid the lazy "crazy/dangerous loner" caricature: suspicion is not violence, and most people with paranoid traits are far more often isolated and harmed than harmful. Do not collapse PPD into psychosis — they are not "hearing voices" or delusional; their world is *over-interpreted*, not invented. Do not write them as merely a plot-convenient lie-detector; the cost — the loneliness, the corroded relationships, the inability to rest — is the human center. Resist making their suspicion always wrong (mocking) or always right (validating paranoia as wisdom); the truth is partial, which is what makes it tragic. No content that frames surveillance or coercion of others as admirable.

## Interaction Notes
- With **psyche-schizoid-pd** or **psyche-schizotypal-pd**: deepens detachment; the schizoid's indifference plus paranoid distrust reads as a fortress; schizotypal odd beliefs can tip suspicion toward magical or referential thinking.
- With **psyche-ptsd** / **psyche-complex-ptsd**: hypervigilance compounds, but keep the registers distinct — trauma-driven alarm vs. characterological default.
- With **psyche-delusional-disorder**: under extreme stress, suspicion can crest toward persecutory ideation; mark the line carefully and do not blur trait into psychosis casually.
- With environmental condition cards (surveillance states, factional courts, low-trust settings): the world supplies real reasons to be watchful, and the character cannot tell their disorder from accurate threat assessment — a rich ambiguity.
