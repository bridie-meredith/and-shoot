# Antisocial Personality Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card that colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Antisocial Personality Disorder (ASPD) is a pervasive pattern of disregard for and violation of the rights of others, with a marked deficit in empathy, remorse, and the brakes that hold most people back. The essence in one line: the rules other people feel from the inside are, for this character, merely external facts to be weighed, used, or ignored.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Personality Disorders, Cluster B (the "dramatic/erratic" cluster). ICD-11 dimensionalizes personality disorder (6D10) with prominent **Dissociality** and **Disinhibition** trait domains. Diagnosis requires age 18+ and evidence of conduct disorder before age 15 (developmental continuity).
- **Core features:**
  - Repeated unlawful or norm-violating acts; disregard for others' rights.
  - Deceitfulness — lying, conning, using others for advantage or pleasure.
  - Impulsivity and failure to plan ahead.
  - Irritability and aggressiveness; repeated fights or assaults (in a subset, not all).
  - Reckless disregard for the safety of self or others.
  - Consistent irresponsibility — work, finances, obligations.
  - Lack of remorse; indifferent to or rationalizing harm done.
- **Onset & course:** Antecedent conduct disorder in childhood/adolescence; diagnosis from 18. Often most intense in late adolescence and early adulthood, with a meaningful **maturation/burnout effect** — overt antisocial behavior frequently attenuates from the late 30s onward, though the interpersonal-affective core can persist.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 1–4% lifetime; markedly higher in incarcerated and forensic samples. More commonly diagnosed in men, though under-recognition in women is debated.
- **Common comorbidities:** Substance use disorders (very common), other Cluster B patterns (narcissistic, borderline), ADHD history, depression, and elevated overlap with **psyche-psychopathy** as a more severe, affective-deficit-loaded construct.
- **Differential:** Distinguished from **psyche-psychopathy**, which is a related but narrower construct foregrounding shallow affect, fearlessness, and instrumental coldness — ASPD is the broader behavioral category and not all ASPD is high in psychopathic affective traits. Differs from **psyche-narcissistic-pd** (exploitation driven by grandiosity/validation rather than broad rule-disregard) and from antisocial behavior secondary to substance use or a manic episode (state-bound, not characterological).

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character moves through a scene reading leverage. Other people register as variables — useful, obstructive, or irrelevant — and social situations are scanned for angle and exit. Threat and consequence land with reduced weight; fear that would slow another person barely registers, which can read as cool competence under pressure. Boredom is a real and pressing state, pushing toward stimulation and risk. Affect is shallow but performable — they can produce warmth, contrition, charm on cue without the inner correlate. Speech is fluent, confident, and instrumentally aimed; charm is a tool, not a feeling.

## Duration
Chronic and trait-level, with the late-adulthood attenuation noted above. Within a scene the disregard is a stable baseline; what shifts is *engagement* — a target worth pursuing, a constraint worth breaking, or boredom can spike impulsivity and risk-taking. Charm and contrition can switch on and off instrumentally within a single conversation. Genuine, sustained change is possible but slow and effortful, not a scene-level reversal.

## Presentation & Tells
- Effortless, calibrated charm that turns off the instant it stops being useful.
- Lies fluently, including when the truth would serve as well — deception as default tool.
- Breaks rules and commitments without visible guilt; explanations are rationalizations.
- Reads and exploits others' needs, fears, and vanities with accuracy.
- Bored fast; reaches for stimulation, risk, or provocation.
- Shallow remorse — apologies that are tactical and dissolve under scrutiny.
- Cool under threat where others would flinch; consequence-blind decisions.
- A history that does not add up — burned bridges, unfinished obligations, a trail.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, the moral weather other people describe simply is not there — and its absence is unremarkable, not anguished. The inner voice runs as cost-benefit, not conscience: *what does this get me, what does it cost, who is in the way.* Other people are vivid as instruments and flat as ends; their pain is information about leverage, not a thing that lands as pain. The character is often genuinely puzzled by the intensity of others' guilt and loyalty — it reads as a useful weakness in them and an absurdity in the abstract. There is real intelligence in the social reading; they are frequently the most accurate person in the room about what everyone else secretly wants. Boredom is the closest thing to suffering — a flat, pressing emptiness that stimulation only briefly fills. They narrate themselves as clear-eyed where others are sentimental, free where others are caged by rules they merely *feel*. What they do not look at — because there is no native handle for it — is the slow cost of a life of burned connections; the loneliness, when it surfaces, is read as other people's failure to keep up rather than as a wound of their own making. Crucially, the absence of remorse is not the same as the *presence* of cruelty: harm is usually a byproduct of pursuit, not its goal.

## Dramatic Affordances
This character reaches for advantage and breaks the constraint everyone else feels from the inside — and that freedom makes them magnetic, dangerous, and useful by turns. They open scenes of the con, the manipulation that everyone but the mark can see, the cold competence that solves the problem no scrupulous person could, the charmer whose warmth you cannot trust and cannot quite resist. They are the one person who will do the necessary terrible thing without flinching — protagonist or antagonist depending on aim. The drama lives in the gap between their fluency and their hollowness, and in the people who love them anyway and pay for it. The quiet truth lives in the rare crack — the flicker of something like loyalty or boredom-deep emptiness — that the character cannot name and will deny.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with ASPD; this card renders a character, not a verdict. The single most damaging trope to refuse: **ASPD is not synonymous with violence or serial murder.** Most people with ASPD are not violent killers; the disorder is a broad pattern of rule-disregard and exploitation, and many never seriously harm anyone. Do not equate ASPD with psychopathy — they are related but distinct, and psychopathy is the narrower, more affective-deficit-loaded construct. Resist the "born evil monster" framing: conduct-disorder antecedents, environment, and adversity are part of the etiology, and the maturation effect means this is not a fixed sentence. Render the *lack of empathy and remorse* honestly without making cruelty the character's purpose — harm is more often instrumental byproduct than goal. People with ASPD can and do form attachments, hold jobs, and change. No glamorization of exploitation or violence, and no operational detail for conning, coercing, or harming others.

## Interaction Notes
- With **psyche-psychopathy**: treat as a more severe, affective-deficit-foregrounded overlay on the ASPD base — useful when a character needs the colder, more fearless, more instrumental register. Keep the constructs distinct in any cast note.
- With **psyche-narcissistic-pd**: a common and combustible pairing — exploitation plus grandiose entitlement; the narcissistic wound supplies motive the pure antisocial pattern lacks.
- With **psyche-borderline-pd**: opposite Cluster B poles can pair for relational fireworks — borderline intensity of attachment against antisocial detachment is a classic destructive dyad; render both with their full interiors, not as villain-and-victim.
- With **psyche-conduct-disorder**: the developmental antecedent — useful for backstory or a younger version of the character.
- With substance-use condition cards: a very common real-world overlap that escalates impulsivity and consequence-blindness; keep the substance layer as compounding, not the whole explanation.
