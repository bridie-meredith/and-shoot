# Bulimia Nervosa — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card that colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Bulimia nervosa is an eating disorder of secret cycles: episodes of feeling out of control around food followed by compensatory behavior to undo them, all hidden, all shadowed by shame, with self-worth bound hard to shape and weight.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Feeding and Eating Disorders. ICD-11 — Feeding or eating disorders, code grouping 6B81.
- **Core features:**
  - Recurrent episodes of binge eating — eating an unusually large amount in a discrete period — marked by a felt loss of control.
  - Recurrent inappropriate compensatory behavior aimed at preventing weight gain.
  - The cycle recurs over time with a regularity that defines the illness; self-evaluation is unduly tied to body shape and weight.
  - Typically maintained at or above expected weight, which makes the illness easy to hide and easy for others to miss.
  - Driven by a tide of shame and relief — the compensation buys a brief, false reset before the pressure rebuilds.
- **Onset & course:** Onset usually in late adolescence or early adulthood. Course is often chronic and relapsing, with cycles that can run for years; medical risks arise from the physiological strain of the cycle.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 1–1.5% lifetime among women; meaningfully present across other genders and under-recognized.
- **Common comorbidities:** `psyche-major-depressive-disorder`, anxiety disorders, `psyche-borderline-pd`, substance use disorders (e.g. `psyche-alcohol-use-disorder`), and impulse-control difficulties; history overlaps with `psyche-anorexia-nervosa`.
- **Differential:** Distinguished from `psyche-anorexia-nervosa` binge/purge type by *not* maintaining significantly low weight; from `psyche-binge-eating-disorder` by the presence of compensatory behavior (BED has bingeing without it).

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character lives in a tide — a building pressure, a loss-of-control surge, a crash of shame, a fragile flat calm, then the pressure again. Energy and mood swing with the cycle. Attention is split: half-present in the room, half-tracking food, the body, the next private window. The aftermath of a compensatory episode leaves the character drained, tender, often physically unwell, and working hard to look normal. The whole performance costs enormous, invisible effort.

## Duration
Chronic and cyclical. Within a scene the character can hold a composed surface indefinitely, but the urge can build under pressure, conflict, or after eating; once the cycle's pull starts, it is hard to interrupt with reasoning. The crash of shame follows reliably and colors everything after.

## Presentation & Tells
- Disappearing after meals; long bathroom trips timed just so; running water to cover sound.
- Food that vanishes; secret eating; a fierce privacy around the kitchen at certain hours.
- A composed, often high-functioning surface — bulimia frequently hides behind capability and charm.
- Fluctuating mood keyed to a cycle the others in the room can't see.
- Knuckle or hand marks, swollen jaw, dental wear, hoarse voice, sore throat — physical residue carried as "I'm just run down."
- Shame-flinch at food talk, body talk, or being seen eating; alternating with forced casualness.
- Impulsivity that may spill into other domains — spending, substances, relationships.

## Interiority / POV Voice
There are two of the character and they hate each other. One wants — wants hugely, wants past reason, wants to fill something that food was never the right size for — and one judges, and the judge always wins after the fact and never in the moment. The binge is not pleasure; it is a kind of blackout, a place where the wanting takes the wheel and the self goes quiet, and afterward the self comes back to find the wreckage and has to clean it up before anyone sees. The compensation is the cleaning-up. It feels like control reasserted, like a slate wiped, like *now I can start over* — and the relief is real and lasts an hour. Then the pressure begins to rebuild, and the character knows exactly where it ends, and goes there anyway, and despises themselves for the knowing. Most of the energy goes to the secret: the timing, the cover, the face. To be found out would be to be seen as disgusting, which is how they already see themselves, only now out loud. So they keep it. The shame is the lock and the key both.

## Dramatic Affordances
This condition gives a character a double life rendered with precision — the competent public self and the wrecked private one, and the terror of the seam between them showing. The drama lives in proximity to discovery: the roommate who hears the water, the partner who notices the food, the moment the surface cracks. It opens scenes of impulse and aftermath, of relief curdling into self-hatred, of intimacy threatened by a secret too shameful to hand over. The quiet truth lives in the first time the character lets someone see the wreckage and is not turned away. Arcs run on the contest between the wanting-self and the judging-self, and on whether anyone can hold both without flinching.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with this; the card renders a character, not a verdict, and never a how-to. Do NOT describe purging or compensatory methods, frequencies, quantities, or any actionable mechanic — name that the cycle exists; never write its operation. Refuse the caricature of weakness or greed: the binge is loss of control, not gluttony, and the illness is not a moral failing. Reject the assumption that the character "looks the part" — bulimia usually leaves the body in a normal range and hides behind high function, so the popular thin-and-frail image is wrong here. Do not play the secrecy for disgust or comedy. Recovery is slow and non-linear; do not stage a single confession as a cure.

## Interaction Notes
Stacks with `psyche-major-depressive-disorder` (the shame-crash deepens it) and with `psyche-borderline-pd` (shared impulsivity and self-attack), and may co-travel with `psyche-alcohol-use-disorder` or other substance use as parallel cycles of urge-and-shame. Alternates historically with `psyche-anorexia-nervosa`; shares the binge phenomenon with `psyche-binge-eating-disorder` but differs by compensation. In high-surveillance or low-privacy environmental conditions the secret becomes nearly impossible to maintain, which can force either collapse or disclosure — a strong dramatic pressure point.
