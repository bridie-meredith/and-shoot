# Avoidant Personality Disorder (AvPD) — Psyche Profile

## Description
This is a psyche-profile condition card: it colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority (not a location). Avoidant personality disorder is a pervasive pattern of social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy, and hypersensitivity to negative evaluation — a person who longs for closeness and connection but is so braced for rejection that they keep the world at a careful, lonely distance.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Personality Disorders → Cluster C (anxious/fearful) → Avoidant Personality Disorder. ICD-11's dimensional model maps the picture onto pronounced *detachment* combined with *negative affectivity* (the wanting-but-fearing axis distinguishes it from cold detachment).
- **Core features:**
  - Avoidance of work or activities involving significant interpersonal contact, for fear of criticism or rejection.
  - Unwillingness to get involved with people unless certain of being liked.
  - Restraint in intimate relationships for fear of being shamed or ridiculed.
  - Preoccupation with being criticized or rejected in social situations.
  - Inhibition in new interpersonal situations because of felt inadequacy.
  - A self-view as socially inept, unappealing, or inferior; reluctance to take risks for fear of embarrassment.
- **Onset & course:** Roots often in childhood/adolescent shyness and experiences of rejection; consolidates by early adulthood. Chronic and trait-like, though it can soften with sustained safe relationships and therapy.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 1.5–2.5% in the general population.
- **Common comorbidities:** Social anxiety disorder (psyche-social-anxiety-disorder) — closely overlapping; dependent (psyche-dependent-pd) traits; depressive and other anxiety disorders.
- **Differential:** Distinguished from social anxiety disorder mainly by degree and pervasiveness — AvPD is a broader, more entrenched self-and-relationship pattern (the two heavily overlap and may be a spectrum). Distinguished from schizoid PD (psyche-schizoid-pd): the schizoid person doesn't *want* closeness; the avoidant person aches for it but is too afraid to reach.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character moves through social space braced — scanning faces for the first flicker of disapproval, hearing rejection in neutral tones, reading ambiguity as the worst case. Attention narrows onto the self-as-seen ("how am I landing, what do they think") rather than onto the moment. Affect is held in, voice often quiet, body small and self-effacing. Energy drains fast in exposed situations and recovers in solitude or with a trusted few. The longing for connection runs constantly under the avoidance.

## Duration
Chronic and trait-like across a lifetime, but the moment-to-moment state shifts with felt safety. Within a single scene, one warm signal — a kindness, a sign of being liked — can briefly open the character up, and one ambiguous frown or laugh can close them down for the rest of it. Familiar, accepting company eases the guard; new people, evaluation, or any spotlight tightens it.

## Presentation & Tells
- Hangs back at the edges — late to speak, quick to defer, first to leave.
- Declines invitations, opportunities, promotions that would mean exposure ("I'd just hold everyone up").
- Self-deprecation that pre-empts the criticism they're braced for.
- Avoids eye contact, speaks softly, apologizes reflexively, hedges opinions.
- Reads neutral or ambiguous reactions as rejection; replays interactions afterward for evidence of having embarrassed themselves.
- Warmth and real wit that only emerge once they feel genuinely safe — often surprising people who'd written them off as cold.
- Long-considered "no" to closeness they actually want.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, every room is an exam they're failing. The narrating voice runs a constant low commentary of anticipated verdict — *they can tell, they're being polite, they'll be relieved when I go* — and edits every impulse before it reaches the surface: the joke not made in case it lands wrong, the hand not raised, the person not approached because the rejection is already vivid and certain. The wanting is the cruel part: the voice is not indifferent to people; it is starving for them, watching the easy ones from across the room with an ache it would never show. After every interaction comes the replay, scouring for the moment they exposed themselves, the awkward thing they said, magnified and looped. A neutral face is read as distaste; silence is read as judgment; the absence of warmth is taken as its opposite. Solitude is relief and prison at once — safe from the verdict, but the loneliness the avoidance buys is the very thing the character most wants to escape. What it refuses to look at is the possibility that the rejection it's so sure of mostly isn't there — that people might actually like them — because to believe that would mean risking the reach, and the reach is the unbearable thing.

## Dramatic Affordances
The character reaches for safety over chance — the unchosen path, the unsent message, the declined offer — which makes their rare reach toward someone enormous and load-bearing. They are the one who would be wonderful if they could only believe it, the quiet person with a rich withheld interior. Drama lives in the gap between longing and avoidance: the relationship that almost happens, the talent never shown, the friend who finally gets through the wall, the moment the character risks exposure and survives it. The slow-burn arc is the natural one — a safe relationship or a forced exposure that, against every prediction, doesn't end in the catastrophe they were braced for. The quiet truth: the loneliness is self-protective and self-defeating at once, and the cure and the fear are the same act — being seen.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with AvPD; this card renders a character, not a verdict. Refuse the dismissive read of "just shy," "antisocial," "stuck-up," or "stand-offish" — the distance is fear, not indifference or snobbery, and the person underneath is reaching, not rejecting. Do not flatten it into mere introversion; introverts are fine being introverts, while the avoidant person is in pain about a connection they want and can't risk. Avoid the convenient narrative where the love of one special person "cures" them overnight; change is real but slow and effortful. Let the character be socially inhibited *and* deeply feeling, warm, perceptive — the withheld interior is the point. People with AvPD harm no one but, often, themselves through the opportunities and closeness they forgo.

## Interaction Notes
Stacks closely with psyche-social-anxiety-disorder (the two blur into one entrenched fear-of-evaluation pattern) and with psyche-dependent-pd (the avoidant person may cling hard to the one or two who got through the wall). With depression the self-as-inadequate belief deepens and the withdrawal widens. Contrast it deliberately against psyche-schizoid-pd in a scene — same outward distance, opposite inner cause (don't-want vs. can't-risk). Environmental condition cards are decisive: evaluative, performative, high-exposure settings (new jobs, parties, auditions, public speaking) magnify every tell; small, familiar, accepting settings let the real, withheld person finally surface.
