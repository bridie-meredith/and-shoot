# Anorexia Nervosa — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card that colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Anorexia nervosa is an eating disorder organized around a relentless drive to restrict intake and an intense fear of weight gain, sustained even as the body is plainly imperilled; control over the body becomes the axis the whole self spins on.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Feeding and Eating Disorders. ICD-11 — Feeding or eating disorders (chapter on Mental, behavioural or neurodevelopmental disorders), code grouping 6B80.
- **Core features:**
  - Persistent restriction of energy intake leading to significantly low body weight relative to what is expected for the person.
  - Intense fear of gaining weight, or persistent behavior that interferes with weight gain, even when already underweight.
  - A disturbance in how one's own body is experienced — self-worth fused to shape and weight, or denial of the seriousness of the current state.
  - Two presentations: a restricting pattern, and a binge-eating/purging pattern; the two can shift over a course of illness.
  - Ego-syntonic in its grip — the illness often feels like an achievement or a source of identity, which is part of why it resists treatment.
- **Onset & course:** Typical onset in adolescence or young adulthood; can begin earlier or later. Course ranges from a single episode with full recovery to a chronic, relapsing-remitting trajectory; among the highest mortality of any psychiatric condition, from medical compromise and from suicide.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 0.3–1% lifetime among women; lower but real and under-recognized among men and across genders.
- **Common comorbidities:** Anxiety disorders (especially `psyche-generalized-anxiety-disorder`), `psyche-major-depressive-disorder`, `psyche-ocd`, `psyche-body-dysmorphic-disorder`; crossover with `psyche-bulimia-nervosa` over time.
- **Differential:** Distinguished from `psyche-bulimia-nervosa` by maintenance of significantly low weight; from `psyche-arfid` by the presence of weight/shape concern (ARFID lacks it); from medical causes of weight loss by the psychological drive and body-image disturbance.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character moves through a scene cold, depleted, and hyper-vigilant. Concentration narrows and frays as the body runs on empty; thought can become rigid and looping. Affect is often flattened or brittle, with irritability spiking around food. The body is loud with sensation the character reframes as virtue — hunger as cleanliness, lightness as proof. Energy is rationed; warmth is hard to keep. Mealtimes are battlefields disguised as ordinary moments.

## Duration
Chronic, episodic-relapsing. Within a single scene the baseline is stable, but proximity to food, a mirror, a scale, a comment on the body, or an expectation to eat can sharpen the whole nervous system within seconds. The flare passes when the trigger is removed or control is regained — not when the character is reassured.

## Presentation & Tells
- Rituals around food: cutting into tiny pieces, rearranging, slowing, drowning in condiments or hot drinks, eating in a fixed order.
- Deflection — "I ate before I came," "I'm not hungry," "I'll eat later" — delivered smoothly, on reflex.
- Layered clothing regardless of weather; covering or hiding the body; or, conversely, scrutinizing it in reflective surfaces.
- Cold hands, brittle affect, fine downy hair, fatigue carried as composure.
- Intense interest in feeding others — cooking elaborately, urging others to eat what they will not.
- Knowing, to the gram and the minute, the content and timing of everything around them.
- Anger or panic, disproportionate and fast, when control is threatened.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, hunger is not deprivation — it is order. The empty stomach is a clean room. There is a voice, and it is not kind, and it is also the only thing that feels like a friend: it keeps score, it praises restraint, it punishes any softness. The character does not see the body others see; the mirror returns a different, larger, wrong image, and the wrongness feels like the one true fact in a world of lies. Food is arithmetic, and the arithmetic never rests — every plate is a negotiation conducted at a speed no one else can see. To eat is to lose. To not eat is to win something that has to be won again tomorrow, and the next day, forever. Underneath the control is terror with no name attached to it, so it attaches itself to the body, where at least it can be fought. The character will refuse to look at the cost — the cold, the fainting, the people frightened for them — because looking would mean the achievement was a sickness, and they are not ready to give up the one thing that makes them feel like enough.

## Dramatic Affordances
This condition opens scenes of intimacy weaponized — the shared meal, the family table, the lover's concern — where care reads as attack and refusal reads as love of self. It gives a character a private, total project no one else can enter, and a secret language of control. The drama lives in the gap between competence and collapse: the character who runs everything while starving, the perfectionist whose perfection is killing them. The quiet truth lives in the moment they are too tired to fight and someone simply sits with them. Arcs of recovery are not linear — relapse is the honest shape — and the card supports the slow, contested work of letting another person's care matter more than the voice.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with this; the card renders a character, not a verdict, and never a recipe. Do NOT include weights, calorie figures, BMI thresholds, or any restrictive behavior described in a way a reader could follow — depict the *experience and surface*, never a method. Refuse the glamour myth: this is not vanity, discipline-as-aesthetic, or a "rich girl's problem." It crosses all genders, body sizes, classes, and ages; you cannot tell by looking, and a character at any weight can be desperately ill. It is not a choice and not attention-seeking. Do not stage recovery as a single decision or a love-cure. Avoid the "thin = anorexic / anorexic = thin" equation. The illness is ego-syntonic, which is the tragedy — write the seduction of the control, not a lecture against it.

## Interaction Notes
Stacks tightly with `psyche-ocd` (the rituals and intrusive arithmetic reinforce each other), `psyche-body-dysmorphic-disorder` (shared distortion of self-image), and `psyche-generalized-anxiety-disorder` / `psyche-major-depressive-disorder` (the affective substrate). Can convert to or alternate with `psyche-bulimia-nervosa`. Against high-control or high-surveillance environmental conditions the character thrives at the cost of deepening illness; against scarcity or famine conditions the line between circumstance and disorder blurs and should be handled with care.
