# Delusional Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card that colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Delusional disorder is the quietest psychotic illness: one or more fixed false beliefs held with unshakable conviction for a month or more, while the rest of the person remains strikingly intact. Outside the orbit of the delusion the character can be lucid, functional, employed, charming — which is exactly what makes the belief so durable and so isolating.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders. ICD-11 — Schizophrenia or other primary psychotic disorders (6A24, Delusional disorder).
- **Core features:** (1) One or more **delusions** lasting one month or longer. (2) The criterion that distinguishes it from schizophrenia: the person has **never met the full active-phase symptom picture** — apart from the delusion (and any hallucinations directly tied to its theme), thought, speech, and behavior are not notably disorganized. (3) Functioning is **not markedly impaired** and behavior is not obviously bizarre to others — outside the delusion's reach, life looks ordinary. Subtypes name the theme: **persecutory** (being conspired against, harassed, poisoned), **jealous** (a partner's infidelity), **erotomanic** (a usually higher-status person is secretly in love with them), **grandiose** (special talent, identity, or insight), **somatic** (a bodily defect, infestation, or disease), and mixed/unspecified.
- **Onset & course:** Typically middle to later adulthood — often later than schizophrenia. Course is chronic and persistent; the belief tends to consolidate rather than fragment over time, though it can wax and wane in preoccupation.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 0.2% lifetime (uncommon).
- **Common comorbidities:** Depression and anxiety (often *secondary* to living under the delusion's weight), and traits overlapping paranoid personality (psyche-paranoid-pd). The somatic subtype shades toward body-dysmorphic and illness-anxiety territory (psyche-body-dysmorphic-disorder, psyche-illness-anxiety-disorder).
- **Differential:** From **schizophrenia** (psyche-schizophrenia — no other active-phase symptoms, function preserved, behavior not disorganized). From **schizoaffective / mood disorders** (psyche-schizoaffective, psyche-major-depressive-disorder — mood episodes are absent or brief relative to the delusion). From **paranoid personality disorder** (psyche-paranoid-pd — pervasive suspiciousness that stops short of a fixed, specific false belief). From obsessional preoccupation (psyche-ocd, psyche-body-dysmorphic-disorder — where insight is at least partly retained). From a belief that is merely culturally held or, crucially, *actually true*.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character's whole perceptual world is normal except for one warped lens, and everything that passes through that lens is bent toward the belief. A neighbor's glance, a coincidental delay, an itch on the skin, a partner's late return — each is folded into the case as further proof. Attention is recruited: the character collects evidence, builds files, rereads messages, watches for the pattern. Energy and competence are intact and aimed — sometimes formidably — at confirming or acting on the belief. Outside its reach, the senses report cleanly and the person functions; the distortion is local but total within its territory.

## Duration
Chronic and persistent — the belief is the most stable thing about the character, often more stable than their mood or circumstances. Within a scene the delusion does not flicker; it is held with calm certainty until the topic is touched, and then it hardens. What shifts mid-scene is not the belief but the *temperature* around it: a casual remark that brushes the theme can turn an affable conversation tense, guarded, or pleading. Direct contradiction reliably consolidates rather than loosens it.

## Presentation & Tells
- Complete ordinariness — until the topic arises. Then a sudden density and specificity, a case marshaled with dates, names, and evidence.
- Calm, articulate, internally consistent argument that is impossible to dislodge with facts; the logic is sound, only the premise is false.
- Collecting and curating proof: folders, screenshots, logs, photographs, a notebook of incidents.
- Guardedness, hedging, or a quick read of who in the room is "with them" on the matter (persecutory/jealous types).
- Approach behavior toward the imagined lover, or affront when rebuffed (erotomanic type).
- Repeated medical visits, doctor-shopping, or fixation on a body part or "infestation" (somatic type).
- Frustration, hurt, or contempt — not confusion — when disbelieved; the disbelief is taken as naivety or complicity.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, there is no illness — there is a truth that other people are too blind, too compromised, or too kind to see. The conviction does not feel like a feeling; it feels like *knowing*, and it is supported by a real, careful, intelligent reading of real events. That is the trap and the tragedy: the mind works well, the evidence is sincerely gathered, the reasoning holds — only the floor it stands on is false, and the character cannot get under it. Every disconfirmation is metabolized as confirmation (of course they'd deny it; of course there's no proof — that's how good the conspiracy is). There is loneliness in it: the people the character loves keep failing the one test that matters, which is believing them. The inner voice is often lucid, even patient — explaining, marshaling, appealing — and underneath, depending on the theme, runs fear (persecutory), grief and rage (jealous), tender certainty (erotomanic), or quiet superiority (grandiose). What the character refuses to look at: the single thread that, pulled, would unravel everything — so they do not pull it, and they steer the conversation away from anyone who reaches for it.

## Dramatic Affordances
This is the most *arguable* psychosis, which makes it superb for drama: the character is competent, persuasive, and wrong, and the scene can run a long way before the reader is sure which it is. The erotomanic and jealous subtypes drive stalking, confrontation, and the slow corrosion of a relationship; the persecutory subtype builds dread and the question of whether anyone is actually after them; the somatic subtype carries a body-horror intimacy. The signature scene is the *near-conversion* — a loved one almost talked into the belief by its airtight internal logic, or the character almost reached and then retreating. The quiet truth lives in the cost the belief exacts on an otherwise whole life: the job kept, the friendships lost, the partner exhausted, the good mind turned against its own peace. This character reaches for proof and for allies in a way no one else in the scene would — and the reaching is where they slowly cut themselves off.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with delusional disorder; this card renders a character, not a verdict. **Carry the standing corrections: this is not "split" or "multiple personalities"** (that is psyche-dissociative-identity-disorder) — it is a single fixed belief in an otherwise intact mind. **And the violence overstatement is wrong here too:** most people with delusional disorder are not dangerous, and even in the persecutory and jealous subtypes — where the belief can occasionally drive harmful action — the large majority never act violently and are far more often the ones harmed or isolated. Do not write the diagnosis as a stalker-thriller engine by default. Refuse: the "crazy ex" caricature, the conspiracy-theorist played for ridicule, the somatic patient as comic hypochondriac, and the reveal-twist that the delusion was real all along *used cheaply* (the ambiguity is a legitimate tool, but the character's interior dignity should survive it). The belief is sincere and the suffering is real; render the lived experience, not a punchline. No operational detail for self-harm or for harming the targets of a delusion.

## Interaction Notes
- With **psyche-schizophrenia / psyche-schizoaffective**: the boundary is preserved function and the absence of other active-phase symptoms — use delusional disorder for a character who must remain credible and high-functioning right up against the belief.
- With **psyche-paranoid-pd**: a paranoid-PD character runs pervasive low-grade suspicion without a single crystallized false belief; the two can be staged as a spectrum or as two characters who recognize something in each other.
- With **psyche-major-depressive-disorder**: depression here is usually *secondary* — the despair of living unbelieved and besieged; render it as consequence, not cause.
- With **psyche-body-dysmorphic-disorder / psyche-illness-anxiety-disorder**: the somatic subtype neighbors both; the line is insight and fixity — delusional conviction admits no doubt.
- With environmental condition cards: surveilling, isolating, secretive, or rumor-rich settings feed persecutory and jealous themes directly; a setting that physically *could* be conspiratorial makes the ambiguity sharper and the character's read harder to dismiss.
