# Schizotypal Personality Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card that colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Schizotypal Personality Disorder (StPD) is a pervasive pattern of acute social discomfort, cognitive and perceptual distortions, and eccentricities of behavior. The essence in one line: the character lives in a world subtly thicker with meaning, signs, and presences than the consensus one — and that thickness keeps them at the edge of every room.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** Dual-listed by design. DSM-5-TR places it among **Personality Disorders, Cluster A** *and* within the **Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders** chapter — it sits in both groupings, recognized as a personality disorder with a genuine constitutional link to the schizophrenia spectrum. ICD-11 codes **schizotypal disorder (6A22)** within *Schizophrenia or Other Primary Psychotic Disorders*, not within the personality-disorder section — a meaningful nosological difference between the two systems worth noting. (Note this dual placement explicitly in any cast or cross-reference work.)
- **Core features:**
  - Ideas of reference — sensing that neutral events carry private personal significance (short of fixed delusion).
  - Odd beliefs or magical thinking influencing behavior (superstition, clairvoyance, "sixth sense," telepathy-like convictions).
  - Unusual perceptual experiences, including bodily illusions or a sense of presence.
  - Odd thinking and speech — vague, circumstantial, metaphorical, overelaborate, or idiosyncratic.
  - Suspiciousness or paranoid ideation.
  - Inappropriate or constricted affect; eccentric appearance or behavior.
  - Few close friends; social anxiety that does not ease with familiarity and is tied to paranoid fears rather than self-judgment.
- **Onset & course:** Recognizable by early adulthood; childhood/adolescent signs of solitariness, oddity, and underachievement. Chronic and stable; a minority go on to develop schizophrenia or other psychotic disorders, but most do not.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 1–4% of the general population.
- **Common comorbidities:** Other Cluster A patterns (schizoid, paranoid); depressive and anxiety conditions; and, given the spectrum link, elevated familial loading for psyche-schizophrenia.
- **Differential:** Differs from **psyche-schizophrenia** by the *absence of sustained, frank psychosis* — schizotypal experiences are attenuated, reality-tethered, and rarely act on as certainties. Differs from **psyche-schizoid-pd** by the presence of cognitive-perceptual oddity and magical thinking (the schizoid is flat and content, not eccentric and uncanny). The social anxiety differs from **psyche-social-anxiety-disorder** in that it is driven by paranoid suspicion rather than fear of negative evaluation, and does not abate with exposure.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character's perceptual field is porous. Coincidence carries weight; a streetlight flickering as they pass, a stranger's glance, the number on a door — these register as addressed to them, faintly meaningful. Peripheral perception is heightened and unreliable: a sense of presence, a felt charge in a room, a conviction that something is *about* to happen. Attention drifts toward the symbolic and away from the literal. Speech wanders — metaphor stacked on metaphor, the point approached in spirals. Affect is odd: a smile in the wrong place, flatness where warmth is expected. The body carries eccentricity — unusual dress, idiosyncratic mannerism, a rhythm slightly off the room's.

## Duration
Chronic and trait-level. Within a scene the baseline holds; what fluctuates is *referential intensity* — stress, isolation, sleeplessness, or a charged environment thickens the sense of meaning and presence, sometimes toward transient near-psychotic intensity that recedes when the pressure lifts. They generally do not cross into sustained psychosis within a scene, and that boundary is the card's most important calibration.

## Presentation & Tells
- Speech that circles, over-elaborates, or reaches for private metaphor; listeners lose the thread.
- Mentions premonitions, signs, energies, or a "sense" about people and places — held with conviction but not unshakeable certainty.
- Reads private meaning into public events; "that was meant for me."
- Eccentric dress or grooming; an aesthetic at an angle to the room.
- Constricted or off-key affect; uneasy, suspicious warmth.
- Few or no close friends; visibly anxious in groups even among acquaintances.
- Odd ritual or superstition woven into routine behavior.
- A faint uncanniness that makes others uneasy without being able to name why.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, the world is *legible* in a way other people seem to miss — saturated with correspondence and address. The inner voice reads signs: the bird that landed, the song that came on, the way the light fell, all of it part of a quieter conversation the character is half-included in. This is not delusion held as certainty; it is a persistent, textured *almost* — the meaning is felt, weighed, sometimes doubted, rarely dismissed. Thought moves by association and resemblance more than by line, so the narration drifts, gathers metaphor, arrives sideways. There is loneliness here but it is differently shaped than the schizoid's: the schizotypal character *would* connect but cannot find the frequency, and suspects, half the time, that others are not safe. They notice their own oddness as a kind of weather they live inside; they may protect or even cherish the perceptual richness while flinching from the isolation it costs. What they refuse to look at, often, is the possibility that the meanings are not addressed to them at all — because a world that addresses you, even fearfully, is less lonely than a world that does not.

## Dramatic Affordances
This character reaches for the symbolic reading of events where others take them at face value — and that makes them the scene's lay oracle, half-mystic, half-unwell, never sure which. They open scenes of the prophet who might be right, the eccentric at the edge of the group whose strange read on a situation turns out to matter, the person whose porousness to meaning is both gift and affliction. They are the one who notices the wrongness in a room before anyone else and cannot make themselves believed. The drama lives in the undecidability — is the sense a sensitivity or a symptom — and in the cost of being the person no one quite trusts and no one can fully dismiss. The quiet truth lives in their reaching for connection across a gap their own perception keeps widening.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with StPD; this card renders a character, not a verdict. Hold the line on the spectrum link without overstating it: schizotypal is constitutionally related to the schizophrenia spectrum (note the dual DSM placement and ICD-11's psychotic-chapter coding), but **most people with StPD do not develop schizophrenia**, and the card must not write them as a psychotic break waiting to happen. Do not render them as a creepy harbinger or a punchline of weirdness; the eccentricity is lived experience, not set dressing. Do not validate the referential thinking as literal magic (which mystifies a condition) nor mock it as mere foolishness (which dismisses a person) — the honest register holds the *almost*. People with StPD are far more often isolated and vulnerable than threatening. No content framing their distress or perceptual experience as entertainment at their expense.

## Interaction Notes
- **Dual grouping (load-bearing):** treat this card as sitting in *both* the personality-disorder and schizophrenia-spectrum families. When composing a cast around spectrum themes, it can bridge a personality-disorder ensemble and a psychosis arc.
- With **psyche-schizophrenia**: useful as a familial or prodromal-adjacent presence, but keep the boundary firm — attenuated, reality-tethered experience vs. sustained psychosis. Do not let one drift into the other without deliberate, marked escalation.
- With **psyche-schizoid-pd** and **psyche-paranoid-pd**: shared Cluster A withdrawal; schizotypal adds the cognitive-perceptual layer the other two lack, and the paranoid edge often rides along.
- With **psyche-ptsd** / dissociative conditions: heightened sense-of-presence and referential thinking can compound with trauma-driven perceptual disturbance — distinguish constitutional oddity from trauma response.
- With environmental condition cards (charged, liminal, or superstitious settings): the world's own symbolic density meets the character's, and the ambiguity of whether the place is uncanny or the character is becomes a rich, deliberate instability.
