# Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card. It colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD) is a cyclical mood disorder in which severe affective and physical symptoms reliably surge in the late luteal phase of the menstrual cycle and remit shortly after menses begins. Its one-line essence: for one predictable week out of the month, the floor drops out — and then, just as predictably, it comes back.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Depressive Disorders (Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder). ICD-11 — listed under Diseases of the genitourinary system (GA34.41) and cross-referenced in mental-disorder contexts; clinically managed as a mood disorder.
- **Core features:**
  - Symptoms cluster in the final week before menses, begin to improve within a few days of onset of bleeding, and become minimal or absent in the week post-menses.
  - Marked affective lability (sudden sadness, tearfulness, rejection sensitivity); marked irritability, anger, or interpersonal conflict.
  - Marked depressed mood, hopelessness, or self-critical thoughts; marked anxiety, tension, or feeling "on edge."
  - Plus symptoms such as: decreased interest in usual activities, difficulty concentrating, low energy, appetite change/cravings, sleep disturbance, sense of being overwhelmed or out of control, and physical symptoms (breast tenderness, bloating, joint/muscle pain).
  - Symptoms cause significant distress or interfere with work, relationships, or daily life — distinguishing PMDD from common, milder PMS.
  - Diagnosis ideally confirmed by prospective daily symptom tracking across at least two cycles.
- **Onset & course:** Typically emerges in the twenties or thirties; can worsen approaching perimenopause. Course is cyclical and recurrent for as long as ovulatory cycles continue; resolves with menopause (or with cycle suppression).
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 3–8% of menstruating people meet full criteria; milder premenstrual symptoms are far more common.
- **Common comorbidities:** Underlying or co-occurring mood and anxiety disorders (psyche-major-depressive-disorder, psyche-generalized-anxiety-disorder); heightened emotional dysregulation that can resemble or co-occur with psyche-borderline-pd, with which it is easily confused.
- **Differential:** The signature is *cyclical timing* — a baseline mood disorder is present continuously, while PMDD clears in the follicular phase. Premenstrual *exacerbation* of an existing condition (which never fully clears) is distinct from PMDD proper. The luteal-only pattern is the whole diagnosis; without prospective charting it is frequently mistaken for borderline traits, MDD, or "just bad PMS."

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
In the symptomatic window the character's emotional regulation thins dramatically. Small frictions land like large injuries; rejection sensitivity is acute, so a delayed reply or a neutral tone reads as abandonment. Irritability has a short fuse and a long reach — the character snaps and then floods with remorse. Sensation intensifies and turns unpleasant: light too bright, sounds too loud, the body bloated and tender and unfamiliar. Concentration scatters; energy crashes; cravings spike. Then the bleed begins and, often within a day, the system resets — clarity returns, and the character looks back at the past week as if at someone else's behavior. The defining behavioral impact is this *oscillation*: the same person, monthly, in two registers.

## Duration
Cyclical and time-locked. Within a scene, mood during the luteal window is intensely labile — it can swing within minutes, unlike the stable flatness of chronic depression. Across the month the trajectory is predictable: a build through the late luteal phase, a peak just before menses, and a relatively swift remission as bleeding starts. What shifts it mid-scene is interpersonal triggering (a perceived slight detonating disproportionate hurt or anger) — and, at the macro level, the calendar itself.

## Presentation & Tells
- A marked, recurring monthly pattern — friends, partners, the character themselves may track it ("it's that week again").
- Sudden tearfulness or rage over something minor, followed by genuine confusion or shame at the size of the reaction.
- Heightened rejection sensitivity — re-reading messages, interpreting neutral cues as hostility or distance.
- Physical tells: bloating, breast tenderness, a fatigued and uncomfortable carriage, cravings.
- Conflict initiation that is out of character for the person's baseline temperament.
- A striking "return to normal" a day or two into menses — the calm after, sometimes with regret over what was said.
- Often, a private dread of the approaching window — the character can feel it coming.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, the cruelty of PMDD is that during the window the symptomatic perception feels *completely real and justified*. The inner voice in the luteal phase is raw, certain, and persecuted: everyone is pulling away, nothing is going right, the character is unlovable and has always been, and the evidence is everywhere. Rejection sensitivity makes every interaction a potential wound; a partner's tiredness becomes proof of waning love. The thoughts are not experienced as "my hormones talking" — they are experienced as the truth finally surfacing. There is often a tormenting double-awareness in characters who know their pattern: a sliver of the mind that recognizes *this is the week, this might not be real* — which the surging emotion overrides anyway, so the character can watch themselves catastrophize and be unable to stop. Then menses begins and the fog clears with almost vertiginous speed, and the inner voice changes register entirely — calm, embarrassed, grieving the damage done by a self that no longer feels accessible. The load-bearing tension of the POV is that gap: the person who feels everything that week and the person who has to clean up after them, living in the same head on a monthly loop.

## Dramatic Affordances
This character reaches, in the window, for confrontation, withdrawal, or reassurance-seeking with an intensity that startles everyone — including the version of them that returns a week later. The drama lives in the cycle's predictability colliding with the symptoms' felt-realness: a recurring monthly crisis that strains relationships precisely because it *recurs*, and because the calm version has to repair what the symptomatic version broke. Powerful scenes: the partner learning to ride the wave without taking the bait; the character feeling the window approach with dread; the morning-after clarity and the apology that the loop will undo again next month. The quiet truth lives in the post-menses recognition — the character is not "two-faced," they are one person at the mercy of a biological tide, and the most honest scenes hold both selves with compassion rather than choosing one as the "real" one.

## Fences — Responsible Use
- Real people live with this; it is a recognized medical condition, not "PMS as an excuse" and not a punchline. The dismissive "is it that time of the month?" trope is exactly the stigma the card refuses — render it, if at all, as something *said to* the character, never as the narration's view.
- Do not write PMDD as generic "crazy woman" hysteria. The severity is cyclical and time-locked; the character is fully herself the rest of the month. Honor both registers.
- The symptomatic perceptions are real to the character but are not the narration's truth — render the felt reality without endorsing the catastrophic conclusions as fact.
- Do not conflate PMDD with psyche-borderline-pd; the distinguishing line is cyclical clearing. Avoid the lazy slide from "emotionally intense woman" to a personality-disorder caricature.
- NO operational or method detail regarding self-harm or suicide, despite the real elevation of such distress in the luteal window. Render the felt experience — the dread, the raw certainty, the clearing — never instruction; route toward tracking, support, and care.

## Interaction Notes
- With psyche-major-depressive-disorder or psyche-generalized-anxiety-disorder underneath, PMDD presents as a monthly *exacerbation* layered on a continuous baseline — the trough deepens late-luteal but the baseline never fully clears, which is a key differential signal.
- With psyche-borderline-pd, the two can co-occur and amplify each other catastrophically — cyclical hormonal lability stacked on baseline emotional dysregulation; render the monthly window as a predictable intensification, not as the origin of the personality pattern.
- With an environmental condition card (relational stress, a high-stakes deadline, isolation), the ambient pressure colliding with the luteal window produces the worst scenes — render the window as the amplifier that turns a manageable stressor into a crisis, with the calendar as the hidden variable an outside-POV character may not see.
