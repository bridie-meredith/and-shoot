# Excoriation (Skin-Picking) Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
This is a psyche-profile condition card. It colors a *character* — their behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Excoriation Disorder is a body-focused repetitive behavior in which a person recurrently picks at their own skin, causing lesions, despite repeated attempts to stop. Like its hair-pulling sibling, it lives between urge and relief and is most often a private, concealed struggle.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders (Excoriation / Skin-Picking Disorder). ICD-11 — Obsessive-compulsive or related disorders (Body-focused repetitive behavior disorders, 6B25).
- **Core features:**
  - Recurrent skin-picking resulting in skin lesions.
  - Repeated attempts to decrease or stop the picking.
  - Clinically significant distress or impairment, often social and shame-driven.
  - Not attributable to a substance or another medical/psychiatric condition.
  - Common sites: face, arms, hands; often targeting perceived imperfections, blemishes, scabs, or irregularities — real or minor.
  - Can be "focused" (deliberate, urge-relieving) or "automatic" (out of awareness during other activity).
- **Onset & course:** Often begins in adolescence, sometimes around the onset of acne; can begin later. Chronic with a waxing-waning course tied to stress.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 1.5–2% lifetime; more often reported in females.
- **Common comorbidities:** Hair-pulling (trichotillomania), anxiety and depressive disorders, OCD, body dysmorphic disorder.
- **Differential:** Distinguish from picking secondary to a true dermatological condition; from BDD (in BDD, picking is driven specifically by appearance preoccupation — the conditions overlap); from OCD (no intrusive obsession or feared outcome — the behavior relieves an urge/tension); from non-suicidal self-injury (different intent and function).

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character's fingertips read the skin for irregularities — a bump, a rough edge, a healing spot — and an irregularity, once found, calls to be smoothed, removed, "fixed." Tension or restlessness builds and the act discharges it, delivering a brief, real relief the character doesn't fully choose. Awareness varies — sometimes deliberate, often a surfacing from absorption mid-task. Affect afterward tilts toward shame and frustration. Attention splits — fingers working, mind elsewhere — and the body carries the visible cost: marks the character then manages, covers, and dreads being asked about.

## Duration
Chronic as a condition; the urge and behavior are highly state-dependent within a scene. Boredom, idle hands, stress, concentration, and access (a mirror, a free hand, a found irregularity) raise it; engagement, occupied hands, and awareness lower it. A character can move through an absorbing scene untouched, then drift into picking in a still moment. Concealment — long sleeves, makeup, angle and light management — is a continuous background load the scene can pressure.

## Presentation & Tells
- Marks, scabs, healing spots, or scarring on face, arms, or hands — frequently concealed.
- Fingertips moving over the skin, searching by feel; a hand drawn to a particular spot.
- Concealment: long sleeves in warm weather, makeup, bandages, avoidance of bright light, swimming, or close attention.
- A flicker of being "caught" — the hand pulled away, sleeve tugged down, subject changed.
- Picking that surfaces in idle or absorbed moments (on the phone, at a mirror, while thinking) without the character first noticing.
- Avoidance of intimacy, touch, or situations exposing the affected skin; lingering at mirrors then turning away.

## Interiority / POV Voice
There's a spot, and now that you've found it you can't leave it — it's rough, it's *wrong*, and your fingers have already decided they're going to make it smooth, even though you know exactly how this goes, even though smoothing it never once ended the way the want promised. The relief comes, brief and undeniable, and right behind it the cold arrival of what you've done: the mark you'll be covering tomorrow, the question you'll have to deflect. You meant not to. You always mean not to. The promise burns off in the quiet of an ordinary moment, your own hands carrying it out half before you've noticed. The shame lives less in the act than in the aftermath — the geometry of sleeves and makeup and which way to turn your arm, the constant low management of not being seen. *Just leave it alone*, they think, as if leaving it alone were a thing you hadn't tried a thousand times. It isn't dramatic. It's small, constant, private — a quiet argument with your own fingers that the room can't see you having.

## Dramatic Affordances
Excoriation opens scenes of hidden struggle under a steady surface — a character composed at cost, managing marks no one in the scene is meant to see. It powers intimacy and exposure beats: the vulnerability of short sleeves, a partner noticing, the fear and relief of being known without judgment. It dramatizes the chasm between *just stop* and the lived impossibility of stopping by will. The drama lives in the small recurrences — the hand caught and pulled back, the sleeve tugged down, the broken morning promise — and in shame meeting compassion, or failing to. The quiet truth lives in the ordinariness and persistence of it, and in a person managing it daily without anyone clapping.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Excoriation is not vanity, not a bad habit, not weakness of will, and crucially it is **not** equivalent to self-injury — it is a recognized body-focused repetitive behavior with a distinct urge-relief function, and conflating it with self-harm is a misrepresentation to refuse. "Why don't you just stop" is the harmful response to avoid putting in a sympathetic mouth uncorrected. Render *presentation and lived experience* — the urge, the relief, the shame, the concealment, the visible marks managed afterward — never operational or instructional detail about the act itself; show that picking happens and how it feels and looks, not a method. The comorbid shame and isolation deserve gravity, not spectacle or disgust. Real people live with this and lead full lives around it; the card renders a character, not a verdict.

## Interaction Notes
Closely paired with `psyche-trichotillomania` — the two body-focused repetitive behaviors frequently co-occur and share the tension-relief-shame architecture; a character may carry both. Stacks tightly with `psyche-body-dysmorphic-disorder` (when picking targets a perceived appearance flaw, the conditions fuse and concealment compounds) and is adjacent to `psyche-ocd` (same DSM family, urge-relief rather than obsession-ritual mechanism). Anxiety conditions like `psyche-generalized-anxiety-disorder` raise the urge load. Environmental condition cards interact through idle and access states: mirrors, bright light, long quiet scenes, waiting, screen time, and any low-stimulation hands-free setting raise the likelihood the behavior surfaces; high-engagement or hands-occupied settings lower it.
