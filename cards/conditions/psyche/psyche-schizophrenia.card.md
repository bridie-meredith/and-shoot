# Schizophrenia — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card that colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Schizophrenia is a chronic psychotic disorder in which a person's experience of reality is reorganized: perception, belief, thought, motivation, and emotion can each be pulled out of their usual shape, sometimes vividly (hallucinations, delusions) and sometimes by subtraction (flattened affect, social withdrawal, thinning of speech and will).

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders. ICD-11 — Mental, behavioural or neurodevelopmental disorders → Schizophrenia or other primary psychotic disorders (block 6A2x; schizophrenia is 6A20).
- **Core features:** Diagnosis turns on two or more of the following during an active phase, at least one being a positive symptom — (1) **delusions** (fixed beliefs not yielding to counter-evidence, often persecutory or referential); (2) **hallucinations** (most often hearing voices; can be any sense); (3) **disorganized speech** (loosened associations, derailment, tangents); (4) **grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior**; (5) **negative symptoms** (diminished emotional expression, avolition, alogia, anhedonia, asociality). Functioning in work, relationships, or self-care drops markedly. Some signs must persist (in some form) for at least six months.
- **Onset & course:** Typically late teens to mid-thirties; often earlier in men than women. Frequently preceded by a **prodrome** of social withdrawal, odd ideas, and declining function before the first full break. Course is chronic, usually episodic-with-residual: acute episodes punctuate a baseline that may carry persistent negative or cognitive symptoms. Outcomes vary widely — some recover substantial function, especially with early treatment and support.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 0.3–0.7% lifetime.
- **Common comorbidities:** Depression, anxiety disorders, substance use disorders (especially tobacco, cannabis, alcohol — see psyche-alcohol-use-disorder, psyche-stimulant-use-disorder), and elevated suicide risk. Schizotypal personality features may shade the prodrome (psyche-schizotypal-pd).
- **Differential:** Distinguished from **schizoaffective disorder** (psyche-schizoaffective — prominent, sustained mood episodes overlap the psychosis) and **bipolar/depression with psychotic features** (psychosis only during mood episodes). From **delusional disorder** (psyche-delusional-disorder — delusions without the other active-phase symptoms, function otherwise preserved). From **brief psychotic disorder** (psyche-brief-psychotic-disorder — under a month, full return to baseline). Substance-induced psychosis and medical causes must be excluded.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character's channel to the world is no longer clean. Salience misfires: a passing glance, a license plate, a song lyric can arrive freighted with private meaning. Voices may overlay the room — commenting, narrating, sometimes commanding — so that the character is dividing attention with something no one else can hear. Thought may run fast and slip its rails, or thin out to near-silence. Affect can go flat: the face doesn't move much, the voice loses its melody, even when something inside is happening. Motivation can collapse — not laziness, but a flattening of the engine that turns intention into action. Self-care, hygiene, and sleep-wake rhythm slide. In acute phases, the boundary between inner and outer event blurs.

## Duration
Chronic and lifelong as a vulnerability, but symptom intensity is not constant. Within a single scene the character's baseline (which may include residual negative symptoms) is fairly stable; an acute psychotic state is more volatile and can shift with stress, sleep loss, a triggering remark, a perceived threat, a missed dose, or substance use. A character can move within one conversation from guarded coherence to derailment if pushed; equally, a grounding presence, a familiar routine, or a quiet room can settle them.

## Presentation & Tells
- Speech that derails — starts on topic, slides sideways, lands somewhere unrelated; or answers a question with a private logic the listener can't follow.
- Pausing mid-sentence, glancing to the side, or sub-vocalizing — attending to something off-channel.
- Flat or incongruent affect: a level voice, an unmoving face; or a smile at the wrong moment.
- Guardedness, suspicion, scanning the room; reading meaning into ordinary objects or remarks.
- Reduced output — short answers, long silences, little spontaneous speech (alogia).
- Withdrawal: declining invitations, letting contact lapse, retreating into a room.
- Disorganization in the practical surface — mismatched clothing, neglected hygiene, a derailed plan, an errand abandoned halfway.
- Neologisms or idiosyncratic phrasing; concrete or oddly literal interpretation.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, the world is *more*, not less — over-meaningful. Coincidence stops feeling like coincidence. The mind keeps catching connections and the connections feel true, with a certainty that's hard to argue with because it isn't reached by argument; it simply arrives, whole. When the voices are present they are not metaphor — they have timbre, direction, intent; the character may know intellectually that others don't hear them and still cannot make them less real. Thought can run quick and bright and uncatchable, leaping rails, or it can go quiet and slack so that reaching for a word is like reaching into fog. There is exhausting work in keeping a face on for other people, in passing — and a specific loneliness in being told the most vivid thing in your day didn't happen. The inner voice may be wary (everyone seems to be in on something), or weary (the effort of ordinary function is enormous and invisible to others), or — in the flatter registers — strangely far away, watching its own life from one room over. What the character often refuses to look at directly: the gap between what they're sure of and what the people they love keep gently insisting is so.

## Dramatic Affordances
This character reaches for meaning where others see noise — and is sometimes right about the texture of a thing while wrong about its cause, which makes them a destabilizing truth-teller. Drama lives in the negotiation over reality itself: who is believed, what counts as evidence, the love that keeps trying to reach across an unshared world. Scenes of *passing* — holding composure while attending to something off-channel — carry enormous private tension. The arc of a first break is a horror and a grief for everyone around it; the arc of recovery and management is quieter and rarely told. The quiet truth lives in the ordinary moments between episodes: the character as a full person with humor, skill, and longing, doing the daily labor of staying anchored. Relationships strain on the question of trust and on caregiver exhaustion, but also deepen in the moments of being genuinely seen.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with schizophrenia; this card renders a character, not a verdict, and not a plot device. **Correct the myth directly: schizophrenia is not "split" or "multiple personalities."** It is a disorder of perception, thought, and motivation — distinct from dissociative identity disorder (psyche-dissociative-identity-disorder), with which it is constantly confused in popular usage. A character with schizophrenia does not "switch personalities." **Correct the violence overstatement directly:** the large majority of people with schizophrenia are never violent, and as a group they are far more often *harmed* — victimized, exploited, neglected — than harmful. Do not use the diagnosis as shorthand for menace, unpredictability, or evil. Refuse: the gibbering-asylum caricature, the disorder revealed as a "twist," symptoms played for horror, the character defined entirely by illness. The voices are not the character's true malevolence leaking out; affect-flatness is not coldness of heart. Render symptoms for characterization, never as a manual — no operational detail of self-harm. Give the character interiority, competence, and want beyond the diagnosis.

## Interaction Notes
- With **psyche-schizoaffective**: the boundary is mood — co-occurring sustained depressive or manic episodes push toward the schizoaffective picture; keep the distinction crisp.
- With **psyche-major-depressive-disorder**: post-psychotic depression and negative symptoms can blur together; the grief of insight after an episode is real dramatic ground.
- With **psyche-alcohol-use-disorder / psyche-stimulant-use-disorder**: substance use is common, complicates the picture, and can precipitate episodes; render the entanglement, not a moral failing.
- With **psyche-schizotypal-pd / psyche-paranoid-pd**: shared spectrum texture — odd beliefs, perceptual distortions, suspicion — without the full active-phase break; useful for a prodromal or milder adjacent character.
- With environmental condition cards: high-stimulus, chaotic, or surveilling settings (crowds, noise, perceived watching) amplify positive symptoms; quiet, structured, trusted settings settle them. Sleep-deprivation and isolation conditions are destabilizers.
