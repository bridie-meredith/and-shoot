# Gambling Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card that colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority (not a location). Gambling Disorder is a behavioral addiction — the only one currently classified alongside the substance use disorders — in which the character gambles compulsively and cannot stop despite escalating loss, chasing the action and the next bet long past the point of harm.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Substance-Related and Addictive Disorders, Non-Substance-Related Disorders (Gambling Disorder); the recognized behavioral addiction. ICD-11 — Disorders due to substance use or addictive behaviours (Gambling disorder; specified as predominantly online or offline).
- **Core features:**
  - Need to gamble with increasing amounts to reach the desired excitement (a behavioral tolerance).
  - Restlessness or irritability when trying to cut down or stop.
  - Repeated unsuccessful efforts to control, cut back, or stop.
  - Preoccupation — reliving past bets, planning the next, finding money to gamble with.
  - Gambling to escape distress or to relieve a low mood.
  - "Chasing losses" — returning to win back what was lost, the defining cognitive trap.
  - Lying to conceal the extent of involvement; jeopardizing or losing relationships, work, or opportunity; relying on others to relieve desperate financial situations.
- **Onset & course:** Can begin in adolescence or later adulthood; often episodic with periods of greater and lesser intensity, and can be chronic or progressive. Recovery is achievable with treatment and support.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 0.5–1% point prevalence in adults; higher rates of subclinical problem gambling.
- **Common comorbidities:** depression (psyche-major-depressive-disorder), bipolar disorder (psyche-bipolar-i), substance use disorders (psyche-alcohol-use-disorder), ADHD (psyche-adhd), and narcissistic personality traits (psyche-narcissistic-pd) in a subset.
- **Differential:** Distinguished from professional or social gambling (controlled, bounded, with limits kept) and from gambling within a manic episode (psyche-bipolar-i) — the disorder is the loss of control, the chasing, and the continued betting despite mounting harm, present outside of mood episodes.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character moves through the scene tuned to a frequency no one else hears — the live possibility of the next bet, the open game, the result still pending. In the action, the body lights up: heart fast, attention tunneled to a hot bright point, time collapsing, everything else in the room going grey and distant. Anticipation is the drug; the moment before the outcome is the peak. Between sessions a restless, irritable itch sets in, an unease that only the action settles. Mood tracks the running balance, but more than that it tracks the *aliveness* — the dead flatness of ordinary hours against the charge of being in play.

## Duration
Often episodic and can be progressive; within a scene, governed by proximity to the action. A character near a live bet or an open opportunity is lit and tunneled; one cut off from it is restless and irritable; one deep in a chase is gripped by a logic that overrides everything. The state can shift fast — a tip, a screen, a result, an unexpected windfall, or a sudden loss can swing the interior weather inside a single scene.

## Presentation & Tells
- Attention pulled to scores, odds, results, screens, or the next opportunity even while ostensibly present for something else.
- Restlessness and irritability when away from the action; a settling, focusing calm when back in it.
- Money behaving strangely — secrecy around finances, borrowing, sudden flush or sudden broke, possessions quietly gone.
- Escalation: stakes climbing over time, the old level no longer enough to register.
- Lying and concealment specifically around the extent — vague accounts, hidden statements, defensiveness when finances are named.
- The chase: after a loss, a charged, narrowed, almost compelled return to win it back, immune to the obvious math.
- Time disappearing — long, unaccounted absences; obligations and relationships bending around sessions.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, the bet is aliveness — the one place the character feels fully switched on, where dull ordinary existence sharpens to a single bright edge and the self comes online. The interior voice is fluent in odds, near-misses, and story: every loss reframed as almost-won, every pattern read as a sign, the next bet always the one that turns it. Chasing has its own remorseless logic — *I'm down, so I have to keep going, stopping now just locks in the loss, one more will set it right* — and from inside it feels not like recklessness but like the only rational move, the responsible move. The near-miss does the most damage: it reads to the character not as a loss but as almost-victory, proof the win is close, and it pulls harder than a clean win would. The accounting is magical and self-serving — wins remembered vividly, losses softened or rewritten, the running total quietly fictional. Underneath the action runs an escape: the bet silences a low mood, a dread, a flatness, and the character gambles to not-feel as much as to win. What the inner voice refuses to look at is the real ledger — the true total, the cost to the people who trust them, the size of the hole — and beneath the bravado often runs shame and a cornered, frightened wish to be out of it that the next bet keeps drowning.

## Dramatic Affordances
This character reaches for the bet — for the aliveness, the escape, the story of the win that fixes everything — where another would count the cost and walk away. The drama lives in the chase and its airtight wrong logic, in the secrecy and the slow hollowing of trust, in the windfall squandered and the desperate borrowing, in the near-miss that pulls them back when they had almost left. Rich vein: the lie that finally surfaces; the loved one who discovers the true total; the win that funds the deeper fall; the rock-bottom that is a number, not a substance; the recovery that has to make a flat, un-charged life feel worth living. The quiet truth lives in what the character is escaping — the deadness or despair the action drowns out — and in the cornered shame beneath the showmanship.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with Gambling Disorder; the card renders a character, not a verdict. Refuse the framing of mere greed or weak character — this is a recognized behavioral addiction with real neurobiological underpinnings, not a moral failing or a simple love of money. Refuse the comic-degenerate-gambler caricature; render the person and the genuine compulsion. Take the harm seriously — financial ruin, fractured families, and despair are common — without reducing the character to a cautionary symbol. Recovery is real and achievable; relapse is part of the illness, not proof of worthlessness. Do not depict the "system" or "method" that a character believes will win — render the belief and its grip, not anything that reads as instruction or a real betting scheme. NO operational detail that could function as a guide to gambling itself.

## Interaction Notes
Stacks with depression (psyche-major-depressive-disorder) — gambling becomes escape from the low, and loss deepens the low, forming a punishing loop. Overlaps with bipolar disorder (psyche-bipolar-i), where reckless betting can spike during manic episodes; the distinction is whether the gambling stands on its own outside of mood episodes. Co-occurs with substance use disorders (psyche-alcohol-use-disorder, psyche-stimulant-use-disorder), often layered with the action. With impulsivity-linked conditions (psyche-adhd) the inhibition gap can compound. With environmental condition cards: a live venue, an accessible screen or game, an unexpected windfall, a fresh loss, or financial pressure should all be played as live interior pulls toward the next bet, not as background.
