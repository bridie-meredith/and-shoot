# Oppositional Defiant Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
This is a psyche-profile condition card. It colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Oppositional Defiant Disorder (ODD) is a pattern of angry/irritable mood, argumentativeness and defiance toward authority, and vindictiveness that runs hotter, longer, and more reflexively than ordinary willfulness. Its one-line essence: a person braced against being commanded, who reads instruction as an attack on the self and answers it with friction.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR, Disruptive, Impulse-Control, and Conduct Disorders. ICD-11: Disruptive behaviour or dissocial disorders (Oppositional defiant disorder, 6C90).
- **Core features:**
  - Frequent loss of temper; touchy, easily annoyed, often angry and resentful.
  - Arguing with authority figures; actively defying or refusing to comply with rules and requests.
  - Deliberately annoying others; blaming others for one's own mistakes or misbehavior.
  - Spitefulness or vindictiveness — keeping score, settling it.
  - Behavior is persistent (months, not a bad week) and out of proportion to the person's age and context.
  - Crucially, it does not require violating others' basic rights or major societal norms — that is the line into conduct disorder.
- **Onset & course:** Usually emerges in preschool to early school years; rarely first appears after early adolescence. Often wanes with maturity but can persist into adulthood or escalate toward conduct disorder in a minority.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 3% of children and adolescents, with a modest male skew in childhood that narrows by adolescence.
- **Common comorbidities:** ADHD (very high overlap), anxiety and depressive disorders, DMDD, and later conduct disorder. The mood/irritability dimension predicts depression and anxiety; the defiant dimension predicts conduct problems.
- **Differential:** Distinguish from ordinary stubbornness (ODD is more frequent, more impairing, and crosses settings or strains relationships); from conduct disorder (ODD lacks the aggression, destruction, deceit, and rights-violation); from DMDD (where severe persistent irritability and explosive outbursts dominate); and from ADHD-driven non-compliance (driven by inattention/impulsivity, not by the will to refuse).

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character moves through a scene with an antenna tuned to
authority and unfairness. Affect regulation runs short: irritation
spikes fast and recovers slowly, so they arrive at a scene already
half-loaded, primed to read the next instruction as the next
imposition.

Attention narrows onto the person giving the order rather than the
task — the *who's-making-me* eclipses the *what.* Energy is
reactive: they conserve until something demands compliance, then
surge. Speech tilts toward the rhetorical question, the
counter-demand, the "why should I." The body braces — crossed
arms, a planted stance, a chin that comes up.

The condition is most visible at the moment of any instruction,
transition, or boundary, and quietest when no one is trying to
move them.

## Duration
Chronic as a trait pattern; episodic in expression. Within a scene the baseline is a low simmer that any demand, correction, or perceived slight can bring to a boil within seconds. What shifts it mid-scene: being given a genuine choice rather than an order, being addressed as a peer, a respected figure de-escalating without backing down, or — the other direction — a power-play that escalates them in a single beat. Fatigue, hunger, and an audience all shorten the fuse.

## Presentation & Tells
- Answers a request with a question: "Says who?" / "Why do I
  have to?" — the order is met with a challenge to its standing,
  not the task.
- Technical compliance laced with contempt — does the thing,
  slowly, badly, with a look that says it cost them.
- Keeps a running ledger of others' unfairness and produces it on
  cue, dated and itemized, going back further than anyone expects.
- Blames reflexively; the fault is always upstream of them, and
  the story arrives pre-built.
- Deliberately needles — finds the small thing that gets under
  someone's skin and does exactly that, watching for the flinch.
- Body language of refusal: planted feet, crossed arms, deliberate
  slowness, eye-roll, the held stare, the half-turn away.
- The chin that comes up; a voice that flattens into the
  rhetorical, the "fine, whatever," the over-controlled calm that
  is louder than shouting.
- Calm with peers and warm with allies — the friction is reserved
  almost entirely for whoever holds power over them.
- Apologies, when they come, are bargained for, hedged, and rarely
  felt as owed.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From the inside it does not feel like defiance — it feels like
defense. The world keeps handing them orders dressed up as
reasonableness, and reasonable is the trap: comply and you've
conceded that they were right to make you. So the inner voice is
forever litigating. *They always decide for me. They never ask.*

Fairness is the obsession — not abstract fairness, but the
specific tally of who got away with what, who was let off, who
never has to answer for anything. A correction doesn't register as
information; it registers as a verdict on their worth, and the
body answers before the mind does.

There is a hot satisfaction in the no — a small reclaimed
sovereignty — chased almost instantly by the cost: the slammed
door, the relationship strained again, the bewildering distance
between how reasonable they felt and how the room read them. They
are not stupid; they often know they're escalating and feel unable
to stop, watching themselves dig in, because backing down now
would mean the other person won.

The thing they refuse to look at: how much they want, underneath,
to be on the same side as the person they're fighting — and how
much it would cost to admit it.

## Dramatic Affordances
- The scene where authority must be obtained, not assumed — every order is a negotiation, and the holder of power has to *earn* the room.
- Mentor/ward and parent/child arcs where love and friction are the same gesture; the slow, hard-won pivot from adversary to ally.
- The character who says the unsayable to the powerful because deference was never installed — useful, dangerous, sometimes right.
- The quiet truth: the defiance is a question — *will you stay even when I make it hard?* The drama lives in whoever answers yes.
- A foil to the people-pleaser; the engine of a power struggle that no one can quite win.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real children and adults live with ODD, and it is often a response to chaotic, harsh, or inconsistent environments — not simple "bad behavior" or "bad parenting" as a verdict on either party. Refuse the caricature of the irredeemable brat or the budding criminal; ODD is not a prophecy of conduct disorder or antisocial personality, and most who carry it grow out of it. Do not collapse it into pure villainy — the defiance is defensive, not predatory. Honor that the mood/irritability is genuine suffering, not just an inconvenience to adults. The card renders a character, not a verdict. No operational detail; characterization only.

## Interaction Notes
- **With psyche-adhd:** Extremely common pairing. The impulsivity and frustration of ADHD feed the defiance; the non-compliance reads doubled. Distinguish the "can't" (ADHD) from the "won't" (ODD) in the same character — both can be live in one beat.
- **With psyche-conduct-disorder:** ODD is a frequent precursor; a character can sit on the line, where defiance is starting to shade into rights-violation. Useful for an escalation arc — but keep the line legible.
- **With psyche-dmdd / psyche-intermittent-explosive-disorder:** The irritable dimension of ODD can co-present with explosive outbursts; pair carefully and let one register dominate so the character stays readable.
- **With environmental condition cards:** Crowding, noise, surveillance, and authority-dense settings (a barracks, a classroom, a courtroom) all shorten the fuse and bring the condition forward.
