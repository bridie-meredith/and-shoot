# Generalized Anxiety Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card. It colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) is chronic, free-floating, hard-to-control worry that attaches to ordinary life — work, health, money, the people one loves — and runs on regardless of whether any single fear is real, with a body kept on low boil by the keeping.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Anxiety Disorders. ICD-11 — Mental, behavioural or neurodevelopmental disorders → Anxiety or fear-related disorders (6B00, Generalised anxiety disorder).
- **Core features:**
  - Excessive, hard-to-control worry across multiple domains, more days than not, for at least six months.
  - The worry is diffuse and migratory — it leaves one topic and lands on the next; "if not this, then that."
  - Physical signature: restlessness or feeling keyed-up, easy fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance.
  - Anticipation of catastrophe out of proportion to actual likelihood; the mind rehearses bad outcomes as a form of imagined preparation.
  - Worry is experienced as both protective ("if I stop, something will go wrong") and exhausting.
  - Reassurance helps for minutes, then the worry re-seeds elsewhere.
- **Onset & course:** Often insidious; many report having "always been a worrier." Median onset around the thirties but frequently traceable to childhood/adolescence. Typically chronic and fluctuating — worse under stress, rarely fully absent.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 5–6% lifetime; women diagnosed roughly twice as often as men.
- **Common comorbidities:** Major depressive disorder, panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, insomnia disorder; alcohol use as self-medication.
- **Differential:** Distinguished from ordinary worry by uncontrollability, breadth, duration, and functional cost. Unlike panic disorder, the fear is sustained and diffuse rather than acute peaks; unlike social anxiety, it is not confined to evaluation; unlike OCD, the worries are about real-life concerns rather than ego-dystonic intrusions met with rituals.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character moves through a scene already braced. Attention is split — one channel tracks the conversation, another scans for the thing that could go wrong. The body holds tension as default: shoulders up, jaw set, a low background hum of muscle ache by evening. Sleep is shallow and the morning arrives pre-loaded with dread. Decisions cost more because each one is run through a private risk model. Energy drains faster than the day's events explain; fatigue is the tax on vigilance.

## Duration
Chronic and trait-like, but volume-modulated. Within a single scene the worry rarely vanishes — it can be quieted by absorption in a task, a trusted person's steadiness, or a clear and bounded problem to solve, and it spikes with ambiguity, waiting, silence from someone who should have called, or any open loop. A reassuring answer lowers it briefly; a new uncertainty re-arms it.

## Presentation & Tells
- Asks "are you sure?" and "but what if—" more than the situation warrants; seeks reassurance, then doesn't quite keep it.
- Restless hands — picking, tapping, twisting a ring, smoothing the same crease.
- Over-prepares: backups, contingency plans, leaving early, double-checking locks and stoves and sent messages.
- Catastrophizes aloud in small ways — narrates the worst version of a delayed text, a cough, a quiet boss.
- Trouble landing on a decision; circles it, audits it, second-guesses after committing.
- Tight breathing, a swallowed sigh, the apology reflex ("sorry, I'm probably overthinking this").
- Reads neutral cues as warnings; a closed door, a short reply, a long pause all get a story.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, worry isn't a feeling so much as a job that never clocks out. The mind runs branches — *if the call doesn't come by six, then,* and then it builds the *then,* and the *then* has its own *then.* It feels like responsibility. To stop worrying would be to take your hands off the wheel, and some superstitious part is certain the worrying is what's holding the road. The narrating voice notices exits, timings, the exact phrasing of what someone said and what they might have meant by leaving a word out. It refuses to sit with not-knowing; not-knowing is the unbearable part, worse than a confirmed bad answer, because a bad answer can at least be acted on. There is a particular fatigue to it — the tiredness of someone who has run a hundred fire drills before lunch for a fire that didn't come. And underneath, a flicker of grief: the knowledge that the people around them got to simply *have* the afternoon, while they spent it guarding a future that mostly never arrived.

## Dramatic Affordances
This character is the one who saw it coming — sometimes uselessly, occasionally right, and the story can play either. They reach for the contingency no one else packed, the question no one else asked, the early warning. Drama lives in the gap between their vigilance and others' ease: the relief of being held by someone calm, the friction of being told to relax, the quiet vindication or quiet shame when the feared thing does or doesn't happen. Arcs bend toward learning to tolerate an open loop, to hand the wheel to someone else, to discover that the worrying was never what kept anyone safe. The tender truth: their worry is love wearing armor — proof of how much there is to lose.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with GAD; this card renders a character, not a verdict, and not a flaw to be cured by a single pep talk. Avoid the "uptight nag" and the "neurotic comic relief" who exists to be told to calm down. Worry is not a personality defect or a failure of willpower — "just stop thinking about it" is exactly the instruction that doesn't work, and a scene that lands that as wisdom is writing the disorder, not against it. Do not equate anxiety with weakness, fragility, or low competence; many high-functioning, deeply capable people carry it invisibly. Don't render recovery as the disappearance of all fear — it's the loosening of the grip. No operational detail on self-medication or harmful coping.

## Interaction Notes
Stacks heavily with **psyche-major-depressive-disorder** — the anxious-depressive blend where dread and futility feed each other (worry about a future the depression says is hopeless). With **psyche-panic-disorder**, the chronic background worry is punctuated by acute attacks, and fear-of-the-next-attack becomes one more worry domain. With **psyche-social-anxiety-disorder** the worry narrows hard around evaluation. **psyche-insomnia-disorder** is both cause and effect — the keyed-up body won't release into sleep, and the sleeplessness sharpens the next day's worry. Against environmental condition cards, ambiguous or high-stakes settings (waiting rooms, delayed travel, silent phones, looming deadlines) amplify presentation; clear structure, a defined task, and a steady companion damp it.
