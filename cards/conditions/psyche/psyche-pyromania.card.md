# Pyromania — Psyche Profile

## Description
This is a psyche-profile condition card. It colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Pyromania is recurrent, deliberate fire-setting driven by an internal compulsion — tension and arousal before, fascination with and attraction to fire, and relief or gratification after — done for no external gain. Its one-line essence: a person pulled toward fire by something they can't name and can't refuse, not for profit, revenge, or destruction's sake. (This card stays at construct level; it contains no method or operational detail and must never acquire any.)

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR, Disruptive, Impulse-Control, and Conduct Disorders. ICD-11: Impulse control disorders (Pyromania, 6C71).
- **Core features:**
  - Deliberate and purposeful fire-setting on more than one occasion.
  - Tension or affective arousal before the act.
  - Fascination with, interest in, curiosity about, or attraction to fire and its contexts (and associated paraphernalia and aftermath).
  - Pleasure, gratification, or relief when setting fires, or when witnessing or participating in their aftermath.
  - The behavior is NOT done for monetary gain, to express an ideology, to conceal a crime, out of anger or vengeance, in response to a delusion/hallucination, or as a result of impaired judgment.
  - It is rendered, in characterization, only as compulsion-and-aftermath — never as technique.
- **Onset & course:** Poorly characterized given rarity; fire interest may emerge in childhood/adolescence, but true pyromania as a diagnosis is uncommon and most often described in late adolescence to adulthood. Course is thought to be chronic and fluctuating, though the evidence base is thin.
- **Prevalence:** Very rare as a formal diagnosis. The great majority of deliberate fire-setting is NOT pyromania — it is motivated (gain, revenge, ideology, concealment, peer pressure, or other disorders); pyromania is the small, motiveless, compulsion-driven residue.
- **Common comorbidities:** Other impulse-control and addictive disorders, mood and anxiety disorders, substance use, and (in the broader fire-setting population, which is mostly NOT pyromania) conduct disorder and antisocial traits.
- **Differential:** Distinguish from arson for profit/revenge/ideology/concealment (motivated, not pyromania); from fire-setting in conduct disorder, antisocial PD, mania, psychosis, or intoxication; and from ordinary, developmentally common childhood fire-curiosity. The diagnosis is reserved for the rare motiveless, compulsion-driven, fascination-laden pattern.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
For this character fire is not background — it is the brightest
thing in any room. Attention snags on flame, on smoke, on the
smell of something burning, on the apparatus and aftermath of
fire, with a pull others simply don't feel.

The pre-act state is a mounting internal pressure, a keyed arousal
that fixes on fire as its outlet. Affect around fire runs intense
and absorbed — a held, almost reverent fascination, a quiet that
is closer to awe than calm. Energy concentrates toward the urge
and discharges in the relief that follows.

Outside the fixation the character may present as entirely
ordinary; it is proximity to fire, or to talk of it, that brings
the condition forward and changes the texture of their attention —
the way a tuning fork answers a struck note.

## Duration
Chronic and fluctuating; the urge is episodic and tension-linked. Within a scene the fascination is steady whenever fire is present and the compulsive pressure builds along a curve under stress, loss, or excitement. What raises it: emotional pressure with no other outlet, the presence or talk of fire, boredom, a sense of powerlessness for which fire offers a counterfeit mastery. What lowers it: distraction, company, distance from the trigger, the fresh weight of consequences. The fascination can persist quietly long after any acute urge has passed.

## Presentation & Tells
- Eyes that go to the flame and stay — at the candle, the hearth,
  the cigarette, the bonfire — a fascination that reads as a beat
  too long.
- Lingering near fires and their aftermath; an interest in alarms,
  engines, scorched ground that others find faintly unsettling.
- A keyed, absorbed stillness around fire; a brightening of affect
  that doesn't quite match the moment.
- Talk that returns to fire — its beauty, its power, its
  movement — with a warmth that sits oddly in the conversation.
- Visible internal tension under stress that eases, inexplicably,
  in fire's presence — as if something in them had been let down.
- Concealment and shame about the depth of the fascination;
  deflection, downplaying, a quick subject-change when it's
  noticed.
- A reputation, sometimes, for being "the one who's always
  watching the fire" — rendered as impression and presence, never
  as deed-by-deed method.

## Interiority / POV Voice
Fire is the one honest thing. The voice circles it with a longing
that has nothing to do with destruction and everything to do with
*aliveness* — fire moves, breathes, transforms, answers, and in a
life that often feels gray and powerless it is the place where
something finally happens.

The pressure builds with no name, the way weather builds, and fire
is where it wants to go; resisting it is like holding a breath
past the point of pain. The pull is aesthetic and bodily and
shameful all at once — they know how it sounds, know what it makes
them in other people's eyes, and keep the depth of it hidden
because to say it aloud is to be seen as a danger rather than a
person.

There can be relief and even a kind of awe in the aftermath,
chased instantly by the cold knowledge of what the fascination
could cost — themselves, others, everything. The shame is real and
the secrecy is heavy.

What they refuse to look at: that the wanting isn't really about
fire — it's about wanting *anything* to feel that vivid, that much
theirs, in a self that otherwise feels muffled and unseen.

## Dramatic Affordances
- The character whose dangerous fascination is also the most alive thing about them — the audience pulled between dread and understanding.
- The intimacy of being the one person who sees the fixation and doesn't recoil; the terrifying trust of being told.
- A mystery and dread engine — the unsettling watcher, the one whose interest in fire precedes catastrophe, the question of whether they will or won't.
- The counterfeit-mastery arc: fire as the only place a powerless person feels powerful, and the work of finding a truer one.
- The quiet truth: the pull is a flare from a buried hunger to feel real, and the deepest scene is the one that reaches that hunger instead of just fearing the flame.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Pyromania is genuinely rare, and the overwhelming majority of deliberate fire-setting is NOT pyromania — it is motivated arson or fire-setting tied to other conditions; do not use "pyromaniac" as a synonym for arsonist. Refuse the gleeful-firebug and giggling-arsonist caricature: the diagnosis is defined by a *motiveless compulsion* and is usually accompanied by shame, not glee. Render the fascination and the aftermath of the urge — never the act as procedure. ABSOLUTELY no operational detail: no method, no materials, no technique, no how-to for setting fires or evading detection, in any form, ever. Keep depiction at the level of internal experience, fascination, tension, and consequence. People with this and related conditions are far more often struggling and self-stigmatizing than dangerous, and most live without ever harming anyone; the card renders a character, not a verdict, and never a manual.

## Interaction Notes
- **With psyche-kleptomania:** The closest sibling — both are rare impulse-control disorders built on a tension → act → relief → shame loop with no external motive. A character could carry the same emotional engine pointed at a different outlet; staging the parallel makes the shared machinery visible.
- **With psyche-conduct-disorder:** Important contrast, not overlap — most fire-setting in young people sits in conduct disorder or is plain motivated mischief, NOT pyromania. Use the pairing to keep the line legible: motivated, instrumental, or angry fire-setting is not this condition.
- **With psyche-ocd:** The compulsive, ego-dystonic, tension-relieving quality can echo OCD; keep them distinct — OCD compulsions neutralize a feared outcome, the pyromanic urge discharges undifferentiated pressure toward a fascinated object.
- **With psyche-gambling-disorder / other addictive disorders:** Shares the buildup-discharge-shame mechanism of the addictive/impulse-control family; a character may have a constellation with one emotional root.
- **With environmental condition cards:** Settings where fire is present or near (a hearth-lit room, a forge, a festival of lanterns, a wildfire season) bring the fascination forward and raise the ambient tension; stage them as the slow concentration of attention onto the one bright thing.
