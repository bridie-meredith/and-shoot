# Conduct Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
This is a psyche-profile condition card. It colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Conduct Disorder (CD) is a repetitive, persistent pattern of behavior in childhood or adolescence that violates the basic rights of others or major age-appropriate norms — aggression, destruction, deceit, and serious rule-breaking. Its one-line essence: a young person operating outside the social contract, often because, somewhere upstream, the contract failed them first.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR, Disruptive, Impulse-Control, and Conduct Disorders. ICD-11: Disruptive behaviour or dissocial disorders (Conduct-dissocial disorder, 6C91).
- **Core features:**
  - Aggression toward people and animals (bullying, threats, fights, cruelty).
  - Destruction of property.
  - Deceitfulness or theft (lying to obtain things or avoid obligation; taking what isn't theirs).
  - Serious violations of rules (truancy, running away, defiance of age-appropriate limits).
  - The pattern is persistent and pervasive across settings, causing clinically significant impairment.
  - A specifier — "with limited prosocial emotions" (callous-unemotional traits: lack of remorse, shallow affect, unconcern for performance, lack of empathy) — marks a more severe, more stable subset; most children with CD do NOT carry it.
- **Onset & course:** Two subtypes by onset — childhood-onset (before age 10, more severe, more often male, worse prognosis) and adolescent-onset (more often situational, better prognosis, frequently remits). A subset, especially childhood-onset, progresses toward antisocial personality disorder in adulthood; many do not.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 2–5% of children and adolescents, with a male predominance that narrows in adolescence.
- **Common comorbidities:** ADHD (very high overlap), ODD (frequent precursor), substance use, depression and anxiety, learning disorders, and trauma/maltreatment histories.
- **Differential:** Distinguish from ODD (which lacks rights-violation and serious norm-breaking); from adjustment-related acting-out (time-limited, stressor-linked); from antisocial PD (a diagnosis reserved for adults 18+ — CD is the youth picture); and from delinquency driven purely by a deviant peer context without the broader behavioral pattern.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character reads a scene for hierarchy and opportunity rather
than warmth — who is strong, who is weak, what can be taken, where
the exits are. Threat-detection runs hot; many have learned, often
the hard way, that the world is adversarial, so they enter braced
for it.

Affect can be either over-reactive — quick aggression, a short
road to the fist — or, in the callous-unemotional subset, flat and
unbothered where others would be moved. Attention is present-tense
and consequence-blind: the future discounts steeply, so threats of
later punishment land soft.

Energy is opportunistic, conserved until there's something to be
got. Speech can be charming and instrumental or curt and
challenging; either way it's pointed at getting something. The
condition surfaces most at the boundary — the rule, the property
line, the vulnerable target, the authority figure new enough to
test.

## Duration
Chronic as a pattern; the disorder is defined by persistence across a year or more, not a single incident. Within a scene the behavior is steerable: a credible deterrent, a respected figure, a genuine stake the character cares about, or a real relationship can hold the line; a power vacuum, an easy mark, or a humiliating challenge can break it. The adolescent-onset, situational character is far more shiftable than the childhood-onset, callous-unemotional one. Group context is a major amplifier — what they won't do alone they'll do for an audience.

## Presentation & Tells
- Reads a room for weakness and advantage first; tracks exits and
  who's watching before they track anything else.
- Rules treated as obstacles to route around, not lines to respect
  — lying and taking framed, to themselves, as simply getting by.
- Bravado and dominance displays; testing whether a new authority
  can be pushed, and how far.
- A history that precedes them — suspensions, run-ins, broken
  trust, a reputation that arrives in the room first.
- In the warmer presentation: easy charm deployed instrumentally,
  switched on for a purpose and dropped when it stops working.
- In the callous-unemotional presentation: unsettling calm,
  shallow affect, indifference to others' distress, genuinely
  unmoved by punishment.
- Loyalty that is fierce but narrow — a code, often, just not the
  official one; the people inside the circle are defended hard.
- Cruelty, when it appears, rendered as outcome and reputation —
  never as method or instruction.

## Interiority / POV Voice
The world arrives pre-sorted into predators and prey, and the only
sane question is which you'll be today. Trust is a liability that
has already cost them; softness got punished; so the inner voice
is hard, fast, and transactional — *take it before someone takes
it from you.*

Rules are other people's tools for keeping what they have, and the
character knows this with the certainty of someone who's been on
the wrong end of the rules being enforced. There can be a real
exhilaration in the break — the freedom of someone who has stopped
waiting for permission — and beneath it, in most (not the
callous-unemotional few), a buried ache they won't name: the
wanting-to-belong they decided long ago was a sucker's game.

In the callous-unemotional voice the texture is different —
cooler, more strategic, genuinely unmoved where others would
flinch, the emotional weather simply absent rather than
suppressed. There is no struggle there, only calculation, which is
exactly what makes it rare and what makes it cold.

What nearly all of them refuse to look at directly: the child who
got here, and what was done to that child before the armor went
on.

## Dramatic Affordances
- The kid the system has given up on, who is exactly one adult-who-doesn't-leave away from a different life — the intervention arc.
- The mark who turns out to have read the room better than anyone; competence and danger in the same young body.
- The found-family and the broken-family in tension; the code beneath the chaos.
- The contrast study: situational, redeemable acting-out against the rarer callous-unemotional cold — and the moral question of whether they can be told apart in time.
- The quiet truth: the disorder is frequently a wound's behavior, and the most powerful scene is the one that finally asks what happened *to* them, not just what they did.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Conduct disorder is a youth diagnosis — render a child or adolescent, never an adult (the adult construct is antisocial PD), and never as a finished verdict, because most do not become "criminals" or antisocial adults; the adolescent-onset majority frequently remit. Refuse the bad-seed and budding-serial-killer caricatures: CD is heavily associated with trauma, maltreatment, poverty, and adversity, and the behavior is far more often a survival adaptation than innate evil. Do not equate CD with the callous-unemotional specifier — that's a minority subset; flattening every CD character into a remorseless predator is both false and stigmatizing. Show the upstream wound without using it to excuse harm to others; hold both. For any aggressive, cruel, or destructive content: characterization and outcome ONLY — no method, no how-to, no operational detail of harm to people, animals, or property. The card renders a character, not a sentence.

## Interaction Notes
- **With psyche-oppositional-defiant-disorder:** ODD is a frequent precursor; a character can be staged mid-progression, defiance hardening into rights-violation. Keep the line legible — ODD argues; CD takes and harms.
- **With psyche-adhd:** Very high overlap; impulsivity and poor consequence-weighting feed the conduct problems. Distinguish impulsive trouble (ADHD-driven) from instrumental trouble (CD-driven) within the same character.
- **With psyche-antisocial-pd:** The adult endpoint for a subset — useful for a multi-timeline arc, but most CD does NOT become antisocial PD; never treat the progression as automatic.
- **With psyche-psychopathy:** Relevant only to the callous-unemotional subset; reach for this pairing rarely and carefully, and never as a default reading of CD.
- **With environmental condition cards:** Settings of scarcity, danger, surveillance, or institutional control (a group home, a hard neighborhood, a war zone) bring the adaptive logic of the condition forward and make its survival-value legible.
