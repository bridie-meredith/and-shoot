# Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder (OCPD) — Psyche Profile

## Description
This is a psyche-profile condition card: it colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority (not a location). Obsessive-compulsive personality disorder is a pervasive pattern of preoccupation with orderliness, perfectionism, and control — at the cost of flexibility, openness, and efficiency — in which doing things *correctly* and *thoroughly* becomes a moral imperative that governs the self and, often, everyone nearby. **It is a distinct condition from OCD** (see Fences).

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Personality Disorders → Cluster C (anxious/fearful) → Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder. (Note: the *similarly named* OCD is classified separately under Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders — see psyche-ocd.) ICD-11's dimensional model maps OCPD onto pronounced *anankastia* (perfectionism, rigid control, orderliness) usually with *negative affectivity*.
- **Core features:**
  - Preoccupation with details, rules, lists, order, and schedules to the point that the main purpose is lost.
  - Perfectionism that interferes with task completion (standards too strict to ever be met).
  - Excessive devotion to work and productivity to the exclusion of leisure and relationships.
  - Over-conscientiousness, scrupulousness, and inflexibility about matters of morality, ethics, or values.
  - Inability to discard worn-out or worthless objects; reluctance to delegate unless others submit to their exact way.
  - Miserliness toward self and others; rigidity and stubbornness.
- **Onset & course:** Consolidates by early adulthood; chronic and trait-like. Often ego-syntonic — the person tends to see their standards as correct rather than as a problem — so insight and change come slowly.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 2–8%; one of the more common personality disorders, somewhat more often diagnosed in men.
- **Common comorbidities:** Anxiety disorders, especially generalized anxiety (psyche-generalized-anxiety-disorder); depressive disorders; hoarding disorder (psyche-hoarding-disorder); some overlap with — but distinct from — OCD (psyche-ocd).
- **Differential:** Distinguished from OCD (psyche-ocd) by the absence of true obsessions and compulsions — OCPD is a rigid character style, not intrusive thoughts and ritual neutralizing (see Fences for the full line). Distinguished from healthy conscientiousness, diligence, or culturally valued thoroughness, which OCPD exceeds into impairment and inflexibility.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character perceives the world as a set of things to be ordered, corrected, and done *right*. Attention drives down into detail, often losing the larger purpose to the perfection of its parts. Affect is controlled, earnest, frequently tense; spontaneity and play come hard. Energy pours into work and standards; rest reads as waste and produces guilt rather than relief. The body holds tension — jaw, shoulders, a braced uprightness. Disorder, error, and other people's looser methods register almost physically, as something wrong that must be fixed.

## Duration
Chronic and trait-like across a lifetime, and notably stable within a scene — the rigidity does not swing the way mood-driven states do. What can shift it momentarily is felt loss of control: an enforced deviation from plan, a delegated task done "wrong," a deadline that forces good-enough over perfect, which produce visible distress, irritation, or a doubling-down on control. Sustained safety and relationship can loosen it slowly, over arcs rather than scenes.

## Presentation & Tells
- Lists, schedules, systems, labeled and ordered everything; visible distress when the order is disturbed.
- Re-does others' work, corrects small errors, can't let an imperfection stand.
- Won't delegate, or delegates and then hovers and reclaims — "it's faster if I just do it myself."
- Late or never finished because nothing is ever quite good enough to release.
- Works through evenings, weekends, holidays; treats leisure as indulgence; guilt at rest.
- Rigid on rules and the right way; moralizing, stubborn, hard to talk out of a position.
- Frugal to the point of strain; reluctant to spend on self or others; keeps useless things "in case."
- Earnest and literal; humor and improvisation come hard; precise, qualified speech.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, there is a right way to do everything, and the right way is not a preference — it is *correct*, and doing it any other way is a small dereliction the voice can feel in the body. The narrating voice is occupied with standards: the flaw in the otherwise-finished thing, the corner someone cut, the detail that isn't yet perfect and therefore isn't yet done. Control is the safe medium and its loss is the threat — when a plan bends, when someone insists on their own looser method, when the deadline forces *good enough*, something tightens and the voice wants to fix it, redo it, take it back into its own hands where it will be done properly. There is a moral weather over all of it: not just *wrong* but *irresponsible*, *lazy*, *not careful* — applied to others and, harder, to the self, which is held to a standard it can never quite satisfy, so satisfaction stays perpetually one correction away. Rest doesn't feel like rest; it feels like leaving the work undone, and the guilt is real. What the voice will not look at is the cost — that the perfectionism is finishing nothing, that the people it corrects are pulling away, that the control it grips so hard is buying it a narrower and lonelier life — because to loosen the grip would be to admit that the world isn't actually under management, and that prospect, unnamed, is the most frightening of all.

## Dramatic Affordances
The character reaches for order and correctness where others would let things slide — the one who catches the fatal error, holds the standard, does the thorough unglamorous work no one else will, and can be relied on absolutely when it counts. They are also the one who cannot let the good-enough plan proceed, who reworks the team's output into the ground, who chooses the principle over the person. Drama lives in the gap between the standard and the life it's eating: the family that wanted them present instead of perfect, the project strangled by polish, the relationship that needed flexibility they couldn't give. The arc of learning that *done* can beat *perfect*, that control is partly illusion, that love asks for presence over correctness, is the natural and quietly powerful one. The quiet truth: the rigidity is anxiety's armor — a bid for a world that can't disappoint or surprise — and under the corrections is often a person trying, conscientiously and exhaustingly, to be good.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with OCPD; this card renders a character, not a verdict. **Do not confuse OCPD with OCD (psyche-ocd) — they are different conditions that share a name and little else.** OCD involves unwanted, distressing *intrusive thoughts* (obsessions) and *compulsive rituals* performed to relieve anxiety, which the person typically recognizes as excessive and *does not want* (ego-dystonic). OCPD has no such obsessions or rituals; it is a *character style* of rigid perfectionism and control that the person usually experiences as simply correct (ego-syntonic) — they don't fight it; they defend it. Render OCPD as a personality, not as someone tormented by intrusive thoughts; that's a different card. Refuse the trivializing "I'm so OCD about my desk" shorthand, which misnames both conditions. Avoid flattening OCPD into a cold, loveless martinet; let the conscientiousness, reliability, and genuine effort-to-be-good show alongside the rigidity, and let the anxiety underneath the control be visible. People with OCPD harm no one through the disorder — the cost falls largely on themselves and, in friction, on those close to them.

## Interaction Notes
Stacks with psyche-generalized-anxiety-disorder (the control is anxiety management; the two reinforce each other into chronic tension) and with psyche-hoarding-disorder (the can't-discard trait can deepen into genuine hoarding). It can co-occur with — but should be kept distinct from — psyche-ocd; a character could conceivably have both, and the card should make the two layers legible rather than blur them. Comorbid depression often follows the exhaustion of unmeetable standards. Environmental condition cards matter: high-stakes, detail-critical, rule-bound settings (surgery, audit, engineering, law) reward the trait and hide its cost; loose, improvisational, deadline-soft, or relationship-centered settings expose the friction and the toll. Pair deliberately with a looser, more spontaneous figure for the central relational tension.
