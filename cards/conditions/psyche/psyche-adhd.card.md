# Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) — Psyche Profile

## Description
This is a psyche-profile condition card: it colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority (not a location). ADHD is a neurodevelopmental difference in the brain's attention-regulation and self-regulation systems — not a deficit of attention so much as a difficulty *directing* it on command — producing inconsistent focus, impulsivity, and (in some) restless motor energy.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Neurodevelopmental Disorders → Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder, with three presentations (predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, combined). ICD-11 — 6A05 Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (Neurodevelopmental disorders grouping).
- **Core features:**
  - Inattention: difficulty sustaining attention on non-preferred tasks, careless errors, losing things, distractibility, forgetfulness in daily routines.
  - Hyperactivity: restlessness, fidgeting, difficulty staying seated, a sense of being "driven by a motor."
  - Impulsivity: blurting, interrupting, difficulty waiting, acting before consequence registers.
  - Executive-function difficulty: time-blindness, task initiation and switching trouble, working-memory load problems, weak prioritization.
  - Emotional dysregulation: quick, large emotional responses; low frustration tolerance; rejection-sensitivity (not a formal criterion but commonly reported).
  - "Hyperfocus": paradoxical intense lock-in on highly engaging tasks, hard to interrupt.
- **Onset & course:** Symptoms present in childhood (several before age 12). Chronic and lifelong for most; hyperactivity often softens with age while inattention and executive difficulty persist into adulthood.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 5–7% of children and around 2.5–4% of adults worldwide.
- **Common comorbidities:** Specific learning disorder (psyche-specific-learning-disorder), oppositional defiant disorder (psyche-oppositional-defiant-disorder), anxiety, depression, autism spectrum (psyche-autism-spectrum), tic disorders (psyche-tourettes).
- **Differential:** Distinguished from ordinary distractibility by pervasiveness, early onset, and functional impairment across settings; from anxiety (where attention narrows from worry, not novelty-seeking) and from situational understimulation.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character's attention is a spotlight that swings on its own current, not on command — pulled by novelty, motion, and the most interesting thing in the room, which is rarely the assigned thing. Energy runs fast or stalls completely; transitions between tasks cost more than the tasks themselves. Time is felt as "now" and "not-now," with little in between, so deadlines arrive as ambushes. Small frictions produce large, fast emotion. When something *does* catch — a problem, a person, a story — they can vanish into it for hours, deaf to the room.

## Duration
Chronic and lifelong, but moment-to-moment state is highly variable. Within a single scene, focus can fracture on a noise, a thought, or a passing want, then snap back. Interest, stakes, novelty, movement, deadline-pressure, sleep, and (where it exists) medication all shift the state. A boring meeting and a crisis can produce two different people an hour apart.

## Presentation & Tells
- Interrupts, finishes others' sentences, jumps topics mid-thought.
- Fidgets — bouncing a knee, clicking a pen, fiddling with whatever is in reach, drifting to stand or pace.
- Loses or forgets things; pats pockets, retraces steps, "I had it a second ago."
- Starts three things, finishes none; a wake of half-done tasks.
- Drifts visibly mid-conversation — eyes go elsewhere, comes back with "sorry, what?"
- Blurts the answer, names the thing everyone was tactfully not naming.
- Hyperfocus: doesn't hear their name, startles when touched, surfaces disoriented.
- Self-deprecating shorthand for late, lost, forgotten — practiced apology.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, the world is *loud* — not in volume but in candidates. Every passing thing is a door, and the inner voice keeps walking through them: the assigned task sits in peripheral vision while attention chases the interesting tangent, fully aware it shouldn't, unable to make the want and the should line up. There is a particular shame-loop — *I know how to do this, I've done it before, why can't I just start* — and the start is the wall, a flat cliff with no handhold, while the deadline is an abstraction until it is suddenly a wall of fire. Time doesn't pass evenly; it pools and then drops away. Thoughts arrive in bursts and leave before they're caught, so good ideas evaporate and the narrator grabs at them. When the right thing catches, though, the noise resolves into one clear bright line and the world goes quiet and good, and they ride it past meals, past dark, past the people calling them — which is its own kind of trouble. Underneath runs a long sediment of having been called lazy, careless, too much, not trying — and a private knowledge that they were trying, very hard, at something no one could see.

## Dramatic Affordances
The character reaches for the thing in the room nobody else will touch — the blurted truth, the impulsive kindness, the leap before the plan. They are the one who notices the odd detail because their attention was off-leash, and the one who has lost the critical document. Drama lives in the gap between intention and follow-through: promises meant sincerely and dropped, the partner exhausted by carrying logistics, the brilliance that can't be made to perform on schedule. Hyperfocus is a gift and a trap — the project finished in one inspired night, the relationship neglected during it. The quiet truth lives in the cost of masking: the energy spent looking organized, the apology reflex, the relief of finally being with someone who doesn't read the chaos as not caring.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with ADHD; this card renders a character, not a diagnosis-as-punchline. Refuse the lazy-childish-irresponsible caricature and the "everyone's a little ADHD" minimization — this is a real, impairing, lifelong condition. ADHD is not a failure of will or character, and not the same as low intelligence; many have keen, fast, lateral minds. Do not write it as only a hyperactive boy who can't sit still — inattentive presentations (often quiet, often in girls and women, often missed for decades) are equally real. Avoid medication as moral verdict in either direction. Hyperfocus is not "so they CAN focus, they just don't want to." Let the character be competent and struggling at once.

## Interaction Notes
Stacks readily with psyche-specific-learning-disorder (shared executive load compounds school/work shame) and with psyche-autism-spectrum (the "AuDHD" profile — competing needs for novelty and sameness pull against each other). With anxiety or depression, the executive paralysis deepens and self-blame sharpens. With psyche-oppositional-defiant-disorder the impulsivity reads as defiance. Environmental condition cards matter: high-stimulation, time-pressured, or rigidly procedural settings amplify the tells; quiet, interest-rich, externally-scaffolded settings can make the same character look effortlessly capable.
