# Schizoid Personality Disorder — Psyche Profile

## Description
A psyche-profile condition card that colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority — not a location. Schizoid Personality Disorder (SzPD) is a pervasive pattern of detachment from social relationships paired with a restricted range of emotional expression. The essence in one line: the character is genuinely content alone, and the company of others reads less as comfort than as demand.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Personality Disorders, Cluster A (the "odd/eccentric" cluster). ICD-11 dimensionalizes personality disorder (6D10) with a prominent **Detachment** trait domain (social and emotional withdrawal); the discrete category was folded into the trait-and-severity model.
- **Core features:**
  - Neither desires nor enjoys close relationships, including family.
  - Almost always chooses solitary activities.
  - Little or no interest in sexual experiences with another person.
  - Takes pleasure in few activities; affect is flat or muted rather than distressed.
  - Lacks close confidants other than first-degree relatives, and does not seem to miss them.
  - Appears indifferent to praise or criticism alike.
  - Emotional coldness, detachment, or flattened affect to outside observers.
- **Onset & course:** Recognizable by early adulthood, with childhood antecedents of solitariness. Chronic and stable rather than episodic; rarely seeks treatment because the pattern is not experienced as a problem by the person living it.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 1–5% in community estimates; figures are uncertain because affected people seldom present for care.
- **Common comorbidities:** Other Cluster A patterns (schizotypal, paranoid); depressive conditions; sometimes overlaps with autistic-spectrum social patterns and avoidant traits, which must be differentiated.
- **Differential:** Differs from **psyche-avoidant-pd** — the avoidant person *wants* closeness but fears rejection; the schizoid person is indifferent to it. Differs from psyche-autism-spectrum, where social difficulty is developmental and often paired with a *desire* for connection plus difficulty achieving it, not a lack of desire. Differs from psyche-schizotypal-pd by the absence of cognitive-perceptual oddities and magical thinking. Differs from psyche-depression by the *enduring, ego-syntonic* nature of the withdrawal — not a loss of pleasure but a baseline.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character moves through social space with low reactivity. Faces register as information, not invitation. Noise, crowds, and demands for warmth read as static — tolerated, navigated, exited. Affect is even; the voice carries little melodic variation. They are rarely bored alone and rarely energized by company. Touch and intimacy are neutral-to-unwanted rather than charged. Their attention runs inward — to ideas, systems, solitary craft — and the social foreground that pulls other characters simply does not pull them.

## Duration
Chronic, stable, ego-syntonic. Within a scene the detachment holds steady; it does not "flare." What shifts mid-scene is the *demand level* — sustained pressure to perform warmth (a wedding, a confrontation, an appeal) increases visible strain and withdrawal, not emotional opening. They do not warm up over the course of an evening the way others do.

## Presentation & Tells
- Brief, factual answers; lets silences sit without discomfort.
- Declines invitations without apparent regret or need to justify.
- Indifferent to gossip, status moves, and bids for closeness.
- Pursues solitary, absorbing work — and is good at it, unbothered by isolation.
- Flat or minimal facial expression; little reciprocal smiling.
- Unmoved by praise or insult to a degree others find unnerving.
- Physically self-contained — minimal gesturing, low proximity-seeking.
- Polite but unreachable; people describe them as "a wall" or "somewhere else."

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, there is no ache. This is the crucial thing the card must protect: the schizoid character is not lonely in the way an outside observer assumes. The inner world is full — of thought, of pattern, of the steady pleasure of a solitary task done well — and the social demand that others experience as nourishment registers as *expenditure*. The inner voice does not narrate longing; it narrates observation at a slight remove, as though watching the warm-blooded business of other people through glass. Emotion is present but quiet, like weather in another country. They notice they are expected to feel more, to want more, to perform connection — and they comply when they must, the way one performs any social form, with no inner correlate. What they refuse to look at, if anything, is the question of whether the glass was always there or whether it was built; that inquiry has no foothold because the present arrangement is not painful. The texture of their thought is cool, precise, unhurried, and — this matters — frequently at peace. The drama is not their suffering. It is the suffering of those who love them and cannot get in.

## Dramatic Affordances
This character reaches for solitude where others reach for one another — and that inversion makes them the still point that every relationship in the scene bends around. They open scenes of the unreachable parent, the partner who cannot be reached, the brilliant solitary whose competence is inseparable from their distance. They are the one person unmoved by the social currents that move everyone else, which makes them either an anchor or a void depending on who needs them. The drama lives in the people orbiting them — and in the rare, costly moment the character chooses, deliberately and without native impulse, to do a warm thing for someone, because they decided to, not because they felt it. The quiet truth lives in showing that contentment-in-solitude is real and not a wound to be healed.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with SzPD; this card renders a character, not a verdict. The single biggest error is writing the schizoid character as secretly broken and merely awaiting the right person to "thaw" them — the romance trope that pathologizes a valid way of being. Detachment here is not repression, not trauma-frozen grief, not a cry for help; it is a temperament. Do not conflate it with autism, depression, or avoidant fear-of-rejection — those are distinct and have *desire* somewhere in them. Do not write coldness as villainy: indifference is not cruelty. Equally, do not romanticize the isolation as enviable freedom while erasing the genuine cost their distance imposes on others. The honest version holds both — their peace is real, and the people who love them grieve a person who is right there.

## Interaction Notes
- With **psyche-paranoid-pd**: detachment plus distrust reads as a sealed fortress; the paranoid edge supplies a reason for distance that the pure schizoid does not need.
- With **psyche-schizotypal-pd**: shared Cluster A withdrawal, but schizotypal adds odd beliefs and perceptual quirks the pure schizoid lacks — distinguish the flat-and-content register from the eccentric one.
- With **psyche-avoidant-pd**: superficially similar surface, opposite interior — pairing them in a cast lets the contrast (indifference vs. longing-behind-fear) do thematic work.
- With **psyche-major-depressive-disorder**: a depressive episode *over* a schizoid baseline is a real and dangerous overlap — anhedonia can mask deepening depression precisely because withdrawal looks normal for this character; useful, sober dramatic ground.
- With environmental condition cards (high-demand social settings, courts, communal living): the world's pressure to perform belonging supplies external friction without requiring the character to change inside.
