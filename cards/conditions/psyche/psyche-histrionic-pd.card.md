# Histrionic Personality Disorder (HPD) — Psyche Profile

## Description
This is a psyche-profile condition card: it colors a *character's* behavior, perception, and interiority (not a location). Histrionic personality disorder is a pervasive pattern of excessive emotionality and attention-seeking — a person whose sense of being real and valued depends on being *seen*, and who reaches for warmth, drama, and approval the way someone underwater reaches for air.

## Clinical Profile
- **Taxonomy:** DSM-5-TR — Personality Disorders → Cluster B (dramatic/emotional/erratic) → Histrionic Personality Disorder. ICD-11 has dropped discrete personality-disorder categories in favor of a dimensional model (degree of severity plus trait domains); the histrionic picture maps largely onto the *disinhibition* and *negative affectivity* domains with marked attention-need.
- **Core features:**
  - Discomfort when not the center of attention; works to regain the spotlight.
  - Emotional expression that is rapid, shifting, theatrical, and shallow — large feeling, quickly changed.
  - Interaction often colored by inappropriate seductiveness or provocativeness, across contexts where it doesn't fit.
  - Uses physical appearance to draw attention; impressionistic, vague speech short on detail.
  - Suggestibility — easily influenced by others or by circumstance; relationships felt as more intimate than they are.
- **Onset & course:** Personality patterns consolidate by late adolescence/early adulthood. Chronic and trait-like, though intensity can soften with age, maturation, or therapy.
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 1–2% in community samples; historically diagnosed more in women, though this partly reflects gendered clinical bias.
- **Common comorbidities:** Other Cluster B disorders, especially narcissistic (psyche-narcissistic-pd) and borderline (psyche-borderline-pd); dependent traits (psyche-dependent-pd); depressive disorders; somatic-symptom presentations.
- **Differential:** Distinguished from narcissistic PD (where the bid is for admiration of *superiority*, not warmth/attention as such) and from borderline PD (where the core is identity instability and abandonment terror, with self-harm; HPD's emotionality is more performative and less self-destructive). Ordinary expressiveness or extraversion is not a disorder absent impairment.

## Sensory / Behavioral Impact
The character is exquisitely tuned to the room's attention — who is watching, who has drifted, where the warmth is — and orients toward it like a plant to light. Affect runs hot and fast and visible: feelings arrive at full volume and change before they've finished. Energy lifts in company and flattens, sometimes alarmingly, when alone or ignored. Speech is vivid, sweeping, and light on specifics. The body participates fully — gesture, touch, posture all dialed up to be read across a room.

## Duration
Chronic and trait-like across a lifetime, but moment-to-moment the state swings with the audience. Within a single scene, a withdrawal of attention can collapse a glowing mood into hurt or sudden flatness, and a fresh pair of eyes can restore it just as fast. Crowds, novelty, and admiration animate; being overlooked deflates.

## Presentation & Tells
- Enters a room as if entering a stage — adjusts to be seen, finds the light, the center.
- Stories told in bold strokes and superlatives ("the most amazing," "absolutely devastating"), thin on concrete detail.
- Warmth that arrives fast and intense — "I feel like I've known you forever" on a first meeting.
- Flirtatious or charming register that doesn't quite read the context it's in.
- Emotions large and quick — tears, delight, wounded silence — and noticeably brief.
- Visible deflation, restlessness, or a fresh bid when attention moves elsewhere.
- Appearance used expressively; reactions calibrated, half-consciously, to who's watching.

## Interiority / POV Voice
From inside, attention is oxygen. When eyes are on them the world is warm and bright and *true* — they feel real, lovable, alive — and when the eyes leave, something dims that they can't quite name as panic but that moves like it. The narrating voice runs in feeling, not fact: it remembers the *texture* of an evening — how golden it was, how everyone adored them — and not who said what or what was actually decided. Connections register as deeper and faster than they are; a kind stranger becomes, in the space of an hour, someone they'd trust with anything. There is a perpetual, mostly unexamined performing — not lying, exactly, but heightening, rounding everything up to be more vivid — and underneath it a thin cold thread of *am I too much, did they see I needed that*, quickly papered over by the next bright thing. Being alone is the hardest weather; in an empty room the self goes faint, and the voice reaches outward for someone to be reflected in. What it refuses to look at is the suspicion that the warmth it gives and gets is keyed to applause — that it has never quite found out who it is when no one is watching.

## Dramatic Affordances
The character reaches for the room — they will warm a frozen gathering, charm the gatekeeper, turn a dull scene incandescent, and say the generous extravagant thing no guarded person would. They are the one who makes others feel briefly fascinating, and the one whose feeling-weather can hijack a scene from its purpose. Drama lives in the gap between the dazzle and the want underneath it: the partner who can never give enough attention, the friendships that ignite and cool, the moment the performance fails and the real, frightened need shows through. The quiet truth lives in the loneliness of being loved for the show — and in the genuine generosity and emotional courage that the same wiring produces, the willingness to feel out loud in a room of people holding back.

## Fences — Responsible Use
Real people live with HPD; this card renders a character, not a verdict. Refuse the misogynist shorthand that flattens it into "drama queen," "attention whore," or manipulative-by-nature — the attention-seeking is a real, painful regulation strategy, not a scheme, and the seductiveness is rarely calculated cruelty. Do not treat it as inherently female or as a synonym for vanity. Avoid the "fake / shallow person" verdict; the feelings are genuinely felt even when they're large and brief. People with HPD are far more often the ones hurt — used, dismissed, taken less seriously than they deserve — than predators. Let the warmth be real warmth and the need be a real need, not a con.

## Interaction Notes
Stacks with psyche-narcissistic-pd (attention plus admiration-hunger — a person who must be both adored and central) and with psyche-borderline-pd (the theatricality fuses with abandonment terror, raising the stakes of every withdrawal). With psyche-dependent-pd the suggestibility deepens into a need to be carried. Comorbid depression hits hardest in the unwitnessed hours. Environmental condition cards shape the tells sharply: a crowded, social, high-warmth setting brings out the best charm; an austere, indifferent, or solitary setting strands the character and surfaces the underlying ache.
