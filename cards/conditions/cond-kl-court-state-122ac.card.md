# KL Court State — 122 AC (Fire & Blood Canonical)

## Description

The political and personnel state of King's Landing and the Targaryen court at story open (122 AC, late Viserys I reign). All information is drawn from Fire & Blood canon. Where F&B and HOTD-show diverge, F&B wins. This card is the lore-anchor for the political ambient Taylor is attempting to read through Gold Morning pattern-recognition. Characters, their locations, their factional positions, and their relationships to Taylor's objective (preventing Lucerys Velaryon's death) are all fixed here.

## Sensory Impact

Court state is political ambient, not sensory condition. Its sensory texture is felt through: which household liveries appear in which districts, the quality of tension in how smallfolk discuss the royal family, the frequency of Watch deployment pattern changes near the Sept and the Red Keep, the volume of noble-quarter traffic.

## Duration

Active as political ambient throughout the project. The court state shifts across the book's arc (Viserys continues to decline; factional maneuvering intensifies) but the story closes before the Dance ignites. This card describes the 122 AC open-state; drift is acknowledged but not enumerated here.

## The King and His Decline

**Viserys I Targaryen:** On the throne, declining. The nature of his decline (progressive illness, increasing isolation from court business, reliance on Alicent's management of his environment) means Alicent's faction has effective control of access to the king. Viserys's public declarations of Rhaenyra's succession stand; his practical ability to enforce them narrows as his health deteriorates. He does not appear frequently in street-level or Flea Bottom contexts; he is a rumor, a presence felt through the behavior of those around him.

**Small Council composition:** Otto Hightower was dismissed as Hand of the King in 120 AC. The current Hand is not yet the critical fact — the critical fact is that Otto is operating advisory influence from outside the formal position. He retains network access through Alicent and through his relationships with council members. His absence from the formal record is the operational condition Taylor may misread: Otto is *more* dangerous without the Hand's title than with it, because his actions are less documented and his reach is harder to trace.

## The Factions at 122 AC

**The Green faction (Alicent's faction):**
- **Alicent Hightower:** Queen; consolidated court control; manages Viserys's household and his access to the council. The practical power of the court runs through her. Her public position: loyal to Viserys, invested in his comfort and his heirs' futures. Her factional position: positioning Aegon II for succession.
- **Otto Hightower:** Off the Small Council since 120 AC; operating as external advisor through Alicent. The intelligence architect. His network in KL remains active — he built it over years as Hand and does not abandon it. Access to Taylor through this network becomes the first-trade mechanism in act one.
- **Aegon II Targaryen:** Seventeen at 122 AC (born 107 AC). Not yet a disciplined political operator; the throne-aspiration is established in Green faction thinking but Aegon himself is not active in the maneuvering. A teenager with Targaryen privilege and an undisciplined temperament. Dragon rider (Sunfyre).
- **Aemond Targaryen:** Sixteen at 122 AC (born 106 AC). Claimed Vhagar after Laena Velaryon's death (120 AC). Disciplined, driven, bearing the loss of his eye from the 120 AC incident with Lucerys. The most significant Green military asset; also the most dangerous personality. The convergence line with Taylor's prevention-logic runs through Aemond.
- **Helaena Targaryen:** Approximately thirteen at 122 AC (born 109 AC). The Green faction's softest figure — not politically active, married to Aegon II, carrying the weight of what canonically awaits her in the Dance. Relevant to this story as a peripheral figure; not a primary interlocutor for Taylor.

**The Black faction (Rhaenyra's faction):**
- **Rhaenyra Targaryen:** Named heir; based at Dragonstone with Daemon. Not physically present in KL at story open. Her influence in the city operates through Velaryon household representatives and through the court figures who have not committed to Green.
- **Daemon Targaryen:** In Pentos at 122 AC (canonical F&B: Daemon spent time in Pentos before returning to Rhaenyra). Volatile; a military asset the Black faction cannot fully direct. His absence from KL is a strategic simplification for Taylor's opening quarter.
- **Lucerys Velaryon:** Approximately eight at 122 AC (born 114 AC). One of Rhaenyra's sons by Laenor Velaryon (with the paternity question that is part of the political charge). Based primarily at Dragonstone. Not regularly present in KL. His periodic visits to KL (court appearances, family obligations) are the windows Taylor's objective depends on. Dragon rider (Arrax). Taylor's stated objective — "if Lucerys lives, the war does not start" — is both analytically correct and insufficient as a prevention strategy (the structural conditions that produce the Dance do not require Lucerys's death as their only ignition point).
- **Jacaerys (Jace) Velaryon:** Approximately ten at 122 AC (born 112 AC). Lucerys's older brother. Not Taylor's primary focus but part of the Rhaenyra-heir picture.
- **Rhaenys Targaryen ("the Queen Who Never Was"):** Lady Corlys's wife; Meleys rider; not a court-resident figure but available as a canon personality. Not directly relevant to Taylor's operational position at 122 AC but part of the Black faction's background.

## The Key Structural Fact for Taylor's Objective

Lucerys Velaryon's canonical death (in 129 AC, during the Dance) is caused by Aemond Targaryen's Vhagar. The deaths are: Aemond on Vhagar kills Lucerys and Arrax over Shipbreaker Bay when the killing is, by Aemond's own account, not his intent. The Dance does not require Lucerys's death as its sole ignition — the Dance begins with other events — but Lucerys's death is a major early escalation point.

Taylor's prevention-logic ("if Lucerys lives, the war does not start") is her cold-utilitarian framing. It is partially correct about cause-and-effect. It is wrong about sufficiency — Lucerys's survival does not prevent the structural conditions that make the Dance inevitable. Taylor cannot see this from Flea Bottom in 122 AC, seven years before the event. The gap between her prevention-logic and the structural reality is the irony engine.

**For narrative use:** Any scene showing Taylor's understanding of the 122 AC court state should route through her pattern-recognition precog (reads structures, not specifics), her street-level insect-network observation, and what Otto Hightower's network has told her (filtered through Otto's agenda). She does not have direct access to court-level intelligence. She has inference, pattern-recognition, and sponsored access.
