# Maester Network Behavior — Citadel-to-Lord Soft Surveillance at 84–99 AC

## Description

How the Citadel-to-lord maester network operates as a soft surveillance and information system in the Riverlands at 84–99 AC. Covers: the placement function, ravens as the fast channel, literacy and anomaly detection, how anomalous findings travel upward, the Citadel's institutional peak under Jaehaerys's reign, and what Septon Barth's death (~99 AC) means for the network's future behavior.

This card governs how Taylor's anomalous characteristics (extraordinary literacy, unusual cognition, the ignition event) can travel through the maester system. It is the mechanism by which her anomaly can reach ears she did not intend to reach.

## Sensory Impact

The maester network has no ambient sensory expression. Its presence is felt as potential: the awareness that anything a maester observes is entered into a system that extends from the nearest castle to Oldtown. The chain is invisible unless you know it is there. Taylor knows it is there.

## Duration

Persistent across the project window. The network is stable throughout 84–101 AC. The only significant change: Septon Barth's death (~99 AC) reduces the collaborative efficiency between the maester network and the Faith network, which had been operating unusually well under his influence.

## The Placement Function

Every lord of any consequence has a maester. The Citadel places them — a lord requests a maester, the Archmaester assigns one based on the lord's needs (medicine, accounting, raven-keeping, scholarly counsel) and the available candidates. The maester swears his primary loyalty to the Citadel and to the lord he serves, in that order, in theory; in practice, a maester who has served a lord for decades develops personal loyalty to the house. The placement function gives the Citadel an observer in every significant castle in Westeros.

**Relevance for Taylor's project:** House Ryger of Willow Wood is a minor lord. Minor lords may or may not have their own dedicated maester — if they do, it is often a junior or less distinguished graduate. More likely for the Ryger scale: they share access to a maester with other minor Tully bannermen, or they use a traveling maester for episodic consultations rather than having a resident one. The lord's administrative apparatus (Aldric Pryor's level) has Citadel access but not necessarily a maester in residence. A traveling maester arriving in Fairstead for census-adjacent purposes is a significant event, not a routine presence.

## Ravens as the Fast Channel

Ravens are the Westerosi information network's fast channel. They carry messages between castles that have raven rookeries. The raven system does not extend to market towns — Fairstead does not have a raven rookery. Ravens fly to and from the lord's hall, to Riverrun, to King's Landing, and between the noble houses.

**Implications for Taylor:**
- Information about Taylor that travels through the raven network travels through the lord's hall first. Pryor controls what enters the raven network from Ryger's end. He decides when something is worth writing a raven for.
- Information that reaches Riverrun or King's Landing via raven has traveled faster than any road message. But it still originates with the lord's administrative apparatus.
- The maester at Riverrun (or wherever the nearest well-staffed castle maester is) processes incoming raven traffic. Anomalous intelligence from minor lords travels upward through the Great House network.

## Literacy Detection and Anomaly Flagging

Maesters are trained to detect anomalies in the population they encounter. One of the most reliable anomaly signals is extraordinary literacy in a smallfolk child.

**What triggers a maester's attention:**
- Reading at adult speed or comprehension when the child's stated education (sept-taught) should not produce that level
- Handwriting that is practiced and consistent rather than laborious
- Accounts-keeping ability that indicates regular arithmetic practice
- Valyrian script recognition (this is a significant flag — Valyrian literacy in a smallfolk child has no plausible innocent explanation)
- The quality of questions asked — a child who asks questions that imply adult-level understanding of the subject matter

**Taylor's specific exposure:** She reads at adult speed. She can do accounts-level arithmetic. Her handwriting is practiced. She is actively suppressing her Valyrian script recognition (she does not let herself slip into reading the Valyrian marginal notes aloud). The cover (father is a dyer-with-accounts, septon supplemented) is internally coherent but produces a level of literacy significantly beyond what that cover should generate. A maester spending more than ten minutes with her on any administrative task will notice.

**What happens when a maester notices:**
The maester notes the anomaly in his administrative record. If the anomaly is significant enough, he writes a letter to Oldtown or to the nearest Archmaester contact, framing it as an interesting observed case. The Citadel does not take operational action based on such letters in routine cases; the letters enter an archival system. However, if multiple maesters from different locations write about similar anomalies associated with the same name or description, the pattern becomes visible to someone at Oldtown reviewing the archive.

More immediately: the maester mentions the anomaly to the lord he is serving (or, in the case of a traveling maester, to the lord who contracted his services). A lord who hears from his maester that "the Ashford girl reads unusually well for her stated education" adds that to Pryor's existing file. The two tracks (Pryor's administrative track and the maester's observation track) converge at the lord level.

## How Anomalous Findings Travel Upward

**Step 1 (local observation):** A maester encounters Taylor in Fairstead — most likely during an official visit associated with Pryor's administrative duties. He observes her literacy and possibly her other anomalous characteristics.

**Step 2 (lord's report):** The maester reports to the lord whose administrative mission he is supporting. Pryor receives this as confirmation of his existing file.

**Step 3 (Citadel archival record):** The maester writes a letter to Oldtown flagging the anomaly as a case of interest. This letter travels by road and arrives at Oldtown in weeks. It enters the archive.

**Step 4 (pattern detection at Oldtown, if applicable):** If Taylor's anomalies are significant enough to generate multiple letters from multiple maesters who encounter her over the years — or if the Ignition event generates specific inquiry — Oldtown may respond with a letter of guidance to the relevant lord's maester or to the circuit maester in the Stoney Sept area. This is a slow process. The Citadel does not dispatch investigators; it writes letters.

**Step 5 (Septon Barth's cooperative-governance legacy):** During Jaehaerys's reign, the Citadel and the Faith maintain an unusual degree of collaborative information-sharing — Barth's legacy. A maester who notices Taylor might, in this era, mention his observation to the local circuit septon during a shared administrative meeting. This is the overlap point where Rowan's theological track and the maester's analytical track might briefly intersect.

## The Citadel at Institutional Peak (84–99 AC)

The Citadel is at its highest institutional coherence and prestige in Jaehaerys I's reign. Septon Barth himself was a maester before he became a septon — his career embodies the Citadel-Faith bridge. His period as Hand of the King (the longest in Jaehaerys's reign) institutionalized Citadel cooperation with crown governance.

What this means concretely:
- Lord's maesters are well-resourced and their counsel is taken seriously
- The Citadel's archival systems are functioning efficiently
- Letters that reach Oldtown are actually processed, not lost in an overwhelmed archive
- The collaborative information-sharing with the Faith is at its peak

As the series progresses toward Barth's death (~99 AC), this peak begins to erode. By the Council horizon, the Citadel is still functional but the Barth-era cooperative grace is gone. Maesters and the Faith operate more independently. This means the information convergence (maester observation + Faith observation triangulating on Taylor) is more likely to occur early in the series than late — which has implications for when Taylor's anomalous characteristics can be triangulated.

## The Traveling Maester Walk-On

The character `westerosi-traveling-maester` (from the cast candidates) represents the specific instance of a maester appearing in Fairstead for administrative purposes. This is not a resident maester — Ryger is too minor for that — but a maester contracted for specific administrative tasks (census-adjacent record-keeping, literacy assessments, the occasional medical need).

**Specific scene function:** The traveling maester arrives in Fairstead approximately at the ignition aftermath (86–87 AC), when Pryor has decided that the anomaly warrants a more formal assessment. The maester's job is to assess the Ashford household's administrative compliance and to observe the Ashford girl specifically. He will notice her literacy within minutes of a sustained interaction. His report to Pryor and his letter to the Citadel constitute Taylor's first entry in the Citadel's archival system.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-westerosi-customary-authority-jaehaerys`:** The maester's administrative mode is the context where literacy detection happens. The behavioral rules (eyes down, short answers) that Taylor must follow in administrative contexts are also the context where slipping up in those rules by demonstrating too much competence is most damaging.

**With `cond-suppression-policy-progression`:** The maester's observations feed into Pryor's file at Stage 1–2. The convergence of Pryor's administrative file and the maester's anomaly report is one of the triggers that can move Pryor from Stage 2 to Stage 3.

**With `cond-faith-of-seven-jaehaerys`:** The Citadel-Faith collaborative model means maester and Faith information tracks can intersect. This intersection is more likely in the Barth-era (84–99 AC) than after. Rowan's theological assessment and the maester's literacy assessment are never formally compared — but the same lord receives both reports.

**For auditor use:** The maester network does not take direct operational action against Taylor. It generates records and reports. If a scene implies a maester actively directing suppression of Taylor (rather than reporting upward), that is a role violation — the maester's role is observation and counsel, not enforcement.
