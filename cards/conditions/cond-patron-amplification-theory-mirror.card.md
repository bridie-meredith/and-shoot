# Patron Amplification-Theory Mirror — The Wrong Theory of Taylor (Binding)

## Description

The patron's theory that dragon-proximity amplifies Taylor's flicker. The theory is wrong. This card records: the theory's content, the evidence the patron holds for it, why the evidence is misleading, how the patron uses the theory operationally, and the structural seam where the theory's wrongness produces the collision. A binding constraint at the end of this card prohibits any agent from writing a scene where the patron explicitly acknowledges the wrong-theory during the alignment phase.

**Source authority**: `active-project/staff/showrunner/world-notes.md` — all rules carried from `[1b]` are binding.

## Sensory Impact

The amplification theory has no direct sensory expression. Its presence is felt in the way the patron arranges situations — the consultation sessions near the Dragonpit, the specific proximity experiments, the patron's manner of interpreting Taylor's distress. For Taylor, the theory's effects are felt as the growing dependency of the relationship on something she cannot correct: the patron is building a strategic framework on top of a wrong reading of her, and she does not have the access or the vocabulary to fully disabuse them.

## Duration

Active from the establishment of the patron-Taylor relationship through the mid-structure collision. The theory becomes a collision point when the bad act's consequences make the wrong-theory impossible to ignore. Before the collision, the theory operates invisibly — it is the patron's working assumption, not a stated proposition.

## The Theory

**What the patron believes**: Dragon-proximity amplifies Taylor's flicker. When Taylor is near a dragon, the flicker fires more frequently, delivers more accurate information, and produces readings that are more actionable for strategic purposes.

**The theory's operational implication**: The patron believes that Taylor near a dragon is a better intelligence asset than Taylor at a distance. This belief drives the patron to arrange for Taylor to be near dragons when the patron wants the most reliable information — particularly for high-stakes strategic questions (e.g., which dragon-rider is most likely to produce the catastrophic outcome).

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "The patron's interest in Taylor is built on a MISTAKEN theory — they believe dragon-proximity amplifies Taylor's flicker. This theory is wrong; the flicker remains involuntary and unreliable, and near a dragon it costs Taylor (Gold-Morning trauma trigger; interpretive cost, not informational gain). The patron misreads Taylor's reaction-intensity as proof of their amplification theory."

## The Evidence the Patron Has

The patron's evidence for the theory is real — the observations that produced the theory are accurate observations. The interpretive error is in how those observations are explained:

**Observable 1 — Taylor goes still near dragons.** True. Near a dragon, Taylor's body registers the Gold Morning trauma trigger — dragons-at-proximity are Scion-scale threat-pattern activators in her nervous system. She goes still, her attention arrests, her sensory processing narrows to the threat-source. The patron reads this as the flicker firing and orienting.

**Observable 2 — Taylor speaks in fragments near dragons.** Sometimes true. The near-dragon flicker does fire with greater intensity, producing more visible behavioral expression — including fragments spoken aloud. The patron records these fragments and notes their frequency near dragons versus in other settings.

**Observable 3 — Taylor's near-dragon fragments sometimes prove accurate.** Partially true. The flicker fires more frequently near dragons (more noise), and some of those fires are accurate. The accuracy rate is not higher; the absolute number of accurate fragments is higher because the total number of fragments is higher. The patron has not controlled for this distinction — the patron is counting accurate instances, not measuring accuracy rate.

**Observable 4 — Taylor's distress near dragons is visible and consistent.** True. The Gold Morning trauma response is real and consistent. The patron reads this as evidence that something significant is happening (the flicker is working harder). The distress is the signal; the patron misidentifies what it signals.

## Why the Theory Is Wrong

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "Near dragons, it fires with greater violence (more cost, more fragmentation, more interpretive noise) because of the Gold Morning trauma trigger — dragons-at-proximity are Scion-scale threat-pattern activators in Taylor's nervous system. The increased output is increased noise, not increased signal. The patron cannot distinguish this."

The theory is wrong because the patron has no mechanism to distinguish increased-noise from increased-signal. From the patron's observational position, both produce the same surface signature: more fragments, more distress, more behavioral tell. The patron's error is epistemic — they are measuring output quantity when they need to be measuring output quality, and they have no calibration tool for output quality because they cannot independently verify the flicker's accuracy in real time.

**The compounding factor**: Taylor's interpretive framework (Gold Morning pattern-recognition) is most active near dragons because dragons are the strongest activators of her threat-pattern system. This means her near-dragon fragments are not only more frequent; they are more interpretively loaded — they arrive already shaped by the Gold Morning lens, already mapped onto the Scion-catastrophe template. The patron observes this as "more actionable." It is more loaded, not more accurate.

## How the Patron Uses the Wrong Theory

**Operationally**: The patron arranges consultation sessions at or near the Dragonpit for high-stakes questions. The patron's protocol (informal, never explicitly stated) is: for ordinary intelligence needs, Taylor in any accessible setting; for questions about dragon-riders and succession-adjacent military-strategic matters, Taylor in proximity to the dragons.

**The bad act's staging**: The patron arranges for Taylor to be in proximity to the specific dragon and its rider at the Dragonpit because the patron believes this proximity will produce Taylor's most reliable reading of the strategic threat. The patron accepts Taylor's near-dragon flicker report (the burning-city glimpse, the rider's silhouette) as the highest-quality intelligence available because it was produced in the patron's preferred "amplified" conditions. This acceptance is the patron's authorization of the bad act.

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "They build the bad act on this foundation: if Taylor's flicker near the Dragonpit shows her the dragon above a burning city, the patron accepts this as a reliable sighting."

## The Structural Seam

The patron's wrong theory is the structural seam where the collision lives.

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "The patron's wrong-theory-of-Taylor is where the collision lives. When Taylor's prevention action (the kill) produces the opposite of prevention, and the patron realizes that their amplification-theory was the framework through which they authorized the bad act, the collision is not betrayal — it is the mutual recognition that their shared framework was wrong."

**Betrayal vs. collision**: The patron's collision with Taylor is not a betrayal because the patron was not working against Taylor. The patron's interests and Taylor's interests were genuinely aligned during the period of alignment — both wanted to prevent the catastrophic dragon-rider outcome. The collision is the mutual recognition that the prevention-logic was built on wrong premises. The patron cannot accept this recognition without confronting the fact that they authorized Taylor's kill based on wrong intelligence. Taylor cannot accept this recognition without confronting the fact that the patron's wrong theory enabled her action. Neither party is clean.

*From world-notes.md (advisory, from taste-judge):* "False-ally's betrayal must land as a mid-structure compounding factor, not as the climactic recognition. Climax belongs to the protagonist-as-antagonist beat."

The collision lands mid-structure. The climax is Taylor's recognition that she is her own antagonist — that the outcome she produced is the one she was trying to prevent, and that the cause-chain leads through her own choices, made in good faith, using a framework that was wrong for this world.

## Binding Constraint

*From world-notes.md (binding):* "BINDING: no agent writes a scene where the patron explicitly acknowledges wrong-theory during alignment phase."

**Operational scope of this constraint**: The alignment phase runs from the patron-Taylor relationship's establishment through the collision point (the point in mid-structure when the bad act's consequences make the wrong theory impossible to ignore). During this entire phase, the patron does not explicitly acknowledge that the amplification theory is wrong, question its premises, or express doubt about its validity.

**Why this is binding**: The wrong theory is invisible to the patron while it is operating. If the patron acknowledged its wrongness during the alignment phase, the theory would not be operationally binding — it would be a known-uncertainty rather than an invisible structural assumption. The patron must genuinely believe the theory for the collision to land as collision rather than as calculated betrayal.

**What is permitted**: The patron may express uncertainty about specific readings. The patron may note that a given fragment was unclear or that the near-dragon readings were difficult to interpret. These are object-level observations about specific intelligence instances, not meta-level acknowledgment that the amplification theory itself is wrong. Object-level uncertainty is realistic and permitted; meta-level wrong-theory acknowledgment before the collision is a violation.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-flicker-discipline-mirror`:** That card describes the correct account of the flicker's behavior near dragons (increased noise, not increased signal). This card describes the patron's incorrect account and how the patron arrived at it.

**With `cond-smallfolk-court-access-mirror`:** The patron's use of the wrong theory creates the access chain to the Dragonpit interior. The umbrella mechanism is deployed in service of the amplification-theory protocol. Both cards must be loaded for the Dragonpit interior scenes.

**With the patron's persona card**: The patron's voice and behavior in scenes must be consistent with the theory being an unexamined working assumption, not a stated proposition. The patron does not say "I believe dragon-proximity amplifies your flicker" directly; they simply arrange things as if this is true. The theory is structural, not articulated.

**For auditor use:** Any scene where the patron explicitly names the amplification theory as a belief proposition and affirms it is a mild redundancy but not a violation. Any scene where the patron explicitly names it and then questions it during the alignment phase is a binding constraint violation. Any scene where the patron's strategic behavior is inconsistent with holding this theory (e.g., using Taylor far from dragons for the high-stakes dragon-rider assessment) is a violation.
