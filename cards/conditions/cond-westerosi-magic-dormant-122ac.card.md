# Westerosi Magic — Dormant at KL Court Layer (122 AC)

## Description

The operative state of Westerosi native magic as it affects the KL court layer at 122 AC. Westerosi magic exists as rumor, cultural residue, and occasional genuine phenomenon — but it is not a reliably accessible mechanic at the court layer where this story operates. Greenseer capability, Valyrian pyromantic tradition, and faceless-man craft are colorable background, not narrator-confirmed mechanisms that characters can invoke or that the story treats as load-bearing.

This is a distinct constraint from `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`: that card prohibits shard-derived parahuman capability in Planetos entirely. This card governs Planetos's own native magic, which exists but operates at a level inaccessible to KL court observers in 122 AC.

## Sensory Impact

Magic's absence is not felt. Rumors of magic are felt through the quality of how characters speak about the unusual — the hedge, the "they say," the attribution to the old days or the far north or the priest who came from Essos. The register is not flat denial and not credulity; it is the specific texture of people who know the category "magic" and have no reliable access to any instance of it.

## Duration

Persistent throughout this project. The Dance of Dragons (which produces some native magic surface expression) is beginning at the story's close, not present as an active story layer.

## The KL Court Layer — What Magic Is and Isn't

**Rumor-permitted:** Maesters at the Citadel know the dragonglass-candle tradition and regard it as pseudo-scientific curiosity, not operative magic. Septons have categories for the miraculous and the diabolical; they apply them to unusual events, but the Seven do not intervene directly in mortal affairs in a narrator-confirmed way. Common people have folk categories (the touched, the god-marked, the cursed) that accommodate unusual events without requiring a magic-as-mechanic explanation. Taylor's insect control will be categorized through these folk frames.

**Not narrator-confirmed:** The narrative does not confirm that any Westerosi native magic is active in KL during this project's scope. Greenseer dreams, glass-candle visions, shadowbinder work — if any of these appear as atmospheric color or as things characters believe in, they are not confirmed by the narrator as operational. Taylor may encounter characters who believe they have prophetic gifts; the narrative does not validate those beliefs as literally accurate.

**Dragon fire is not magic in this sense:** Dragons are biological animals with specific capabilities. Their fire is a biological mechanism, not a magical mechanism in the rumor-permitted sense. Dragon proximity is covered separately by `cond-dragon-proximity-122ac`.

## What This Constraint Prohibits

**No native Westerosi magic solving plot problems.** No glasss-candle vision that provides Taylor with useful intelligence. No greenseer dream that warns a character of her presence. No faceless-man-style power deployed against Taylor as a plot mechanism. These elements may exist as atmosphere; they cannot be the mechanism through which plot obstacles are generated or resolved.

**No Taylor recognizing equivalences between Westerosi magic and Worm physics as load-bearing.** Taylor's pattern-recognition may cause her to notice structural similarities (the glass-candle's light-in-darkness aesthetic, certain warging behaviors). She may note the resemblance internally. Those resemblances cannot become mechanistic — she cannot use a glass-candle to extend her range or interact with a warg's connection to activate something useful. Recognition is not equivalence; equivalence is a fence violation.

**No magical reveal of Taylor's nature.** No Westerosi magic user correctly identifies Taylor's capability through magical means and acts on that knowledge in a plot-consequential way. Her identification by Westerosi characters happens through ordinary observation, inference, and the whisper-chain mechanics described in related condition cards.

## Interaction Notes

**With `cond-westerosi-superstition-frame`:** That card covers the folk epistemology through which unusual events (including Taylor's fauna control) are categorized. This card covers the operative state of native Westerosi magic as a mechanic. Together they define what magic is (folk category) and is not (narrator-confirmed mechanic) in this project.

**With `cond-no-parahuman-infrastructure`:** These are distinct prohibitions. One covers Worm-universe parahuman mechanics on Planetos (prohibited entirely). The other covers Planetos-native magic mechanics at the KL court layer (present as rumor, not operative as mechanic). Do not conflate them.

**For auditor use:** Flag any scene where a Westerosi magic mechanism (greenseer, glass-candle, shadowbinding, faceless-man craft) produces a narrator-confirmed effect that advances or impedes plot. Flag any scene where Taylor uses a Westerosi magic system's structure as a technical pathway for extending her capabilities. Flag any scene where magical revelation of Taylor's nature drives plot.
