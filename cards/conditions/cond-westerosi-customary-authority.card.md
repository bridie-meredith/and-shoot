# Westerosi Customary Authority — Riverlands Smallfolk Social Physics at 120 AC

## Description

The behavioral constraints governing how Riverlands smallfolk interact with authority figures at 120 AC. This card defines the social physics of Taylor's operating stratum — the default postures, the available compliance behaviors, the specific costs of deviation from expected norms, and what various kinds of wrongness look like in a smallfolk child in this environment.

This is the ambient behavioral layer that everything in Taylor's daily life runs against. It is not a special circumstance; it is the water.

## Sensory Impact

The social physics of deference are physically embodied. When a man-at-arms arrives in a village common, every adult smallfolk body undergoes a postural shift within seconds: eyes drop, weight shifts back, shoulders relax to signal non-threat. This is not performed; it is automatic for people who grew up in this social world. The absence of these behaviors is visible immediately — a person who does not make the shift is noticed before anyone has consciously decided to notice them.

This physical vocabulary is the tell that Taylor cannot entirely suppress. Her body is eleven years old and Westerosi-born; she has some of these reflexes. Her adult cognition pushes back against them. The result is a performance that is close but slightly off — a beat late, a fraction wrong in the direction of the movement, eyes that drop and then return too fast. In a child, this reads as strange. In a child around adult men with authority, it reads as dangerous-strange.

## Duration

Persistent. This is the ambient social structure, not a temporary event.

## Default Postures by Authority Type

**Armed men (men-at-arms, knights, mounted soldiers):**
- Eyes down on approach
- Short answers, nothing volunteered
- Physical positioning: stepped back and to the side, out of direct confrontation with the authority figure's sightline
- No asking questions of the armed man
- Requests or problems directed toward whoever appears to be in charge, briefly and formulaically: "Begging your pardon, ser, we—" before the actual content

**Septons:**
- Different register than armed men: horizontal rather than vertical. You do not look down at a septon; you meet his eyes with appropriate humility but not avoidance.
- The septon's authority is moral and social; it does not require the same physical deference as armed authority.
- Disputes with a septon: not done openly. You comply with the septon's direction or you go around it quietly. You do not argue theology with a septon in public.

**Maesters:**
- Similar to septons in register: institutional rather than physical authority. The chain is the signal; the deference is toward the institution, not the man.
- However: a maester conducting a census is in a different mode than a maester practicing medicine. In census mode, the maester has quasi-administrative authority and the physical deference rules apply at a reduced intensity.

**Lords and castellans:**
- Full physical deference. Eyes down, stance receded, do not speak unless spoken to.
- Speaking first to a lord marks you immediately as not-of-this-stratum — either too high to need these rules or too ignorant to know them.
- In practice, smallfolk never address a lord directly; they address his steward or his men-at-arms, who relay as necessary.

## Literacy as Red Flag

Literacy in a smallfolk child is not a positive signal at 120 AC. It is a red flag. The available interpretations of a literate smallfolk child are:

- **Sept education:** The child has been in a septon's household and received minimal reading instruction. This is the most charitable and most common interpretation. It is the interpretation that Taylor's situation nominally supports.
- **Unusual origin:** The child is not where it is claimed to be. Literate smallfolk children sometimes turn out to be the children of merchants, traveling scholars, or minor ruined lords who have been placed in a smallfolk household for safety or concealment. This interpretation makes the child a person-with-a-secret.
- **Forgery potential:** A literate person in a smallfolk household is potentially capable of producing false documents (indenture records, land claims, identity papers). This interpretation makes the child a threat to the administrative process.

None of these interpretations are favorable for someone trying to stay off the census's special track.

**Taylor's specific exposure:** Her literacy is at a level that exceeds what sept education produces. She can read Valyrian script (she has practiced on the septon's books, several of which have Valyrian marginal notes). She can do accounts-level arithmetic. She processes written text with adult-level comprehension speed. The maester will notice all of this before she realizes he's noticed.

## The Competence Problem

A child who speaks to authority with adult confidence is misread in one of three ways in this social world:

1. **Mentally affected:** The child is touched, cursed, or otherwise cognitively abnormal in a way that places her outside normal social categories. This is protective (she's not threatening) and exposing (she's memorable and notable).
2. **Taught:** The child has been coached by someone with an agenda. This makes the child an instrument of whoever taught her and draws attention to who might have that agenda.
3. **Dangerous:** The child is something that is pretending to be a child. This is the interpretation that arrives when the strangeness accumulates past a certain threshold.

Taylor can pass as mentally affected (strange but harmless) if she is careful. She cannot pass as a normal child. The choice is between strange-and-dismissed and strange-and-flagged.

## Available Resistance Modes for Smallfolk

**Delay:** A smallfolk who moves slowly through a process, needs to be asked again, has to fetch a document, needs to speak to a family member before answering — this person is annoying but not threatening. Delay buys time.

**Disappear:** Before the authority arrives. Requires advance notice. Converts the problem into a fugitive-status problem, which is different and may be more survivable.

**Claim illness:** A person who is too ill to work is not useful to the census. Temporary measure; the census records the claim and expects re-assessment.

**Invoke competing authority:** Appeal to a septon's protection, another lord's claim, a knight's patronage. Creates a procedural dispute that must be resolved. Dispute resolution takes time. Time is a resource.

**Comply and document:** Provide the census information accurately, establish attached status correctly, survive the process cleanly. This is the best outcome and is only available to people who have genuinely valid protected status.

**Direct refusal:** Not viable. Results in immediate use of force. This is not an option in Taylor's functional toolkit unless she is prepared to escalate to violence, which at age eleven in a body with no fighting training is not a realistic path.

## What Deviation Costs

A smallfolk who deviates from expected deference behaviors in a census situation does not typically get beaten immediately. The consequences are:

- **First deviation noted:** the officer or maester notes it in the record. The deviation is associated with the person. This creates an open flag.
- **Accumulated deviations:** the person is treated as a problem case. Problem cases get flagged for higher review rather than being processed through the standard track.
- **Flagged review:** once in review, a person's status is suspended pending determination. They may not leave the area. They may be held at the castle while review proceeds.

For Taylor: the path from strange-child → flagged-for-review → held-at-harrenhal-pending-determination is the failure path she must not walk. Any deviation that lands her in review is a potential path to Harrenhal.

## Cross-House Deputization (120 AC Lannister-Hightower Accommodation)

Cross-house deputization for specific record-classes is canonical under the political accommodation between Lannister command and Hightower administration in 120 AC. A Lannister officer may hold a concurrent commission from a Hightower castellan deputizing him to document and file on a defined subject class (e.g., anomalous/unattached persons in shadow settlements), and records filed under that commission carry standing in the deputizing lord's record system. The deputizing lord retains adjudication authority; the deputy holds field intake and filing rights only.

This accommodation is jurisdictional, not personal. It does not confer general wardship authority, household membership, or cross-house loyalty obligations. Its scope is limited to the subject class named in the commission.
