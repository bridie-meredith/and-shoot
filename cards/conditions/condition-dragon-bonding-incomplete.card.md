# Condition: Dragon Bonding Incomplete

## Description

In Targaryen-era Westeros, a dragon without a rider is categorically anomalous. The Targaryen political order is built on the premise that dragons are bonded to Valyrian bloodline riders — that the rider-dragon bond is the mechanism by which dragons are made into political assets rather than uncontrolled forces. An unbonded dragon is therefore a problem the entire political framework is structured to resolve: either bond it or destroy it.

Taylor Hebert is an unclaimed dragon in a precise technical sense: she has not been bonded to a Targaryen rider, and will not be, because she is herself a conscious entity who declines the role of mount. The Westerosi framework has no category for this. It is not a dragon who hasn't been found yet; it is not a dragon too wild to approach; it is something the political framework cannot correctly describe.

## Sensory Impact

This is a political and social condition with specific behavioral manifestations:

- **For Westerosi observers:** every time Taylor demonstrates non-standard dragon behavior (using insects for reconnaissance, refusing fire when provoked into fire scenarios, making deliberate spatial choices that imply strategy), it produces a cognitive gap. The observer's dragon-model is wrong. They update toward "unusual dragon" or "enchanted dragon" or "divine sign," all of which are their framework's attempts to incorporate the anomaly.
- **For Taylor:** she is operating in a body the world is treating as a political problem. Every interaction she has with humans is colored by their attempt to categorize her. She is always, to some degree, being assessed for bondability.
- **For institutions:** the Targaryen court's protocols assume a dragon can be bonded or destroyed. A dragon that cannot be bonded and is too politically significant to casually destroy creates a procedural deadlock.

## Duration

Persistent throughout the series unless resolved. Resolution paths:
- Taylor accepts a rider (she will not)
- The political framework changes to accommodate her (long-arc; requires the Dance and its aftermath)
- She acquires enough power that the claim becomes unenforceably theoretical

## Interaction Notes

**With condition-targaryen-claim-disputed:** This condition is the reason the claim is disputed rather than resolved. A claimable dragon could be claimed. Taylor cannot be claimed. The claim therefore exists in permanent irresolution.

**With rhaenyra-targaryen (horizon):** Rhaenyra will eventually understand that Taylor's anomaly is not a bonding problem — it is a category problem. This understanding positions her differently from every other political actor attempting to resolve the claim through standard means.

**With septon-rowan:** His theological construction is, from a certain angle, a correct intuition applied to an insufficient framework: something is different about this dragon, and that difference requires an explanation. The theology is wrong about the mechanism but correct that the anomaly is not merely behavioral.

**With the cast:** Every character's relationship with Taylor is shaped by this condition. Ser Aemon has decided to work with the anomaly rather than resolve it. Rymer has incorporated it into his model and updated accordingly. Ser Edwyn cannot incorporate it and is in procedural deadlock. Septon Rowan has built a theology around it.
