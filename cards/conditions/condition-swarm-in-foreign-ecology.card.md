# Condition: Swarm in Foreign Ecology

## Description

Governs the state of Taylor Hebert's swarm capability in the Westerosi Riverlands insect ecology. Her power functions on all arthropods within range. The arthropods present in a medieval temperate European-analogue environment are real, numerous, and varied — but they are not the optimized urban ecology of Brockton Bay. Her toolkit works; its specific composition differs from her defaults.

## What Is Present in the Riverlands

**Flies (multiple species):** abundant. Blowflies, horseflies, stable flies. Dense around animal pens, middens, and bodies. Highly mobile; difficult for a lay observer to attribute to a controller; extremely useful for reconnaissance in populated areas. Taylor's reconnaissance default in this environment.

**Beetles:** diverse and plentiful. Ground beetles, bark beetles, dung beetles. Less useful for offensive capability than wasps or spiders but available in large numbers and capable of coordinated area presence.

**Wasps and hornets:** present seasonally. Capable of coordinated stinging attack. More dangerous per-individual than bees; less numerous. Seasonal availability matters — reduced in winter, peak in late summer.

**Spiders:** present throughout the Riverlands in significant density. Wall spiders, cellar spiders, hunting spiders, orb weavers. Spider silk production is available where concentrations are sufficient. Not as easily directed for attack as wasps but valuable for silk production and reconnaissance in enclosed spaces.

**Earwigs and centipedes:** present in soil and under wood. Limited offensive utility; useful for confirming presence in enclosed spaces.

**Ants:** present but not in the large colonial concentrations Taylor is accustomed to from Brockton Bay's warmer-climate ant populations. Ant usage is possible; ant-swarm density for offensive purposes requires specific terrain (near established colonies). Not a default resource.

**Lice and fleas:** present on people and animals throughout this environment. Sensitive for reconnaissance — Taylor can maintain contact through what is on a person without their knowledge. This is contextually significant and not a capability she has typically publicized.

**Worms and grubs:** present in soil. Not arthropods; Taylor cannot control them.

**Mosquitoes:** present near water sources. Useful for covert single-bug reconnaissance.

## What Is NOT Present

- Giant insects (no Worm-earth analogue fauna)
- The ant colonies of Brockton Bay's density and organization
- The specific cockroach density of an urban environment
- Taylor's tailored bug silk production at Brockton Bay scale (available but requires spider concentration work-up time)

## Capability Assessment by Scene Context

**Open field or road:**
Good insect density in warm seasons; limited in cold seasons. Flies and beetles available almost everywhere. Wasp/hornet offensive capability seasonal. Reconnaissance through fly density: functional.

**Inside a dwelling:**
House flies, cellar spiders, earwigs, fleas, and lice. Reconnaissance: very good (flies can access every space). Offensive: limited (no wasp concentration; spider stings are negligible). Covert monitoring: excellent.

**Forest:**
High diversity, high density in warm seasons. Ground beetles, horseflies, bark beetles, wood wasps, orb weavers. Offensive and defensive capability better here than in cultivated land. Silk production feasible with concentration time.

**Village common or enclosed settlement:**
Moderate density. Flies around middens and animal pens; spiders in building joints. Lice and fleas on animals and people. Reconnaissance excellent; offensive capability limited by absence of wasp concentration.

**Near water (river, pond, marsh):**
Mosquitoes, water beetles, certain fly species. Good reconnaissance density. Limited offensive concentration. The density shifts with season.

**Winter:**
Insect density drops significantly. Flies survive near heat sources (hearths, animal pens). Most wasp and hornet populations die off or go dormant. Beetle populations reduce. Taylor's effective radius drops; her offensive capability is substantially reduced. Reconnaissance through surviving fly populations and any lice/fleas on people is the primary cold-weather tool.

## Sensory Feedback Differences

Taylor feeds through bug senses. The sensory palette of Westerosi insects differs from Brockton Bay's:

- Flies provide visual input that is fragmented and compound-eye (high motion-sensitivity, low resolution). Good for detecting movement; poor for reading text or faces at distance.
- Spiders provide both visual and vibration/touch input. Useful for close-range monitoring in enclosed spaces.
- The ambient insect sound profile differs from urban Brockton Bay — more rural patterns, seasonal variation, fewer of the specific industrial-zone species she knows well.

She can adapt. The adaptation requires scene-time. Her read of a new insect's sensory profile is not instant; it normalizes over hours of use.

## Seasonal Tracking

The condition's effective toolkit varies by season. Impersonators and studio should track season:

- **Spring:** building density; flies and beetles active; early wasps beginning.
- **Summer:** peak density and diversity; full toolkit available.
- **Autumn:** reducing density; wasps past peak; flies declining; spiders still present.
- **Winter:** minimal above. See winter note above.

## Duration

Persistent. This condition is the baseline for Taylor's power in Westeros throughout the series. It does not change except with seasonal variation.

## Interaction Notes

**Scene-level use:** Before any scene where Taylor uses her swarm capability, the active impersonator should check this condition against the location card's season and terrain type to determine what is available. The condition provides the ceiling; the scene provides the specific context.
