# Monument — Administrative-Observation Apparatus (122 AC)

## Description

Interior monument card for Taylor Hebert at 122 AC. The Earth-Bet institutional-observation infrastructure — the cape-policing and cape-tracking bureaucracy that named her, tracked her movements, and held her inside a system of uniformed institutional surveillance as both its subject and, briefly, one of its numbered members. The monument does not name the apparatus by proper noun on the prose surface; it names the family-of-shape: the recognition that fires when uniformed institutional formations move in organized patterns past a position she occupies. Taylor's interior catches the once-uniform-watcher matrix — she has stood on both sides of the observation axis in that apparatus, and the body-knowledge of both positions travels with her.

Distinct from `monument-override-architecture-prohibition-122ac` (which covers the deployed-capability monument: the mass-control architecture and the vow prohibiting its redeployment). That card covers what she refuses to do with capability. This card covers what she recognizes from having been watched and from having watched — the surveillance-of-her / surveillance-by-her monument, the institutional-observation apparatus as the load. Two different Earth-Bet monuments, two different interior loads. The override-architecture monument fires at the threshold of doing. This monument fires at the threshold of recognizing: the moment when a uniformed formation in organized motion triggers the family-of-shape the apparatus generated, and the interior notes the rhyme before the surface does.

## Sensory Impact

None at the world level. The monument does not alter the ambient environment. Its presence is entirely interior:

- In Taylor's spatial-behavioral sense: a uniformed formation moving in organized, measured cadence through a position she holds or occupies from cover — the formation's shape and count rhyme with the family of movements she tracked from a controlled observational position in the prior world.
- In Taylor's interior register: a recognition that precedes analysis, arriving in the working-pause after the formation clears. Not alarm; not grief. The interior identifies the rhyme — this formation has the shape of the ones she had reasons to track — and the identification itself is the monument-content. Clinical-of-the-familiar register: the monument notes the pattern and files it as accumulated weight, not as threat assessment.
- In the prose surface: no signal unless the narrator-interest and memory-flags facets fire on the triggering beat. The monument is interior-facing; its expression is in the facet layer, not in the world-state.

## Duration

Permanent across the project. The Earth-Bet observation apparatus is a carried weight, not a temporary condition. The family-of-shape recognition does not diminish across the project arc; uniformed institutional formations in 122 AC Kings Landing will continue to trigger the rhyme because the structural resemblance (organized uniformed presence moving in counted cadence through a city's geography) is real and repeating. The monument does not resolve.

**Monument-lighting frequency:** Low to moderate. The trigger pattern requires a uniformed institutional formation moving in organized motion past or through a position Taylor occupies or monitors — not merely the presence of guards or soldiers at rest. The Watch on patrol, a column moving in counted formation, a uniformed procession through a district she is working: these are trigger-eligible. Scattered soldiers, a single guard at a post, a door-man: these do not reach the threshold. When the monument lights, the beat is quiet — the formation has passed, the count settles, the interior reaches backward. The monument fires in the working-pause, not in the confrontation.

## Monument Body

### The apparatus

The institutional-observation apparatus on Earth-Bet was the cape-policing and cape-tracking bureaucracy that processed Taylor as both a subject (named, tracked, assessed) and, for a period, a numbered member operating under its organizational logic. The apparatus held a particular posture: it watched from inside the same structure it was policing, using capes to track capes, uniformed members moving in institutional formation through a city's geography to apply the observation infrastructure to the population it classified. Taylor knows the apparatus from the inside of a doorway — the phrase carries both meanings: she stood in doorways watching uniformed formations for operational reasons, and she stood inside the apparatus's own structure looking out through its organizational logic at the world it classified.

The apparatus generated a family-of-shape: organized uniformed presence moving in measured cadence through a city, tracking positions and populations, the count as a record of the coverage. The shape is not unique to the prior world — the Watch is the same shape, differently staffed, differently purposed, differently lethal in different ways — but the recognition fires because Taylor's body-knowledge of the shape is deep. She internalized the cadence of institutional observation as a tool and as a subject; the cadence is now a trigger-pattern.

### The observation axis

Taylor occupied both positions on the observation axis in the prior world: the observed (numbered, assessed, tracked by the institutional apparatus) and the observer (operating within the apparatus's framework, moving in its formations, counting coverage, reading positions). The monument carries both valences. When a Watch formation passes in measured count past her position, the interior does not cleanly separate which side of the observation axis the recognition comes from; it registers both. She had reasons to track that cadence. She also had reasons to be wary of being tracked by it. The family-of-shape holds both positions simultaneously, and the interior files them as a single accumulated weight: the once-uniform-watcher matrix, the apparatus she knew from within and from below.

### The rhyme structure

The monument fires on rhyme, not on identity. The Watch in 122 AC Kings Landing is not the cape-policing apparatus. It does not have the same mandate, the same infrastructure, or the same relationship to her. But the structural rhyme is real: uniformed institutional members moving in organized measured motion through a city's geography, covering ground, constituting a record of presence. The rhyme is what the interior catches — not a confusion of eras, but a recognition of family-of-shape. The interior notes the rhyme and the distance simultaneously: this formation has the shape of the ones she tracked; it is not those formations; she carries both facts at once.
