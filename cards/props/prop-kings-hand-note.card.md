# Prop — The King's-Hand Note

The most load-bearing prop in the arc. A single document travels from verbal indulgence to sealed parchment to orphaned license to heist mechanism across three books and nine chapters. It is planted in I.1, grows in I.2, is sealed in I.9, fires as a master key in I.10, becomes legally unrevocable through its signer's death in III.1, and closes the heist in III.10. The spine's longest single-prop arc.

---

## Physical Description

**In its final form (after I.9):** A parchment document, roughly letter-size, written in a clear administrative hand. The text grants "the princess" (or "the Lady Saerys") "what she requires for her good works" — a deliberately broad formulation approved without grasping how broadly it would be read. The hand belongs to a royal secretary; the words were dictated or approved by Viserys himself.

**The seal:** At the document's close, the wax impression of the King's Hand's seal — or the King's own seal. The seal is applied in red wax. The impression is the authentication. Per canon ship-heist mechanics: "authentication rests on the seal; a slightly-broken seal is itself the standard alibi for irregularities; she needs one genuine impression on the wrong document." This seal is genuine. This impression is real. The man who made it will die before anyone can ask him to revoke it.

**Physical state:** Well-preserved. The parchment is stored flat, rolled tight, protected from moisture and handling. By Book III it has survived six years, two sea voyages, and the Dance of the Dragons without losing the seal. The seal is intact. This matters.

**Document weight and size:** single parchment folio, small and portable — not a formal charter. This is an informal grant, which gives it both its flexibility (broad phrasing, general application) and its authority (the King's own seal on even an informal document overrules every officer below him).

## Full Lifecycle — Chapter by Chapter

This section is the functional-state tracker across the arc. Bones authors need to know which state the note is in at any given chapter.

### State 0 — Verbal indulgence (Chapter I.1 → I.2)

**Form:** Not a document yet. Viserys's spoken word — "give the child what amuses her" — is the original grant. PLANT[NOTE] in I.1.

**Legal weight:** The King's expressed preference, communicated verbally to household staff. Effective as a soft master key within the household; not portable, not permanent, not producible as evidence.

**What Saerys does with it:** Trades on it immediately as a standing authorization for charitable work and still-room access. She acts as though she has paper when she has only a father's warmth.

### State 1 — Standing license (Chapter I.2)

**Form:** The verbal indulgence formalized to a written standing license recording the King's ongoing approval of "the princess's charitable works." FIRE[NOTE→license].

**Legal weight:** Portable and producible. Overrules every household officer and most court officials below the Hand. Not yet sealed — administrative authority, not formal grant.

**What Saerys does with it:** Anchors every bill of exchange to the Braavosi factor. The license is the trade empire's constitutional founding document: the root authority under which every subsequent instrument is legitimate.

### State 2 — Sealed parchment (Chapter I.9)

**Form:** The standing license upgraded to a sealed document. "The princess shall have what she requires for her good works." FIRE[NOTE→sealed]; PLANT[ORPHAN-SEAL] fires simultaneously — this exact seal is the one cashed in Book III.

**Legal weight:** A sealed document bearing the King's impression is a formal grant. It overrules every household officer, every maester, every septa, every court functionary below the King himself. The septa's true accusation in I.10 is perfectly correct and completely helpless against it.

### State 3 — Master key (Chapter I.10)

**Form:** Same sealed parchment. FIRE[NOTE→Lock I].

**Legal weight:** Deployed at maximum effect against the septa's authority to confine Saerys. The note is not disputed — the septa has no standing to challenge the King's own seal. The note wins without argument.

**Operational detail:** mechanism's first run at lowest audit-difficulty (the septa is a household official; her authority is real but bounded; the seal is her ceiling). The trick works cleanly. The reader sees the full trick at I.10 so that when the same mechanism runs at higher difficulty in II.10 and III.10, they recognize it.

### State 4 — Standing license (operational) (Chapters I.10 through III.1)

**Form:** The sealed parchment in Saerys's possession as a live operational instrument. The factor network operates under it. The manifests reference it. The bills of exchange reference it.

**Legal weight:** Full, live, and continuously effective. The King is alive; the seal is genuine; every transaction under it is authorized.

**What Saerys does with it:** Not carried day-to-day. It lives in secure storage, referenced but not produced outside moments where authority must be demonstrated. Its existence is the operational authority.

### State 5 — Orphaned license (Chapter III.1 onward)

**Form:** Same sealed parchment. No physical change. The signer is dead. FIRE[ORPHAN-SEAL]; FIRE[GIFT:VISERYS].

**Legal weight:** The most complex and most load-bearing state. The seal is genuine. The impression is real. The man who made it is dead — but death does not invalidate a seal's authenticity. It invalidates the living signer's ability to revoke, amend, or clarify. The note cannot be called back. It cannot be modified. It cannot be disputed on grounds of the signer's intent, because the signer no longer exists to be asked.

**The orphan dynamic:** she is trading on a dead king's seal. Every transaction made under it after III.1 is technically legitimate (the seal is real; the grant was real) and practically unverifiable (the signer cannot be consulted). The document is too old to be forged, too authentic to be challenged, and too unrevocable to be stopped. The orphaned license is more powerful than the living license precisely because it has lost the signer's ability to say no.

**Account-book annotation:** "the standing license: amended status; operational; the signer's death does not affect the seal's authority; the account remains open; the work continues."

### State 6 — Heist mechanism #3 (Chapter III.10)

**Form:** Same sealed parchment. Deployed as authentication anchor for the heist's paper construction. FIRE[HEIST MECH 3].

**Legal weight:** The heist requires one genuine impression on the right document. The orphaned license provides it. The harbor master inspecting the heist's paperwork finds a sealed grant that predates the war, bears a genuine King's-hand impression, and authorizes the Crown's plague-stores provisioning. The authentication is correct. The impression is real. The man who could say "I didn't mean it that way" is two years dead.

**The mechanism:** "authentication rests on the seal; she needs one genuine impression on the wrong document." The seal is not slightly-broken. The seal is in excellent condition. There is no irregularity visible. The document is too correct.

**Narrative payload:** the heist is the funeral she has refused since the cradle, inverted — where everyone else would bury the dead, she files them, closing every account by sailing away from all of them at once. The paperwork victory IS the grief, refused in its final form. The note is how she converts her father's death into an instrument of escape. The love that was also the cage springs the lock one final time, posthumously.

## What It Actually Is

A real document with real legal weight, used in full accordance with its actual authority. Saerys does not forge the note. The grant says what she says it says. The seal is genuine. The authority is real.

What exceeds strict sanction: the phrasing is broad enough to cover far more than the signer intended. "What she requires for her good works" covers the still-room, the factor network, the manifests, the bills of exchange, the trade empire, and ultimately the heist. The signer intended "give the child her herbs." What he authorized, in writing and under seal, is considerably larger.

This is not fraud. It is interpretation. She made sure the document said what she needed before she accepted it.

## Affordances / Uses

- **Authority anchor:** establishes legal authorization for all transactions in her name; the factor network's constitutional document
- **Override instrument:** produces on demand to overrule any authority below the King; effective from I.10 onward
- **Heist authentication:** provides the genuine impression that makes the III.10 paper construction unassailable
- **Proof of standing:** in any context where Saerys's right to operate must be demonstrated, the note is the demonstration

## Sensory Hooks

- Parchment texture: smooth on the writing surface, slightly rough on the back; quality royal-household parchment
- Wax seal: red, dense, bearing a clear impression; slightly raised from the parchment surface; pressing a thumb to it communicates quality and age
- Ink: dark, formal, administrative hand, even strokes; no decoration; official and competent
- Smell: old parchment, a slight wax note from the seal, the specific dry smell of a well-stored document that has traveled but not deteriorated
- Weight: lighter than it should be for what it does

## Portability

Portable; deliberately not carried day-to-day. Stored securely, not with the account-book or the dose-log. On sea voyages, travels in a waterproofed oilskin envelope inside a locked document case.

---

## Carry State

**Books I-II:** secure storage, not on the person during normal operation. Produced on demand (I.10 and implied occasions).

**Book III:** orphaned license shifts from operational document to heist instrument; carried with her in the final chapters.

Concealment: not inherently suspicious; it is a royal grant and looks like one. If searched, it is legitimately what it appears to be. This is the best possible concealment.
