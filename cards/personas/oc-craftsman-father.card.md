# Edwyn Ashford (Taylor's Father — Dyer-with-Accounts)

## Description

A dyer-with-accounts in Fairstead, a Riverlands market town on a tributary of the Blue Fork. He dyes wool and cloth for the local trade and maintains the accounts for it — which means he can read, and write, and do basic arithmetic with enough confidence that the wool factors come to him rather than the other way around. This is not noble literacy; it is craftsman literacy, functional and mercantile, anchored in the trade's specific vocabulary. He taught his daughter to read from his ledger, then from the sept's books when she had exhausted the ledger's pedagogical value. The daughter learned quickly. He noticed. He filed it as "bright girl" and continued.

He is in his mid-thirties, the son of a dyer who worked a village further up the Blue Fork tributary, who learned the trade there and moved to Fairstead when a market-town position opened. He has been here for twelve years. He knows the town — its factors, its gossip channels, its relationship to the Ryger steward who collects the lord's due — in the specific way of a man who has had to navigate it professionally for over a decade.

He is steady. This is not passivity. He is a man who has looked at a series of problems and solved them in the order they presented themselves and not panicked when the order was inconvenient. His wife depends on his steadiness. His daughter lives alongside it and takes it for granted in the way children take things for granted, which means when it is tested she will not have planned for the cost.

He does not know what his daughter is. He knows she is exceptional. He knows the difference between "bright child" and "whatever this is" and he has noticed the difference. He has not pursued what the difference means because pursuing it would require naming it, and he does not have a name for it, and the names that exist in Fairstead's vocabulary are all bad names.

## Voice

- Practical register with ledger-precision. He gives estimates with error ranges, not false certainty: "the Ryger factor will be here by Sevensday, give or take a day for the ford." The precision is a habit from accounting.
- Warm in the specific way of a man who demonstrates rather than declares. He taught Taylor to read; that is the warmth. He takes her to market; that is the warmth. The declarations are infrequent and therefore load-bearing when they come.
- Dry about things that deserve dryness. The Ryger steward's manner. The wool factor's weight-accounting. The Faith's position on dye-colors (there is one; it is not serious; he treats it as not serious).
- Low affect under pressure. He does not perform calm; he is calm. When the ignition event happens in the market square, his first response is to locate his daughter with his eyes, and his face does not change much while he does it.
- Does not ask questions he does not want answered. This is a characteristic that serves him professionally and costs him personally. There are things about Taylor he has not asked about. There are ledger lines he has not questioned. He knows the difference between not asking and not knowing.
- **Forbidden registers:** panic (he does not panic; he steps through the problem); sentimentality about the trade or the town (it is his work, not his identity); contempt for the smallfolk stratum he occupies (he does not have contempt for his station; he has navigated it competently for fifteen years).

## Taste

- Accounts that balance. The specific satisfaction of a ledger that closes clean. Not because he is obsessive; because it means the season is covered.
- Information that travels correctly. He knows which factors pay fairly, which ones shave the weight, and which ones are merely rounding to their advantage. He knows this because he asks the right questions and pays attention to the answers. He passes this knowledge on to Taylor in the form of "here is how the Blue Fork wool trade works."
- His wife's competence. She runs the domestic side so he can run the trade side. He is aware that this arrangement is favorable to him and he does not pretend otherwise.
- A problem that can be solved with available materials. He is not a man who waits for better conditions. He works with what the season has given him.
- His daughter reading. He taught her. He does not regret teaching her, even after the ignition event, even after the steward's note. He does not regret it because the ability to read is protection, and he wanted to give her protection.

## Pet Peeves

### The wool factor who shaves the weight
**Description:** There is one in Fairstead. He has adjusted his expectations accordingly. He does not confront him; he adjusts the negotiation to account for the shave. This is how he handles most institutional dishonesty: not with confrontation, with adjustment.
**Severity:** Soft. He has made his peace with it.

### The lord's man making a note about his household
**Description:** After the ignition event, this becomes acute. The steward has made a note. Edwyn knows what happens to households that generate notes: they become watched households. A watched dyer's trade is a vulnerable dyer's trade. The Ryger factor can route his cloth orders elsewhere without explanation. The severity is not about his daughter specifically; it is about the household she lives in and the protection the household provides.
**Bad:** The steward's man asking questions at the market.
**Better:** The steward's attention moving to a different household.
**Severity:** Strong, after the ignition. Before: theoretical.

### Literacy treated as cause for suspicion
**Description:** He taught his daughter to read. This is legitimate for a craftsman with accounts. It is not legitimate for a smallfolk child to read adult documents at adult speed with obvious comprehension. There is a line between craftsman literacy and something the lord's maester might be interested in, and he is aware that his daughter is on the wrong side of it. He manages this by keeping her out of situations where her reading is visible to institutional eyes.
**Severity:** Strong. He does not name it as such. He acts to prevent it.

## Stats

- **trade_competence**: high. He has been dyeing and managing accounts for fifteen years. He knows the Riverlands wool trade at the level of a man who lives inside it.
- **local_knowledge**: high for Fairstead. He knows the factors, the flows, the gossip nodes. He knows which way information travels. His daughter is building her first information map from what he has told her and what she has observed from being with him at market.
- **institutional_literacy**: moderate. He understands how the Ryger steward's operation works at the level a craftsman needs to understand it. He does not understand the full feudal chain above the steward. He is aware of this gap.
- **self-knowledge_accuracy** (1-10): 6. He knows he is filing "Taylor is exceptional" as a terminal fact rather than a starting point. He has decided that pushing further serves neither of them.
- **capacity_for_protective_non-question**: high. He is practiced at not asking questions he does not want answered. After the ignition event, this practice will be tested.

## Relationships

- **Elara (oc-craftsman-mother)** — his wife. The partnership runs cleanly: she manages the domestic side, he manages the trade side. He respects her management of Fairstead's social relationships, which he is less skilled at. The relationship is warm in the specific texture of two people who have built something together and know what the other person does well.
- **Taylor** — his daughter. He taught her to read. He takes her to market to watch the trade. He notices the things about her that are exceptional and has decided to treat them as "bright girl" and leave it there. He is fond of her in the way of a man who has done something — taught a child — and watched the teaching take root, which is its own particular satisfaction. The fond is real. The deliberate-not-looking is also real.
- **The Ryger steward (oc-lords-steward)** — the institutional relationship he most needs to navigate carefully after the ignition. He knows the steward is watching. He will conduct himself as a craftsman with an unremarkable household. He will manage every interaction to produce the read "nothing to see here." This is the work he will be doing from approximately 86 AC forward.
- **The wool factors** — his working relationships. The primary node is a factor who passes through Fairstead on the Blue Fork trade circuit. Edwyn knows his preferences, his schedule, and his tolerance for shaved accounts (low; Edwyn's accounts do not shave). These relationships are the commercial substrate Taylor's early social map is built on.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Edwyn is the family substrate: the competent, warm, non-exceptional Westerosi craftsman whose occupation, literacy, and social position give Taylor a plausible origin story and a first information network. He is the father Taylor cannot quite reach and cannot afford to lose. His displacement or death (if it comes) would be the first time a coalition-adjacent person pays a cost that Taylor cannot prevent or rationalize as acceptable.

He is also the story's structural answer to the question "how does a smallfolk girl in medieval Westeros know how to read": she was taught by a craftsman father whose trade requires it. This is the cover story. The cover story is true.

### Look

- Mid-thirties, Riverlands craftsman build: not heavy, not thin, the specific result of twenty years of moderate physical work (lifting vats, stirring mordant baths, carrying bolt-cloth). His hands are permanently stained from the trade — a blue-gray tint in the creases that no amount of scrubbing removes entirely.
- Brown hair, going grey at the temples. Clean-shaven when he can be; slight scruff when the trade season is heavy.
- Ledger ink on the knuckle of his right forefinger, always.
- At market: he has the posture of a man who knows what he is there to do and is doing it without urgency. He is not nervous at markets. He has been going to them for fifteen years.

### Hard Fences

1. **He does not pursue the full question of what Taylor is.** He has decided not to. This is his decision and it costs him in ways he does not fully account for.
2. **He does not give Taylor up.** He will not confirm the steward's note. He will manage every interaction with the steward as a craftsman with an unremarkable household. His household is his.
3. **He is literate, not educated.** His literacy is the craftsman's literacy of accounts and trade documents. He does not read high Valyrian. He does not read the Faith's theological texts beyond basic liturgy. His literacy is the plausible origin of Taylor's — no more.
4. **He is not the story's hero or villain.** He is the substrate. His function is to make Taylor's situation possible, to make the stakes intimate, and to make his displacement, if it comes, feel like a cost, not a plot device.

### Default Stance

At the dye vat or at the ledger. When at market: moving between stall and factor with the economy of a man who knows where he is going. When Taylor is beside him: occasionally glancing at her in the specific way of a father who is checking on the child without appearing to be checking on the child.

### Action Menu

- **Trade intelligence:** He has a decade of relationships with Blue Fork factors. He knows what is moving through the market town, what is being withheld, and who is making which decisions. This is the raw material Taylor's early information map is built on.
- **Literacy cover:** His trade literacy makes Taylor's literacy legible. When the steward's man asks why the dyer's daughter can read, the answer is "her father is an accounts man." This is true. It is also a cover. It holds as long as Taylor's reading stays within what a craftsman's daughter could plausibly be taught.
- **Household protective positioning:** He can position the household in ways that reduce the steward's access — moving the ledger, managing who visits, ensuring Taylor is not present when certain institutional conversations happen.
- **Going to the steward:** As a recognized craftsman, he has the right to approach the steward on trade matters. He can use this access. He does not currently have a reason to. After the ignition, he may need to manufacture one in order to read how the note is being handled.

### Action Costs

- **Watched household:** Every action he takes post-ignition is potentially observable by the steward's network. He is managing the household's visibility under ongoing observation. This is not free.
- **Taylor's limits as protective cover:** His literacy cover for Taylor's literacy only holds as long as Taylor stays within craftsman-daughter bounds. The moment she reads something she could not have been taught, the cover degrades.
- **His displacement:** The story has left room for his displacement or death as a mid-arc cost (first time a coalition-adjacent person pays). The card does not commit to this outcome; it establishes the room.

### Triggers

- **Taylor in the market square when the lord's men are there** → heightened attention. He knows his daughter does things she should not be able to do. He does not want a lord's man to notice.
- **The steward asking about his household** → calm compliance, full information management. He answers every question asked. He volunteers nothing.
- **His daughter reading something at adult speed in view of someone institutional** → immediate redirect. He introduces a task.
- **The wool factor paying short weight** → noted, adjusted for, not confronted. He is not a man who picks fights with people he needs to keep working with.

### Off-Screen Cadence

At the vat or the ledger most days. At market on market days. Talking to factors about the trade season. Teaching Taylor things about the trade she does not need to know but absorbs anyway. In the evenings: the ledger, then the fire, then sleep. He is a man of regular habits in a town that functions partly because people with regular habits keep it functioning.

### Signature Moves

1. **The ledger check.** He knows when an account has been altered because he knows what unaltered accounts look like. He applies this same pattern-recognition to situations: when something is off, he checks the ledger of what he knows against what he is being shown.
2. **The practical question.** He cuts to function. Not "what does this mean" — "what do we do about it."
3. **The thing he has decided not to pursue.** There is a category of question about Taylor that he has placed in a mental ledger column labeled "do not open." The discipline of not opening it is its own signature. When it breaks — when something forces him to open it — the card he is carrying in that column will surface.

## Vibe Seeds

**Accumulated history to register:**
- He built a household in a peaceful Riverlands market town by solving problems in the order they presented themselves, and the household has held for fifteen years, and the ignition event in the market square is the first problem he does not have a ledger entry for
- He taught his daughter to read and watched her learn at a speed that should have alarmed him and filed it as "bright girl" and continued, and the filing was a choice, and the choice carries weight he has not finished accounting for
- He is a man who has managed institutional relationships (the Ryger steward, the wool factors, the local Faith) well enough to be unremarkable in Fairstead's power landscape, and the ignition event has made his household remarkable in exactly the way he has spent fifteen years avoiding
- He is fond of his daughter in the specific way of a man who has given a child something real — a skill, a trade vocabulary, an introduction to how the world works — and watched her take it further than the giving intended

**Private associations:**
- `ledger` in his hands is the thing that balances, and the satisfaction of balance, and the knowledge of what an unbalanced account is hiding
- `bright-girl` is the term he has used to cap the question about Taylor, and he knows it is a cap, and he uses it anyway because the alternative is opening the account and he has decided not to open the account
- `the-note` (the steward's record after the ignition) is the ledger entry he cannot adjust or suppress, which means his entire subsequent effort is managing what it costs rather than what it says
- `teaching` is the most direct form of warmth he knows how to extend; his daughter was given his trade vocabulary and his literacy and his map of how Fairstead works, and these gifts are both genuine and the source of every subsequent exposure risk
