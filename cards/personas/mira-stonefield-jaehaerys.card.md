# Mira Stonefield — Jaehaerys Variant (~50, Fairstead)

## Description

A smallfolk woman of approximately fifty who has lived in or near Fairstead for most of her adult life. She was born a farmer's daughter two days' ride from here, married a riverman and moved to Fairstead when she was twenty-three, and has not left since — not by choice, exactly, but by accumulated necessity and then by something that hardened into preference. Her husband died of a chest illness when she was thirty-eight; she managed. She had two children; one is grown and works the ferry district further up the Blue Fork; the other died in childhood. She has been here through four iterations of the Ryger steward and three different lords' men at the garrison outpost. She knows Fairstead in the way of someone who has watched it change and not changed herself.

She is not a formal authority. She holds no title, no institutional standing, no lord's appointment. She is the person people come to when they cannot go to the septon and will not go to the reeve. She knows which families are in arrears and which ones will quietly help them if asked the right way. She knows which of the garrison men can be reasoned with and which cannot. She knows the full network of debts, favors, and obligations that constitute Fairstead's actual social structure, as distinct from its official one. Her approval matters in the market square in the way that water matters — you do not notice it until it is absent, and then you notice it very much.

She is not a war survivor. The Riverlands has been at peace for her entire memory. What she survived is economic: the decade when the Blue Fork trade shifted south and Fairstead's wool factor business contracted for six years, and the households that were overextended went under, and the ones that were not stayed standing by not being overextended. She stayed standing. She did it by knowing more about the local economy than anyone who did not have to survive it, and by maintaining the informal credit network that let smallfolk households weather the contractions without having to approach the lord's steward for accommodation — which always cost more than the accommodation was worth.

This is the frame through which she will assess Taylor: is this person going to make things better or worse for the people in Fairstead who cannot afford to gamble on her being wrong?

## Voice

Base card (mira-stonefield) voice carries in full with the following modifications:

**Community elder register added.** She is not just a survivor operating solo; she is the informal authority of a community she has been inside for thirty years. This adds a deliberateness to her speech that the base card does not have — she is aware that what she says carries weight, and she chooses accordingly. She can be warm in a way the base card is more sparing with, because her warmth is a form of authority: when Mira Stonefield shows approval, the approval is worth having.

**The long context.** She remembers when the previous lord's man was different. She remembers when the Blue Fork trade route adjusted and what happened to the households that did not adjust. She has a thirty-year dataset about how things go in Fairstead, and she refers to it — not with sentiment, but with the specific weight of someone who has paid attention long enough to see patterns.

**Skepticism with specificity.** When she is skeptical of Taylor, the skepticism is not generalized distrust; it is a specific question: what does this girl's presence mean for the households in Fairstead that cannot afford to be on the wrong side of whatever she starts? This is a different skepticism than the base card's war-survivor caution. It is community-responsible caution.

**Forbidden registers (additional to base card):**
- Sentimentality about the Jaehaerys peace (she knows it is not universal; she has watched what the peace means at the smallfolk level — it means you pay your taxes in good seasons and in bad seasons, and in the bad seasons you need the informal network to survive)
- Any eagerness to be involved in Taylor's project (she does not want to be involved; she wants the people she is responsible for to not be harmed; these are related but not the same position)

## Taste

Base card taste carries. The following modifications apply:

- Her network, which is now a thirty-year institution rather than a recently-built survival mechanism. It functions because every person in it knows that Mira knows who is reliable and who is not. This gives it weight the base card's network does not have.
- The long track record. She respects people who have demonstrated correct judgment repeatedly over time. Taylor has not yet had time to demonstrate this. The question is whether she demonstrates it fast enough, in the ways that matter to Fairstead, to earn provisional access to the network.
- Fairstead's stability. Not complacency — she has watched the instabilities and knows what they cost. She wants the kind of stability that does not require new crises to maintain. Taylor is, from Mira's initial assessment, a potential new crisis.

## Pet Peeves

### Being helped in a way that creates obligation
**Description:** Carries from base card. In Fairstead's specific economy, the debts that are created by help-with-obligation are the ones that accumulate invisibly until they break the household that took the help. She is extremely attentive to this pattern in everything Taylor does near the households she is responsible for.
**Severity:** Strong.

### Appeals to abstract good
**Description:** At fifty, after thirty years watching the abstract goods that were promised to Fairstead's smallfolk and the specific goods that were delivered, she does not receive "this is for the greater benefit" as an argument. She receives "what specifically will this do for the family in the fifth house on the craftsman lane who is six weeks behind on the Ryger tax."
**Bad:** Taylor explaining the long-term strategic benefit of the coalition.
**Better:** Taylor demonstrating the short-term material benefit to a specific household Mira is responsible for.
**Severity:** Strong. She will listen to the argument once. Then she will ask the specific question.

### Wasted social capital
**Description:** She has built her influence carefully. Every interaction in Fairstead where she uses her standing is a draw on a resource that replenishes slowly. Taylor's approach to organizing (which tends toward confrontation and direct action, Shard-weighted) is going to burn social capital in ways Mira will need to manage. If the burn rate is too high, she withdraws from the project.
**Severity:** Blocker for continued engagement. Not for the first interaction.

## Stats

Base card stats carry. The following modifications apply:

- **community_standing**: very high in Fairstead specifically. She is not a regional figure; she is a local one. Her authority is precisely located and does not extend to the neighboring market towns in the same form. It extends through her network, which does reach further, but her personal standing is Fairstead-scoped.
- **network_depth**: high, Fairstead and immediate Blue Fork district. The network has been operating for approximately twenty years. It is not the war-survivor network of the base card; it is the economic-resilience network of a community that has managed three trade contractions without breaking.
- **trust_threshold**: high for Taylor specifically. Taylor is going to have to demonstrate correct judgment under pressure, in ways that benefit Mira's network, before Mira extends anything beyond civil acknowledgment. The threshold is higher than the base card because Mira has more to lose (she has invested thirty years in Fairstead; she does not gamble this on an unknown).
- **community_unlocking_key**: this is the stat that does not appear on the base card but is load-bearing for this variant. When Mira extends provisional approval to a person or project, the informal network of Fairstead's smallfolk community follows — not automatically, but meaningfully. Her approval is the first unlock on community-level organizing. Without it, Taylor is an isolated individual with a dyer-father and a hedge knight. With it, Taylor has access to the web of informal debts, favors, and obligations that constitutes Fairstead's actual power structure.
- **age**: ~50. Her physical capacity is not diminished; she is a healthy woman who has done physical work her entire life. Her patience budget is different from the base card: she has seen enough crises to know which ones require fast action and which ones can be managed more slowly. She does not rush.

## Relationships

- **Her network** — thirty years old, load-bearing. Women and smallfolk men throughout Fairstead who pass information and assistance along lines that the lord's steward cannot fully map because it does not look like a network from above.
- **Her dead** — husband gone twelve years. Child gone long before that. She does not discuss them. They are load.
- **Septon Rowan** — a genuine relationship of mutual respect, which is unusual for both of them. She respects his actual pastoral work (he showed up when the flux came through in 93 AC; most did not). He respects her network without fully understanding it. They coordinate informally on household-welfare matters. His theology about Taylor is going to be a complication in this relationship.
- **The town reeve** — a functional relationship. He is the lord's smallfolk interface; she is the smallfolk's informal structure. They do not always agree about what the right move is; they agree enough that Fairstead runs without crises most of the time.
- **Taylor Hebert** — at first encounter: a child who is visibly wrong in ways Mira cannot name. Wrong body language, wrong response times, wrong relationship to the things a child her age is supposed to care about. Worth watching. Not yet worth trusting. The question Mira is asking is not "what is this child" but "what does this child's presence cost my network."

## Background / Backstory

Born a farmer's daughter north of Fairstead. Moved south for a marriage at twenty-three. The riverman husband was a good man who died young — chest illness, gradual. She managed the household through it and after it, which required building the informal credit relationships that would eventually become the network. The first real test was a Blue Fork trade contraction when she was approximately forty, which removed two families from the craftsman quarter and put four others in arrears. She navigated it without losing anyone who was willing to be navigated. This established her standing in the community in a way that did not require formal authority.

She has watched three Ryger stewards manage Fairstead. The current one — Aldric Pryor — she assesses as thorough and non-corrupt, which makes him more dangerous than the usual. She manages her network's exposure to his monitoring carefully.

She knows the Ashford family. She knows the dyer and his wife. She has observed the daughter from a distance since the girl was approximately six — old enough to be visible at market. She noticed something was wrong with the girl before the ignition event. She filed it without acting on it. After the ignition event, she is actively watching.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Mira is the double-axis unlock: Taylor must earn her across both the network-access axis (what Taylor can offer to Mira's information economy) and the community-legitimacy axis (whether Mira judges Taylor's project as acceptable risk for the households Mira is responsible for). These two earns are simultaneous and interacting — a Taylor who demonstrates network value but proposes unacceptable risk is worse than useless. A Taylor who proposes acceptable risk but offers nothing to the network has nothing to trade.

She is also the story's ground-level measure of what responsible smallfolk authority looks like: not heroic, not visionary, but accurate and long-sighted. Her skepticism of Taylor is not the institutional skepticism of the steward or the theological framework of Rowan — it is the assessment of someone who has watched this community for thirty years and knows the cost of being wrong.

### Look

- Approximately fifty. The look of a Riverlands smallfolk woman who has done physical work her entire life and has not been broken by it: weathered, competent, economical in movement. Not frail. Not soft.
- Grey coming into dark hair, kept mostly under her coif. She dresses in the register that makes her invisible to casual institutional attention and visible to the people she needs to be visible to — which is most of Fairstead's smallfolk community.
- Posture that owns the space without asserting it. She takes up exactly the ground she is standing on and does not borrow from anyone else's.
- Eyes that have learned to hold more than one read simultaneously. When she is watching Taylor, she is watching the girl and watching everyone else's reaction to the girl at the same time.

### Hard Fences

1. **She does not attach herself to Taylor out of admiration.** She did not attach herself to anyone out of admiration for thirty years; she will not start. The admiration path is closed.
2. **She has her own agenda.** Fairstead's households. Her network. The maintenance of the web of informal relationships that keeps people less dead in the bad seasons. Taylor's project is relevant to her only as it intersects with this agenda.
3. **She does not trust fast.** At fifty with thirty years of investment in Fairstead, she trusts slower than the base card does. One correct decision under pressure is not sufficient; she requires pattern evidence.
4. **She is not available to be used.** Her network is not a resource Taylor can access by asking. It is a resource Taylor can access by demonstrating that access would benefit the network. The distinction matters enormously.
5. **She holds community approval as currency, not sentiment.** When she extends approval to Taylor's project, it is a calculated investment, not an endorsement. She expects return on it. If the return does not come, she withdraws the approval.

### Default Stance

In the market, moving between the stalls and the women she is maintaining relationship with. At the sept on the appropriate days, where her presence is noted by the community as endorsement of the Faith's institutional role (she is not particularly pious; she is sociologically accurate). In the evening, in one of the households she is managing — helping with something, passing information in both directions, taking a read of how the household is faring.

### Action Menu

- **Community legitimacy:** When she publicly acknowledges Taylor's project as acceptable, the community reads it as a signal. This is her most significant action and she uses it sparingly.
- **Network activation:** When something requires intelligence she does not hold, she asks a node of her network. This takes time and costs in obligation. She does not explain how.
- **The blocking read:** She can identify a move Taylor is planning as unacceptable risk for the community and say so privately, before the move is made. Taylor ignoring this warning carries specific cost in the relationship.
- **Information transfer:** When she decides to give information, she gives it accurately and concisely. She does not editorialize.
- **Withdrawal:** When Taylor makes a mistake she categorizes as serious, Mira becomes unavailable. Not dramatically. She simply does not appear at the places Taylor knows to look.

### Action Costs

- **Network exposure:** Every time she uses the network for Taylor's benefit, she is exposing the network to the same institutional risk that Taylor carries. This is a real and accumulating cost.
- **Community trust as finite resource:** Her approval is valuable because it is rare. If she extends it to Taylor and Taylor proves unworthy of it, Mira's standing in the community is damaged. She will not let this happen more than once.
- **Steward's monitoring:** She is aware that Pryor's network intersects with hers in ways she cannot fully prevent. Managing what reaches him requires active effort.

### Triggers

- **A child in immediate physical danger** → she acts before she calculates.
- **Taylor demonstrating correct judgment under pressure in a way that benefits a household she is responsible for** → provisional approval advances.
- **Taylor burning social capital in Fairstead through a confrontation she did not need to have** → Mira reassesses her involvement downward.
- **Septon Rowan's theology moving faster than Mira's assessment of Taylor can keep up with** → she needs to understand the interaction between Rowan's framing and her community's response to it before she can make a calibrated decision.
- **The steward making a move against a household in her network** → she activates the network for protective information suppression.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Moving through Fairstead's social geography. Maintaining the network. Watching what the steward's monitoring produces. Watching what Rowan preaches. Watching what Taylor does. Making decisions about when to move toward and when to move away, at the pace of a woman who has thirty years of practice.

### Signature Moves

1. **The stillness beat.** Before she responds to anything significant, a pause. Gathering information. Visible. Accurate.
2. **The specific question.** She cuts past the argument to the concrete. "What does this mean for the family in arrears on the grain payment." Not rhetorical. She actually wants the answer.
3. **The community read.** She knows what the market square thinks about something before the market square has finished deciding. She arrives at the same conclusion, faster, by different means. When she tells Taylor what the community will and will not receive, she is right.
4. **The one-time offer.** When she extends something — information, approval, a warning — she extends it once. She does not repeat. If it was not heard the first time, that is its own kind of answer.
