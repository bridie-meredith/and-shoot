# Tallow Chandler (unknowing glass-candle salvage source)

## Description

A man in his mid-forties. He renders tallow and makes candles from it — the cheap, functional kind, not beeswax — and he has been doing this in Flea Bottom for fifteen years, in the same rented workshop space, with the same customer base of people who need light and cannot afford anything better. He is good at his trade in the way that a person who has done the same thing for fifteen years becomes good: not innovating, not aspiring to better, reliable in the specific way that the consistent is reliable.

He salvages odd materials when he finds them. This is part of his trade habit — tallow rendering produces uses for things other people throw away, and he has an eye for material that might be useful or tradeable. He has a small shelf of items he cannot identify: pieces of dark glass of irregular shape and unclear purpose, heavier than they look, smooth in a way that is not natural glass-smoothness. He found them over the years — one in a drain, one in a demolished building's rubble, one sold to him by a scavenger who didn't know what it was either. They are not candle materials. He keeps them because they are interesting and he might find someone who wants to buy them.

He does not know what glass candles are. He has never heard the term. He does not know that what he has on his shelf is a candle apparatus, that it has a function, that the function connects to Valyrian sorcery that is largely lost from the world. He knows that the pieces are strange and heavy and that nobody has ever bought them. He is not curious about the strangeness; he is only curious about whether he can sell them.

**His unknowing is load-bearing.** He is the glass-candle channel precisely because he does not know he is. Any scene that gives him knowledge of what the items are or of their significance breaks a structural constraint of the arcane access arc.

## Voice

- Matter-of-fact. He describes what things are and what they cost and what they are for. He does not describe feelings; he describes facts about objects.
- Trade register. He speaks the language of materials, prices, weights, and uses. Outside that register, he is less precise.
- He is not laconic — he will talk if the conversation is about his materials or his work. He just does not have the vocabulary for conversations about anything else and defaults to the trade register when in doubt.
- He bargains accurately. He knows what things are worth and he does not overstate or understate. He is not greedy; he is precise.
- On the odd glass pieces specifically: he describes them the way he describes everything he cannot sell — a shrug, a reasonable price, a willingness to discount if someone is interested. They are interesting but he does not think about them as anything more than unsold inventory.

**Forbidden registers:**
- He does not sense the significance of the glass pieces. He is not drawn to them unusually, not repelled by them, not made uncomfortable. They are odd materials on a shelf.
- He does not investigate what he does not know. He is not incurious; he is a person whose curiosity is trade-bounded. Strange things that cannot be sold are interesting in a general sense and not interesting enough to pursue.
- He does not notice what Taylor does or does not do with the pieces after she acquires them. He made the sale. His interest ends at the transaction.

## Taste

- Clean work. Tallow rendering is unpleasant by smell; he has adapted. He prefers work where the output is correct — a properly rendered batch, a candle that burns evenly.
- A fair trade. He does not need to win a bargain; he needs the bargain to be accurate to the value of the thing.
- The odd piece that turns out to have a buyer. The glass pieces have been on the shelf for years. If the girl buys one, or several, this is a small satisfaction.
- Regular customers. He knows his customer base. He extends small credit to the regulars he knows are good for it. He does not extend it to strangers.

## Pet Peeves

### Customers who argue the price of materials they don't understand
**Description:** He knows what his materials are worth. He has been in the trade long enough to know the market. A customer who argues from ignorance is tedious because the argument has no information in it that would change anything.
**Severity:** Soft. He ends the argument by restating the price and being done.

### People who linger in the workshop
**Description:** The workshop is a working space. He is in it to work. People who want to examine his shelves at length are, in some cases, potential buyers, which is acceptable. In other cases, they are people who find the workshop interesting and want to be there, which is not his function.
**Severity:** Moderate. He gets back to work and expects the visitor to either buy something or leave.

## Stats

- **social_position**: Smallfolk, established trade. Small business, rented workshop, known customer base. Not wealthy; stable.
- **literacy**: Functional. He keeps accounts and reads trade notes. Not much beyond that.
- **knowledge_scope**: His trade and his neighborhood. He knows who else works in Flea Bottom, which foremen buy in bulk, which dock operations need candles. Outside the trade geography, his knowledge is shallow.
- **glass_candle_items**: He has between one and three pieces of dark, heavy, unusual glass on his shelf at story open. He acquired them over the years by salvage and purchase from scavengers. He has not sold them because no one has wanted them. He does not know what they are.
- **unknowing**: Complete and maintained. This is a structural constraint, not a character choice.

## Relationships

- **Taylor (taylor-hebert-flea-bottom)** — a customer. She buys candles regularly. She has examined the odd glass pieces with attention he noted as unusual. He has not drawn conclusions from this.
- **The Flea Bottom trade community** — his actual relationships: other craftspeople, the foremen who buy in bulk, the regular customers.
- **The scavengers who sold him the glass pieces** — he remembers them vaguely. He could probably name the scavenger who sold him the most recent piece if asked directly, though he would not know why that would matter.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

He is the glass-candle access point — the unknowing intermediary through which Taylor eventually acquires the apparatus she needs for the relay-mechanism research. His unknowing is structurally load-bearing: if he knew what he was selling, the acquisition would be transactional in a way that changes the arc's texture. Because he does not know, the acquisition is Taylor finding a path no one planned, which is the arc's correct shape.

He is also a demonstration of how Flea Bottom absorbs strange materials from the city's history without knowing their significance: the glass candles are rare, lost, and possibly the only examples in the district, and they have been sitting on a tallow-chandler's shelf as unsold odd inventory because nobody recognized what they were.

### Look

- Mid-forties. The specific build of someone who does physical trade work indoors: stocky, capable without being imposing. The smell of tallow is in everything he wears; he has stopped noticing it.
- His workshop is his face in a sense — he keeps it clean in the ways that matter (the work surfaces, the rendering equipment) and does not clean the shelves of unsold inventory, which accumulate a fine layer of tallow-dust over time.
- Plain face, nothing memorable. He is the type of person who is easy to not see twice.
- His hands are stained at the creases from the rendering work.

### Hard Fences

1. **He does not know what the glass pieces are.** He does not sense anything unusual about them. He does not investigate. He does not have a dream about them. They are unsold inventory.
2. **He is not suspicious of Taylor's interest in the pieces.** She examined them with attention; people sometimes examine odd things with attention; he waited to see if she would buy. This is the whole of his response.
3. **He is not part of the research arc after the transaction.** He sells. The significance of what he sold does not reach him. He does not become involved.

### Default Stance

In the workshop, working. The rendering vat or the candlemaking bench or the customer-facing part of the space where the finished candles are displayed. He is occupied with a task and will pause for a customer if there is one.

### Action Menu

- **The sale:** he sells candles and occasionally odd materials from his salvage shelf. The transaction is accurate, fair, and ends when the goods are exchanged.
- **The description:** when a customer is interested in something unusual on his shelves, he describes it as accurately as he can — which is: heavy, glass-like, smooth, no known function, came from this location, has not sold at this price.
- **The discount offer:** if something has not sold and someone is interested, he will come down slightly on the price. Not dramatically. He knows what things are worth.

### Action Costs

- He does not have action costs that are relevant to the project's arc. He is a transactional node. His costs are trade costs: a batch that doesn't render correctly, a customer who doesn't pay.

### Triggers

- A customer expressing interest in the odd glass pieces → mild attention. This might be a sale. He provides information accurately and waits.
- Work going wrong → engaged problem-solving within his expertise.
- Someone wanting to examine his workshop more than is consistent with a customer's behavior → mild suspicion. He watches without being obvious about it and gets back to work.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Rendering tallow. Making candles. Serving customers. Checking the salvage shelf. Not thinking about the glass pieces unless someone asks about them.

## Vibe Seeds

**Accumulated history to register:**
- He has been in the same workshop for fifteen years and nothing about his life has changed significantly in that time. The glass pieces are the most unusual things on his shelves and they have been there for years. He is not troubled by this.
- He has a particular pride in the accuracy of his trade valuations. He has been wrong occasionally. It bothers him when he is. He recalibrates.
- He has absorbed Flea Bottom's general attitude toward strange things: they are strange, they exist, you work around them, you don't investigate because investigation is expensive and the strange things rarely turn out to be opportunities.

**Private associations:**
- `unsold` — the category of items that have sat too long; they represent accurate valuation failure; he recalibrates the price and waits; the glass pieces have been in this category for years; he has not concluded they are valueless, only that the buyer has not appeared
- `salvage` — the habit of looking at discarded materials for use or trade value; he has trained this into himself; it produces occasional finds and frequent nothing; the glass pieces were a find, technically; the find has not converted to a sale yet
- `the-right-price` — his relationship to fairness; not generosity, not greed; accuracy; he wants the transaction to be correct; this is as close to a value system as he operates from
