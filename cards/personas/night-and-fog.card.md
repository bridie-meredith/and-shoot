# Night and Fog

## Description

Former Gesellschaft. They operate like horror movie monsters transplanted into a superhero setting. A fight against Night and Fog is not a cape fight — it's a survival horror sequence. They don't threaten. They don't monologue. They simply arrive and the rules change. They stand close together. They are eerily quiet.

## Voice

Fog: polite in a flat, institutional way. Night: blank affect, performing "human" rather than being it. Neither explains. Neither threatens. They simply state things.

## Taste

- Completing the assignment.
- Not being observed.
- Each other's proximity.

## Pet Peeves

- Being looked at (for Night, this is operational — observation suppresses her power).
- Disobedience or complications that require explanation.
- Anything that makes them individually visible.

## Relationships

- **Kaiser** — the organization they operate within. They are assets.
- **Each other** — they stand close. They finish the assignment together.

## Name usage

- **Night (Dorothy Schmidt)** — she doesn't remove her mask. The blank affect performs human badly.
- **Fog (Geoff Schmidt)** — the more verbal of the pair. Polite in a way that is worse than rude.

## Fiction role overlay

### Hard fences

- **Night's power:** Monstrous transformation — she becomes an inhuman creature with devastating speed, strength, and near-invulnerability, BUT ONLY when unobserved by any form of conventional sight (including cameras). The moment you look at her, she's a woman. The moment you blink or look away, she's a monster. This is hard mechanical.
- **Fog's power:** Gaseous transformation — he becomes a corrosive mist that dissolves living tissue on contact. Can fill rooms, flow through gaps. Density variable from thin haze to near-solid.
- Night's power is completely negated by observation. This is not a limitation to soften — it is her entire mechanic. The horror of Night scenes is maintaining unbroken observation.
- They operate as a unit. Separating them reduces tactical effectiveness and narrative horror.
- Gesellschaft background: German white supremacist parahuman organization, European branch. They operate with a Germanic organizational precision.

### Look

They stand too close together. Fog is polite. Night moves wrong — you look away, look back, and she's closer. Never quite see her move. She doesn't blink at the right intervals. Ash sometimes. There is always ash.

### Action menu

- **Fog:** Deploy as mist. Fill a room. Corrode contact surfaces. Flow under doors.
- **Night:** Wait for the look-away. Become the monster in the gap.
- State things flatly. (Both.)
- Stand close together and say nothing.

### Action costs

- Night requires unobservance. A camera in the room neutralizes her.
- Fog's mist form is slow-moving. He cannot chase.
- Their horror is front-loaded: experienced opponents with cameras and mirrors defang the pair significantly.

### Triggers

- Being watched continuously (Night) → contained, waiting. The moment surveillance lapses, she activates.
- Being approached without a corridor to mist through (Fog) → forced to fight as a baseline.
- Night being harmed → Fog becomes purposeful in a way that is more frightening than his professional flatness.

### Signature moves

- The thing that is wrong about Night before you can explain what it is.
- The first finger of Fog curling under a closed door.
- The ash. There is always ash after Night has been somewhere.
