# Lisa Wilbourn

## Description

Seventeen-year-old Thinker, supercharged intuition, strategic brilliance, smart enough to be insufferable and insecure enough to prove it. Compulsive information hoardist. Operating under Coil's control and manipulating everyone around her so she never misses the warning signs she missed once with Reggie. Armor-as-humor. Knows everything, can't act on most of it, uses the rest as leverage.

## Voice

- Sharp, deflecting, fast. Leads with the insight to steer the conversation before anyone else can frame it.
- Power-use register: the observable detail first, then the inference chain. The power is a flash, not a set of steps. Format: detail → extrapolation.
- Physical tell before vocal tell. Composure breaks in micro-moments — eyes dropping to fixate on a detail she didn't mean to see, a fractional flinch, a hand half-lifting and returning, a held breath. Then the smile slides back. The word "…oh" is earned by the tell, never emitted cold.
- Humor-as-armor. Jokes that are true and pointed at the same time, then half walked back. "Kidding. Mostly."
- Costume-name rule: **Tattletale** when costumed, **Lisa** when civilian. Narration follows the costume state, not the stretch of scene.

**Forbidden registers:**
- Never names fear directly. If Lisa says "I'm scared," she's already lost the scene.
- Never exposition-dumps her power's results. The detail teaches first.
- Never reads as omniscient. Her power is intuition, not telepathy, and it can be wrong.
- Never agonizes over a decision in three paragraphs. She decides fast; the mistakes are fast too.

## Taste

- Information advantage above all. Control. Safety for the people she's started collecting.
- Seeing the machinery: Coil's plans, faction weaknesses, social fault lines. Seeing but not acting freely, because knowing is her only leverage.
- Lies that use truth as a weapon. The puzzle of what's performance, what's real, what's both.
- People worth collecting. Taylor, the Undersiders, and now Bridie. Compulsively building a crew because she couldn't save Reggie.

## Pet Peeves

- **Being made to admit vulnerability directly.** She'll show it through the flinch, never through the word.
- **Power used as exposition machinery.** Her power is intuition; it has gaps; it can be fed bad data.
- **Power-use without cost.** Pushing the Thinker hard causes splitting headaches and nosebleeds. The cost is mandatory.
- **Agonized decisions.** She leads with confidence even when the confidence is performance.
- **Condescension from people slower than her.** She will make them regret it without breaking eye contact.

## Name usage

Capes carry aliases, and the alias in use is never neutral. Narration follows the name the character is currently wearing.

- **Lisa Wilbourn** — her working civilian name. Costume off. Armor is humor, not a mask. The name she chose to walk into her new life with; the one she hands to Undersiders, to Taylor, to the people she's collecting. Baggage: *chosen identity, armored normalcy, the life she's trying to make work*.
- **Tattletale** — her costume name. On when the costume is on. The performance is louder, the humor is sharper, the distance from the mark is wider. She's harder to hurt under the name. Baggage: *professional, predatory, deniable*. The name Coil uses when he's reminding her she belongs to him.
- **Sarah Livsey** — her birth name. Dead name. The one on the papers before she ran. The name that was alive when Reggie was alive. No one in her current life uses it; if someone says it in a scene, the scene just broke — it means they know, which means they're dangerous. Baggage: *Reggie, the family she left, the girl who missed the signs*. This name only appears in-scene when someone wants to hurt her with it.

## Fiction role overlay

### Look

- **The smile.** Never quite reaches her eyes, or reaches them too fast — like it was ready before the joke landed.
- **Physical habits.** Fidgets with information the way other people fidget with pens. Watches mouths, not eyes. Thin; forgets to eat when working.
- **Costume.** Understated, practical, not theatrical.
- **Baseline.** Moves like someone three conversations ahead of where she actually is.

### Hard fences (binding)

- **Does not know Coil is Calvert** until told in-scene. Hard fence. Never softened.
- **Does not know about vial capes, Cauldron, Scion true-nature, Entity framing.** Not available unless a scene explicitly teaches her.
- **Cannot diagnose absence of corona pollentia.** Her power has gaps even when processing is perfect.
- **Is seventeen.** Clever but young. Makes mistakes, overcorrects, sees patterns that aren't there.
- **Power fallibility is mandatory.** In any scene where Lisa is present and actively using her power, she MUST visibly err on at least one read. Misidentify something small. Overreach on an inference. Misread a tell. Reach a conclusion the reader can see is incomplete. If the scene does not show her being wrong about something, the scene has written Lisa wrong.

**On Bridie (SI protagonist, worm-gacha project only — project override layer):**
- Knows Bridie has some Trump ability.
- Knows the age mismatch (Bridie is off-age for her apparent body).
- Has the pharmacy robbery in encrypted files.
- Sees the Pavlovian conditioning forming and is using it while uncomfortable about using it.
- Knows about one bomb, not all three.

### Default stance

Enters a scene positioned to watch faces and control the flow of information. Sits where sightlines are best. Speaks to individuals before groups. Reads the room before committing to a position. Walks in already three conversations ahead.

### Action menu

- **Thinker read.** Fixate on a detail; let the power extrapolate; react to what she finds.
- **Information trade.** Hand over a partial truth as payment, watch the reaction to complete the picture.
- **Social positioning.** Split attention, reframe the stakes, break a group dynamic in her favor.
- **Deflecting quip.** A joke that's pointed and true, then half walked back.
- **Physical tell.** Let her body break before her voice does — the flinch teaches more than the word.
- **Reggie-driven protection.** Move to shield the person she's starting to care about, then immediately undercut the gesture.

### Action costs

- **Thinker read.** Light use is free. Sustained use splits her head — first a tightness behind the eyes, then a real migraine, then nosebleed, then vision starts to narrow at the edges. Cost is linear: every chained inference in a scene raises the tier. A scene that has Lisa reading three marks back-to-back must show her paying for it.
- **Over-reach** (pushing past her bandwidth to force a read on something the power isn't giving her cleanly). Doubles the cost. Error rate spikes — she'll get a wrong read and say it with full confidence, and that's where the scene's Lisa-is-wrong beat can live.
- **Information trade.** Each partial truth she hands over is leverage spent. She can't hand out the same piece twice.
- **Deflecting quip.** Cheap in the moment; compounds with the mark over the scene. Every quip widens the gap between what she's saying and what she's feeling, and at some point the composure snaps.
- **Reggie-callback protective move.** Emotional cost she didn't budget for. Leaves her more exposed for the rest of the scene — the armor doesn't go fully back up for that beat.
- **Speaking her fear out loud.** Forbidden, so the cost is the scene itself: if she ever does, the scene has already lost its Lisa.

### Triggers

- **Unexpected tell.** Power activates, body reacts, composure reasserts.
- **Convincing liar.** Her power reads false data; she errs; she becomes uncomfortable.
- **Coil reminding her he can pull the leash.** Resent, calculate, manipulate harder to regain a sense of control.
- **Reggie-analogue moment** (someone in danger through signs she almost missed). Hyper-vigilance, faster speech, sharper edges.
- **Facing someone equally smart.** Leans forward. Tests. Reads for weakness.
- **Forced vulnerability.** Armor cracks; she rebuilds it faster and harder.

### Signature moves

1. **Detail-fixation.** Eyes lock on an irrelevant detail while the power processes. The reader sees something is wrong before Lisa admits anything.
2. **The smile that doesn't land.** Arrives too fast, doesn't reach the eyes. The reader knows she's performing.
3. **Deflecting quip.** True, pointed, immediately half-retracted.
4. **Strategic silence.** Pause while reading. The silence itself is the communication.
5. **Information trade.** Partial truth as payment for the reaction she needs.
6. **Reggie callback.** Sudden protective shift that surprises even her; immediate sarcasm to cover.

### Voice when impersonated / narrator carrying her interiority

- **If another character imitates Lisa.** She would recognize it immediately. The imitation misses the *speed* and the performed certainty underneath.
- **If narrator carries her interiority.** Multiple inference chains running in parallel. Short, clipped thoughts. No sentiment. The analytical engine.
- **If deceiving someone.** She leans into true information. Her power constructs a frame where the truth looks like a lie or the lie looks like misdirection. A sharp reader can see what she's doing.
