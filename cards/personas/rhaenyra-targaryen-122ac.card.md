# Rhaenyra Targaryen — 122 AC Variant (Age ~25)

## Description

Rhaenyra Targaryen at approximately twenty-five years old, at Dragonstone. The base card covers her as a thirteen-year-old heir; this variant encodes the specific 122 AC configuration required for this project.

In 122 AC: she is named heir, married to Laenor Velaryon (the marriage is political and their domestic arrangement is its own matter), recently widowed of Laena Velaryon through grief-adjacent death in 120 AC, the mother of Jacaerys Velaryon (age ~3) and Lucerys Velaryon (age ~2). She is at Dragonstone rather than court — the position of a named heir whose succession is contested enough that presence at court would require daily management of the contest, and whose removal to Dragonstone is a managed distance. She is not at King's Landing in 122 AC. She is at Dragonstone, and Dragonstone is her court now.

The succession question in 122 AC: Rhaenyra is the named heir but Alicent's sons exist, they are male, and the Green faction has been consolidating for years. Viserys I's position that his daughter is his heir has not wavered — but Viserys is declining and the Green faction is not waiting for him to change his mind; they are managing the institutional reality that will obtain when he cannot hold the question closed. Rhaenyra knows this. She has known it for years. Her agenda at 122 AC is not reactive; it is active: maintaining Black faction loyalty, managing Dragonstone as a functioning political base, attending to the succession question from a position of dignified distance rather than daily court combat.

Her active succession agenda puts pressure on Taylor's choices throughout. Taylor's intelligence deliveries consolidate the Green faction; Green faction consolidation damages Rhaenyra's position; Rhaenyra is at Dragonstone pressing her claim through faction management and court-adjacent maneuvers. Taylor does not work for Rhaenyra; she works against Rhaenyra's position, knowingly, and the contempt Taylor develops is in part specific to knowing that she is the instrument of Rhaenyra's foreclosure.

She does not know Taylor exists. She is observable primarily through court rumor, intelligence-product inference, and Black-faction-figure encounters in KL.

## Voice

**Register:** claimant register, dignity-under-erosion. She is not aggressive; she is not performing victimhood. She is a woman who has spent years holding the line of her own legitimacy against a sustained institutional campaign to redraw it, and the register that has developed in this holding is a specific combination of formal authority and controlled frustration.

**What is observable through court rumor and intelligence inference (Taylor's access):**
- Her letters to Viserys come to court and are read or not read; the pattern of response-or-non-response is itself an intelligence product.
- Black-faction figures who travel between Dragonstone and KL carry specific news-register that Taylor can read for faction temperature.
- Otto's intelligence reports on Rhaenyra's factional maneuvering are a secondary source: accurate about events, wrong about motivation in the way all of Otto's reports are wrong about motivation.

**Dialogue samples (synthesized — from court-rumor reports available to Taylor):**

"My father named me heir before the council. That naming stands."
*(The standing claim in its minimal form; she does not argue; she states)*

"Dragonstone has always flown the heir's banner. I see no reason to change that."
*(Dignified distance; not threatening; the banner is a fact)*

"The council's deliberation on succession is noted. My lord father has not asked me to deliberate."
*(Managed distance; the formal courtesy that holds the claim without engaging the contest)*

**Voice tells:**
- She does not engage the contest at its own level. When the Green faction moves a piece, she names the board configuration, not the move.
- Controlled frustration present in the specific completeness of her register — she is saying more than the words, by using words that require attention to decode.
- When she is with her children, the register drops one register tier. This is not observable through Taylor's access; it is a fact about her private life that the intelligence product cannot reach.

## Taste

- Her own claim, which is also her father's decision, which is also her right. These are not separate items for her; they are the same item from three angles.
- Her children. Jacaerys and Lucerys are young; she is building Dragonstone as the base they will grow into.
- Black-faction loyalty maintained through real political investment, not through performance. She understands the difference.
- The Dragon. Syrax remains. The bond is older now and settles differently than at thirteen.
- Her father's continued life as a structural fact. While Viserys lives, the succession question cannot be finally resolved against her. She is aware of this in the specific way a person is aware of a thing they are not yet ready to calculate the alternative for.

## Pet Peeves

**Her succession treated as an open question**
Same as Alicent's analog, but from the opposite position. For Rhaenyra, the succession is not open; it was resolved in her favor. The Green faction treating it as open is not confusion — it is bad faith. She does not perform frustration about this in court contexts; she registers it in private.
Severity: strong.

**The Velaryon question raised**
The paternity of her children is a question the Green faction raises through appropriate court whispers. She does not engage this publicly. The specific contempt for the Green faction's use of this question — not as an honest uncertainty but as a destabilizing instrument — is present in her register without being named.
Severity: strong to blocker.

**The managed distance from court**
She chose Dragonstone and she continues to find it the correct choice; but the specific experience of watching court maneuvers from a remove, unable to counter them in real time, is a friction that accumulates.
Severity: soft, persistent.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The road not taken — the faction whose continuity Taylor's work undermines. She is not a villain; she is the ironic register of what "the Dance" will cost: her Black faction's loss is one of the costs that Taylor's accurate deliveries purchase. She is the political-register carrier the reader needs to feel the succession struggle as a live question with live stakes, not as a background condition. Her active agenda at Dragonstone makes "Green-faction rise = Black-faction loss" visible as an on-page irony rather than a historical note.

She is also the road Taylor did not take — but this contrast is at the court/faction level, not the smallfolk level (the septon holds the smallfolk-level contrast). Taylor could have delivered intelligence to the Black faction instead. She did not. The reader needs to feel that this was a choice, not an inevitability, and Rhaenyra at Dragonstone with her active agenda makes the alternative feel alive.

### Look

A young woman of twenty-five who carries the Targaryen look — silver-gold hair, violet eyes — with the specific settledness of someone who has been stared at for this reason since childhood and has stopped finding it interesting. She is at Dragonstone, not court; her presentation reflects the heir's dignity without the court's performance register. Practical by Dragonstone standards, which are not Flea Bottom practical but are the practical of someone running a household and a faction.

Observable through court rumor only: Taylor cannot observe her directly. The physical description is a background fact that Taylor might hold from intelligence products; it does not appear in Taylor's direct observation.

### Hard Fences

1. At Dragonstone throughout the project. No direct scene contact with Taylor. Observable only through court rumor, intelligence-product inference, and Black-faction-figure encounters in KL.
2. Her active succession agenda must arrive as a pressure on Taylor's choices, not as background. The chapter contracts must name how her agenda arrives in Taylor's accounting at each relevant delta.
3. She does not know Taylor exists. The observation is one-directional and mediated.
4. Black faction claimant. She is not ambivalent about her claim. The specific register she carries is dignity-under-erosion, not uncertainty.
5. This variant overrides the base card's period-specific configuration. The base card covers ~110-113 AC. In any context where this project is active, this card's 122 AC configuration applies.

### Default Stance

Active management from Dragonstone. She is not waiting; she is working. The specific quality of working from a remove: everything she does arrives at court through intermediaries, and she has calibrated her actions for the transit delay and distortion.

### Action Menu

*Observable through mediated channels (court rumor, intelligence inference) only:*
- **Faction correspondence** — letters to Black-aligned lords; the network she maintains from Dragonstone
- **Dragonstone presence management** — presenting Dragonstone as a functioning alternate court
- **The named claim** — the formal reiteration of her right, sent to her father or to the council in response to specific Green maneuvers

### Off-Screen Cadence

Running Dragonstone as a political base. Managing her children's care. Receiving and sending Black-faction correspondence. Managing Daemon's presence and absence. Running the specific calculus of whether Viserys will live long enough.
