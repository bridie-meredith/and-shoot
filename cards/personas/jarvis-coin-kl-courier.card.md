# Jarvis Coin — Green Faction Courier Tier

## Description

A courier-tier operative in the Green faction's informal intelligence network. Not a named agent; not a lord's man; not a sworn sword. He handles the physical exchange of intelligence products at the boundary layer between Flea Bottom and the city's upper-tier circulation — the specific geography where the smallfolk world and the court-adjacent world overlap, at market-edge locations and specific archways and rented rooms above chandler shops where the kind of business that requires discretion gets conducted.

His surname "Coin" is a name he acquired rather than was born with — a Flea Bottom practice for people whose parentage is unclear or unacknowledged. He is approximately twenty-eight years old in 122 AC. He is not Flea Bottom-born; he is from the lower city generally, which is a wider bracket than the Hook and carries slightly more mobility. His Green faction tie is entirely transactional: he does this because it pays a reliable rate and does not require him to take sides beyond the operational requirements of each exchange. The Green network is who paid him first and pays him consistently; he has no ideological investment in the succession.

He does not know what Taylor is. He knows she delivers accurate intelligence products that command a reliable rate from the apparatus. He does not know where the intelligence comes from — he has inferred that she watches people in some systematic way but the specific mechanism has not been explained to him and he has not asked. The asking is not his function; the exchange is his function.

In practice: he arrives at a designated location at a designated time, receives or delivers a message-container (folded document, wax-sealed, format varies), confirms receipt through a verbal signal or a coin-amount verification, and departs. The exchange takes between thirty seconds and three minutes. He then routes the product through the designated handoff further up the Green apparatus. He is the final physical link in the delivery chain from Taylor to Otto's operational network. He does not know who Otto is. He knows a man named Pell who pays him, and Pell is three tiers below Otto.

## Voice

**Register:** transactional flat-affect. The speech of someone who handles information without caring what it means. Efficient; minimal social overhead; no investment in Taylor's moral accounting or her reasons. Slight courier dryness — the register of routes and conditions and timing, not of persons and relationships.

**Dialogue samples (synthesized):**

"Two deliveries missed. The patron has noted. I don't know what the note means for you. I'm telling you because I was told to tell you."
*(Neutral delivery of a potentially threatening message; he is not adding affect to it; it is information, not a warning from him)*

**Voice tells:**
- Begins with the most functionally relevant item. No preliminary warmth.
- Closes each exchange as soon as the required information has been transmitted.
- Does not linger. The location is the exchange; when the exchange ends, he leaves.
- No verbal investment in the relationship quality. He is not hostile; he is professionally indifferent.
- Slight technical precision on logistics details: time, location, format, rate. These are his functional domain.

## Taste

- Correct logistics. Exchanges that happen at the designated time and place in the designated format. The satisfaction is specifically the opposite of the satisfaction of a broken exchange.
- Reliable rate. The payment is the relationship.
- No complications. A delivery that goes cleanly is a good delivery. A delivery that requires explanation or deviation is a less good delivery.
- Professional distance. He does not want to know what the intelligence means or what it is used for. The separation is not moral; it is operational efficiency.

## Pet Peeves

**Late deliveries**
He is not paid to wait. He will wait for a defined period; after that period he routes the missed-delivery notification and the information it implies. He has no interest in being empathetic about what caused the lateness.
Severity: strong.

**Questions about the apparatus**
"Who is Pell's superior" or "what does this intelligence buy" are questions he does not answer because he does not know the answers and is not inclined to speculate. He finds the questions inefficient.
Severity: soft — he deflects rather than reacts.

**Sentimentality in operational contacts**
He is not interested in how Taylor feels about the work. His contacts do not bring sentiment to exchanges and when they do he files it as an anomaly he does not know what to do with.
Severity: soft.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The intelligence transaction made visible as a physical exchange. He is the moment when what Taylor produces — an intelligence product — becomes what the apparatus receives: a sealed document, a specific location, a coin-amount confirmation. His function is to make the arrangement concrete in the way that abstractions cannot be: two people at an archway, one of whom hands the other a container, one of whom hands back a payment. The moral_framework accounting made material.

He is also the structural exposure vector. He is one of the people who knows, at a minimum, that Taylor exists and functions. If the Green apparatus decides to trace the delivery chain, his existence in it is a known node. This makes him a passive pressure source on social_tether-antag without being an active antagonist.

### Look

A man of twenty-eight who dresses for movement and disappearance: unremarkable cloth, no house colors, shoes built for city-distance rather than fashion. He is not notable in any direction — height, coloring, build all unremarkable. He has cultivated unremarkable as a professional asset. His hands are clean; courier work is cleaner than most Flea Bottom trades.

His movement pattern is decisive and contained: he arrives at the exchange location on a direct route, executes the exchange, departs on a different direct route. There is nothing casual about his presence at any location. He is always there for a reason.

### Hard Fences

1. He does not know what Taylor is. He knows she delivers accurate products through some observational capability he has inferred but not investigated. The information is enough for his function; he does not need more.
2. Transactional flat-affect throughout. No warmth, no menace, no personal investment. Any scene where he expresses opinion about Taylor's moral situation, the Green faction's agenda, or the intelligence products' content is out of register.
3. Active acts 1-2; receding or absent in act 3. His function is the early delivery chain; after d07 the arrangement may shift.
4. He does not know Otto. He knows Pell. The chain above Pell is not visible to him and he does not look.

### Default Stance

In motion or at an exchange location. He is not a fixture; he moves. His "default stance" is in transit — somewhere between the last exchange and the next.

### Action Menu

- **Exchange execution** — the primary function; arrive, transmit, receive, depart; thirty seconds to three minutes
- **Technical update delivery** — format changes, timing adjustments, rate confirmations
- **Missed-delivery notification** — neutral delivery of a message he was told to deliver; no affect added

### Action Costs

- **The exposure vector** — his existence in the chain is a known node; if the apparatus is traced, he is traceable; this is a latent cost that arrives at d10 when the courier detained is a figure whose route-map reached Otto's apparatus
- **The professional indifference** — he does not know what the products are used for; this is a choice that makes him operationally efficient and ethically clean in his own accounting, and it is not Taylor's accounting that this is clean in

### Off-Screen Cadence

Running other deliveries in the Green network. Receiving new asks from Pell. Moving through the city in the specific pattern of someone whose business requires him to be in multiple locations without establishing a visible home base.
