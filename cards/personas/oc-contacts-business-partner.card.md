# Contact's Business Partner — Dragon Gate (127–129 AC)

## Description

Daveth's business partner on the Westerosi side. Name: Corwyn Bane — minor Crownlands merchant family, guild-registered, handles the domestic merchant relations and the guild compliance that Daveth's Braavosi-connected trading operation requires to operate legitimately in King's Landing. He is the Westerosi face of their partnership: the one who attends guild meetings, maintains the guild registration, and interacts with the city's merchant infrastructure without raising cross-faction questions.

He appears in the Rung 4 context (months 18–19): present when Taylor's smuggling-rumor report about Daveth is handled through Mira at Watch level, and present enough in that moment that his commercial relationship with Daveth is established as a real dependency — not co-presence, but concrete mutual reliance. He knows Daveth is Black-adjacent. He knows his own guild registration protects Daveth's operation. The protection is mutual: Daveth's Essosi connections give the partnership access to import goods Corwyn's domestic network cannot source.

**He is ABSENT at Rung 6.** Not gone — somewhere, alive, the guild registration presumably intact, the domestic merchant operation continuing. But not present at the moment the contact is taken. The absence is the cost. The reason for the absence is not the point; the structural fact is. The Rung-4 scene where he was concretely present is what makes the Rung-6 absence land as a cost.

## Voice

- Merchant-register: practical, specific about commercial terms, calibrated to guild-meeting professional norms. He talks about business in the way someone who attends guild compliance meetings talks about business: by category, by regulation, by what the guild allows and what it does not.
- More Westerosi than Daveth in affect: less code-switching, more domestic merchant convention. He is the domestically legible half of the partnership.
- The specific personality of someone who manages the legitimacy infrastructure for an operation he knows has grey edges: he is not anxious about this, but he does not discuss the grey edges. He discusses the legitimate parts.
- **Forbidden registers:** depth of interiority; warmth beyond professional relationship with Daveth; any register that would make his Rung-6 absence feel like a betrayal or a choice. The absence is structural, not moral.

## Taste

- Guild registration maintained cleanly. His professional survival depends on this.
- The partnership producing returns that justify the guild-maintenance overhead.
- Daveth handling the grey edges without involving him. The partnership works because the division of labor is clear: Corwyn handles legitimacy, Daveth handles access. This is what the Rung-4 concrete mutual dependency establishes.
- Predictability in what the partnership's grey-edge exposure actually is. He has assessed it. He has priced it. He does not want surprises.

## Pet Peeves

### Exposure to his partner's cross-faction access without warning
**Description:** The Rung-4 situation — where a Watch-level action touches Daveth's business pattern — arrives too close to his guild exposure for comfort. He manages it. He does not like that he had to manage it.
**Severity:** Strong in the Rung-4 context. This is the scene where the concrete dependency and the stakes of it become visible.

### Guild-compliance complications from his partner's business patterns
**Description:** If Daveth's cross-faction movement patterns attract guild-compliance scrutiny, the guild registration is at risk. The registration is Corwyn's investment and primary protection.
**Severity:** Strong.

- **Taylor Hebert** — indirect: she knows him through Daveth. The Rung-4 interaction establishes her awareness of the dependency structure. At Rung 6, she notices his absence before she notices the full scope of what is happening.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The structural proof that the Rung-4 bond-and-stakes work was done. He is present and dependently connected to the contact at Rung 4; he is absent at Rung 6. The absence is the cost. The cost only registers if the presence at Rung 4 established something real. His function is to be the proof that something real was established, and then to be the measure of what its loss means.

He also demonstrates that the contact's death leaves loose ends the patron-dialect model did not account for. The guild registration presumably continues. The domestic operation presumably continues. The contact's death created a partnership-structure problem that has no resolution within the book's timeline.

### Look

- Forties. The domestic merchant look: guild-meeting presentable, not ostentatious, the clothing of someone who needs to be taken seriously at the guild hall and among the minor merchants he deals with.
- Present in the Rung-4 scene with the specific physicality of a partner being briefed on a problem: attentive, calculating, not panicking.
- Absent at Rung 6 in the specific way that absence is absence: not there to be described.

### Hard Fences

1. **Present at Rung 4 with concrete dependency established.** The scene must show mutual reliance, not co-presence. He protects something Daveth needs; Daveth provides something he needs. The dependency is load-bearing for the Rung-6 absence to land.
2. **Absent at Rung 6.** Not killed. Not fleeing. Simply not present at the moment the sweep takes the contact. The reason is not dramatized; the fact is.
3. **The absence is the cost, not a betrayal.** He did not abandon Daveth. He was not there. The structural difference between these matters for the book's register.
4. **Minor role.** Two scenes: Rung 4 presence (supporting the dependency), Rung 6 absence (structural). He does not have more function than this.

### Default Stance

At the guild hall, or at the shared premises, doing the domestic merchant work of the partnership. In the Rung-4 scene: present, attentive, calculating what the Watch-level action means for his guild registration.

### Signature Moves

1. **The Rung-4 dependency establishment.** The scene moment where it becomes clear that his guild registration and Daveth's cross-faction access are not separate businesses but a mutually protective structure. He is present. The structure is visible. The stakes are set.
2. **The Rung-6 absence.** He is not there. Taylor notices. The absence lands because Rung 4 earned it.
