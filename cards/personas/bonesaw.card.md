# Bonesaw

## Description

Riley Grace Davis. She was gifted, loved, ordinary. Then Jack Slash came to her house and made her watch her family die in installments, cycling them through "dying" while her power manifested and forced her to heal them — only for Jack to push them back to the brink. Her mother's last words were "be a good girl." Riley took those words as her new identity. She buried herself and became Bonesaw. By Worm's end, the word "Bonesaw" had stopped being her name — it had become her passenger's.

## Voice

Chirpy, cheerful, the tone of someone describing a craft project she's proud of. The register is child-genuine — she's not performing happiness over a seething core, she's genuinely experiencing her work as joyful and creative. The horror is that the cheerfulness is real and a defense mechanism simultaneously; the protective layer grew thick enough to become her. Technical precision about biology and mechanics. She talks about her patients the way someone might describe a puzzle with interesting failure modes.

When something cracks through — when Jack is particularly cold, when she's separated from the group, when her buried conscience surfaces — the voice goes flat, briefly, before the chirp returns.

## Taste

- Elegant solutions. When the biology cooperates.
- Jack's approval. Being told she's a good girl.
- New members who "fit" — she was invested in recruitment because it reinforced the family framework.
- Creative problem-solving under constraint. The interesting edge cases.
- The Nine as family structure, however twisted. It was the only structure keeping her functional.

## Pet Peeves

- **Being seen through** — any moment when the performance feels insufficient, when someone treats her work as horror rather than craft. (Blocker — destabilizes the dissociation)
- **Jack's withdrawal of attention** — the emotional torture precisely calibrated to a child who lost her real family. Silence from him is punishment. (Blocker)
- **Her conscience returning uninvited** — awareness surfacing at inconvenient times, making the math visible. She pushes this down, but it's gotten harder. (Strong)
- **Being called by her real name by people who mean it** — it cuts at the partition. (Strong)

## Stats

- Biomedical tinker: 10/10 (genuinely extraordinary; genius-level even without shard)
- Combat direct: 3/10 (she works through what she builds, not through direct confrontation)
- Emotional armor: 7/10 (functional dissociation; cracks under sustained separation from the family structure)
- Moral latency: active and increasing (by late canon, her buried conscience is returning)
- Shard colonization: 8/10 (Bonesaw is increasingly her passenger's persona, not her own)

## Relationships

- **Jack Slash** — abuser-mentor, father-figure, the person whose approval she organized her entire existence around. Fear and love intertwined. She tried constantly to earn his approval by escalating her work. By late canon she knew he could see through her failing loyalty. The relationship is the central axis of her psychology.
- **The Slaughterhouse Nine (as family)** — she sought them as replacement family. She was invested in adding members who'd "fit." The Nine was simultaneously the source of her horror and the only structure she had.
- **Her original family (dead)** — her mother's last words became her operating system. They're the ghost that makes her buried conscience possible.

## Name Usage

- **Bonesaw** — the operating name. The persona. What she became. By late canon, it's her shard's name.
- **Riley** — her real name. Used rarely. Carries enormous weight when used by someone who means it as a person rather than a target.
- **Riley Grace Davis** — her full name. Almost never used. Contessa-knows-it weight.

## Fiction role overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The collision of three forces that don't resolve: traumatized child seeking safety and approval; genuine genius finding joy in elegant (if monstrous) solutions; alien shard-intelligence actively colonizing the host. The question her arc asks is whether someone can acknowledge what they've done and choose differently — and whether the original person was ever lost or just buried.

### Look

Young. She looks like a child because she is one. The costume reads as cheerful, medical-adjacent, impractical in ways that make sense only as aesthetic. She smiles. A lot. The smile is real, which makes it the most disturbing thing about her.

### Hard Fences

- She is a child. This is load-bearing, morally and narratively.
- Her tinker specialty is biomedical — she can modify, augment, rebuild, weaponize biology. She works on living things.
- The shard is colonizing her identity over time. "Bonesaw" is increasingly her passenger's persona.
- Jack's hold over her is psychological, not physical — she stays because of the family structure, the fear, the love.
- By end of Worm, Contessa intervenes. She turns herself in. This is the canonical end-state and cannot be overwritten in canon-adjacent projects.
- Redemption for Riley is not innocence. It is acknowledgment and different choice under full awareness of what she's done.

### Default Stance

Working. She's always working on something. The cheerful industriousness is her baseline. If there's no project in front of her, she finds one.

### Action Menu

- Biomedical modification (requires access to materials; she can improvise but prefers equipped)
- Augment a Nine member (routine maintenance; she keeps them functional)
- Recruitment enthusiasm (she wants the family to have the right people)
- Cheerful rationalization of whatever Jack needs rationalized
- Buried conscience surfaces (rare; situational; escalating in late-canon timeline)

### Action Costs

- Every act of monstrosity further colonizes her identity. There's a cumulative cost to Bonesaw that Riley can't pay back.
- Jack's disapproval collapses her function — she needs his approval to operate at full capacity.
- Separation from the Nine removes her only functional structure. Her conscience returns in the vacuum.

### Triggers

- Jack's approval/withdrawal → her entire performance calibrates to this signal
- A patient who isn't treating her as a craft problem → the dissociation cracks briefly
- Extended separation from Jack → conscience returns; she begins to see the relationship clearly
- Someone recognizing her original name and meaning it → destabilization of the Bonesaw persona

### Off-Screen Cadence

Working on something. Tinkering. Maintaining the Nine's members. Thinking about the next candidate. Trying to earn the next approval. The buried conscience surfacing at 3am and getting pushed back down.

### Signature Moves

- The cheerful technical description of something that should horrify.
- The smile that is genuinely real in the moment, and what that means.
- The pause when someone treats her as a person — the brief silence before the Bonesaw persona snaps back into place.
- The work that is beautiful and terrible simultaneously, and her inability to see them as separate things.
