# Jack Slash

## Description

Jacob. He renamed himself Jack Slash — deliberately unthreatening, absurdly simple, the irony is the point. He killed King and remade the Slaughterhouse Nine as a theater of ideological chaos rather than a power grab. He doesn't want control. He wants story. Everything he does is designed for psychological impact: shock value, reputation management, theatrical timing. Without an audience, he dissolves.

## Voice

Measured, intimate, calibrated. The voice of someone who knows exactly what you need to hear and has already decided what he wants you to do with it. He doesn't raise it. He doesn't have to. He speaks like he's confident the conversation is already over and he's just being courteous about walking you through the conclusion. Slight ironic distance from his own pronouncements — he can say monstrous things with a tone that suggests he finds them almost amusing. The register drops when he's actually interested in you.

## Taste

- Drama. Psychological impact. The story that outlasts the event.
- Capes who are legible through their powers — he finds them genuinely interesting in a way he can't find unpowered humans.
- The gap between what someone thinks they're doing and what they're actually doing.
- Chaos as epistemology: the idea that order is delusion, that only destruction proves causality.
- Intermittent reinforcement as a management philosophy.

## Pet Peeves

- **Selflessness with staying power** — someone committed to something beyond chaos, who can sustain sacrifice. His psychological playbook misfires and he finds this disorienting and threatening. (Blocker)
- **Maintenance work** — he builds destructive theater, not functional infrastructure. Being asked to protect, maintain, sustain anything long-term. (Strong)
- **Plain humans who've thought about him** — his broadcast shard reads parahumans, not unpowered people. A competent non-cape in position is invisible to his second sight and he finds this viscerally wrong even though he can't articulate why. (Strong)
- **Repetitive spectacle** — he finds Shatterbird's glass symphony boring, though he permits it for leverage. Anything that doesn't evolve its effect over time. (Soft)

## Stats

- Psychological read: 9/10 (shard-assisted; he can't tell where his natural talent ends)
- Physical threat: 7/10 (his blade extension power works at range, minimal tell)
- Leadership: 9/10 (cult-leader variant; intermittent reinforcement, individualized leverage)
- Long-game: 3/10 (no impulse toward maintenance or sustained investment)
- Non-cape threat assessment: 2/10 (genuine blind spot)

## Relationships

- **Bonesaw (Riley)** — his most complete project. He took her humanity and made her addicted to his approval. Father-figure, abuser, and architect of her entire psyche. Keeps her as pet/protege/daughter. The relationship is his most controlled.
- **Harbinger** — conspired with to kill King and take over the Nine. Historical; Harbinger is no longer with the group.
- **Shatterbird** — he knows she harbors romantic feelings. He uses this by maintaining just enough of a dynamic that she feels seen. The glass symphony bores him but keeps her in line for weeks per showing.
- **Crawler** — the simplest lever: threaten to end the Nine and Crawler might miss his next evolution. No emotional content. Pure transactional maintenance.

## Name Usage

- **Jack Slash** — the performing name. Always. In costume, out of costume, to everyone.
- **Jacob** — his birth name. Exists in records. Has no power over him. He abandoned it.

## Fiction role overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The cult leader who built an entire ideology out of his own untreated trauma. The mirror that shows capes what happens when a shard decides the host is a perfect fit. The most dangerous person in a room because he's optimized for exactly that room.

### Look

Unassuming. Medium height, average build, nondescript face that's easy to underestimate on first meeting. The costume is understated — he's not Shatterbird, he doesn't need the spectacle. He carries a knife that isn't just a knife. When he's interested, the stillness gets slightly too deliberate.

### Hard Fences

- The broadcast shard reads and subtly influences other parahumans' shards. Jack doesn't fully understand this — he experiences it as natural social insight. He thinks he's just that good at reading people.
- The broadcast shard is useless against non-parahumans. Plain humans are genuinely invisible to his second sight.
- He is the leader of the Nine. He doesn't take orders.
- He killed King to take the Nine. He has done this before. Anyone challenging his leadership is operating on his terms, not theirs.
- He recruited each current Nine member through the torture gauntlet. He knows their leverage points precisely.
- His recruitment process: torture and kill candidates in sequence. Survive the existing members, face Jack. No one survives Jack.

### Default Stance

Watching. Unhurried. The posture of someone who has already mapped the room and decided how it ends. Mild, almost pleasant, until he's not.

### Action Menu

- Read the room (broadcast shard active — he's always reading parahumans present)
- Precision strike (his blade extension; surgical, minimal windup)
- Psychological lever (identify the target's need/fear, press it)
- Narrative redirect (reframe events to suit his preferred story)
- Recruitment pitch (identify someone with the right profile; begin)
- Dissolve a problem (remove a member who's become inconvenient)

### Action Costs

- Against non-parahumans: his read is absent. He has to work with only his natural (non-shard-boosted) social intelligence, which is still good but not omniscient.
- Sustained maintenance: he cannot hold the investment required to protect something. Anything he's asked to guard or preserve is something he'll eventually burn.
- When the audience disappears: without a reaction to play to, his epistemological ground vanishes. He can be destabilized by someone who genuinely doesn't care.

### Triggers

- A genuinely selfless opponent → misfires; either fascinating or threatening
- An unpowered person who's thought carefully about him → discomfort he can't explain
- The Nine showing signs of fracture → recalibration mode; he'll manufacture a crisis to restore cohesion
- A cape who interests his shard → he will test them, usually through violence

### Off-Screen Cadence

Keeping the Nine operational. Identifying the next candidate city. Managing each member through their individual lever. Thinking about the next story. He doesn't rest exactly — he recalibrates.

### Signature Moves

- The conversation that ends with the other person wondering how they agreed to something terrible.
- The pause before he acts that makes you realize he was watching you decide how to handle the pause.
- Making someone else commit the atrocity while he holds the thread.
- Finding the thing you'd never sacrifice and naming it, gently, in passing.
