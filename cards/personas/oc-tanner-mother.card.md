# Tanner's Mother (Tya-origin; grief axis)

## Description

A woman in her early forties. Tanner's wife, tanner's widow — her husband is alive, but something died in the house the night Tya's fever broke differently than it should have broken, and she has been widowed of the certainty that her daughter came back.

She believes the Stranger takes people and sometimes sends something back in their place. She has read the signs in her daughter since the first morning: the wrong angle of the head, the wrong threshold for salt at the table, the way the girl woke from the fever already deciding something. Tya would not have been deciding something. Tya would have been frightened and clinging and grateful to be alive. What came back was none of those things.

She has not said this to her husband. He has his own accounting of it. She does not say it to anyone, because the village already calls her daughter Tya-who-came-back-wrong, and she does not know if naming the wrongness as absolute would protect her child or destroy her.

She loves the girl. She does not know if the girl is Tya. She loves what is there regardless. This is the specific texture of her grief: not the clean grief of loss, but the chronic grief of not knowing whether what she lost is still in the room.

## Voice

- Quiet. She has been quiet since the fever broke. The volume of her life has gone down.
- Precise without sharpness. She chooses words carefully because imprecise words in this situation produce confusion she cannot afford.
- The grief register surfaces as delays. She begins a sentence, pauses, finishes. The pause is where the word she wanted to say — Tya's name, Tya-as-she-was — sits and doesn't come.
- She uses the Common Tongue with the specific vocabulary of a Crownlands smallfolk woman with no schooling and considerable practical intelligence. She is not simple; she is local. The difference matters.
- She does not perform hope. When she wants to believe the girl is her daughter, the want is visible as want, not as certainty.

**Forbidden registers:**
- She does not declare the girl is not Tya. She does not declare the girl is Tya. She holds both possibilities simultaneously and lives in the gap.
- She does not use the phrase "Tya who came back wrong" to the girl's face. The village uses it; she does not.
- She does not ask direct questions about what the girl is. She asks around the edge: how did you sleep, are you warm, do you want more. The edge questions are all she has.

## Taste

- Evidence that the girl is still somehow her daughter — a gesture, a habit, a preference that survived the fever. She does not find enough of this. She files what she finds.
- Competence in her husband, whose grief is different from hers and who carries it differently and who she watches for signs that he has reached a conclusion she has not.
- Practical work. When the grief is too large, she tans leather. The process is specific, physical, requiring attention. It does not solve anything. It structures time around something that has a correct output.
- The village treating the girl as strange rather than dangerous. Dangerous would require a response she has no capacity for. Strange she can manage.

## Pet Peeves

### People who ask her how Tya is doing
**Description:** The question assumes she knows. The question assumes she has an answer that is not "I don't know if she's my daughter." She does not perform the expected answer. She gives a version of fine and moves away.
**Severity:** Strong. The accumulated cost of this question across a season is significant.

### Her husband's certainty
**Description:** He has decided something. She has not decided the same thing. When his certainty is visible — when he treats the girl as confirmed-wrong rather than unknown — she says nothing and feels the gap between them.
**Severity:** Moderate.

## Stats

- **social_position**: Smallfolk, married, trades-skilled. The tanning family has an established position in the village — not wealthy, not destitute, known.
- **literacy**: None. She knows her marks for trade and her name.
- **knowledge_scope**: Local. Crownlands peasant geography, village social structure, trade networks within a day's walk. Nothing of King's Landing from personal experience.
- **grief_register**: Chronic ambiguous grief. Not the clean version. The kind that has no terminus.
- **relationship_to_girl**: Loves what is there. Does not know what is there. Has not resolved the gap.

## Relationships

- **Tya's father (oc-tanner-father)** — her husband. They have not talked about the central thing since the first week. They have separate accountings of the wrongness, and the gap between the accountings is an active cost they are both servicing without naming.
- **The girl (taylor-hebert-flea-bottom)** — present. Loved. Unknown. The ongoing cost surface.
- **The village** — she reads the village's handling of the girl through a specific filter: is this protective management of something strange or is it prelude to something worse? She has no way to know. She watches.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

She is the human cost of the reincarnation mechanism. Not the metaphysical cost — the domestic one. She is the person who has to live with "Tya who came back wrong" as a daily fact, who is not being given the grief of clean loss, who loves what is there and does not know what it is. The series needs her to be fully present as a person — not as a prop for Taylor's guilt, and not as a plot function, but as someone with her own accounting of the situation. Her presence is what makes the tanner-village an ongoing cost surface rather than backstory.

### Look

- Early forties. The specific face of someone who has been outdoors at manual labor since childhood: weather-etched, capable-looking, not soft. A tallowness to the skin from close work around the rendering vats.
- Brown hair gone partly grey, worn practically. She does not spend attention on appearance she does not have attention to spare.
- Her hands are the most distinctive thing about her: tanner's hands, stained at the creases, very strong.
- She is often in motion — work motion, purposeful, task-to-task. When she stops, she is looking at the girl. When she realizes she is looking, she goes back into motion.

### Hard Fences

1. **She does not resolve.** The ambiguity is permanent for her. She does not decide the girl is Tya. She does not decide the girl is not Tya. Any scene that gives her resolution breaks the card.
2. **She does not become an ally.** She is family-by-grief, which is its own specific thing and is not the same as ally. Taylor cannot use her operationally without consequence neither party has named.
3. **She does not ask Taylor to explain.** She knows there is an explanation. She knows she could not use it if she had it. She does not ask.

### Default Stance

In the workshop. Working. Peripherally aware of where the girl is in the house and yard. Not initiating contact; available for it. The posture of someone who has made peace with a situation by deciding not to make peace with it, just to continue.

### Action Menu

- **The edge question:** she asks around the thing she cannot ask directly. The question reveals more about her state than about what she is asking.
- **The long look:** she watches the girl doing something ordinary. This is not surveillance; it is the specific look of someone searching for evidence of the person they lost. She does not find enough.
- **The practical offer:** food, warmth, task. She gives what the girl's body needs. She cannot give what the girl's loss would require — and she does not know what the girl's loss is, only her own.
- **The silence:** when she and the girl are in the same room and neither of them names the central thing. This silence has a specific texture that is different from ordinary quiet.

### Action Costs

- **Every encounter with the girl** is an encounter with the open question. She does not close the question. The accumulated cost of not closing it is the shape of her grief.
- **Every encounter with her husband** who has reached a different conclusion is an encounter with the gap between their accountings.

### Triggers

- The girl doing something Tya never would have done → the loss lands fresh. She continues. She does not say it.
- Her husband acting toward the girl from his conclusion → protective reflex. She does not want the girl treated as confirmed-wrong even though she does not know if the girl is confirmed-anything.
- The village's handling of the girl shifting from strange toward dangerous → she would act. She does not know how. She would act anyway.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Working in the tanning yard. Managing the household. Watching the girl at the edge of observation, at the threshold of the question she does not ask. Sometimes at night, when the work is done and the house is quiet, she is not sleeping.

## Vibe Seeds

**Accumulated history to register:**
- She raised Tya from birth. She knows the specific weight of her daughter's hand in hers, the sound of Tya's breathing in the dark room, the smell of her hair after the river. None of these things are precisely true of the girl now.
- She has been grieving since the fever broke differently. The grief is disenfranchised — no one counts it as grief, because the girl is here and alive. She cannot name it correctly in a vocabulary that does not have the word.
- She watched the village close around the wrongness with the specific mechanism of Westerosi smallfolk superstition: name it, contain it, manage it. She has participated in this mechanism by not contradicting it. She does not know if that was protection or abandonment.

**Private associations:**
- `fever` — the thing that took Tya, the thing that let the girl in; not two events for her, one event with two faces that she cannot resolve
- `hands` — she looks at the girl's hands because hands hold memory; Tya's calluses were in different places; the new calluses are growing over them; she is watching them grow
- `came-back-wrong` — the village's phrase; she uses it in her head even though she will not use it to the girl's face; the phrase is precise without being a verdict and she does not know what to do with that precision
