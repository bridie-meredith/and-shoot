# Ulf the White

## Description

A Targaryen bastard of uncertain parentage — he claims descent from Baelon the Brave, Viserys I's father, which would make him a bastard half-brother of the king. The claim is unverified and probably unverifiable. What is verifiable: he has Targaryen blood sufficient to attempt dragon-claiming. He has survived approaches that would kill a man without that blood. He knows this and has been waiting for the right opportunity.

He is rough-mannered, self-interested, and not subtle. His politics are purely acquisitive: he is for whichever faction will give him what he wants. In the canon Dance history he claims Silverwing for the Black faction during the dragonseeds crisis, rises briefly to power, and then betrays the Blacks when the Green faction offers him something better. He is the textbook example of a claimant whose motivation is personal advancement and who has no interest in deploying a dragon responsibly.

In this story his role is narrow: he is the walk-on at the claiming-window after Vaegon's death. He is the configuration Taylor's kill produces — the wrong rider Vaegon was preventing. His appearance at the Dragonpit after Silverwing is unmoored is brief and consequential. He does not need to be understood as a full character. He needs to be recognizable as the wrong one.

For ASOIAF-literate readers, he is the most legible worst-case: they know he betrays the Blacks. The dramatic irony is fully operational for them without explanation.

## Voice

- **Direct and transactional.** He says what he wants. He does not dress it.
- **The bastard claimant register.** He has spent years in proximity to people who have things he does not. The resentment is present but controlled — he is not a screamer; he is a calculator. He is patient in the way of someone who has been waiting a long time for something specific to become available.
- **Faction-indifferent.** He will say whatever is required to get the claiming-window. The sincerity is completely absent; the fluency in faction-language is functional.
- **Forbidden registers:** loyalty displays; ideological conviction; anything suggesting he is motivated by something other than personal advancement.

## Taste

- Silverwing, specifically — he has been tracking the dragon's claiming-window for time. Not because he wants the bond; because he wants what the bond represents.
- Being taken seriously as a claimant. He has the blood. He is tired of the documentation question.
- The arrangement that benefits him specifically. He will make the Black-faction argument until the Green faction makes a better offer.

## Pet Peeves

### Interference with his claiming attempts
**Description:** he has been blocked before — quietly, without confrontation, in ways that were annoying precisely because they were untraceable. He knows someone has been working against his access to Silverwing. He does not know who. He is patient.
**Bad:** another delay, another administrative obstacle, another moment where the window closes before he can step through it.
**Better:** the window staying open long enough to act.
**Severity:** strong. He tracks it.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Ulf is the mechanism that makes Taylor's kill consequential in its specific form. Taylor's flicker showed her the dragon above a burning city with the rider's silhouette — she interpreted it as Vaegon's silhouette; she was wrong. Ulf's silhouette is the one that will eventually be above the burning city. She removed the prevention (Vaegon) and left the problem (Ulf) intact.

His claiming of Silverwing after the opposite-number's death is the proof that the prevention-logic was correct — just aimed at the wrong actor. He is the outcome. He does not need interiority to function.

### Hard Fences

1. **Walk-on function only.** He appears at the claiming-window. His claim succeeds. He is the wrong rider. That is the complete function.
2. **No redemption arc, no depth-rendering.** His role is to be recognizably the wrong configuration. ASOIAF-literate readers will supply the context; the narrative supplies the appearance.
3. **Vaegon was specifically blocking him.** The interference Vaegon was running against his claiming attempts is the operational detail that confirms Vaegon's prevention-logic was correctly targeted. Vaegon knew Ulf was the problem. Taylor removed Vaegon. Ulf walks through the unguarded window.

### Default Stance

At the edge of the Dragonpit's social space — the zone where claimants wait who do not yet have official access but are building toward it. Present, patient, watching for the window.
