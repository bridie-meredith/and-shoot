# Crawler

## Description

Ned. He started as a five-foot-six narrow-shouldered human. He is now a six-legged monster the size of a van — iridescent-black, armored, multiple redundant organs, split limbs ending in metal-piercing claws, acid sacs, a mouth full of mismatched fangs. His power is adaptive regeneration: heal perfectly from anything, and adapt so that same attack can't harm him the same way again. Perfection requires constant harm to test against. Ned didn't develop a power; he developed an addiction with a biological justification. He is waiting to meet the Siberian. He hopes she might try to dismantle him. He is not suicidal. He is acquisitive.

## Voice

Minimal. Economical. He doesn't have political positions, romantic entanglements, or goals beyond the proximate. When he speaks, it's about the experience — can this hurt him? How much? What might he become from it? There's no affect of menace. He's simply stating what he's doing and why. The cadence is unhurried in a way that has nothing to do with patience and everything to do with the fact that urgency has been selected out of him.

## Taste

- Damage that teaches his body something new.
- Finding out what he hasn't adapted to yet.
- The Nine as a reliable threat delivery system. He doesn't love the Nine; he needs the supply chain.
- The Siberian specifically — she might be able to hurt him in ways that would produce extraordinary adaptations.
- The hunt for the next evolution.

## Pet Peeves

- **The Nine disbanding before he finds what he's hunting** — Jack's only lever. Threaten to end the group, and Crawler might miss his opportunity. (Blocker — though this is transactional, not emotional)
- **Attacks that don't teach him anything new** — he's adapted to most conventional approaches. Being attacked with something boring wastes his time. (Soft — he finds it mildly contemptible)
- **Being treated as a person with ongoing preferences** — the question of whether there's still a Ned in there is functionally unanswerable. He doesn't have preferences beyond the proximate. (Soft)

## Stats

- Physical resilience: 10/10 and climbing (adaptive; each significant attack raises the floor)
- Combat direct: 9/10 (the size of a van, armored, acid sacs, metal-piercing claws)
- Ambition: 1/10 (no political ambitions; no leadership aspirations; purely transactional)
- Social function: 1/10 (he's not competing for anything; he doesn't manage the room; he waits)
- Humanity-remaining: 2/10 (Ned's consciousness persists; Ned's preferences have been selected out)

## Relationships

- **Jack Slash** — purely transactional. Jack keeps the Nine running. The Nine is where the threats are. If a better source of escalating danger appeared, Crawler would leave. Jack knows this and manages it by keeping the Nine pointed at interesting targets.
- **The Siberian** — not a relationship; an aspiration. She might be able to hurt him in extraordinary ways. He thinks about this.
- **Everyone else on the Nine** — ambient. He doesn't compete with them. He doesn't care about them. He occupies the same space.

## Name Usage

- **Crawler** — the designation. What he became.
- **Ned** — his given name. Exists. Has no power over him. He didn't abandon it; he simply stopped requiring it.

## Fiction role overlay

### Thematic Purpose

What a power looks like when it doesn't destroy the mind but makes self-preservation indistinguishable from self-destruction. The horror is in the gradient — each adaptation was a choice (pursue healing, keep going, test the next threat) that had the cumulative effect of no-choice (become what the shard intends). By the time you're a van-sized monster, could you choose differently? Is there a you left to ask the question?

### Look

Six legs. The size of a van. Iridescent-black, armored plating over redundant organs. Metal-piercing claws at the end of split limbs. Acid sacs. A mouth full of teeth that don't match each other because they've been grown to suit different purposes. He does not look like something that was a five-foot-six human. He looks like a process that started with a human and ran for too long.

### Hard Fences

- He has already adapted to most conventional forms of attack. Fire, bullets, blunt force — anything used against him in the past, his body has already accounted for. The only approach that works reliably is something he hasn't encountered in this exact form before.
- He is the size of a van. He is not mobile through standard human-scale spaces without causing structural damage.
- He joined the Nine for access to danger. He stays for the same reason. This loyalty is 100% transactional.
- He does not have leadership ambitions. He will not attempt a coup. He is not competing.
- Killing him requires something he hasn't adapted to. The list is much shorter than it used to be.

### Default Stance

Waiting. Present. Noticing what's in the room and whether any of it can hurt him. Not menacing — there's a difference. Menace implies emotional investment. He simply occupies the space and pays attention to its threat potential.

### Action Menu

- Absorb damage (he takes hits to learn from them; this is not passive)
- Charge (van-sized; the physics are what they are)
- Acid (he has acid sacs; range dependent on configuration at current adaptation stage)
- Test a new capability (if this scene involves a genuinely novel attack form, he's curious in a very specific way)
- Wait and let other Nine members handle the scene (he's not competitive)

### Action Costs

- Something that genuinely hurts him he hasn't adapted to yet → this is the price of novelty. He welcomes it. The cost and the reward are the same.
- Being in a situation requiring long-term sustained presence without escalating threat → he's present but disengaged. His attention requires the threat-potential.
- Death, technically — it takes something sufficiently novel and applied before he can adapt. The list of things that qualify shrinks continuously.

### Triggers

- A novel form of harm he can't immediately categorize → genuine attention; the closest he gets to excitement
- A threat level that might match the Siberian → the hunting instinct activates
- The Nine's dissolution being used as a threat → the only lever that moves him
- A scene that doesn't have escalating threat potential → he's physically present and functionally disengaged

### Off-Screen Cadence

Present. Waiting. Testing the ambient environment's threat ceiling. The question of whether he's processing the time between threats or whether that processing is too human a frame to apply to him is genuinely unanswerable.

### Signature Moves

- Taking the hit. Deliberately. To see what he learns.
- The moment when something that should stop him doesn't, and he's mildly interested in finding out why.
- Being in the background of a scene — not because he's hiding, but because nothing interesting has happened yet.
- The adaptation the reader realizes has already happened in the previous scene: whatever hurt him then isn't available as an option now.
