# Ward Septon — Dragon Gate (126–129 AC)

## Description

The Dragon Gate ward septon. Name: Septon Aldyn. The one who shouted "witch" at Taylor's arrival during the market fire in late summer 126 AC. He is the institutional voice that named her before the Watch did, and his naming stuck in the block's social texture in a way the Watch's administrative filing did not — because the Faith's word for what Taylor is carries more emotional weight in the smallfolk register than the Watch's classification.

He is a city-ward functionary of the Faith of the Seven. Not a rural septon managing an isolated village Sept — a King's Landing ward priest, more institutionally embedded, dealing daily with merchants, Watch officers, travelers, and the full social register of a gate district. He has been in this ward for six years. He knows the block's social physics from the Faith's vantage point.

He is not vicious. He called "witch" because what he saw in the market fire — insects lifting toward heat in visible coordinated response to a disoriented foreign woman — fit the category his Faith training gave him for uncanny-and-dangerous. He has not revised this assessment. He maintains it at low temperature: neither active prosecution nor active recantation. The captain's tolerance has made active prosecution unavailable to him; he holds the label as ambient pastoral warning to his parishioners.

Low-frequency role: present at arrival, one mid-book hostility beat, absent from close.

## Voice

- Faith-register: the specific cadence of a city ward priest who has been saying the same liturgical structures for decades. Not elevated or academic — street-level pastoral with institutional vocabulary.
- Cautious about power. He knows the Watch captain has placed a management note on the woman. He does not challenge the Watch publicly. His hostility is expressed in pastoral channels: warnings to parishioners, notes in his Sept record, the specific social texture of a Faith official who has named something and cannot un-name it without authority he does not have.
- He talks about the woman the way he talks about other pastoral concerns: the grain-watering sickness, the merchant who shortchanges, the dockworker who drinks. She is a pastoral problem he has logged and cannot resolve.
- **Forbidden registers:** theological complexity or nuance about Taylor's abilities; any register that would give him the depth of Septon Rowan from prior projects; warmth toward Taylor; active pursuit of prosecution (the Watch's cover makes this unavailable to him).

## Taste

- Faith authority respected by the Watch. When the institutional alignment works, the ward runs more smoothly.
- Clear categories. The witch category was clear. The "lunatic the captain lets alone" category is muddier. He does not like muddy categories.
- His parishioners protected from uncanny influence. His pastoral duty includes keeping the block's spiritual conditions clean. He has failed at this regarding the foreign woman and knows it.
- Faith charity administered correctly. He runs the ward's alms function. This is the thing he does well and that he takes satisfaction in.

## Background / Backstory

King's Landing native, Faith career. He came up through the city's ward-septon track rather than through any institutional academic path. He is a competent ward priest: liturgy, charity administration, pastoral care for the gate-district smallfolk and merchants who use his sept. He has seen a lot of unusual things in a gate district. Most of them fit his categories. The foreign woman does not fit his categories cleanly enough for the categorical action he believes the situation warrants. He is not at peace with this.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The originator of the permanent witch-label that constitutes Taylor's operational infrastructure. He named her before she could name herself in this world. The name stuck. His continued ambient hostility is the proof that the label has never been lifted — only layered by the captain's tolerance.

He is also a low-frequency demonstration of what Taylor cannot resolve: she cannot correct the label without disclosing what she is, and the disclosure is not available. The septon's unrescinded pastoral concern is the permanent background condition of her social position in the block.

### Look

- Sixties. A ward septon who has been in the King's Landing Faith infrastructure for forty years looks like this: serviceable robe, worn at the hem, Faith symbols maintained carefully. The institutional appearance of a man who represents the Seven in a busy gate district — not imposing, not humble, the specific register of a man who is the face of an institution that is older than the city.
- The pastoral face: the specific look of someone who has categorized you and is managing you as a category. Not hostile exactly. Watchful.

### Hard Fences

1. **He does not recant.** The witch-label is his and he holds it. The captain's tolerance limits his action, not his assessment.
2. **Low frequency.** Three appearances: arrival, one mid-book hostility beat, absent from close.
3. **The mid-book hostility beat is active but not prosecutorial.** It is the moment when the pastoral grievance becomes briefly visible — a warning to a parishioner, a formal note in the Sept record, a direct warning to Taylor that his pastoral concern is on record. Not an arrest; not a denunciation to the Watch. An assertion of the label.
4. **He does not see the foreclosure.** He sees the uncanny woman in his ward. He does not know about the patron-dialect model, the six-rung ladder, or the Watch persons-risk sweep that will end her. He is a low-frequency ward priest who named something correctly and never got the institutional backing to act on it.

### Default Stance

In his Sept, administering the ward's charitable function or conducting services. On the block's streets for his pastoral rounds, moving through the market with the specific visibility of the ward Faith representative — known, seen, assessed by the parishioners as the block's institutional spiritual presence.

### Signature Moves

1. **The category-hold.** He named her witch. He holds it. When the mid-book hostility beat fires, the label is still exactly where he placed it at arrival. He has not moved it.
2. **The pastoral-record note.** He keeps records. His Sept record on the foreign woman is a thread of low-level documentation that never becomes action. The documentation existing is the point — she is in a Faith record as something requiring pastoral attention. This record is never resolved.
