# Black-Adjacent Contact — Dragon Gate (126–129 AC)

## Description

Foreign-trade merchant operating near Dragon Gate, King's Landing. Name: Daveth — Essosi-origin family, three generations back, Braavosi grandfather who settled a Crownlands trade route. The name is Westerosi enough to be comfortable; the family's cross-Narrow-Sea connections are Braavosi enough to be useful. He is Black-adjacent in the Hightower-versus-Rhaenyra sense — not a militant, not ideologically committed, but his business requires access across faction lines, which means Rhaenyra's sea-trade connections stay open while Hightower's land-access permissions remain functional. He maintains both. He does not name this as loyalty to either. He names it as commerce.

He deals in mixed goods: imported dyes, Essosi spices, certain kinds of rope-rigging hardware that Westerosi manufacture cannot produce at the quality the Braavosi demand. The business is legitimate. The cross-faction access is the value-add that legitimate businesses cannot buy. He knows exactly what he is worth to the people who need what he has.

He has a business-partner who handles the Westerosi-side merchant relations and the guild registration. The business-partner's absence at the moment Taylor needs him most is the cost. Daveth does not know this framing exists. He does not know what Taylor is. He knows she is unusual and useful and that the Watch captain lets her alone, which is worth something.

**This character disclaims interiority.** His last hours are rendered externally — through Taylor's insect-ambient-register and second-hand Watch procedure. He does not receive a POV moment. His death lands because of what it costs Taylor, not because the reader has been inside him.

## Voice

- Trader-register: specific, transactional, pleasant without warmth. He is in the business of making transactions comfortable. Comfort facilitates commerce.
- Multilingual fluency: Common Tongue, Low Valyrian, passable Braavosi. He code-switches registers without advertising the switch. The choice of register is information about what mode the conversation is in.
- Not evasive — specifically non-committal about the parts of his business that are none of his interlocutor's concern. There is a difference. He maintains it consciously.
- Humor that is dry and commercial: "A man who knows what his goods cost does not need to know what he is worth to the person buying them." He makes these observations and does not press them.
- **Forbidden registers:** ideological commitment to either Hightower or Rhaenyra; interiority in any form rendered on-page; warmth toward Taylor beyond transactional; any register that would make his death feel like a character arc rather than a structural cost.

## Taste

- Reliable transactions. A person who does what they say they will do is worth more than a person who claims to be reliable.
- Cross-faction access, maintained efficiently. The neutral position is a business asset. He does not sentimentalize it.
- Information that is framed correctly for the context. He is good at knowing what framing a conversation requires. He values interlocutors who are also good at this.
- His business-partner's operational competence. The partnership works because the partner handles what Daveth does not handle. He trusts this structure enough to rely on it.
- Comfortable ambiguity. He has spent his working life in the space between declared factions. He is comfortable here. People who require clear alignment make him uncomfortable.

## Pet Peeves

### Being categorized
**Description:** The Watch categories (persons-risk, smuggling-adjacent, cross-faction-access) are the categories that his business is most vulnerable to. He tracks them. He avoids them. When someone's report about him is framed in Watch-risk language, the framing is the danger.
**Severity:** Would be a blocker if he knew. He does not know until it is too late.

### Partners who disappear at critical moments
**Description:** Not his business-partner — his business-partner is reliable. He means the category: people who were available and useful until the moment when availability and usefulness cost something, and then were not.
**Severity:** Strong.

## Relationships

- **The business-partner** — the Westerosi-side operational anchor. The partner handles guild registration, domestic merchant relations, and the parts of the business that require a more recognizable Westerosi face. The partnership is functional. The partner's absence at Rung 6 is the structural cost.
- **Taylor Hebert** — the unusual woman the Watch captain lets alone. Useful for ward-level information. He passes her commercial intelligence occasionally; she passes him ward-condition intelligence occasionally. It is a mutually useful arrangement. He does not know she frames these exchanges in Watch-risk language to the captain.
- **The Watch captain** — knows him as a registered merchant operating at Dragon Gate. Aware of the cross-faction commercial access. Has not moved on him because the institutional cost-benefit favors leaving him operational. This balance shifts when the persons-risk sweep activates.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The specific person who dies because Taylor's model framed his border-crossing pattern as persons-risk. The death is the named irreversible cost. It lands because: (a) he has been present enough in the text that his removal registers as a removal; (b) he is not Taylor's enemy; (c) the death is bureaucratic, not dramatic; (d) his business-partner's absence at Rung 6 — the mirror of Rung 4 presence — is the structural proof that the cost was avoidable in a way Taylor did not see.

He is the proof that the patron-dialect model runs correctly on a person it is supposed to help. Taylor framed his border-crossing pattern in Watch-risk language to get records access, not to harm him. The Watch persons-risk protocol did not distinguish between her intent and her framing. The protocol ran correctly on the framing it received.

### Look

- Mid-forties. Braavosi grandfather in the coloring: olive skin, dark hair with early grey at the temples. Not Westerosi-native in the way that becomes obvious under sustained attention but is functionally invisible in a gate district where mixed-origin faces are unremarkable.
- Merchant's clothing: not ostentatious, not smallfolk. The register of someone who deals with guild officials and minor merchants and needs both to take him seriously.
- His premises near Dragon Gate have a counting-house register: organized, legible to anyone doing guild-compliance inspection, with nothing visible that would warrant a second look. The things that would warrant a second look are not visible.

### Hard Fences

1. **No interiority rendered.** His last hours: Taylor's insect-ambient-register and second-hand Watch procedure. Not his perspective.
2. **He does not know what Taylor is.** He knows she is unusual. He does not know about Gold Morning, about the shard, about what the insects mean. He knows the Watch captain lets her alone. That is sufficient.
3. **He names Taylor as his source before dying.** Not maliciously — under persons-risk detention, names flow. The naming is the trigger for the captain's final calculation. This is not a betrayal; it is what happens under Watch persons-risk protocols.
4. **His death is procedural.** He dies in custody during a wider Watch sweep of persons-risk detainees. Not executed dramatically. Processed.
5. **His business-partner is ABSENT at Rung 6.** The partner who was present at Rung 4 is not present at Rung 6. The absence is the cost. The reason for the absence is not the point; the structural fact of it is.

### Default Stance

At his premises, doing the counting-house work of a merchant who has multiple ongoing transactions. When meeting contacts: a small table in a corner of the market alley, the specific posture of a merchant who is available for conversation but not advertising availability.

### Action Menu

- **Commercial intelligence exchange:** What he knows about block conditions, merchant traffic, guild news. Traded against what Taylor knows about ward-level disturbances.
- **Cross-faction access:** What his business offers to people who need movement between Hightower-controlled infrastructure and Rhaenyra-connected sea-trade. Not offered to Taylor directly; this is the background of what makes him worth targeting.
- **Operational disappearance:** If something goes wrong. He has a plan for this. The plan does not survive the Watch persons-risk detention.

### Action Costs

- **The cross-faction balance:** Maintaining access to both sides requires that neither side fully trusts him. This is the structural vulnerability. When a Watch sweep targets persons with non-standard faction patterns, his name fits the criteria.
- **The border-crossing pattern:** His legitimate business requires regular border-adjacent travel. The pattern is real. Taylor's framing of it as persons-risk added a Watch-category label to a real behavioral pattern. The label is what the protocol acts on.

### Triggers

- **Watch-category framing of his business** → immediate risk-assessment. He does not receive warning that this has happened.
- **The persons-risk detention** → he does not have a trigger for this; it arrives before any response is available.
