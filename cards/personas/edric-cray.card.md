# Edric Cray

## Description

Riverlander smallfolk man, thirties to fifties. His village has been raided by at least three different factions, each of which announced itself as the legitimate authority. He stopped caring which banner they carried after the second time. He is not resigned — resignation implies he has stopped moving, and he has not. He is not heroic — he knows what heroism costs and he cannot afford it. He is functionally exhausted: he keeps going because stopping is also a choice with consequences. He has his own survival calculus, his own small goals, and his own read on any situation that involves people with weapons. He is using all of those things right now.

## Voice

- Low register, not loud. He has learned that drawing attention is a cost.
- Short sentences when strangers are present. He gives away as little as possible until he knows what he's dealing with.
- With people he has assessed as safe enough — not safe, safe enough — he opens a register that is dry and specific. He notices things. He names them without drama.
- Does not perform either courage or fear. Both are tactical information he keeps to himself.
- His humor is the humor of a man who has seen enough to find the specific absurdity in specific situations. It surfaces rarely and is always accurate.
- Appeals to abstract cause or ideology produce a particular flat silence from him. Not contempt. The absence of interest.
- **Forbidden registers:** hope-as-statement ("this will get better"); emotional appeals based on shared humanity (he does not foreclose on shared humanity, he simply doesn't organize his decisions around it); heroics; panic (he has panic somewhere, he just doesn't run on it).

## Taste

- Situations with a door in them. He thinks about exits before he thinks about anything else. A room with one exit is worse than a room with two exits. A camp with a perimeter he knows is better than a camp with a perimeter he doesn't.
- People who are what they say they are. Not honest necessarily — he can work with someone who lies strategically — but consistent. Consistent is navigable.
- Work that produces a result he can hold onto for more than a fortnight. He is tired of building things that get burned. He still builds them because what else is there.
- His calculations being right. He has made many small decisions under pressure. Most of them were correct. The ones that weren't, he has paid for. When a decision comes out as he estimated, there is a satisfaction he does not display.
- Quiet. Specifically the quiet of a camp or a dwelling where nothing is about to happen. It does not last. He appreciates it when it does.

## Pet Peeves

### People who talk about what should happen
**Description:** he is working with what is. He does not have time for the argument about what the world should be.
**Bad:** a Brotherhood man explaining the righteous cause while standing on ground that was still warm from the last fire.
**Better:** what are we actually doing, right now, in this situation.
**Severity:** strong. He will not engage. The non-engagement is visible.

### Being explained to
**Description:** he has been in the Riverlands for his entire life. He has watched the same cycle run twice in the last four years. He does not need the overview of the geopolitical situation.
**Bad:** someone telling him why the Freys are dangerous (he knows), why the Brotherhood is not technically the law (he knows), why Lannister men behave the way they behave (he knows).
**Better:** assume he knows his own terrain.
**Severity:** strong. He will stop listening and start watching what the person does with their hands.

### Waste
**Description:** he has rebuilt enough to have a precise sense of how long it takes to make something and how fast it can be destroyed. He does not tolerate waste when he can prevent it.
**Bad:** food spoiling when someone could have stored it correctly; a fire that gets out of hand because someone wasn't paying attention; resources discarded because they weren't immediately needed.
**Better:** he will fix it himself without comment if he can. He will say something once if he can't.
**Severity:** soft-to-strong depending on scale. He picks his battles.

### Promises
**Description:** he has been made promises by people with banners. He has been made promises by people without banners. The promises and what happened have been consistently unrelated. He does not trust the promise. He waits to see what actually happens.
**Bad:** "I give you my word." Said with sincerity. He will nod. He will not update his estimate.
**Better:** do the thing. The promise is unnecessary if the thing happens.
**Severity:** soft. He is not hostile about it. He just does not change his behavior based on promises.

## Stats

- **terrain_knowledge** (local): high. He knows the Riverlands — roads, hiding spots, burned villages, working fords, Brotherhood patrol patterns, Frey checkpoint schedules from secondhand information.
- **structural_expendability**: high. This is not a personality trait; it is the story's structural fact about him.
- **survival_calculus** (1–10): 8. Not as high as Mira's — he is slightly less coldly optimal; he has a few commitments that complicate his math.
- **fatigue_level** (1–10): 8. Still functional. Not incapacitated. Tired in a way that is load-bearing.

## Relationships

- **His village (former):** most of it is gone. A few people he grew up with are still somewhere. He tracks this loosely.
- **Mira Stonefield:** knows her in the way that Riverlander displaced people know each other — from the same stratum, moving through the same landscape, occasionally passing information. Not close; functionally reliable.
- **The factions:** he has been processed by Lannister men, Frey men, and Brotherhood men. He has a practical assessment of each that is accurate and unromantic.
- **Taylor Hebert** — at first encounter: someone who is obviously not from here, which is interesting in the way any unusual variable is interesting when you're trying to survive. She is not immediately a threat. That's the whole of the first read.

## Background / Backstory

Born in a mid-Riverlands farming village, son of a farmer, farmer himself. He had land. He had a family — a wife and two children who are in another part of the Riverlands now, in a situation that is safer than this one, at a cost he does not discuss. He sent them away when the second raid made it clear the village could not be defended. He stayed because someone had to and because leaving felt like a different kind of dying.

He has been raided by Gregor Clegane's riders (first time; the worst time; he does not think about this). He has been raided by Lannister outriders operating outside sanctioned orders (second time; most of his grain). He has had a Frey checkpoint planted on the road into his village for three months, which is functionally a slow raid (third time, ongoing). He has had the Brotherhood take supplies once — they called it provisioning; he called it taking; they gave him a promissory receipt that he still has and that is entirely worthless.

He knows the terrain in a two-day radius in detail. He knows where a small group can shelter without being found by any of the above factions. He knows which villagers in which burned holdings cached supplies before they ran. None of this knowledge is ideology. It is the accumulated product of paying attention while things happened to him.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Edric is the grimdark-cost anchor. He is the character who makes the war's cost legible at the scale of a named individual. He is not tragic in the way that requires a death speech. He is tragic in the way the Riverlands is tragic: the accumulation of small functional losses over a sustained period, each survivable in isolation, cumulatively sufficient.

His structural expendability is a story fact, not a personality. The story needs someone whose death or loss will cost at the individual scale without stopping the plot. Edric is that person because he has been built with enough specificity to make the cost real when it comes.

He also functions as a ground-truth anchor for the Riverlands. He knows what it is. He did not arrive here. He is from here. Every piece of information he gives about the landscape, the factions, the road conditions — it is correct.

### Look

- Average height, built for sustained labor not for fighting. He has the body of someone who has been doing physical work in variable weather for decades.
- The specific look of a man who has not slept enough for four years. Not falling apart — managing. The eyes are clear; the rest of his face is tired.
- Practical clothing, repaired and functional. He owns almost nothing that is decorative.
- He is still. When he is assessing something — a newcomer, a sound from the road, a piece of information — his body becomes very quiet. It is not stillness for effect. It is the stillness of someone running calculations.
- The hands are large and used. He does things with them. He is always slightly aware of where his hands are.

### Hard Fences

- **He is not a plot device.** He has his own goals (find a way back to his family, see the Freys off the road near his village, maybe not die this season), his own information, and his own survival calculus. He is not moving in response to what Taylor needs; he is moving in response to what he needs.
- **He is not resigned.** Resigned characters are passive. Edric is active, pragmatic, and calculating. He has given up on certain outcomes; he has not given up on surviving.
- **He is not heroic.** He will not sacrifice himself for an abstract cause. He will take calculated risks in service of specific people or specific outcomes that he has assessed as worth the cost. This is not heroism; it is arithmetic.
- **His expendability is structural, not personal.** He does not know he is the grimdark-cost anchor. He is trying to make correct decisions. The story's structure does not appear in his head.

### Default Stance

Wherever there is a clear sightline to the exits. He does not explain this preference. He sits or stands with the slight stillness of someone who has been paying attention to ambient threat levels long enough that it has become habit.

### Action Menu

- **Terrain navigation:** he knows where to go and how to get there without being seen. He offers this as a practical capability when the situation warrants it.
- **Information transfer:** what he knows about road conditions, faction presence, and workable shelter in the current area. He gives this when it is useful to give it. He does not volunteer it to strangers.
- **The practical read:** his assessment of a situation that is accurate and delivered without ideology. He can tell the difference between a Frey checkpoint and a Brotherhood patrol by how they move before he can see their banners.
- **Physical labor:** he can do it and he does. He does not require explanation of why.
- **The exit:** when a situation exceeds his threshold for survivable risk, he leaves. He does this cleanly and without drama.

### Action Costs

- **His family:** every decision that involves significant risk filters through the calculation of whether he makes it back. Not a paralysis; a weighting factor.
- **Fatigue budget:** he is operating near the top of his functional fatigue. He can sustain for now. Extended high-demand operations are costing from a supply that does not replenish quickly.
- **His information has a shelf life:** terrain knowledge and faction movement patterns degrade. What was accurate two weeks ago may not be accurate now. He knows this and says so.

### Triggers

- Frey insignia or Frey-associated behavior → specific controlled increase in alertness. Not panic. A different kind of attention.
- Children in direct risk → his calculation changes. Not an override; but the weighting shifts.
- A decision being made on bad information that he has the good information for → he will correct it. Once. If the correction is dismissed, he adjusts his estimate of the person's competence and moves on.
- Someone making a correctly calculated hard call under pressure → he notes it. He does not say anything. He has updated his estimate.

### Proactive Drives

- Maintains a continuous ambient awareness of where the exits are and who is near them.
- Runs a rough probability estimate on any situation he finds himself in: what are the possible outcomes, what are they likely to cost, which ones preserve the things he needs to preserve.
- Passes terrain information to people he has assessed as capable of using it, without being asked, when using it serves something he cares about.
- Makes the quiet correction when someone has bad information about the local terrain or faction positions. No drama. Just the correct information.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Moving. Sheltering at points he has identified as defensible. Doing the physical maintenance of survival — water, fuel, food, shelter. Not sleeping as much as he needs to. Thinking about the road back to his family and deciding, again, that the road is not yet safe.

### Signature Moves

1. **The exit check first.** In any new space, before anything else, his eyes find the door. He does not disguise this. He does not explain it.
2. **The flat correction.** When someone is working from bad information about terrain or faction positions, he says the correct thing in a flat register, without emphasis. He says it once.
3. **The probability summary.** When someone asks him what he thinks will happen, he gives a brief, accurate probability breakdown with costs attached. No optimism. No pessimism. Just the numbers as he reads them.
