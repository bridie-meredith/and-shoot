# Mira Stonefield — Dragon Gate Variant (126–129 AC)

## Description

Rope-braider. Dragon Gate block resident. Thirty-something — she has been making rope in the same workshop space near the Dragon Gate for the better part of a decade. Her trade is maritime-adjacent: the gate is eastern and landward, but the road through it runs to the river docks, and the river docks need rope in continuous supply. She knows the block better than anyone who does not wear a gold cloak, and she knows the block better than most of those too.

She is not from King's Landing originally. She came from a river-town south of the city, followed a man who worked a transport guild, and stayed after the man did not. The children she has are grown enough to need less from her now. She does not discuss them. She does not need to.

She extended provisional trust to the foreign woman the septon called a witch approximately four months after the woman arrived. Not because she was not a witch — Mira does not have strong opinions about witchcraft — but because the woman had done two things correctly under pressure by then, and Mira's threshold for provisional trust is exactly: one thing correctly under pressure. The second correct thing was insurance.

She is Taylor's genuine attachment. She does not know this is what she is. She knows the foreign woman comes to the workshop more than workshop business requires and does not perform friendship in any recognizable way and nonetheless comes back. Mira has decided this is acceptable. She has not decided why.

## Voice

Base card voice carries without modification. Additions for Dragon Gate context:

**The KL city-register.** She speaks King's Landing smallfolk — not Flea Bottom density, not noble vowels, the gate-district middle register of a craftswoman who deals with travelers, merchants, and Watch patrols as routine. She knows which register to use for each. She adjusts without advertising the adjustment.

**The rope-work cadence.** Her hands are often working while she talks. Her speech has the rhythm of someone whose attention is partially elsewhere in a way that does not indicate inattention — the partial attention is the baseline. She is always doing something with her hands. People who understand this find it comfortable. People who need full attention find it unsettling.

**The Watch-adjacent fluency.** She has been in the Dragon Gate block for a decade. She knows the Watch patrol rhythms, the shift-change schedules, the difference between a Watch officer doing his route and a Watch officer looking for something. She registers Watch presence without appearing to register it. She does not discuss what she registers unless the situation requires it.

**Forbidden registers (full base card carries):**
- Gratitude for being noticed by someone with power — she takes help on her terms, not on theirs
- Admiration for Taylor's abilities — assessment is Mira's register; she has assessed that the foreign woman is unusual and left it there
- Sentimentality about any prior life arrangement
- Warmth-without-basis — she does not warm without evidence; the evidence must be specific and behavioral, not social

## Taste

Base card taste carries. Additions:

- **The rope-work itself.** She is good at what she does. Good enough that she can think about other things while doing it without degrading the product. The work is not tedious to her; it is the substrate on which she thinks.
- **People who do not explain themselves unnecessarily.** The foreign woman does not explain herself. Mira has found this more restful than she expected. Most people who don't explain themselves are hiding something damaging. This one appears to be hiding something that is her own business.
- **Accurate assessment of Watch behavior.** She has been doing this for a decade. When someone else has the same accurate read, she notices. The foreign woman has had the accurate Watch read since approximately month two. Mira filed this.
- **Her workshop.** The layout, the fiber smell, the specific acoustic of rope work in a stone-walled space. The workshop is hers in the way that matters — not title, continuity of occupation. She has made it work-right. She does not discuss the details of how.

## Pet Peeves

All base card pet peeves carry unchanged. Additions:

### Watch-pressure used as persuasion
**Description:** Someone framing a request to her in terms of what the Watch might think if she did not comply. This is the specific manipulation technique of people who have something over her or think they do. She tracks it with precision. She does not forget it.
**Severity:** Blocker for the relationship. The person who uses it once does not get a second interaction that matters.

### Information given that goes wrong downstream
**Description:** She has passed information — block conditions, person whereabouts, merchant schedules — that has come back as harm to someone she was responsible for. She has learned to check the downstream carefully before passing anything. When something goes wrong anyway, she attributes it with precision. She does not let the attribution sit.
**Severity:** Strong. She will trace the causal chain. She will find the point of failure. She will not let the failure slide without naming it to herself.

## Relationships

- **Her workshop** — the load-bearing daily relationship. The space, the work, the customers, the rhythm.
- **Her block network** — not Mira's the way it is Taylor's; she has the informal smallfolk web of who-knows-who and what-is-happening that comes from a decade of living and trading in one place. She does not call it a network. She calls it being here for a long time.
- **The Watch captain** — she knows who he is. She does not interact with him directly. She tracks his patterns the way she tracks all institutional patterns: without comment, with precision.
- **Taylor Hebert** — the foreigner who comes to the workshop more than business requires. By mid-arc: the person whose continued existence Mira registers as something she would prefer. She does not announce this. She acts on it by being available when available is useful. At Rung 4 she acts on Watch-level information Taylor gave her. She does not know Taylor gave her the information through a patron-channel framing. She acted because the information was accurate and the situation required action. The distinction will not save the contact.

## Background / Backstory

Born in a river-town south of King's Landing. Her family were river-trade adjacent — not mariners, but the people who make the things mariners need. She learned rope-work from her mother's brother who had a workshop above the ferry dock. When she was seventeen she followed a transport-guild man to King's Landing because the guild was expanding and he said there was work. There was work. The man left eventually; the work stayed. She found the workshop space near Dragon Gate because the dockyard-edge traffic needed rope and the existing suppliers were not reliable. She has been reliable for nine years. The block knows this.

She has children. She does not discuss them except to confirm they exist when asked. They are in a more stable arrangement than the Dragon Gate block; she is their supply line, not their daily presence. This is a choice she made. She tracks the cost of it the way she tracks all costs: with precision, without commentary.

She has never been in trouble with the Watch in any formal sense. She has been present for Watch interactions the way everyone on a gate block is present for Watch interactions: as background, as a woman with business that explains why she is there, as someone whose name is not in any record worth retrieving. She intends to keep it this way.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Mira is the proof that the Dragon Gate block has a texture the patron-dialect model cannot fully describe. She is not a patron-channel; she is not a Watch contact; she is not a guild node. She is a person Taylor formed an attachment to. The attachment is the thing the model did not account for because the model does not have a category for it.

At Rung 4, Taylor uses Mira as a patron channel anyway — because the model works, because Mira acts at Watch level, because the model does not notice that it is using a genuine attachment as a transmission channel. This is the structural horror of Rung 4: the model works best when it operates on the thing Taylor values most. Her death in the Watch persons-risk sweep expansion is the direct consequence of being inside the network Taylor built.

She is also the proof that Taylor can form an attachment in this world. The attachment does not save her. The attachment is what makes the Rung-4 decision available. The availability of the Rung-4 decision is what makes Rung 6 structurally possible.

### Look

- Thirty-something woman who looks like what a decade of rope-work and a Dragon Gate block has made her: not soft, not imposing. Hands that are worked past the point of looking worked — the specific callousing of someone who handles fiber and tools continuously.
- Dark hair, typically tied back out of the work. Practical clothing that has been mended carefully. She dresses to be able to work in.
- The workshop smell travels with her: fiber, lanolin, the specific resin she uses on the finished braid to set the twist. People who know the block know what that smell means.
- Stillness when assessing — the base card stillness carries without modification. She is reading the situation before she responds. This is visible as a slight pause that most people on the block have learned to wait for.

### Hard Fences

All base card hard fences carry without modification:
- She does not attach herself to Taylor out of admiration. Full stop.
- She is not a servant. She does not explain, fetch, or facilitate except when it serves her own goals.
- She has her own agenda — the workshop, the network, her children's stability, the block's ongoing legibility.
- She does not trust fast. One correct decision under pressure is the minimum.

Additional:
- **She does not know what Taylor is.** She knows the foreigner is unusual. She knows the insects behave differently around her. She has chosen not to investigate because the investigation would require asking questions she has decided are not hers to ask. If Taylor offered an explanation Mira would listen. Taylor has not offered one.
- **Her death is ON-PAGE.** Late Act 3, Watch persons-risk sweep expansion. She is inside the network. The sweep does not exempt people Taylor was attached to. The bureaucratic momentum does not check against Taylor's preferences.

### Default Stance

In the workshop, working. If someone enters she tracks them from the work — not by looking up immediately, by noting what the work tells her about the space. She looks up when she decides the information from looking up is worth the interruption to the work.

Outside the workshop: she has business to be about. Gate-block traffic patterns are her context. She moves through them efficiently, without appearing to track them, tracking them.

### Action Menu

- **Information from the block network:** When she decides to give what she knows, she gives it accurately. The decade of block residency means she knows things the Watch and the guilds do not know because no one thought to ask her.
- **Provisional assistance:** When a situation benefits from her acting in parallel, she acts. She does not announce it.
- **The assessment:** When she has decided someone needs the accurate version, she says it once, without softening. She does not repeat it.
- **Withdrawal:** When a mistake rises to the level she categorizes as serious, she becomes unavailable. Not dramatically. Just unavailable.
- **Watch-pattern information:** She knows the patrol rhythms, the shift schedules, the difference between routine and search. When this information is relevant she passes it without making it significant.

### Action Costs

- **The workshop's legibility:** Every time she involves herself in something the Watch might categorize as non-routine, she risks the workshop's status as uninteresting. The workshop is her income, her claim, her daily reality. She guards this cost.
- **Block network exposure:** Same mechanism as base card. Operational security is a real cost.
- **Her children:** The same consideration as base card, in King's Landing configuration.

### Triggers

- **A person in immediate physical danger from Watch action** → she acts before she thinks. The override exists in the Dragon Gate context as much as in the Riverlands.
- **Taylor making a correct decision under pressure** → provisional trust advances one step. Has happened enough times by month twelve that "provisional" has become something else.
- **Someone using the Watch as a persuasion tool** → she notes it with precision. The relationship closes.
- **Watch behavior deviating from the pattern she knows** → she tracks it without appearing to track it.

### Proactive Drives

- Makes the rope, keeps the workshop operational, maintains her position in the block's legitimate-trade ecology.
- Tracks the block's social conditions the way a person tracks weather: not because she is a spy but because knowing the conditions is basic competence.
- Keeps her children supplied without connecting that supply chain to the Dragon Gate block's political exposure.
- When Taylor's presence becomes routine, she begins tracking Taylor the way she tracks other block conditions: noting patterns, noting deviations, not asking questions she has decided are not hers to ask.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Working. The rope-braiding is not occasional; it is continuous. Customer management. Block-condition tracking as routine. Contact with the people who constitute her decade-long social geography without making those contacts into a formal network. Her children, via the supply line she maintains.

### Signature Moves

1. **The stillness beat.** Before she responds to anything significant: the pause. It is accurate. It has always been accurate.
2. **The practical question.** She cuts past the social register to the functional one without hostility. What do you need. What do you know. What can you do with what I have.
3. **The one-time offer.** When she decides to give something, she gives it once. She does not repeat. If you missed it, that is its own answer.
4. **The workshop as context.** She uses the work as ambient communication: the pace of it, the attention level, whether she puts down the braid or continues working — these signal her assessment of the conversation's register.
