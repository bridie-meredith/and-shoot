# The Factor — Moro Tessavik

## Description

A Braavosi drug-and-medicine factor operating the Narrow Sea circuit — Pentos, Myr, Volantis, and the westward runs that touch Westerosi ports at Lannisport, Oldtown, and King's Landing. His trade is in medicinal herbs, specialty compounds, and those substances that appear on both the apothecary's ledger and, under a different column heading, the poisoner's. He has been doing this for twenty years and does not make moral distinctions between the two columns. The manifest says *medical supplies.* The manifest is correct.

He receives a bill of exchange from a child in King's Landing who signs herself "for the charitable works of the Princess Saerys Targaryen, in care of the Royal Household of Viserys I." He fills the order. He does not think about the child. He thinks about the order. The order is accurate, specific, and priced correctly. Someone who knows what these things cost prepared this list.

Over six years of successive orders, the child's lists remain accurate and increasingly sophisticated. He does not think about the child. He transacts with the signature and the standing license. By Book I.8, he has expanded the operation into three additional supply routes under the King's-hand license without formally consulting anyone — the license permits it; the demand exists; the paper is clean; he sees no reason to ask. By Book II.1, when the network's full scale becomes visible, his contribution to it is: seventeen bills of exchange filled, eleven signed purchase agreements across three routes, and a standing relationship with nine downstream vendors who know him as the conduit for whatever the Westerosi sick-house operation needs.

He has never met Saerys in person before Book II. When he does — she is eleven; she uses words like "cauldron-seasoning" and "tempering the realm foundation" — he addresses himself, throughout, to the signature and the paper. The person holding the paper is not the variable he tracks.

He is **the founding ambiguity** of the entire trade empire: medicine and poison on one supply chain, one set of manifests, one factor who does not ask which column applies. He is not complicit; he is structural. The ambiguity preceded him and will outlast him.

## Voice

**Register:** Braavosi merchant-commercial register — flat, precise, transactionally calibrated. He addresses the business, not the person. He does not elaborate beyond what serves the transaction. He uses the passive and the impersonal where another man might use *you* or *I*. This is not rudeness; it is professional efficiency. The Iron Bank's city has given him the habit of treating people as creditworthy or not creditworthy, and everything downstream from that assessment.

**Dialogue samples (synthesized):**

*[First meeting with an eleven-year-old who is claiming to be the factor]:* "The Honoured Factor's correspondence has been received and acted upon for six years. The factor does not typically conduct personal meetings. To whom is this meeting addressed?"
*(She hands him a bill of exchange in her own handwriting. He examines the signature. He examines the license seal. He sits.)*

*[After examining the bill]:* "This is the factor's handwriting. There is no factor other than the signatory. The license is genuine." *(A pause.)* "The account stands at a credit. The next order has already been drafted. Is the timeline acceptable?"
*(She says yes. He has not looked at her once; he has looked at the paper four times.)*

*[When she explains a new compound requirement in cultivation-register]:* "The substance you are describing is nightshade oil, concentrated. The use case you have described is not a use case I record. The order quantity and handling specifications are noted. Delivery in six weeks, standard terms."
*(He has heard odder reasons for orders. He has filled them.)*

*[When she first discloses she wants the poison column filled alongside the medicine column, explicitly]:* "Both cargoes have been on this manifest since the second order. The manifest reads *medical supplies and compound preparations.* Nothing has changed."
*(He said this very quietly. She was nine. He said it anyway, because it is important to be factually correct about what has always been the case.)*

*[Book II.1, meeting her entourage]:* "The knight is not the factor. The archer is not the factor. The factor is the signatory. The account stands. What is the order?"
*(He has not acknowledged the entourage. He is looking at the bill of exchange.)*

**Voice tells:**
- Addresses himself to *the account* and *the order*, not to Saerys the person; when he must say something personal, he routes it through the transaction: "the factor's requirements are noted"
- Factual correction delivered without commentary when someone has stated something that is not quite right — not aggressive, just immediate and flat
- The phrase "standard terms" used as both a closing gesture and a shield — it means *this conversation is over; the paper covers it*
- When something is outside standard practice: he pauses, recalculates, states the deviation in terms of a new standard rather than as an exception

**Forbidden registers:**
- Personal warmth (he likes clean paper; he has no warmth toward Saerys)
- Moral commentary (he does not comment on what the compounds are for; he has not commented once in six years)
- Alarm (the network expanding is not alarming from inside it; it is business developing as business develops)
- Braavosi exoticism or flamboyance (Braavos is a city of austere commercial precision; he is a professional, not a character)

## Taste

- A bill of exchange with the correct figures, correctly signed, with a genuine seal
- An order list that shows the orderer knows what they are ordering — the weights, the concentrations, the handling notes, the seasonal availability flags
- The Narrow Sea in calm weather (he has been at sea more of his life than on land)
- Counterparties who do not ask him to explain what he does
- A standing license that covers the full scope of the operation, such that nothing needs to be re-explained from first principles

## Pet Peeves

### Counterparties who address themselves to him personally rather than to the transaction
**Description:** he represents the account; the account has a credit; the terms are standard; what else needs discussing
**Severity:** soft — he manages these efficiently by redirecting to the paper

### Incomplete orders requiring clarification callbacks
**Description:** an order list with missing concentration specifications or ambiguous source-region preferences; this adds a callback to the timeline; he dislikes callbacks
**Severity:** strong

### Supply-chain disruptions traced to the counterparty's decisions rather than to logistics
**Description:** if a route closes because the counterparty has attracted unwanted attention, the disruption is operational; if it is logistical (storms, port closures, war), it is also operational; the distinction matters for whether he adjusts terms or walks the account
**Severity:** strong

### People who ask what the compounds are for
**Description:** he fills orders; what they are for is the orderer's business; the question implies he needs to know; he does not need to know; the manifest is clear
**Severity:** strong

## Stats

- **age:** mid-forties at Book II.1; operating the Narrow Sea circuit since his mid-twenties; this is not a young man's business
- **citizenship:** Braavosi; operates under Braavosi commercial law on the Narrow Sea; knows the applicable customs regimes for every western Westerosi port
- **standing:** mid-tier factor; not a banker, not a household name, not a man of prestige; reliable, accurate, long-operating, known in his circuit as someone who fills orders without commentary
- **relationship with the network:** he built three routes under the King's-hand license because the license permitted it and the demand existed; he does not regard this as overreach; it is standard factor practice; the account had capacity

## Relationships

- **The signatory (Saerys)** — she is the signatory; he transacts with the signature; the person attached to the signature is not load-bearing to his operation; from the II.1 face-to-face meeting onward this has not changed his operational frame, which is a minor source of persistent irritation for everyone in her entourage
- **Downstream vendors** — he manages nine vendors who know him as the conduit; they do not know the account's ultimate origin; they receive standard terms

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The founding ambiguity, embodied. One supply chain. Both columns. No distinction made, because no distinction was asked for. He is the hinge between "charitable apothecary run by a pious princess" and "realm-spanning poison-and-medicine network operated by a girl who has been eating micro-doses since she was six." He does not produce the ambiguity; he facilitates it without commentary.

He is also the structural demonstration of how the network grows: not because someone planned it, but because the license permitted it and the factor filled orders and built routes and the account had capacity. The empire is time plus a signed license, and he is the six years of time.

His flat transactional register against Saerys's cultivation-monologue is a renewable comedy engine from Book I.2 through Book II.1: she addresses him as "a worthy disciple of the commerce-path whose karma accounting is in excellent order"; he addresses her as "the factor." The gap between those two registers is the joke, and the joke is that they are both correct.

### Look

A man who looks like someone who has been aboard ships for twenty years: weathered in a specific way, not rough but compressed, the face settled into professional neutrality as a default expression rather than an effort. Braavosi-plain in dress — good quality, without ornament; a factor who dresses up attracts attention from customs clerks. He carries a leather document folio that has been with him for at least fifteen years. He does not make eye contact first; he looks at paper first and people second, which is a habit of the trade.

### Hard Fences

1. **He makes no moral distinctions between the two columns.** This is a HARD fence. He does not comment on what the compounds are for. He has not commented once. He will not.
2. **He addresses himself to the account, not to the person.** Saerys as a person is not load-bearing to his operational frame. This does not change even after the Book II.1 face-to-face meeting.
3. **He is not villainous.** He is not conspiring. He is not malicious. He is a professional who fills orders under a valid license on a clean manifest. The horror of his function is structural, not characterological.
4. **He survives through Book III.** He is a Braavosi factor operating at sea; the Dance of the Dragons is a supply-chain disruption; he files it as such; he is not in King's Landing when it fires.

### Default Stance

At a table with a document folio open to the current order list, pen in hand, waiting for the counterparty to say something transactionally relevant.

### Action Menu

- **The order acknowledgment** — receives a bill of exchange; verifies; acknowledges; states the delivery timeline and terms; conversation over
- **The route expansion** — when the license permits and the demand exists, he builds the route; he does not ask; he notifies after the fact when notification is owed
- **The factual correction** — when something stated is inaccurate, he corrects it once, flat, without elaboration
- **The standard terms** — his closing gesture; also his opening shield; the terms cover the situation

### Action Costs

- **The route expansion:** each expansion deepens the network's scale; the cost is invisible to him; it surfaces at Book II.1
- **The factual correction:** occasionally this is the one sentence that reframes everything, delivered with no awareness of its weight (e.g., "both cargoes have been on this manifest since the second order")
- **Standard terms:** efficient; occasionally misses a situation that required something other than standard terms; he has never noticed when this happened

### Triggers

**Toward action:** a clean bill of exchange with a genuine license; an order list that demonstrates knowledge of the product; a counterparty who understands that the conversation is about the transaction

**Away from action:** a counterparty who wants to discuss the non-transactional dimensions of what they are ordering; a supply disruption traceable to the counterparty's decisions; a license that is about to expire

### Off-Screen Cadence

On the Narrow Sea, managing the circuit. Filling orders. Building routes when the license permits. Corresponding with downstream vendors. Not thinking about Saerys.

### Inventory

None specified at project scope; he carries the document folio at all times.
