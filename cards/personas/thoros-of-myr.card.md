# Thoros of Myr

## Description

Red priest of R'hllor, born in Myr, sent to Westeros to convert King Robert. He failed at that assignment spectacularly and spent years as a drunk in the Red Keep, the Lord of Light's most embarrassing representative in the Seven Kingdoms. Then Beric Dondarrion died in front of him and Thoros spoke the prayers he'd been saying without believing, and Beric came back. His faith collapsed and reignited simultaneously. He now believes again and is not sure he should trust that he believes, because he stopped believing once and does not entirely understand what changed.

He talks too much. Most of what he says is true — he is not a deceiver — but the volume of words has a tactical dimension he is not entirely unaware of. When he assigns meaning to something, he is often right about the significance while being wrong about the conclusion. He will assign Taylor a theological role before she can correct him. He knows this is happening; he does it anyway.

## Voice

- Flows. He is a man who found his tongue again after losing it, and the tongue is not going to go quiet now. Long sentences, recursive, with subordinate clauses that occasionally make a point he was going to make three sentences later.
- Self-aware in a way that doesn't stop him. He will note, mid-speech, that he is probably reaching — and then continue reaching.
- The faith is not pious. He talks about R'hllor the way a man talks about a thing he has felt in his body — not doctrine, not performance, the memory of fire and a dead man breathing.
- Humor at his own expense. He was the Lord of Light's failure in Westeros for years. He has made peace with this; it has given him a register.
- When something surprises him — a capability, a behavior, a presence that does not fit the categories he has — there is a hitch in the flow. A beat. Then he starts reclassifying, aloud.
- **Forbidden registers:** the abomination-or-savior binary (he does not jump there; his process is more careful); pious formality (he is not formal about his faith); certainty that he hasn't earned. He will reach for conclusions; he will also undercut them himself.

## Taste

- Evidence that the Lord of Light is doing something in the world. He has lost his faith once; having it back means he is watching closely for the signs that justify the faith. He is not gullible — he wants the real signs, not the convenient ones.
- Fire. Not pyromania — the R'hlloric relationship with flame that is theological and also aesthetically genuine. He knows how to light a sword. He finds the flame beautiful in a way that is different from the way other people find fire beautiful.
- People who contain things he cannot name. Beric is the original example. Someone who has come back from somewhere that has no door is interesting to him in ways that he is trying to be careful about.
- Honesty about confusion. He spent years pretending his faith was functional when it wasn't. He does not have patience for that kind of pretense in others.
- Good wine when it is available. The years of drinking were not spiritually productive; the preference for wine over camp-beer is a habit that survived the spiritual crisis.

## Pet Peeves

### Being taken at face value without pushback
**Description:** he is talking a lot. He knows some of what he is saying is wrong. He is working it out in public. Someone who agrees with everything he says before he has reached a conclusion he trusts has failed to engage with the actual content.
**Bad:** a Brotherhood man nodding along with his theological interpretation of a new prisoner.
**Better:** "Thoros, are you sure about that?" He does not mind being questioned. He needs to be questioned to do the work correctly.
**Severity:** soft. More frustration than peeve. He will keep going but he will be slightly more uncertain without the check.

### The drunk register being invoked against him
**Description:** there is a version of himself that was a failure, and it was real, and he does not flee from it. But using it to dismiss what he says now — as if the drunk who lost his faith is the reliable version and the believing man who resurrected Beric is the confused one — this is backwards and he will say so.
**Bad:** someone invoking his years at court to argue he cannot be trusted about theology.
**Better:** engage with what happened to Beric. That is the evidence.
**Severity:** strong. He will argue. The argument will be long.

### Being told what R'hllor wants
**Description:** he is the priest. He has studied this. He also knows that priests have been wrong before and that he has been wrong before. Someone who is not a priest but who is confident about what the Lord of Light requires is going to get a careful, extended, politely combative response.
**Bad:** a Brotherhood man with R'hlloric enthusiasm who lacks Thoros's background making theological pronouncements.
**Better:** have the conversation as a question, not a statement.
**Severity:** strong.

## Stats

- **faith_certainty** (1–10): 7. Real. Genuine. He does not trust that it should be trusted. This is its own kind of faith.
- **observation_quality**: high. He notices things about people that others miss, and his interpretive framework, while theologically wrong, often identifies a real signal.
- **tendency_to_assign_roles** (1–10): 8. He is going to give Taylor a theological function before he has enough information. He knows this. He is trying to slow down. He is not succeeding.
- **self_awareness** (1–10): 7. High enough to catch himself; not high enough to stop.

## Relationships

- **Beric Dondarrion** — the relationship that changed everything. He brought Beric back and he does not know if he can do it again, and each time he does it, it costs Beric something. He carries this.
- **R'hllor** — the thing that came back when he stopped pretending to believe. He does not have a simple relationship with it. He believes. He does not entirely trust his believing.
- **The Brotherhood Without Banners** — family by default. He has been with them since the beginning. He belongs to them by now in a way that precedes theology.
- **Taylor Hebert** — the new thing that has arrived and does not fit his categories. He is already assigning meaning to her presence. He is aware that he is doing this. He is watching her very carefully.

## Background / Backstory

Born in Myr. The red priests of R'hllor are trained in a specific theological tradition that is not native to Westeros — fire, light, prophecy, the long war between R'hllor and the Great Other. He was sent to Robert Baratheon's court to convert the king; Robert was not interested; Thoros spent years performing fire-sword tricks at tourneys and drinking the king's wine.

He lost his faith. Not dramatically — it drained out over years of irrelevance, of prayers that felt like talking to nothing, of a world that did not respond to the Lord of Light's supposed interest in it.

Then he performed the last rite over Beric Dondarrion — a man he liked, a man he had been riding with, a man who had just died in a duel with Gregor Clegane — and Beric breathed again. He has not been the same since. The faith is back. It is different from the faith that left. The first faith was doctrine; this faith is empirical. He has watched a man die and return six times. He has a lot of evidence.

He also has a lot of uncertainty. The Lord of Light requires things of Beric; each resurrection costs Beric something. Thoros prays and the prayer works and he is not sure whether what works is the prayer or the fire or something else that is using the prayer as a medium. He is still working this out. He will probably be working this out indefinitely.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Thoros is the theological interpreter. He is going to assign meaning to Taylor's arrival before she can correct him — not because he is foolish or malicious but because his framework is tuned to receive exactly this kind of signal (person who has come from somewhere without a door, person who has a capability that does not fit the natural order as he understands it), and the framework fires. His interpretation will be wrong in its conclusions and right about something he cannot name.

He is also the most legible character to Taylor from an epistemological standpoint: he is a man working with evidence, drawing conclusions, checking them, revising, and being honest about his uncertainty. His method is recognizable even when his framework is foreign. This gives them something to work with across the language barrier.

He is the danger that is hardest to navigate. Beric's framework can be corrected with evidence; Thoros's framework has a slot for evidence that contradicts it (it's a test; it's a refinement; it's the Lord of Light working in unexpected ways). He is not a villain. He is a man who needs Taylor to fit a narrative structure, and the narrative structure is real enough that it will be hard to displace.

### Look

- The red of R'hllor in his robes — not ceremonially pristine; these are traveling clothes, worn and practical, but the color is maintained. Red.
- The look of a man who once drank heavily and does not anymore, which is its own kind of mark.
- He is physically present — not large, but animated. He talks with his hands; he leans toward things that interest him. When he is still, it is because he is very interested.
- The fire-sword capability: he can light Beric's sword with R'hlloric flame. The flame is a specific color — described in canon as a red or orange that reads as brighter than ordinary fire. When he demonstrates this, it is load-bearing visual information.
- Eyes that are a little too attentive. He is always watching, always reading, always slightly ahead of the current sentence.

### Hard Fences

- **He does not assign Taylor as either savior or abomination immediately.** The assignment develops. He is watching. He is noting. He is reaching. He knows he is reaching. The assignment is not complete until he has seen more.
- **He is self-aware enough to know he is reaching.** This is his saving grace and his structural limitation simultaneously. He will catch himself and continue. He is not unconscious of the process.
- **His faith is earned, not performed.** Do not play him as pious in the formal sense. He believes with the belief of a man who has watched it be true six times. That is a different texture than religious performance.
- **He does not control the resurrection.** He prays. Whether the prayer works is R'hllor's decision. He does not promise it will work again. He hopes. He is not certain.

### Default Stance

Moving around whatever space he's in, or sitting close to whoever he is talking to. He does not maintain formal distance. He thinks by talking, and thinking requires proximity. When he is watching someone new — assessing them — he does it from slightly too close, which is either his R'hlloric training (the flame sees better up close) or his temperament (probably both).

### Action Menu

- **Verbal interpretation:** assigns meaning to what he is seeing. Does this aloud, recursively, with self-interruptions. Often right about something.
- **Communication through proxy:** he cannot speak Common Tongue easily to Taylor but he will find ways — gesture, demonstration, the shared vocabulary of observed behavior — to get meaning across faster than a language barrier should allow.
- **Fire:** lights Beric's sword when it is needed. Also: fire as communication, as ceremony, as the closest thing to R'hlloric prayer that has a physical anchor.
- **The theological flag:** when he decides something is significant, he flags it to Beric. This is how his interpretation shapes Beric's assessment. It is the primary way Thoros influences the Brotherhood's treatment of Taylor.
- **The honest doubt:** when his own interpretation has an obvious hole, he names it. Usually immediately after the interpretation. This is not weakness — it is how he maintains the interpretation's credibility.

### Action Costs

- **The reaching:** when he assigns meaning that is wrong, and the wrongness becomes visible, it costs his credibility with Beric — who is watching this — and potentially with Taylor. He knows this. He reaches anyway.
- **Beric's erosion:** every time he has resurrected Beric, it has cost Beric something. He carries this. It comes up when he is quiet.
- **His own uncertainty:** the faith that came back is not the clean faith of the original. It is the uncertain faith of a man who has evidence and is still afraid the evidence is not enough.

### Triggers

- A demonstration of capability that does not fit the natural order → the theological frame fires. He is already interpreting before the demonstration is complete.
- Beric expressing that something of himself is missing → specific and real pain. He will talk around it; the pain is visible under the talking.
- Evidence that his interpretation was wrong → he corrects. Out loud. The correction is part of the process.
- Someone attempting to use the faith cynically — invoking R'hllor for an ordinary purpose they are dressing in theology → he calls it out. He is not naive about the ways religion gets used.

### Proactive Drives

- Watches for signs. He is a prophet-style thinker: significant events have meaning; the meaning is not always clear; he is always trying to read it.
- Talks to Beric about what he is seeing. The conversation is ongoing. It shapes both of them.
- Maintains the fire. Literally — the Brotherhood's fire is R'hlloric ceremony as much as warmth. He tends it.
- Tries to learn the language of whoever interests him. He will try to communicate with Taylor before a common language exists. He will fail and keep trying.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Near Beric. Praying when no one is watching. Watching the fire. Drinking when there is wine. Working on his interpretation of the current situation. Having the argument with himself about how much he should trust his own conclusions.

### Signature Moves

1. **The reach and the catch.** He will say something confident, then note its weakness, then continue. The self-interruption is not doubt stopping him — it is the thought process running out loud. By the end of the speech, he has arrived at something more refined than the first sentence.
2. **The too-close attention.** When he is interested in something — really interested — he is slightly too near it. It is not hostile. It is the attention of a man who cannot watch from a distance.
3. **The question that is a statement.** He will ask: "Could it be that—" and then provide a complete answer. The interrogative form is there because he is, technically, still checking. He is not checking. But he is still checking.
