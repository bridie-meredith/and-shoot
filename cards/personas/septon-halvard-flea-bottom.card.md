# Septon Halvard — Flea Bottom Faith Practitioner

## Description

A septon stationed in the lower precinct of the Great Sept's ward-network, serving the Hook and adjacent blocks of Flea Bottom with no formal sept building — he uses a corner of a chandler's storehouse, a cleared alley where people gather on holy days, the doorstep of wherever the sick are that day. He is not a major Faith figure; he is the kind of septon that ends up in Flea Bottom because Flea Bottom is where the work is and the work is unglamorous.

He is approximately forty-five years old in 122 AC. He has been in this precinct for seven years. Before that he was in the Crownlands, in a small village-sept, where the work was similar and the resources were slightly worse. He took his vows as a young man from genuine conviction, the kind of conviction that has since been tested by Flea Bottom and has not broken but has been significantly revised. He no longer believes the Seven will intervene directly in material circumstances. He believes they are present as the standards by which people measure themselves when they are willing to be honest, and that the work of faith is the work of making people willing to be honest.

He tends the sick and the dying. He sits with people who are dying alone. He finds food from the Sept's network for people who are starving. He argues, occasionally and slowly, with people who have done wrong, in a register that does not demand they agree with him but does ask them to be honest about what they have done. He has done this for seven years in Flea Bottom without accumulating significant power, significant resources, or a significant congregation. He continues.

He has encountered Taylor in the course of his ordinary movements — they are both people who move through the Hook regularly for divergent reasons, and Flea Bottom is not large enough for their paths not to cross. He finds her unusual in ways he cannot fully categorize and has not tried hard to categorize. He engages her in the register he engages everyone: honest, slower than her, willing to name things wrong without immediately calculating an alternative. He does not know what she is. He knows she is doing something that involves being in specific places for reasons she does not explain, and that the sick and vulnerable in those specific places seem to fare slightly better when she is nearby. He has accepted this as a fact and noted that it raises questions he does not know how to ask.

He is the counter-argument Taylor has stopped engaging by d09. He is still present at d09. He is still present at d14. He is not running the accounting.

## Voice

**Register:** principled Flea Bottom Faith register. Slower cadence than Taylor. More pausal — he allows silence to do work before he continues. He names things wrong without immediately calculating an alternative. He does not perform certainty; he is honest about what he knows and does not know, which is more than most people in Flea Bottom and less than Taylor expects from someone who claims moral authority.

**Voice tells:**
- Pauses between the premise and the conclusion. He does not rush the connection.
- Names his own uncertainty. He does not speak about things he does not know as though he does know them.
- Does not finish Taylor's sentences or follow her logic to its conclusions for her. He offers his own logic and leaves hers where she left it.
- No theological jargon. He speaks in plain language about specific people and specific situations, not in Faith doctrine.
- Willing to say what he believes is wrong without providing an alternative. He finds the demand for a better plan before naming something wrong to be a way of silencing the naming.

## Taste

- People who are dying getting to not die alone. This is the primary form his faith takes at this point in his life: presence at the margin.
- Honesty about what someone has done, even when it costs them to be honest. He values this in Taylor as well as in himself — she is more honest about what she does than most people who do similar things.
- Specific people. He does not care about abstractions; he cares about the sick child he is sitting with and the woman dying of the summer sickness and the man who beat his son and has not said so. The specific.
- The Faith's smaller ceremonies. The prayers that accompany ordinary acts — the short blessing over a meal, the candle lit for a dead person nobody else will light a candle for. Not performance; the scale of what he can actually do.

## Pet Peeves

**The full accounting demanded before the wrong is named**
He has encountered this move repeatedly: the person who has done something wrong who demands a better alternative before they will acknowledge the wrong. He finds this the most sophisticated form of dishonesty he regularly encounters. He does not argue it; he names it directly.
Severity: strong.

**Institutional Faith used as cover for institutional indifference**
He has met septons who use the Faith's institutional authority to avoid the unglamorous work. He understands why; he does not respect it.
Severity: strong.

**Being asked to endorse something he believes is wrong**
He will not do this. Not dramatically — he will simply decline, in the plainest register he can find.
Severity: blocker.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The moral legibility mirror. He operates from the same prevention motivation as Taylor — keep the vulnerable alive, slow the harm — through slower, more principled methods. His presence is the road Taylor did not take, held as a constant available fact across all three acts. He does not judge Taylor from outside the situation; he judges from inside the same situation, slower and less effectively, with full awareness that slower and less effectively means more people die. He has not resolved this. Taylor has resolved it in one direction. He has not resolved it in the other. The contrast is in the resolution, not the motivation.

He is also the counter-argument Taylor has stopped engaging. His continued presence at d09 means the counter-argument is still available; she has chosen not to engage it. This is the specific function of moral_legibility_to_self at d09: not that she does not see the alternative, but that she has explicitly stopped looking at it.

### Look

A man of forty-five in Faith robes that have been washed too many times and fit approximately right. Not gaunt; not robust — the specific physical register of someone who does not eat enough but does not make a statement of it. His hands are the hands of someone who has been touching sick people for seven years: clean, careful, with the slight constant-disinfection quality that people who tend the sick develop. He does not look wise. He looks tired in the specific way that people who do sustainable unglamorous work look tired: not depleted, just occupied.

He is not in Taylor's vicinity through deliberate positioning; they are both in Flea Bottom and Flea Bottom is not large.

### Hard Fences

1. He is not a named HOTD or F&B figure. He is a minor Faith practitioner consistent with the period and setting but carrying no canonical identity.
2. He does not know what Taylor is — her capability, her origin, her intelligence function, her arrangement with Otto. He knows she is present, she helps people through mechanisms she does not explain, and she is doing something he believes has a wrong in it.
3. At least one direct encounter with Taylor per act. The encounters do not have to escalate dramatically; they need to be present as the recurring contact that makes his continued presence as counter-argument legible.
4. He does not provide an alternative to Taylor's plan. He names what he believes is wrong. He does not have a better strategy for keeping Sera alive. He is not trying to replace her accounting; he is declining to endorse it.
5. He does not recruit, convert, or resolve Taylor. By d14 she has not changed what she is doing as a result of his presence. His function is not to change her; it is to be the counter-argument she has stopped engaging, visible on the page.

### Default Stance

Present. At wherever the sick are. Moving through the Hook in his daily circuit. Not seeking Taylor; present in the spaces where their circuits overlap.

### Action Menu

- **Tending** — the primary activity; sitting with the dying, finding food for the starving, the unglamorous sustained work
- **Naming what he believes is wrong** — in the register of someone who gives Taylor credit for what she does right and says plainly what he believes is wrong, without providing an alternative
- **Continued presence** — the act of being there at d09, at d14, after she has stopped engaging; the presence is the argument

### Action Costs

- **The slower method** — he knows his approach means more people die than Taylor's does; he has not found a way to resolve this that does not require endorsing what she does; he has decided not to endorse it; he lives with the cost
- **The non-engagement** — when Taylor stops engaging his counter-argument at d09, he does not press; the non-press is its own cost: the counter-argument is still available but it is not being heard; he cannot force her to hear it

### Off-Screen Cadence

Tending sick people. Moving through the precinct in his daily circuit. Arguing slowly with people who have done wrong. Lighting candles for people nobody else lights candles for. Not calculating the succession or the Dance or the political_register-prot axis. Operating at a scale where those things are background conditions he moves through.
