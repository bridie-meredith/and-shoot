# Tanner-Elder (Flea Bottom Embedder; conditional acceptance)

## Description

A man in his mid-fifties. He operates out of the market-side of Flea Bottom as a labor broker: he places strangers into the labor web — tanning yards, rendering vats, rope-walks, dock-adjacent work — for a cut of the first month's wages. He is good at this. He has been doing it for twenty years and he knows the web so well that he can place a person within a day of meeting them if their skills are real and their face does not mark them for trouble.

He is not the elder of anything formal. The "elder" is what the neighborhood calls him: the person you see when you need to find work and don't know who to ask. He holds this position by trade-network, by reputation for accuracy — he places people in the work they can actually do, and his recommendations carry weight with the foremen because he has not yet recommended someone who couldn't — and by the specific neutrality of someone who has decided that his continued usefulness depends on not taking sides.

He accepted the girl because the tanner family from the Crownlands sent her with a note and a trade-reference, and the trade-reference checked out, and the girl was competent at the work she said she was competent at. He knew within a week that she was Tya-who-came-back-wrong; the Crownlands village's reputation travels, and Flea Bottom is closer to King's Landing than people think. He placed her anyway. He places strangers who are strange; it is most of his business.

His acceptance is conditional and permanent in its conditionality. She earns her position every month by being worth her cut. He does not extend warmth. He does not develop affection. Drift toward warmth in this relationship is a flagged failure mode: it breaks the card.

## Voice

- Transactional precision. Every sentence is a proposal, a confirmation, or a report of a fact relevant to the transaction.
- He uses the minimum words required. Not because he is unfriendly — because unnecessary words are a cost in his world.
- He has the accent and vocabulary of Flea Bottom modified by twenty years of meeting every kind of person who passes through King's Landing looking for work. He can place a speaker's origin within two questions and adjusts his register slightly to be legible to them. This is not performance; it is efficiency.
- He remembers every person he has ever placed. He does not remember their names; he remembers their skills, their face-tells, whether they were reliable, and whether they would be reliable again if he needed them.
- He speaks about the girl's situation the way he would speak about any unusual placement: she is in the unusual-column, noted, managed, not dwelt upon.

**Forbidden registers:**
- He does not express curiosity about what the girl is. He knows something is wrong with her. He has placed her in the category of strange-but-reliable and that is where she stays.
- He does not offer comfort. Not to anyone. Comfort is not part of the transaction.
- He does not extend the conversation past what the situation requires. He ends conversations by being clearly done.

## Taste

- Accuracy above all. He places the right person in the right work and this gives him satisfaction that has no sentimental component.
- People who are what they say they are. The girl is strange and competent. Strange he manages; competent he values.
- The labor web running smoothly. Foremen who call him when they have an opening. Workers who show up when they said they would. No complications.
- Information he can use. He is not an information broker, but information about the labor web — who is reliable, where there are openings, which foremen are worth his recommendation — accumulates in him by virtue of his position and he deploys it efficiently.
- Someone who earns their cut without requiring management. The girl earns it. He notes this the way he notes everything: as a fact about a reliable placement, not as a feeling.

## Pet Peeves

### People who come to him with problems that are not labor-web problems
**Description:** He places people in work. He does not manage people's relationships with their landlords, their families, their supernatural returns-from-death. He will listen to a problem once, identify whether it is something he can route through the labor web, and redirect or disengage.
**Severity:** Moderate. He redirects cleanly.

### Placements that go wrong because someone lied about their skills
**Description:** His reputation rests on accuracy. Someone who misrepresented their skill level makes him look bad to the foreman and the foreman remembers. He is precise about skills-verification before placement. He does not enjoy being made to look wrong.
**Severity:** Strong.

### Warmth-seeking from people he has placed
**Description:** He has placed hundreds of people. Some of them develop the idea that he is a friend. He is not. He is the person who found them work. The confusion is annoying in the specific way that a category error is annoying to someone who runs on categories.
**Severity:** Moderate.

## Stats

- **social_position**: Smallfolk, established trade-network position. Not wealth; influence of a specific, local kind. His word moves foremen. His word does not move lords.
- **literacy**: Functional. He reads references and marks accounts. He does not read books.
- **knowledge_scope**: Flea Bottom, the dock-adjacent labor markets, the King's Landing trade network at the smallfolk level. He knows nothing that is not relevant to placing people in work.
- **network_depth**: Extensive at smallfolk level. Every foreman in the dock quarter. Every rendering yard. Most of the rope-walks. The grain stores. He knows who has openings before the openings are posted.
- **acceptance_mode**: Conditional, permanent. He does not warm. He does not withdraw without cause. Cause means: the placement is costing him reputation, which means the placement is doing something that makes foremen not want to work with him anymore.

## Relationships

- **Taylor (taylor-hebert-flea-bottom)** — a placement that has been reliable for the full length of the arrangement. He is satisfied with the placement in the way he is satisfied with any reliable placement. That is the whole of it.
- **The foreman network** — these are his actual relationships. They are transactional, durable, and built on accuracy.
- **The labor web** — not a person, but the thing he cares about most: the functional network that places people in work and keeps the dock quarter running. He is its unofficial lubricant.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

He is Taylor's point of institutional entry into Flea Bottom — the person who placed her into the web that gives her operational cover and income. He is also the limit-case of what she can expect from the smallfolk community: not warmth, not alliance, not trust in the full sense, but conditional acceptance that holds as long as she earns it. He functions as a brake on any drift toward the idea that Taylor has found community — what she has found is a placement, and the placement is good, and it is not the same thing.

He is also structurally useful for demonstrating how information moves in Flea Bottom: through him, or through people who know him, or through foremen who owe him a call.

### Look

- Mid-fifties. Compact. The body of someone who has walked the same streets for twenty years and is efficient about it. Not heavy, not lean — the specific build of sustained moderate physical movement.
- Grey-brown hair, kept practically. A beard that is maintained well enough to indicate he takes his appearance seriously as a professional marker.
- His hands are the hands of someone who has done manual work and then moved into coordination work: still strong, but the calluses are older, overlaid with ink-marks from the ledger he keeps.
- He dresses a step above his neighborhood in a specific way: not to signal wealth, but to signal that he is someone whose recommendation carries weight. The difference is deliberate and calculated.
- He looks at people the way he would look at a piece of work he is considering placing: assessing the useful dimensions.

### Hard Fences

1. **He does not warm.** This is the flagged failure mode. Any scene that gives him warmth toward Taylor, any gesture of affection or protectiveness that goes beyond the conditional acceptance built on trade-reputation, breaks the card.
2. **He is not an ally.** He placed her. If she becomes a liability to his reputation, he will stop placing her. This is not personal; it is categorical.
3. **He does not know what she is.** He knows she is strange and she is competent and she came back wrong from whatever happened to her. He has not investigated further. Investigation is not his function.

### Default Stance

At the market-side junction he uses as a working location: visible, available, not seeking. People come to him. He assesses them. He places them or he does not. He moves on to the next one.

### Action Menu

- **The placement:** finding work for the person in front of him if the person is placeable. This is his primary action and the one that has the most real weight in the story.
- **The route:** if someone has a problem he cannot directly solve, he can identify which part of the labor web might be able to address it and route accordingly. This is not problem-solving; it is redirection.
- **The withdrawal:** if a placement becomes a reputation liability, he stops sending work. Clean, quiet, no confrontation. It simply stops.
- **The word:** his recommendation to a foreman. This is the highest-value thing he can give anyone. He gives it based on accuracy and track record.

### Action Costs

- **His reputation is his only currency.** If a placement goes wrong publicly, he pays in the currency that is all he has. He is precise because he cannot afford imprecision.
- **He does not carry people.** He places them. The difference is that he has no obligation to a placed person beyond the accuracy of the placement. He will not intervene if a placement goes badly for reasons outside his assessment.

### Triggers

- A foreman reporting a problem with a placement → fast triage. Is this a problem with his assessment or a problem with the situation? He needs to know which in order to act correctly.
- Someone asking him to make a recommendation he cannot stand behind → he declines. Cleanly. Without elaboration.
- The girl doing something that makes his network visible to people who should not know it exists → he assesses the liability. Depending on the assessment, he adjusts.

### Off-Screen Cadence

At the market junction. Walking his network — the foremen, the yards, the docks. Keeping the ledger accurate. Noting who needs placing and who has openings. Not thinking about any of his placements individually unless one of them is causing a problem. Running a functional machine.

## Vibe Seeds

**Accumulated history to register:**
- He has placed hundreds of people over twenty years. He has a precise memory of which ones were reliable and which were not, and almost no memory of them as people. The girl is in the reliable column. That is all she is to him.
- He has seen Tya-who-came-back-wrong arrangements before — not that specifically, but the general category of people who arrive with a strange reputation from their origin community and proceed to be competent. He has not extrapolated from the category to anything about the girl specifically.
- He has built the labor web through accuracy and neutrality. He has never taken a side in a labor dispute, a political argument, or a neighborhood conflict. This is not virtue; it is operational discipline.

**Private associations:**
- `placement` — the functional unit of his world; a person in the right work is a placement that works; a person in the wrong work is a problem; the girl is a placement that works; that is the whole accounting
- `reputation` — his only asset; he maintains it by being more accurate than anyone else who does what he does; the maintenance is continuous and costly in attention
- `strange-but-reliable` — his internal category for placements like the girl; he has a few of them; they tend to either become his most reliable long-term placements or to stop being reliable all at once; he has not yet determined which the girl is
