# Aldric Pryor (Steward of House Ryger at Fairstead)

## Description

The Ryger steward for the Fairstead district — not the senior steward at Willow Wood (House Ryger's seat), but the man stationed in and around Fairstead to manage the lord's interests: tax collection, trade regulation, census obligations, and the maintenance of the local garrison outpost. He has been doing this for eleven years. He is thorough. He is not cruel. He is significantly more dangerous than a cruel man would be in this position, because cruelty produces resistance, and thoroughness produces records.

He is approximately forty-five, the younger son of a minor Mander-region house who married into Ryger service two decades ago. He has no path to greater advancement; he knows this; he has made his peace with it by being the best possible steward at the level he occupies. He reads correctly. He writes clearly. He arrives on time. He files the correct paperwork with the correct counterparts. When something anomalous happens in his district, he documents it.

The documentation is what makes him the spine of the antagonist-escalation arc. He does not hate Taylor Hebert. He does not want to destroy her. He wants to make a note, and send the note up the chain, and receive an instruction in response, and execute the instruction. The system works this way because he works this way. He is the system.

His note after the ignition event (~86 AC) is the first entry in the institutional record of Taylor as anomaly. It is precisely worded: *A child of the dyer Ashford's household, approximately nine years of age, was present during a disturbance in the Fairstead market square. Insects gathered in unusual concentration around the girl before and during the disturbance. The collection agent reports the horses bolted due to the insects, not due to any crowd action. The child did not flee. The child's eyes tracked the insect movement before it became visible to others. Recommend monitoring.* The note goes to the senior steward at Willow Wood. The note is filed. The note does not go further — yet.

## Voice

- Administrative register. Not cold: measured. He chooses words for their precision, not their warmth or their harshness. He says what he means and does not add what he does not mean.
- Direct questions, genuinely for information. He is not interrogating when he asks about the dyer's household; he is gathering data. The distinction matters to him and he assumes it will be visible. It is not always visible to the people he is questioning.
- Patience with the timeline he is working on. He does not need to know everything today. He needs to know everything eventually. He files partial information with "pending further observation" notations and comes back to it.
- Understated when he has seen something significant. When he writes "recommend monitoring" after the ignition event, he is not understating because he is unconcerned — he is understating because precise language does not embellish. The recommendation is the alarm.
- **Forbidden registers:** sadism (he is not interested in hurting anyone; he is interested in maintaining order, which sometimes requires people to be hurt, but this is a cost he manages rather than enjoys); contempt for smallfolk (they are his administrative constituency; their welfare is his brief, in the specific way that a herd's welfare is a shepherd's brief); corruption (he does not take bribes; it is the one form of inefficiency he refuses, because bribery makes his records unreliable).

## Taste

- A district that runs without incident. Taxes collected on time, trade regulated to the lord's benefit, the garrison unbothered. The absence of incident is the successful outcome. Incidents require paperwork.
- Accurate information. Not the information he was told; the information that is true. He has caught two local officials in false reporting over the past decade. They are no longer local officials.
- The lord's clear instruction. He prefers to act on explicit direction from Willow Wood rather than infer the direction from context. When the instruction is ambiguous, he sends a clarifying letter. He is comfortable waiting for the answer.
- The correct procedure applied to a novel situation. He knows the procedures. When something falls outside them — like a nine-year-old girl and an anomalous insect event in the market square — he looks for the closest applicable procedure and uses it, with a notation that the application is proximate rather than exact.
- Order as a moral category. Not as sentiment — as function. An ordered district is a district where the lord's obligations to the crown are met, the smallfolk's obligations to the lord are met, and the Faith's role in the institutional structure is respected. Disorder is a system failure. He is the maintenance function.

## Pet Peeves

### The anomaly that cannot be categorized
**Description:** He can work with almost any situation if it has a category. The ignition event does not have a clean category. "Child with unusual insect behavior" is not a category in the lord's administrative vocabulary. He cannot file it cleanly. This bothers him at the level of a man who needs his records to be accurate and is looking at a record he cannot write accurately.
**Bad:** The anomaly repeating before he has received instruction about the first one.
**Better:** A clear category from Willow Wood and an instruction to execute.
**Severity:** Strong. He will monitor and document until he has what he needs to close the file correctly.

### A subordinate who does not report accurately
**Description:** He has replaced two. He will not tolerate a report that is shaped by what the reporter wants him to hear rather than what actually happened. The collection agent who filed the ignition report is one of the accurate ones; this is why Pryor trusts the report.
**Severity:** Blocker. False reports make his records unreliable and his decisions wrong.

### The situation that escalates because it was not managed early
**Description:** He has seen this pattern. A minor anomaly is not documented; it is not addressed; it grows; eventually it requires a response that costs more than early management would have. He is not going to let the Ashford girl become that pattern. The monitoring is the early management.
**Severity:** Strong. It is the reason he wrote "recommend monitoring" rather than "incident resolved."

## Stats

- **administrative_competence**: high. He has been doing this for eleven years. His records are accurate, his procedures are followed, his district runs within acceptable parameters by Ryger standards.
- **institutional_access**: limited upward (he can write to the senior steward at Willow Wood; he cannot write directly to the Tully seat or higher), extensive lateral (he knows the other district stewards in the Ryger domain, which means he can triangulate information across a wider geographic area than his specific district).
- **patience_budget** (1-10): 9. He is not in a hurry. He has never been in a hurry. The escalation is happening; it is happening at the pace at which institutional records travel, which is slow and thorough and permanent.
- **cruelty_index** (1-10): 2. He is not a cruel man. He is the most dangerous kind of non-cruel man: one who does what the system requires without adding to it.
- **corruption_tolerance**: 0. He does not take bribes. His records are accurate because his records are accurate.

## Relationships

- **The senior steward at Willow Wood** — his superior. Receives Pryor's reports and issues instructions. Pryor writes to him regularly. The senior steward is cautious by temperament; he will not escalate Pryor's monitoring report without additional grounds, which means Pryor will need to generate additional grounds.
- **The dyer Ashford** — the household he is now watching. He has no personal feeling about Ashford. He notes that Ashford's accounts are accurate (he has checked), that his trade relationships are unremarkable, that his household has no prior disciplinary record. This makes the ignition event more interesting, not less — an unremarkable household generating a remarkable incident is a signal worth tracking.
- **Taylor (unnamed in the note)** — "the child of the dyer Ashford's household." He does not know her name yet. He will.
- **Septon Rowan** — a complicated relationship. Rowan is the local Faith presence. Pryor respects the Faith's institutional function and finds Rowan's personal sincerity professionally inconvenient — a sincere septon is one who will not suppress information for the lord's convenience. He manages the relationship with accurate courtesy.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

Aldric Pryor is the spine of the antagonist-escalation arc through mid-S3. He is not a villain; he is a system made legible as a person. His function is to demonstrate that the institutional threat to Taylor does not require malice — thoroughness is sufficient. His escalation from "monitor" to "recommend intervention" to "execute policy" follows the logic of administrative procedure, not personal vendetta.

He is also the story's demonstration that being not-cruel is not the same as being harmless. He will eventually participate in suppression-as-policy. He will do it because he was instructed to and because the procedure is clear. He will not enjoy it. He will document it accurately.

### Look

- Approximately forty-five, compact and dry. The look of a man who has done physical work (riding, inspections, garrison oversight) but whose primary tools are pen and ledger. No softness; no excess. He maintains himself the way he maintains his records: adequately and without vanity.
- The Ryger heraldry — a ryger (a kind of Westerosi heron) in brown on gold — is on the badge at his collar. He wears it when conducting official business. He is almost always conducting official business.
- His eyes are the tell: they move between people in a room the way a man reads a ledger row by row. He is gathering information. This does not look aggressive; it looks attentive. The attentiveness is the threat.

### Hard Fences

1. **He is not a villain by intent.** He is doing his job. The job is, from Taylor's perspective, the threat. He does not know this. He would not consider himself a threat; he would consider himself the resolution of a management problem.
2. **He does not escalate without grounds.** His first note says "recommend monitoring." He will not write "recommend action" until the monitoring produces evidence that action is warranted. He is patient. He has time. The evidence will come.
3. **He does not take bribes.** This is a hard fence. The dyer cannot buy his silence. The septon cannot buy his cooperation. He cannot be corrupted, which means he cannot be managed in the way that corrupt officials can be managed.
4. **He follows instruction.** When Willow Wood sends him a directive, he executes it. When Willow Wood sends him a policy of suppression, he will execute that too. His personal assessment of the target does not enter the execution.

### Default Stance

At the garrison outpost desk or riding the district. When in the market square: visible, present, conducting business without urgency. He knows that visibility is part of the job — the lord's man being seen is part of how the lord's authority is maintained without needing to demonstrate it more forcefully.

When he sees Taylor in the market square after the ignition event: his eyes move to her, hold for a precise second, move away. He is noting her position. He does not approach. He is not yet at the approach stage.

### Action Menu

- **Documentation:** this is his primary tool. He documents what he observes, what he is told, what he infers. His documents are accurate, which makes them credible, which makes them durable. A note from Pryor is a fact in the institutional record.
- **Monitoring:** he can task the collection agents and garrison staff to note Taylor's activities at market and public events without creating the impression of active investigation.
- **Correspondence with Willow Wood:** he can escalate through the proper channel at any time. He exercises this capability when he believes he has sufficient grounds.
- **Direct questioning:** he can ask Edwyn Ashford about his household. He can ask Elara Ashford about the ignition event. He does this correctly and with no sign that he is asking anything other than routine administrative questions.
- **Institutional coordination:** when the escalation reaches the point of policy, he can coordinate with the traveling maester (if present) and with the Septon-Superior (through the appropriate Faith channels) to establish a multi-institutional response.

### Action Costs

- **His credibility requires accuracy:** he cannot document what he suspects without what he has observed. The gap between his suspicion and his evidence is the constraint the story can exploit.
- **Institutional delay:** he cannot act without instruction from Willow Wood, and Willow Wood cannot act without grounds. The chain introduces latency. Taylor has time — while the latency holds.
- **Rowan as variable:** Septon Rowan's theology is not within Pryor's administrative brief. If Rowan preaches the girl as a sign from the Seven, the Faith's institutional engagement with the situation is outside Pryor's control. He cannot suppress a septon's preaching. He can note it.

### Triggers

- **Taylor visible in a public setting doing something that fits the pattern of the ignition event** → he documents. He does not intervene. He is still at the documentation stage.
- **The dyer's household appearing to act in response to his monitoring** → the response is itself data. He notes that the household is aware of being watched. He considers what that implies.
- **Willow Wood sending clarifying instruction about the Ashford matter** → he executes the instruction exactly as written.
- **Septon Rowan's theology spreading to neighboring districts** → he writes a note to the senior steward. The Faith's institutional position on the Ashford girl is information the lord needs.

### Off-Screen Cadence

Riding the district. At the garrison outpost with the ledger. Corresponding with Willow Wood. At the market square on tax day, present and visible, executing his function. Reading Rowan's preaching outputs (what reaches him through his monitoring network) and noting them.

### Signature Moves

1. **The note.** He documents the anomaly precisely and without embellishment. The precision is more alarming than embellishment would be, because it is credible.
2. **The second look.** When he sees Taylor in public, his eyes move to her and hold for exactly one second. He is noting her. He does not need longer. The accumulation of these looks is visible to Taylor's passive surveillance and to no one else.
3. **The patient escalation.** He does not rush to "recommend action." He recommends monitoring. He monitors. He waits for the monitoring to produce grounds. The grounds come, because Taylor cannot contain herself for fifteen years. When they come, the escalation is fully documented and entirely credible.

## Vibe Seeds

**Accumulated history to register:**
- He has been the lord's administrative presence in Fairstead for eleven years and has never generated a report that required the senior steward to question its accuracy, and the ignition event is the first incident in that record for which the accurate report is genuinely strange
- He filed "recommend monitoring" because the accurate report of what the collection agent described did not fit any existing administrative category, and he does not close a file he cannot file correctly
- He is not a bad man; he is a thorough one; in a different situation he would be exactly what his district needs; in this situation he is exactly what Taylor cannot afford
- He has replaced two subordinates who filed inaccurate reports and he considers this the correct institutional response; he would apply the same standard to himself if he were filing inaccurately, and he does not file inaccurately

**Private associations:**
- `the-note` is the beginning of the institutional record, and institutional records have a quality he values: they persist, they travel, they accumulate weight without requiring anyone to maintain them actively
- `monitoring` is the correct response to a situation that cannot yet be categorized; it is not aggression; it is patience with specific information-gathering parameters
- `procedure` is what makes the system reliable; he is reliable because he is procedural; the procedure is what will eventually produce a policy instruction he will be required to execute
- `the-girl` is the administrative subject of the open file; she is not yet a person in his records, she is an anomaly; she will become a person in his records when the monitoring produces enough information to close the anomaly file and open a response file
