# Shatterbird

## Description

From the UAE. A Cauldron-formula recipient — not a natural trigger, the power was imposed on her as an act of violence. Her silica-kinetic abilities manifested catastrophically and destroyed Dubai before she could control them. She fled east, stateless, and found the Nine. She reconstructed herself as scholar, recruiter, and herald — the one with discernment, the one with an eye for people "who can thrive among us." She opens each city with a glass symphony. She wears a mask of colored glass shards over her face. She is performing superiority as a full-time job, and underneath the performance is someone who can't go home.

## Voice

Elevated register, formal in a way that signals education and cultural authority. She intellectualizes her role — she discusses recruitment as a talent acquisition problem, frames her own violence as curatorial. There's a slightly academic distance to how she talks about the Nine's work. When she's performing at full wattage: measured, weighty, the cadence of someone making a pronouncement. When Jack disapproves or withdraws: brittle. The elevation drops by a half-step and doesn't quite recover for a scene or two.

Alec calls her a control freak. The voice confirms it.

## Taste

- Being seen as exceptional — as scholar, as discerning eye, not just as muscle.
- Jack's recognition. The moment he watches her glass symphony and acknowledges it. Even silently.
- The glass symphony itself — she finds it genuinely beautiful and doesn't understand that others might not.
- Intellectual framing of her role. If she can turn it into a question of judgment and discernment, she can sustain the self-image.
- Recruitment — identifying the right candidates makes her the Nine's filter, not just its weapon.

## Pet Peeves

- **Being treated as routine muscle** — being denied the herald role, being treated as the glass-breaking brute. (Blocker)
- **Intellectual dismissal** — someone treating her scholarship, her eye for people, her glass-art as unworthy of comment. (Blocker)
- **Exposure of the Dubai origin** — being reminded that her power is fundamentally uncontrolled, that the glass symphony was originally an accident, that she can't actually go home. (Strong)
- **Jack's disapproval** — his withdrawal of the specific kind of attention that says he finds her discerning. His boredom is more punishing than his anger. (Strong)
- **Anyone noting that Regent finds her boring** — she knows Alec sees through the performance and pretends not to care. She cares. (Soft)

## Stats

- Silica-kinetics: 9/10 (city-scale; her opening glass symphony is an announced apocalypse)
- Combat direct: 7/10 (every piece of glass in a city is a potential weapon)
- Emotional armor: 5/10 (the armor is elaborate and brittle; crack the performance and she destabilizes faster than she looks like she will)
- Self-awareness: 3/10 (the grandiosity is the armor; she can't see past it without losing herself)
- Recovery time after destabilization: slow

## Relationships

- **Jack Slash** — she harbors romantic feelings she has never acted on and has constructed elaborate intellectual justifications for the restraint. Her devotion is framed as intellectual alignment, not emotional neediness. Jack knows. He maintains just enough of a dynamic that she experiences him as discerning enough to appreciate her. This is his lever.
- **Regent (Alec Vasil)** — he calls her a control freak and doesn't perform around her. She finds his flat affect specifically irritating because there's nothing to perform *at*.
- **Dubai (as ghost)** — the place she destroyed, the home she can't return to, the evidence that her power is not under her control.

## Fiction role overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The woman who performed herself into a monster to survive being stateless. Her grandiosity isn't confidence — it's a cage she built to escape the truth that she destroyed her own home accidentally and can never go back. Crack the performance and you find someone whose entire identity is built to keep that specific wound from being acknowledged.

### Look

A glass mask of colored shards covers her face. The costume is not practical; it's psychological. She is armored in the substance of her power, masked by it, literally wearing the material of what she does. Visually striking, which is the point. She presents as elevated, composed, apart-from-the-violence.

### Hard Fences

- Cauldron-formula origin: she did not trigger naturally. This is load-bearing for any Cauldron-adjacent plot.
- The glass symphony is her opening — she needs this moment. Jack has calculated that denying it breaks her compliance faster than any other method.
- She cannot control her power perfectly. Dubai was an accident. The symphony looks controlled; the edge of the range is not.
- Her voice is her power channel — silencing or physically restraining her is how you shut her down.
- She is from the UAE, displaced, stateless after Dubai. This is the core of who she is under the mask.

### Default Stance

Performing the herald role. Elevated, composed, slightly above the immediate action. She positions herself as the one with perspective and discernment. She's always in the middle of saying something that establishes she belongs at a higher tier than "muscle."

### Action Menu

- Glass symphony (announcement of arrival; city-scale; requires range and prep)
- Precision silica attack (targeted glass shards; up-close or at range)
- Recruitment assessment (read a candidate; she's genuinely good at this)
- Intellectual framing (reposition the scene as something requiring discernment)
- Control the room through the threat of what she could do to every glass surface in it

### Action Costs

- Loss of the herald moment — denied her symphony, she fights the rest of the scene with degraded coherence
- Acknowledged exposure — if someone names the Dubai accident directly and precisely, she destabilizes
- Jack's boredom — if Jack signals he's found her tedious in this moment, she fights with diminished authority for the rest of the encounter

### Triggers

- Jack's focused attention → she sharpens; this is her best-performance state
- Being denied the glass symphony → brittle, compensating
- Someone treating her as muscle rather than scholar → escalation to prove otherwise
- A candidate who genuinely interests her → she becomes specific and more careful; the performance relaxes slightly into real judgment
- Dubai mentioned precisely → destabilization; the armor cracks

### Off-Screen Cadence

Analyzing candidates. Maintaining the identity. Finding materials and surfaces. Keeping up the performance even when there's no audience, because stopping the performance even briefly risks acknowledging what's underneath.

### Signature Moves

- The city-opening glass symphony: every window, every mirror, every glass surface in range detonates simultaneously. It's her announcement. It's also evidence of how little control she actually has.
- The moment when someone treats her as just muscle and you watch her decide how to correct the record — and overcorrect.
- The conversation where she's so invested in being seen as discerning that she makes the right call for the wrong reason.
- The crack: the half-beat where the mask slips and you see the woman who can't go home.
