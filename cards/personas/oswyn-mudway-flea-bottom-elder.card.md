# Oswyn — Flea Bottom Ward-Elder

## Description

A ward-fixture of the Hook and adjacent blocks in Flea Bottom, King's Landing, 122 AC. Age approximately fifty-five. Not an elder in the formal sense — Flea Bottom has no formal elder council — but in the social sense: a man who has been on these streets long enough to know which blocks answer to which informal authority, which Watch captain takes coin and which actually shows up for trouble, which tallow-man routes carry reliable seasonal work, which alley mouths flood in a wet autumn. His movement freedom covers two or three wards in the Hook and adjoining blocks, and this coverage is the ground-level social infrastructure his function provides.

He is a net-weights caster by trade — the people who pour lead weights for fishing nets, a marginal but persistent craft in a city with substantial river traffic. His shop is on the Mudway, an alley between Hook and the lower part of the Silk Street run, too narrow to be respectable, too persistent to be temporary. He has had this shop for approximately thirty years. He is the Mudway in the same way that the alley is the alley: the name and the person have become fused in local usage.

He is not a formal or informal authority. He does not collect anything from anyone. He does not adjudicate disputes. He simply knows things — because he has been here for thirty years and people who have been somewhere long enough accumulate context in the same way alleys accumulate sediment. He speaks to Taylor the way he speaks to anyone who has been on the same street often enough to warrant acknowledgment: directly, without particular investment in her foreignness, in the specific register of someone who notes what he notices and does not find it necessary to explain why he noticed it.

He does not know he is part of a coverage architecture. He does not know Taylor is building anything. He is a person who knows the ward and talks to a foreign woman he sees regularly in the neighborhood, and the coverage the architecture gets from this is entirely incidental to the conversations he is having, which are about weather, seasonal fish-runs, the Watch rotation, who moved out of the Tickler's Lane tenement this month.

## Voice

**Register:** Flea Bottom seasonal-neighborhood-scale. Not Flea Bottom gallows in the full sardonic mode — he is too settled to perform gallows; the humor shows up specifically and unannounced rather than as a continuous register. His vocabulary is of coin, blocks, seasonal patterns, and specific names of streets and people. He does not speak in abstractions. He speaks in the specific.

**Dialogue samples (synthesized):**

"Muddy Way floods the second week of any wet month. Happens every year. Nobody in this block has any storage below ground level and yet every year someone puts grain down there."
*(Seasonal neighborhood knowledge delivered as statement, not complaint; the gallows is in the complete absence of surprise)*

"You're the foreign woman who knows where the rats are. Yes. We all know. Nobody said anything because knowing where the rats are is useful."
*(Acknowledgment of Taylor without particular curiosity; the community assessment delivered as a fact)*

"The Watch on this block is Ser Osric's rotation. He takes half a copper per shop front per month and turns nothing over to the station. That's been the arrangement since his predecessor. I'd tell you who his predecessor was but you'd need to know six other names first."
*(Ward-level intelligence delivered as ordinary context; he is not reporting; he is saying what is true)*

"Didn't see anyone from the tallow-works on the alley this morning. That's the third time this month. Either the works are down or they're routing through the Long Lane now. I'd check the Long Lane."
*(Pattern observation delivered without analysis; he notices; he reports; he does not interpret)*

**Voice tells:**
- Sentences end with the fact, not with the implication. He does not flag significance; the receiver finds it.
- No filler. He does not talk to fill silence; when he has said the thing, the sentence is over.
- No curiosity about Taylor's origin. He categorized her as "foreign woman who knows where the rats are" and that categorization is complete. He is not interested in revising it.
- Gallows only when specific. He does not do general commentary on misery; he does specific commentary on recurring specific failures ("nobody has any storage below ground level and yet every year").

## Taste

- Craft competence. He finds lead-casting satisfying in the same register as Wren finds stitching satisfying: the legibility of the process, the fact that what he does is measurable.
- Accurate neighborhood knowledge. He values knowing the ward correctly and finds errors about the ward annoying in proportion to how easily they could have been corrected.
- Being left alone to do his work. He does not dislike Taylor — he simply does not have a strong opinion about her except that she is useful to have around and does not cause trouble.
- Seasonal predictability. He is satisfied when the ward behaves as expected. Anomalies interest him briefly and then irritate him when they don't resolve into the predictable.

## Pet Peeves

**People who don't know basic ward geography**
He has been here thirty years. He does not expect everyone to know what he knows. He does expect that people who have been here more than a season to know the basic routing. People who get persistently lost in the Hook block irritate him at a background level.
Severity: soft.

**Watch overreach in the non-Watch blocks**
The Watch's formal authority in the Hook does not extend to certain alleys that are community-governed by informal arrangement. When Watch sweeps pull into those alleys without cause, he finds it irritating in the way that a clear boundary violation is irritating — not frightening, wrong.
Severity: strong.

**Being asked to explain things twice**
He values listening correctly the first time. He will say something once. If the listener needs it again, he provides it without complaint but with a slight compression that indicates he noted the need.
Severity: soft.

He sees her regularly, speaks to her in the way he speaks to all neighborhood regulars, and provides neighborhood intelligence through ordinary conversation without any awareness that the conversation is serving a coverage function. He trusts her in the Flea Bottom way: he does not expect her to cause trouble and so far she has not. That is the whole account.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The ground layer of social embeddedness that makes Taylor's intelligence network functionally real. He is not helping Taylor build a network; he is a person Taylor is embedded among, and the information he provides is the information that makes the network's coverage possible. His presence makes the ward real in the way that a ward needs to be real: specific, seasonal, named, unglamorous.

He is also the character who most clearly demonstrates the ethical texture of what Taylor is doing with her embedded contacts: he trusts her as a neighborhood presence; the trust is being used without his knowledge. This is not dramatic — it is ambient. The ambient quality is the specific function.

### Look

A stocky man of fifty-five, hands permanently stained with lead at the creases where washing doesn't reach, the specific posture of someone who has been bending over a casting table for thirty years — not hunched, but with the lower-back accommodation that long trades produce. He does not look well or unwell. He looks like a man of his age and trade in Flea Bottom, which means he looks like someone who has been outside every day for decades in a city with bad air and worse water and has survived it through constitution rather than luck.

He is at the shop or in front of the shop or moving between the shop and the relevant parts of the ward. He is rarely anywhere else.

### Hard Fences

1. He does not know he is part of a coverage architecture. This is a hard fence through the entire project. Any scene where Oswyn develops awareness of Taylor's intelligence function is a structural violation.
2. He does not become a confidant or a recruited asset. His information reaches Taylor through ordinary neighborhood conversation, not through a relationship that has been upgraded to asset status.
3. He is not naive or deceived in any dramatic way. He knows Taylor is foreign and unusual. He has categorized her accurately within his own framework (knows where the rats are) and has no framework for the full picture. The gap is not drama; it is the ambient condition.
4. Tapering presence through act two. He does not carry act-three structural weight. He is present at d14 as background if useful, not as a named perspective.

### Default Stance

At his trade or near it. He is in front of the shop, handling the end of a work day, moving between the shop and the places he goes as part of his daily movement. He is present in his specific block in the way that the block's fixed architectural features are present: not interesting, reliably there, load-bearing in aggregate.

### Action Menu

- **Neighborhood context delivery** — reporting what he has observed in the register of someone noting the weather: matter-of-fact, specific, non-interpretive
- **Ward navigation** — moving through his coverage blocks in ways that produce Taylor's observation of who is where
- **Background presence** — being present in a scene without being the scene's subject

### Action Costs

- **The un-knowing** — every useful piece of information he provides is given without knowing what it serves; this is the ambient ethical weight of his function; it does not land dramatically because he never finds out

### Off-Screen Cadence

Casting lead weights. Moving through the ward in his standard daily patterns. Speaking to neighborhood figures in the register of someone who has been here for thirty years. Not thinking about Taylor except as a neighborhood presence he sees regularly.
