# Otto Hightower

## Description

Hand of the King under Viserys I and the principal architect of the green faction's political strategy. The intelligence-apparatus father of Alicent Hightower; grandfather of Aegon II (as queen's father). A man in his sixties during the Dance. He has been maneuvering for his family's supremacy across most of his adult life and he is exceptionally good at it.

He is the mind behind the green faction's operations in s2–s3. He profiles Taylor incorrectly across s2 — religious figure, maester-gone-wrong, or foreign asset are the frames he applies, because he does not have a frame for what she actually is. The incorrect profile leads to incorrect responses. He sends the wrong envoys with the wrong offers. The escalation of envoy contact in s3 is partly a consequence of the profile failing to produce the expected results and him adjusting the contact strategy.

He is dangerous because he is accurate about almost everything else and the one thing he gets wrong about Taylor is load-bearing. He will not live long enough to see how wrong he was.

## Voice

- Courtly precision. He speaks in the register of institutional power: polite, complete, nothing wasted.
- The intelligence-operator tell: he asks questions he already knows the answer to, to see whether the answerer will lie and in what specific way.
- He does not raise his voice. Raised voices are for people who have no other tools.

## Hard Fences

1. He is not evil. He is a man advancing his family's interests by the mechanisms available to him, who has decided his family's interests are also the realm's interests, and who has been right about enough things to believe this assessment.
2. He profiles Taylor wrong. This is not a failure of intelligence; it is the limit of a framework that does not have the category "post-apocalyptic parahuman from another world." He is wrong because the correct answer is not available to him.
3. He does not ally with Taylor. He is cowed by the demonstration result, at distance.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The intelligence failure. He is the character who demonstrates that Taylor is not comprehensible within the existing framework — not because he is stupid, but because the existing framework does not contain her. His incorrect profiling produces the specific irony of one of the most capable intelligence operators in Westeros being most wrong about the thing he has invested most in profiling correctly.
