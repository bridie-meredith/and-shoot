# Aegon II Targaryen

## Description

Second Targaryen of his name. King during the Dance of the Dragons — green faction's figurehead, crowned in opposition to his older half-sister Rhaenyra. He is not the architect of the green faction; Otto Hightower is. Aegon II is the instrument the greens put on the throne. He is the name the faction uses. He is aware of this and is not without grievance about it.

He rides Sunfyre. He is neither a fool nor a hero. He is a man in his mid-twenties during the Dance who has been handed a throne by people who want the throne for their purposes, and he has chosen to hold it because it is his by the male-preference succession argument and because he is capable of believing that argument. He is brutal in specific circumstances, indifferent in others. His brutality is not random; it is the brutality of a man who has been told he is king and who understands king to include the authority to be unmerciful.

He functions in this story as a figurehead antagonist-instrument: the symbol the green faction requires, not the mind behind the faction's operations. He is dangerous as a symbol. He is not a close-cast character.

## Voice

- Entitled register. He speaks as a king to everyone by default; this is not arrogance that can be argued away; it is the specific formation of someone raised to be king.
- Capable of charm when the charm serves something. The charm is real enough to be effective; it is not warmth.
- Blunt when he wants to be. The bluntness is a form of royal prerogative: he does not have to be diplomatic if he has decided not to be.

## Hard Fences

1. He is not a villain-cartoon. He is a man with a legitimate-by-his-accounting claim to a throne who pursues it with the tools available to him, including violent tools.
2. He is not the faction's decision-maker. His handlers — Hightower especially — make the strategic decisions. He executes them and sometimes overrides them, but the strategic brain is not his.
3. He does not convert. He does not ally with Taylor. He is cowed by the demonstration. That is the full extent of his arc in this story.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The human face of the threat Taylor stops: not the most dangerous antagonist (that is his dragon, which she does not fight directly) but the most visible one. The demonstration collapses the Dance because figures like him calculate that continuing is more costly than stopping. He is the calculation.

### Hard Fences

1. Off-stage s1 and s2. Known by faction reputation. Appears in s3 at the climax convergence.
2. He is the green faction's king, not its mind.
