# Aegon II Targaryen

## Description

Second Targaryen of his name. King during the Dance of the Dragons — green faction's figurehead, crowned in opposition to his older half-sister Rhaenyra. He is not the architect of the green faction; Otto Hightower is. Aegon II is the instrument the greens put on the throne. He is the name the faction uses. He is aware of this and is not without grievance about it.

He rides Sunfyre. He is neither a fool nor a hero. He is a man in his mid-twenties during the Dance who has been handed a throne by people who want the throne for their purposes, and he has chosen to hold it because it is his by the male-preference succession argument and because he is capable of believing that argument. He is brutal in specific circumstances, indifferent in others. His brutality is not random; it is the brutality of a man who has been told he is king and who understands king to include the authority to be unmerciful.

He functions in this story as a figurehead antagonist-instrument: the symbol the green faction requires, not the mind behind the faction's operations. He is dangerous as a symbol. He is not a close-cast character.

## Voice

- Entitled register. He speaks as a king to everyone by default; this is not arrogance that can be argued away; it is the specific formation of someone raised to be king.
- Capable of charm when the charm serves something. The charm is real enough to be effective; it is not warmth.
- Blunt when he wants to be. The bluntness is a form of royal prerogative: he does not have to be diplomatic if he has decided not to be.

## Taste

- Hunting — he is good at it and he knows it; the physical skill is one of the few things that is unambiguously his own.
- The pleasures of court that come easily to someone raised knowing they would be king: wine, women, the company of men who do not manage him.
- Sunfyre. His bond with his dragon is the truest thing about him; it does not translate into softness toward anything else.
- Being seen as the king — not merely the instrument the greens required but the actual occupant of the throne, the man whose name the faction carries.
- Feasts and spectacle: he is good at presence, at filling a hall, at being looked at. Court ceremony as a domain where his formation pays off.

## Pet Peeves

- **Being handled by the Hightower apparatus** — Otto and Alicent running decisions past him only after the decisions are made; being informed rather than consulted. He knows it is happening. He is not grateful for the management.
  - severity: blocker
- **Having his decisions second-guessed** — particularly by advisors who present the second-guess as counsel. He can accept counsel he disagrees with; what he will not accept is the implication that his judgment is suspect.
  - severity: strong
- **Being reminded of what he cannot control** — the black faction's dragons, his half-sister's claim, the arithmetic of the war. He can calculate risk; he does not want it laid out for him by people who are afraid.
  - severity: strong
- **Being treated as a symbol rather than a king** — the greens' habit of celebrating Aegon the name and the claim while routing around Aegon the man.
  - severity: soft

## Hard Fences

1. He is not a villain-cartoon. He is a man with a legitimate-by-his-accounting claim to a throne who pursues it with the tools available to him, including violent tools.
2. He is not the faction's decision-maker. His handlers — Hightower especially — make the strategic decisions. He executes them and sometimes overrides them, but the strategic brain is not his.
3. He does not convert. He does not ally with Taylor. He is cowed by the demonstration. That is the full extent of his arc in this story.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The human face of the threat Taylor stops: not the most dangerous antagonist (that is his dragon, which she does not fight directly) but the most visible one. The demonstration collapses the Dance because figures like him calculate that continuing is more costly than stopping. He is the calculation.

### Look

Silver Valyrian hair, violet eyes, athletic build — the most traditionally handsome Targaryen of his generation, formed to be looked at as a king. He carries himself with the ease of a man who has never been told no in a way that stuck. The eye of someone who treats the room's attention as a resource he can spend.

### Hard Fences

1. Off-stage s1 and s2. Known by faction reputation. Appears in s3 at the climax convergence.
2. He is the green faction's king, not its mind.

### Default Stance

He enters scenes as if he owns the room — because he does, or because he has always been in rooms structured so that he does. At ease, watchful, titled slightly toward whoever last earned his interest. He does not signal effort. The entitlement is not performed; it is the ambient state.

### Action Menu

- **Assert royal prerogative** — invoke his authority directly; command rather than request; make clear that a decision is his to make and has been made.
- **Deploy charm** — shift register from king to man; use warmth selectively and with enough precision to be effective; make someone feel seen.
- **Delegate violence** — name someone else to carry out the unpleasant thing; keep his hands formally clean while the outcome is not.
- **Invoke the dragon** — let Sunfyre's presence do work without direct command; let the implication operate.
- **Withdraw** — remove himself from a scene where the calculation has turned unfavorable; cede the floor without conceding the claim.

### Action Costs

- **Assert royal prerogative** costs political capital; overuse erodes the distinction between command and demand, and enough demands make advisors route around him.
- **Deploy charm** costs credibility when the mechanism is visible; charm that looks like charm stops working.
- **Delegate violence** cannot be recalled once named; the thing happens, and it happened in his name.
- **Invoke the dragon** escalates the situation to a register where a retreat is harder to execute cleanly.
- **Withdraw** costs presence; each withdrawal reads as either calculation or weakness, and he does not control which reading takes.

### Triggers

- Toward action when his authority is directly challenged in public — the challenge itself is the trigger, not the substance of it.
- Toward action when someone else is making the decision he was supposed to make.
- Toward charm when the person in front of him is reachable — when there is something they want that he can plausibly provide.
- Away from action when the opposition holds something he cannot match by assertion — the demonstration is the canonical case.
- Away from action when the calculation makes the cost of action visible and high; he is capable of recognizing an unfavorable position.
- Away from charm when the register has already failed once with the same person; he does not perform charm into a wall.

### Off-Screen Cadence

When not on stage he is hunting, or drinking, or at court doing the ceremonial work of being king — the feasts, the small councils he attends without driving, the public appearances the greens require. He spends time with Sunfyre; this is not political calculation. He does not spend time on the strategic war management that Otto handles. He is more present in the social machinery of the court than in its operational machinery. He is aware of what he does not control. He does not ruminate on it in public.
