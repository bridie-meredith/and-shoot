## The directive

Retire the cold ledger/accounting/apparatus register as a prose mode. The narrator (Taylor) still
perceives the city through her insect-network and still keeps a cold moral accounting in her *head* —
but the **prose renders concrete physical and human action first**. The apparatus is a lens, never the
grammatical subject of narration, never the unit by which events are reported. The tragic substance arc
(Khepri-haunted refusal → instrumentalization → the war she tried to prevent) is **unchanged**. Only the
narration register changes.

## What "no ledger" forbids (the failure vocabulary, from the shipped book)

- Abstraction-as-subject: "the gap propagated," "the count closed," "the node foreclosed," "the feed
  returned the body," "the ledger took the entry," "the lane went silent" (when a person was *killed*).
- Bookkeeping verbs standing in for events: "I closed the entry," "I filed the contact," "I priced the
  cost," "I lifted the stylus and the not-descending was the entry."
- Coined apparatus nouns doing narrative work: "the feed," "the count," "the rushwick-feed,"
  "ward-junction body," "cost-tell," "filled-slot-pending."
- Staccato one-bone-per-paragraph stage-direction blocks ("I held the feet. / The insects swelled. /
  I exhaled.").

## What it keeps

- Taylor's coldness and control-instinct — rendered as *what she notices and how little she lets herself
  feel*, not as accounting vocabulary.
- The insects as a concrete, sensory presence (heat, weight, small bodies on stone) — perceived, not
  tabulated.
- The moral ledger as an interior *idea* she refuses to let certain costs into — nameable in dialogue and
  reflection, but not the grammar of every sentence.

## LOCKED voice target — c01 opening, re-rendered no-ledger

This is the calibration exemplar the re-cascade renders against (alongside `voice-exemplar.md`). It is a
**target demonstration, not a shipped draft** — the production re-render goes through the chain, not by
hand. Bones source: `theater/bones/b01-c01.md` (bones 1–27). Compare against the shipped opening in the
archived baseline.

> *I had won my last world by becoming a hive of bugs, and I had not meant to wake. I woke anyway, in
> another body — a woman with no name in this city, in King's Landing, in the year before the year before
> the dragons danced. The flies came with me, two hundred metres of them, thick in the air. The first
> thing I decided was that nothing I had done at the end would be done here.*
>
> *Three weeks in this ward, and what I had learned was how heavy it is to do nothing. I slept in the
> angle of a Hook alley where the drain ran out, and the air there was full of small bodies I would not
> use. I came to be useful without taking control. That was the rule I set before anything else. It is the
> oldest thing I have left.*
>
> ---
>
> The drain ran thin along the angle of the lane, and the morning smelled of tallow-smoke and fish. I kept
> still against the wall — keeping still was the one discipline I had carried out of the last world that
> still did what it was built for, and it cost me something to hold, every hour I was awake.
>
> Then a child went down.
>
> I heard it before the crowd did: the small wet catch of a breath that could not get all the way in,
> somewhere past the fish-cart that had stopped crosswise in the lane. The people nearest her did what
> crowds do — they pressed in to see, and the lane closed over her like water.
>
> I did not so much decide it as find it already decided. The flies were on the walls and in the seams of
> the stone, and I set the nearest of them crawling, low and sudden, across the backs of hands and necks —
> the kind of thing a body flinches from before the mind has a word for it. The dozen people closest to the
> girl stepped back without knowing why they stepped. A gap opened around her, and through it I could see
> her now: six, maybe seven, her lips gone blue-grey, her mother's hands useless on her chest.
>
> "Fever," I said, loud, over their heads. "Not the croup. She needs air. Stand back — who knows her?
> Fetch them."
>
> It was true, and being true it moved them. Hands went up. Someone ran. The girl's chest lifted, caught,
> lifted again.
>
> I let the flies settle. The gap stayed open on its own now, the crowd thinning the way a Flea Bottom
> crowd thins once a thing is handled and no longer worth standing in. The fish-cart man looked at me. Two
> women at the edge of the press looked at the lane and not at me, which was better.
>
> An old man took the mouth of the lane and stood in it. I knew him a little — Oswyn, a ward-elder, thirty
> years on these streets, the kind of man who notices a thing a week before he has the word for it. He
> settled his hands at his apron and looked at me the way you look at a tool you have just watched do work
> you did not expect of it. I turned my face to the alley, to give him less to read.
>
> And the stitch-maker's girl — eleven years old, a watcher, the kind who studies cloth before she cuts it
> and studies people the same way — found my face, eyes first. I put her where I keep the things I do not
> write down. Then I held still, and I did not feed anyone.

**Why this is the target:** every event is now legible (the child, the bugs, the gap, the call, the
elder, the girl) while Taylor stays cold and controlled. The insects are *felt*, not tabulated. The
moral fence ("things I do not write down," "I did not feed anyone") survives as character interiority,
not as ledger grammar. ~440 words from the same 27 bones.

## Re-cascade plan (STAGED — separate run)

**Mechanism:** per-chapter re-cascade in `revise` mode (RUNBOOK § Producing revisions). Restore the
archived project to `active-project/` first; baselines already archived.

**Order (worst-first, so the target calibrates against the hardest cases early):**
1. **c01–c02** — staccato bones-leak + abstraction origin. Highest-value proof the new gates hold.
2. **c10–c19** — the interior-sameness / repetition cluster + heaviest ledger register. Run `/and-cohere`
   across this stretch after re-cascade to break the structural repetition (not just the verbal register).
3. **c03–c09, c20** — lighter touch; c07/c13/c16/c20 are already the most readable (dialogue-heavy) and
   need the least conversion.

**Per chapter:** `/and-write <slug> revise --from-signals` → `/and-review bones` (now enforces
ABSTRACTION-AS-SUBJECT + SCENE-ABSTRACT-DOMINANT) → `/and-facets` → `/and-stitch` (Phase 4 LEDGER-REGISTER
prohibition + Phase 9 NAIVE-FOLLOW gate). Re-thread (Phase 10) where continuity moves; rebuild the
consolidated manuscript; regenerate the `completed-works/` export last.

**Cost:** ~30–90 dispatches/chapter × 20 = a multi-hour unattended run. Drive it per the RUNBOOK
chapter-production discipline (R1–R5) or in worst-first batches with checkpoints.

**Note on the substance contract:** the signature's state-axis *names* lean on ledger metaphor
("moral_framework as operating compass," "cost ledger"). Those are *substance* labels, not prose
instructions — they stay. DEC-0115 governs the *prose register*, not the axis machinery. The re-cascade
does not re-author the signature; it re-renders against the no-ledger fence.
