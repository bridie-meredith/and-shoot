# Red Keep — Interior (span-A configuration)

The internal spaces of the Red Keep as the protagonist (a child royal resident) inhabits them: Maegor's Holdfast as cage and home; the Great Hall as occasion-space; the royal apartments as the senior administration of her sect; and the still-room as the actual workplace. This card covers the spaces she moves through regularly. It is not a complete floor-plan; it is the geography of her daily operations.

---

## Geography

The Red Keep stands on Aegon's High Hill, the highest point of King's Landing — stone towers visible across the city, approached from the city through the outer gatehouse and across the outer bailey. The castle complex contains multiple buildings: the Great Keep (throne room and formal reception spaces), the Tower of the Hand, the towers of the small council, the Maester's Tower, the sept of the castle, servant and garrison quarters, gardens, and — at its fortified heart — **Maegor's Holdfast**.

**Maegor's Holdfast** is the innermost defensive shell: twelve-foot walls, a spiked dry moat, a single drawbridge, and the royal nursery within. It is a fortified box within a fortified castle. This is where the protagonist lives. This is the cage.

The internal distances are not large — the Red Keep is a castle, not a city — but they are strictly governed. Moving from one zone to another requires navigating the social geography of who is expected where and who is watching the passages.

---

## Layout

### Maegor's Holdfast

The royal nursery and family apartments. The bridge (when up) makes it genuinely isolated; the bridge (when down, which is almost always) makes it a controlled zone. The walls are real but the primary containment is the septa-and-chaperone structure, not the stone.

- **The nursery (early):** the original scene. The window facing the inner courtyard. Cold stone floors that are warmer near the hearth. The nursery has a smell: milk, wax, the specific clean-soap smell of surfaces maintained for a child's presence.
- **The protagonist's chamber (as she grows):** progressively less nursery and more laboratory. By the close of Book I it contains an unauthorized second account-book, several items the Septa's reports describe as "objects of unclear provenance," and a smell the household has stopped investigating.
- **The drawbridge approaches:** the bridge mechanism is in the gatehouse; she has never been in position to control it directly; the bridge is a fact of geography, not a tool.
- **The mother's solar (adjacent):** the administrative center of the household she lives in; she is summoned there for household matters and attends correctly; she does not find it useful except as a source of intelligence about the household's current concerns.

### The royal apartments

The chambers and corridors of the royal family, between Maegor's Holdfast and the Great Keep. Formal spaces for receiving visitors; informal corridors connecting them. Sibling rooms are adjacent; the protagonist uses the proximity.

- **The corridor between chambers:** the primary movement space; watched during daylight; slightly less watched at specific hours she has mapped. She does not need it to be unguarded — she needs it to be counted as occupied (via the performed-illness loophole).
- **The odd sibling's room:** adjacent; the protagonist visits freely; the household has stopped noting these visits as unusual. The still-room smell and the insect-collection smell are compatible enough that they blend.
- **The maester's consulting room (outer apartments):** she attends officially for lessons; she has also learned the maester's schedule well enough to know when the consulting room is empty and accessible.

### The Maester's Tower / still-room

The operational center of the project. She obtained access to the maester's still-room through a combination of persistent curiosity, competence-as-foothold (she has been fixing his supply ledgers since she could read), and the specific maester's personality (he disapproves of her but cannot function without the fixed ledgers and does not investigate how they got fixed).

- **The still-room proper:** medium-sized, stone-floored, with a cold-water basin along one wall and drying-racks along the other. Bundles of dried herbs. Ceramic jars in labeled rows (labeled by the maester; her relabelling system is parallel and invisible unless you know where to look). A small brazier for heating compounds. The smell is layered — old dried herbs underneath, recent work on top, and a third register she has introduced that the maester cannot identify.
- **The maester's supply cabinet:** the primary acquisition source. Initially locked; she solved this within a moonturn of acquiring still-room access. She restocks items she removes with near-identical replacements where possible. The cabinet's inventory is known to her in detail. The maester's knowledge of his own cabinet's inventory is an approximation.
- **The maester's library:** she has been rearranging this library since she was five. The rearrangement is "cultivationally accurate" by her taxonomy and functionally incomprehensible to anyone using the standard Citadel organization system. The maester has accepted the rearrangement because the texts he uses most frequently are now, somehow, more accessible. He does not know why.
- **The back stair from the still-room:** a servants' stair that connects the still-room level to the supply delivery area on the outer ring. She found this early. The Septa does not know it exists.

### The Great Hall / throne room approaches

She attends when required. She sits in the correct position. She has used the time to observe which household staff move through which corridors at which hours. The Great Hall's seating arrangement means she has an unobstructed sightline to three exits and the Hand's side entrance.

- **Court occasions:** formal; she attends; she observes; the observations go into the operational database that informs the watcher-subtraction calculations
- **The gallery above:** accessible on certain occasions; she has been in it twice on ostensibly legitimate grounds; the view from the gallery across the hall floor is operationally useful

### The sept of the Red Keep

She attends devotions. She is observed to attend devotions. She is not present in the same cognitive way that the Septa would prefer. The sept has a specific acoustics — sound carries in particular directions and not others — that she has mapped over years of sitting in the same spot during services.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**Maegor's Holdfast:**
- Cold stone, warmer near hearths. The specific smell of a space maintained for children — cleaned frequently, wax and soap. Stone walls that radiate cold in winter and do not warm quickly. Small high windows that produce specific directional light at specific hours.
- The drawbridge mechanism: a chain-and-counterweight sound when operated; audible throughout the holdfast; she can tell from the sound whether the bridge is going up or coming down.

**The still-room:**
- The most significant sensory location in the project. Dominant: dried herbs (complex; varies by season as new bundles replace old). Secondary: recent compound work (sharper; varies by what she is working on). Tertiary: the specific chemical register she has introduced over the past years that no one has identified (a clean-acid smell under the herbs, faintly metallic when the brazier is active).
- The cold-water basin is audible when the stone trough fills from the pipe: a specific trickle that marks refill time. She uses it to calibrate work-sessions without a candle-clock.
- The back stair smells of stone and dust; there is no light source; she has memorized the step count.

**The royal apartments and corridors:**
- Beeswax candles (not tallow; the household distinction is maintained throughout the apartments). The specific smell of court-appropriate cleaning and the cedar/lavender of stored clothing in the chests. A formality to the air that the still-room entirely lacks.
- Tapestries absorb sound; movement through the formal corridors is quieter than through the servants' passages. She uses both.
- The specific echo pattern of the corridor between chambers: sound travels approximately twelve paces before attenuating; she has learned the corridor's acoustic geography.

**The Great Hall:**
- Stone and open volume. High ceiling that makes sound travel differently than enclosed spaces — the gallery acoustics are useful; she is less audible from the floor than the floor is to her. Rushes on the floor during ordinary use; swept and sanded for formal occasions. The Iron Throne's specific visual weight at the far end — she is aware of it without looking at it.

## Exits

**Maegor's Holdfast:**
- Primary: the drawbridge (the only official exit; always monitored; always crossed with official purpose and usually a watcher)
- Practical: she does not escape Maegor's Holdfast by a different route; she escapes the system by operating within it (the loopholes)

**Still-room:**
- Primary: the main still-room door, to the corridor outside the maester's tower
- Secondary: the back stair (servants' delivery access; connects to outer ring supply area; the Septa does not know this exists)

**Royal apartments:**
- Primary: the formal corridors connecting apartments, monitored during daylight
- Via the odd sibling's room: the household treats the protagonist's presence there as normative and does not track it closely

**The Great Hall:**
- Multiple exits (she has mapped them); the gallery above has its own access via a stair she has located

## Hazards

**The watcher problem (primary early hazard):**
The cage is the attendant, not the door. Every space she operates in is potentially watched. The hazard is not architecture — it is observation. Her primary operational work is mapping who is watching what when, and subtracting watchers from the room through legitimate cover (the still-room is a lesson; the sibling's room is a family visit; the Sept is devotions). The hazard escalates if a watcher begins cross-referencing accounts.

**The septa's report-filing:**
Correctly-observed alarming behavior, correctly-reported, reaching the King through the filter and accomplishing nothing. The hazard is that if the filter fails — if the King is shown enough reports in close enough succession that the pattern becomes undeniable — the King might act.

**The supply cabinet:**
She raids it; she restocks it. The hazard is the maester actually inventorying carefully, which he does not because she has made herself operationally necessary to his ledger-maintenance. The dependency is the defensive moat.

**The back stair:**
Undocumented; structural soundness unclear in a few places; no light source; known only to her. If anyone else discovers it, its usefulness ends.

**Maegor's Holdfast's isolation function (double-edged):**
The twelve-foot walls and dry moat protect the royal family. They also protect the protagonist's still-room work from external inspection — the household is accustomed to having undisturbed royal-family space inside Maegor's, and this norm is operational cover.

## Ambient Interruption Hooks

- **The still-room compound failure:** something she is working on produces an unexpected effect — smell, color change, audible reaction. The question is whether anyone outside the still-room registered it.
- **The cabinet inventory discrepancy:** the maester notices a gap in his supply; he has a theory about the gap; his theory is wrong but it involves questioning the junior staff, which produces proximity to the accurate answer.
- **The drawbridge going up unexpectedly:** the audible chain-and-counterweight sound; Maegor's Holdfast is closing for an unscheduled reason; whatever she was going to do via the standard route is interrupted.
- **A Kingsguard on the wrong corridor:** someone armed and authorized has turned up in a passage she considered lightly watched; she has forty minutes' worth of account-book she needs to move.
- **The odd sibling observing from the doorway:** she has been standing in the still-room doorway for an unknown amount of time, watching the protagonist work, and has made a sideways observation that may or may not describe what she is seeing accurately.
- **The maester's library, rearranged again:** she has moved something he needs and he is searching for it with increasing irritation; he will find her within the hour if she does not return it.
- **The factor's letter arrived via the below-stairs route:** correspondence that officially does not exist, delivered by someone the household knows by a different name, sitting under a loose flagstone in the supply delivery area at the base of the back stair.
- **Court function scheduled with less notice than usual:** she has three hours to move the current still-room project to a location that will not be discovered during the room-preparation for the function. Three hours is either enough or not, depending on what the project is.
