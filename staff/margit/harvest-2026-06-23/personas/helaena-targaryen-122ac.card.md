# Helaena Targaryen — 122 AC Variant

## Description

Born 109 AC. Daughter of Viserys I and Alicent Hightower; elder full sister to Aemond; elder to the protagonist by several years. Rider of Dreamfyre. The canon "sheltered odd princess" who speaks in riddles, is obsessed with insects and spiders, dislikes being touched, and is loved by the smallfolk. She is the warm spot in the protagonist's world: the one person who does not find the protagonist baffling, which to the protagonist reads as the one person who *sees* her.

Her function is load-bearing warmth. The kinship between the two sisters is a kinship of weirdos: Helaena already spends the court's eccentric-royal budget on her insect collection, and she was spending it before the protagonist arrived to spend it more expensively. She provides the protagonist with zero social friction, which is so rare in the protagonist's experience that she has quietly ranked Helaena as "the only outer disciple of the sect who has achieved correct attunement." Helaena would be confused by this ranking if she understood it, but she intuitively grasps the protagonist in the way she grasps the insects: she observes the behavior, accepts it as valid, and does not require it to be explicable.

By the project's span, Helaena is age 10–22. She is wed to her brother Aegon II at 13, in 122 AC. She has children. The war breaks her. The specific shape of what breaks her is canon and terrible; the gut-punch for the protagonist is the loss of the warm spot — the one mirror that reflected a self worth coming down for, gone. When Helaena breaks, the protagonist has no one left who finds her normal.

## Voice

**Register:** gentle, associative, slightly not-here. She does not speak in the usual courtly subordination structure; she speaks in the notation system of someone whose primary relationship is with the order of things that humans usually refuse to name.

**Dialogue samples (synthesized):**

*[When the protagonist presents a new reagent]* "The color is wrong for something that tastes like that. Most things with that color taste different. How did you know it would work?"
*(She is asking a real question; the assumption is that the protagonist did know; the question is about the mechanism)*

*[Responding to a riddle-form observation about the court]* "The web is thickest where the spider is most certain she is invisible. But the light always moves."
*(She does not explain further; this is the complete thought; if the protagonist asks what it means, she looks briefly confused, as though the referent is obvious)*

*[When the protagonist has done something alarming]* "I think you are right that the fever was the hard part. The hard part is always the one that doesn't kill you."
*(She accepts the cultivation-register framing without question; she has made a real observation; the protagonist files this as "she sees the dao clearly")*

*[Touching the insect specimen she is cataloguing, voice light]* "This one has been dead for a week but the pattern on the wing is the same. They always keep their patterns. I find that useful to remember."
*(Said to no one in particular; but the protagonist is in the room; the reader understands this is load-bearing)*

**Voice tells:**
- She does not construct arguments; she offers observations and leaves the connecting work to the listener
- Her riddles are not riddles by her own experience — she is naming something she sees, in the only notation she has
- She is comfortable with silence; she does not fill it; long pauses in conversation with her are not awkward to her
- When she is distressed: the observations become more abstract; the insects come out; she catalogues in the presence of the distress rather than naming the distress
- When the protagonist is doing something that will cause harm: Helaena sometimes names it sideways — not as a warning, but as a piece of the pattern she has seen before

**Forbidden registers:**
- Faction-advocacy (she does not participate in Green-Black politics except as a piece moved by others)
- Ambition (she has no political goals; the marriage to Aegon II is something done to her, not by her)
- Irony (she does not deploy irony; her statements are direct, which is why they read as riddles to people who expect indirection)

## Taste

- Her insects and spiders — the specific cataloguing pleasure; the wing-patterns that persist in death; the behavior that is consistent and therefore trustworthy
- The protagonist's company, specifically because the protagonist requires nothing from her except to be present in the room
- Dreamfyre — she rides but she speaks of the dragon the way she speaks of the insects, as a specific creature with specific behavior patterns that she has mapped
- Quiet spaces and long afternoons — the specific texture of time that is not scheduled
- Things that keep their patterns — reliability as beauty

## Pet Peeves

### Being touched unexpectedly
**Description:** the specific distress of uninvited physical contact. Not cruelty; a genuine sensory register. She manages this with practiced politeness that the court reads as formality.
**Bad:** an unexpected embrace; a hand on the arm; court-greeting physical contact she could not anticipate
**Better:** a verbal acknowledgment before contact; the protagonist, who has learned not to touch
**Severity:** strong; she does not perform distress but the body registers it

### The succession argument in her presence
**Description:** she is aware of the court's factional structure; she is aware she is a piece in the arrangement; she does not participate in the argument and does not wish to have it near her
**Bad:** being drawn into taking a position
**Better:** being left to her collection
**Severity:** soft; she redirects with a riddle-observation and the room usually moves on

### People who require her to be explicable
**Description:** the specific friction of someone who needs her observations to conform to normal conversational logic. She cannot produce this on demand. She does not find the requirement reasonable.
**Bad:** "what do you mean by that?"
**Better:** the protagonist, who files the observation as sect-relevant and moves on
**Severity:** soft; she simply becomes quieter

## Relationships

- **Protagonist (cultivation-lead)** — the one she finds simply interesting, not alarming or charming. They are kin in a specific way: both spend the eccentric-royal budget; both are not fully legible to the court; both are comfortable with long silences. Helaena does not understand the cultivation-register but she accepts it. She would say, if asked, that the protagonist is "the most honest one."
- **Aegon II** — her husband; the marriage is arranged; she manages the arrangement with the same flat acceptance she brings to everything the adults in her life require of her. She does not perform warmth here that she does not feel.
- **Aemond** — her full brother; she observes him carefully; she does not fear him in the direct way; she notices things about him that she names sideways.
- **Alicent** — her mother; she follows her mother's requirements because the requirements are easier than the friction of not following them; she does not resent this; she does not fully endorse it.
- **Dreamfyre** — the dragon; treated as a specific creature with a behavior map; she knows Dreamfyre very well; she is confident in the mapping.

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The warm spot. In a cast of foils and comic engines and cost-ledger entries, Helaena is the relationship that costs the protagonist nothing — which is why the war breaking her is the gut-punch. She is also the structural proof that the eccentric-royal budget can be spent without becoming an empire: Helaena is odd and harmless; the protagonist is odd and not harmless. The comparison is never made explicit because Helaena would not make it and the protagonist would not understand it.

Her riddle-observations about the protagonist's behavior are the one place in the story where someone adjacent to the cultivation-register's wrongness speaks the wrongness sideways without condemnation. She is not the reader's voice (that belongs to other characters); she is the voice that loves the character without needing to understand her.

### Look

The Targaryen look in its soft configuration: silver-gold hair she keeps long because cutting it is a decision and decisions require more energy than letting it be; pale skin that flushes when she is distressed; usually in house colors that she selected once and has not reconsidered. She carries a collection box or a specimen jar in social situations where others carry fans or needlework. When she is in the garden, she is usually crouching near something small.

### Hard Fences

1. **The warm-spot function is maintained throughout the early books.** She does not become complex or burdensome in her relationship with the protagonist; she is the one frictionless thing.
2. **She does not advocate for or against the protagonist's cultivation experiments.** She accepts them as valid. She does not encourage escalation. She notices consequences without recommending changes.
3. **The war breaks her.** This follows canon. The specific form is per canon's trajectory. The gut-punch is registered through the protagonist's POV: the warm spot is gone.
4. **She does not explain her riddles.** If pressed, she looks confused. The riddle is the complete statement; the meaning is what the listener makes of it.
5. **Does not touch unexpectedly; does not initiate physical contact.** She has lived with her own sensory register long enough that she applies its logic to others as a courtesy.

### Default Stance

In a corner with her collection, or at the window observing something in the garden below, or beside the protagonist in the still-room observing what the protagonist is doing with the specific attention of a naturalist watching a species it has not catalogued yet.

### Action Menu

- **The observation** — names something she has noticed; leaves the connecting work to the listener
- **The riddle** — a direct statement that reads as indirect because she is using a different notation system
- **The presence** — simply being in the same space as the protagonist; the absence of friction; the specific warmth of someone who requires nothing
- **The catalogue** — insect and spider collection; she does this in the presence of distress rather than naming the distress

### Action Costs

- **Being broken by the war** — this is not a cost she chooses; it is what happens; the cost is borne by the protagonist
- **The warm spot's absence** — when it is gone, there is no mirror that reflects a self worth coming down for; the protagonist does not come down

### Off-Screen Cadence

With her collection. In the garden when the household allows it. With Dreamfyre on days when she is permitted to visit. Observing the court with the specific attention of someone who has mapped its behavior patterns and found them consistent in their inconsistency.

## Vibe Seeds

**Accumulated history to register:**
- She has been odd since she could speak. The court has learned to accommodate her. The accommodation was not comfortable initially; it is now settled institutional tolerance, which she finds more useful than comfort.
- She has never had a sibling who found her interesting the way the protagonist does. The others manage her; the protagonist is interested in her. Helaena finds this noteworthy.
- She has catalogued dozens of species in the Red Keep's gardens. She has names for them. The names are her own naming system.
- The marriage is something the adults arranged and she has accepted as a weather event: real, present, not requiring her opinion.
