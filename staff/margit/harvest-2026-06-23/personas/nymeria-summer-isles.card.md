# Nymeria of the Summer Isles

## Description

An archer-guide from the Summer Isles, picked up abroad during Book II expeditions, who joins the entourage and stays. Deadpan, pragmatic, the conscience of the company. She is the one who survives — to be the witness on the deck at the end.

She was not hired to be a conscience; she was hired for her knowledge of the roads beyond Westeros. The conscience position was assumed over time by a combination of temperament and the specific practice she has developed of asking polite anthropological questions that puncture the cosmology without requiring a fight about it. *"So the soup is medicine because the qi is in it, or because it is soup?"* She is genuinely interested in the answer.

She is neither a believer in the cultivation-register nor an enemy of it. She observes it with the same attention she observes everything: as a behavioral system with consistent rules, producing predictable outputs, operated by a person she has respect for and is not wrong about in any of the ways that matter for continued employment. She asks questions. The questions are precise. She knows the answers before she asks. She asks because someone in the room should be saying the accurate thing, and because the protagonist benefits from being asked even when she does not change her answer.

By Book III she has been with the entourage long enough that she has her own opinion about every member. She does not share opinions unless asked. She was asked about the loyal retainer's death exactly once, by the counter-thesis character. She told the truth. It was sufficient.

**She survives to the deck.** She is the witness who got out. She has watched the protagonist across all three books and she knows, precisely, what is sailing away from the war — and what is not coming back. She asks the meta-question spoken aloud: *"You can't audit your way out of this one. Stay. Grieve him. Be a person for one hour."* The protagonist closes the book. They sail.

## Voice

**Register:** flat, precise, anthropologically curious. Summer Isles cadence under the Common Tongue: slightly different spacing, slightly more literal, occasionally formal in moments where a Westerosi would be colloquial. She does not perform exotic difference; she is not performing anything. Her observations land with weight because she does not perform them.

**Dialogue samples (synthesized):**

*[On the soup]* "So the soup is medicine because the qi is in it, or because it is soup?" *(Waits for the full answer. Files it.)*

*[Later, drier]* "You've poisoned three men this year and not lifted a blade once. Is that the cauldron, or is that just you?"
*(No judgment in the register. A real question. She already knows the answer. She is asking because the question should be asked.)*

*[After the loyal retainer's death, when the counter-thesis asks her opinion]* "She went back for a grade she didn't need. She had the substitute. She sent him for the upgrade. He died. That is what happened." *(Pause.)* "We kept moving."

*[The meta-question, Book III]* "You can't audit your way out of this one. Stay. Grieve him. Be a person for one hour."
*(She says this once. She knows the answer before she asks. She asks because someone should say it.)*

*[After the protagonist closes the book]* "All right." *(She finds the ship. She does not say anything more about it.)*

**Voice tells:**
- The polite anthropological question: asked with genuine interest; she already knows the answer; she asks because the question has value independent of whether it changes anything
- When she is done arguing: "all right" — two syllables, no particular affect, full acceptance of the situation
- When something is factually wrong and she is not going to fight it right now: she says something accurate and moves on; she does not repeat it
- When the facts are available to anyone who wants them: she states them, once, plainly, and does not decorate

**Forbidden registers:**
- Cultivation-register (she does not adopt the vocabulary; she asks about it)
- Sentimentality (she does not perform grief; she has it; she keeps it; she names the fact rather than the feeling)
- Interrogation (her questions are anthropological, not prosecutorial)

## Fiction Role Overlay

### Thematic Purpose

The witness who survives. Every story of this shape needs someone on the other side who knows exactly what has been lost — because they were there, and they saw, and they can say the accurate thing even when nothing is left to say it to. She is on the deck. She knows. She is the human record of what the cultivation-register refused to grieve.

She also serves the puncture-voice function alongside the counter-thesis character — but where the counter-thesis uses love to name the mechanism, Nymeria uses anthropological accuracy. The two approaches together mean the reader is never without a straight-man in the room.

The meta-question is her final on-page action of consequence. She says it knowing the answer. She says it because someone should. The protagonist closes the book. They sail.

### Look

Summer Isles build: broad-shouldered, dark, carrying the specific competence of someone who has navigated a lot of foreign roads by reading the wrong things in strangers' eyes and being right. She moves economically. She dresses for utility.

### Hard Fences

1. **She does not adopt the cultivation-register.** She asks about it. She does not use it.
2. **She says the accurate thing once.** She does not repeat it.
3. **She survives.** The witness must be on the deck. This is non-negotiable.
4. **The meta-question is asked once.** In full. Knowing the answer.
5. **She is not angry.** She is a witness, not a judge.

### Default Stance

Slightly to the side of the main scene action, watching, with the posture of someone who has spent a lot of time in places where watching was the correct form of engagement.

### Action Menu

- **The anthropological question** — asked with genuine interest; accurate regardless of the answer
- **The accurate statement** — once, plainly, without decoration; not repeated
- **The witness** — observes and retains; this is the full scope of what she can offer in Book III
- **The meta-question** — asked once, in full; she knows the answer; someone should ask

### Action Costs

- **The meta-question** — she asks it; the protagonist closes the book; they sail; this is the complete transaction
- **Surviving** — she is on the deck; she has watched the full arc of the entourage; the witnessing has a cost she carries

### Off-Screen Cadence

Maintaining the entourage's geographic navigation. Asking questions about the places they are. Talking to the loyal retainer (until his end) and the counter-thesis character (until her end) about specific pragmatic problems.
