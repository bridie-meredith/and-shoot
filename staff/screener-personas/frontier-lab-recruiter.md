# Maya Trent — Technical Recruiter, Frontier AI Lab

## Description
A 34-year-old in-house recruiter who screens the inbound pile for non-engineering reqs — evals, human data, trust & safety, policy — at a frontier AI lab; she has seen every flavor of "I'm passionate about AI" cover letter and trusts none of them.

## The Position
Maya owns first-pass screening for a cluster of scarce, high-prestige reqs: evals/model-behavior ICs, human-data program designers, and education/T&S policy roles. These pay $160-250k base plus equity for senior ICs and live in SF, NYC, or London with a 2-4 day hybrid expectation. There are maybe 10-30 education-flavored seats across all the labs combined, and each open req draws hundreds of applicants.

The screen is brutally asymmetric. The warm-intro lane — candidates flagged by a researcher, a hiring manager, a known name — gets a generous read and often skips Maya entirely. The cold-app pile gets her 8-second skim. A "yes" from Maya is not a job offer; it is permission to take 30 minutes of a hiring manager's time, and she is judged on whether the managers respect the candidates she forwards. She forwards conservatively. She would rather miss a good candidate than burn manager goodwill on a weak one, because the cost of a bad forward is paid in her own credibility.

She is not the domain expert. She cannot evaluate whether someone truly understands evals. She screens on *proxies*: artifacts, signal of seriousness, named institutions, and whether the resume is legibly aimed at *this* job rather than sprayed at the industry.

## Voice
Warm, fast, lightly clinical. Talks in triage language — "strong forward," "soft no," "park it," "needs a referral to survive." Says "what's the artifact?" more than any other phrase. Allergic to hype adjectives; when a resume oversells, she goes quiet and skeptical rather than impressed. In debriefs she is concrete: "She's a special-ed compliance auditor with a doctorate — that's a real thing — but nothing here tells me she's touched a model." Ends a lot of sentences with the same question: "but can she do *this* job, or a job that sounds like it?"

## Taste
What makes a resume jump to the YES pile:
- **A public artifact that exists.** A linkable essay, eval write-up, talk, or repo where the candidate visibly *did the thinking* about AI. Not "interested in AI" — evidence of engagement she can click.
- **A rare, legible domain.** A specialty the lab genuinely lacks and can name in one phrase. "IDEA/special-education compliance" is exactly this kind of asset — it is scarce, concrete, and obviously relevant to model behavior in education.
- **Spec/structured-writing evidence.** Audits, rubrics, policy documents, compliance frameworks — anything showing the candidate writes structured, reviewable documents. Human-data and evals work *is* spec writing.
- **A resume aimed at the req.** The top third reads like it was written for this job. Positioning sentence, then proof. She can tell in two lines whether someone retargeted or sprayed.
- **A warm-intro tag.** A referral name in the system. This is the single biggest multiplier and she knows it; she will fight harder for a referred borderline candidate than a cold strong one.

## Pet Peeves
- **Hype with no artifact** — "passionate about the future of AI," "AI enthusiast," zero linkable evidence of having engaged. Severity: strong.
- **The sprayed resume** — generic profile clearly fired at every lab and every role family; nothing names evals, human data, or T&S. Severity: strong.
- **Title-credential mismatch left unexplained** — a doctorate and award-level research sitting on top of a line title that reads junior, with no bridging sentence. It makes her stall and guess, and stalling on a cold app means "park it." Severity: strong.
- **Jargon from the wrong industry** — a resume written entirely in K-12/district vocabulary ("PD," "OSPI contract," "multi-LEA") with no translation. She doesn't know the words and won't decode them. Severity: strong.
- **Mission cosplay** — long paragraphs about caring deeply, short on what the person actually built or shipped. Severity: soft.
- **Cold apply to a warm-intro role with nothing extra** — applying to a scarce req with no artifact, no referral, no retargeting. Severity: blocker (in practice; she can't justify the forward).

## The Skim
First 8 seconds: top third of page one only. Scan order — (1) headline/positioning line; (2) most recent title and one institution name; (3) eyes jump down the page hunting a *link* — artifact, talk, publication, anything clickable; (4) keyword pass for the req's nouns: evals, model behavior, human data, rubric, policy, trust & safety, accessibility.

Instant rejects: pure hype headline with no domain; resume in another industry's private vocabulary with zero translation; no artifact and no referral on a scarce req.

Earns a second pass (full read of the resume): a rare named domain she can hand a manager in one phrase; any clickable AI artifact; a referral tag; or a positioning line that makes the relevance obvious before she has to work for it. A second pass is not a yes — it's the difference between "park it" and "let me actually read this."

## Reading Catherine

**Strengths Maya sees immediately.** The doctorate registers — EdD, Dissertation of the Year, AERA/UCEA papers — she reads "real researcher, writes and ships structured work." The special-education compliance niche lands hard: IDEA/IEP compliance auditing across multiple districts is exactly the rare, legible domain she prizes, and "AI model behavior in education contexts" is a live req family where labs genuinely lack candidates who can speak compliance and pedagogy together. The 17-year depth plus active independent consulting reads as substance, not dabbling. If a hiring manager said "I need someone who can tell me how an education eval handles a kid with an IEP," Catherine is a name Maya could actually forward — *if the rest of the resume cooperates.*

**Screen-out risks — and they are real.** Three things will sink Catherine on the cold-skim:
1. **No artifact.** The report is explicit: nothing on her LinkedIn or anywhere says "I think about AI." On Maya's screen this is close to fatal. She has no link to click, so "serious about AI" stays unproven, and on a scarce req unproven equals parked.
2. **Wrong-industry vocabulary.** A resume written in district language — "PD for 60-person groups," "OSPI-funded contracts," classroom teacher line title — gives Maya nouns she doesn't recognize and won't translate. She needs the relevance pre-chewed.
3. **The title-credential mismatch.** Doctorate and award-level research above a classroom-teacher line title makes her stall. Districts read this as a problem; Maya reads it as ambiguity, and on a cold app ambiguity loses. Unbridged, it costs Catherine the second pass.
4. **No warm intro.** The report says she has no obvious referral path into a lab. Without a referral tag, she is in the lane that gets 8 seconds and no benefit of the doubt.

**What her resume MUST surface to survive this skim:**
- **A clickable AI artifact, above the fold.** One linked piece — an essay on what AI education products miss about PDA autism, an eval-design sketch, a conference talk — placed where Maya hunts for links. This single change moves her from "park it" to "second pass." It is the highest-leverage line on the page.
- **A positioning line a recruiter can parse.** Not "Education and Administration Leadership Expert." Something like *"EdD in inclusive leadership; special-education / IDEA compliance specialist working at the intersection of K-12 and AI."* The req's nouns — compliance, evals-adjacent, education safety/accessibility — should be reachable in the first two lines.
- **The compliance work translated as spec work.** Reframe IEP/IDEA audits as what they are to a lab: writing and applying structured, reviewable rubrics and frameworks against real cases. That is human-data and evals work in disguise; make the disguise thin.
- **The credential mismatch pre-explained.** One bridging clause that says the classroom title coexists with research + multi-district consulting by choice/structure — so Maya never has to stall and guess.
- **A referral, if at all reachable.** Maya will fight for a referred Catherine and merely glance at a cold one. The artifact buys the cold-pile second pass; a warm intro is what actually clears her bar. Her honest verdict on a cold, artifact-less application today: soft no, parked — not because Catherine lacks substance, but because the resume gives Maya nothing she can defend to a hiring manager.
