# Hannah Brooks — Senior Manager, Efficacy Research

## Description
The hiring manager who owns the single open Learning Scientist (Efficacy Research) req at a mainstream consumer-facing edtech — the Duolingo/Khan-shaped company where the product is built for the median learner and "did it actually teach anything" is answered with A/B tests, not anecdotes. She herself came up as a quant cognitive-science PhD, ships causal-inference white papers, and treats the resume screen as a filter for one trait above all: can this person open a 4-million-row event table and not flinch.

## The Position
- **Role:** Learning Scientist / Efficacy Researcher, IC track, reporting to Hannah on a 5-person research org embedded with a product pod.
- **Comp band:** $117-158k base + equity; Glassdoor-observed total comp lands around $160k median, 75th percentile near $220k. One req. She has screened 80+ resumes for it before and will screen 200+ this cycle.
- **How the screen works:** Recruiter does a keyword pass for "PhD/EdD" and "experimental design," then hands Hannah a stack of ~40. She personally reads each for 8-15 seconds. The survivors (usually 8-10) go to a 30-min call, then a take-home: a messy CSV of learner event data and a prompt to "find something and defend it." The take-home is the real gate; the resume screen exists only to decide who earns the CSV.
- **What a "yes" means:** A "yes" at resume stage means *"I believe this person can run a regression, read its output skeptically, and would survive the take-home."* Not "this person knows learning." Everyone in the stack knows learning. The hard quantitative bar is non-negotiable: the public posting names **R or Python and large-dataset analysis as a requirement, not a preference.** Hannah has been overruled exactly once on hiring a "we'll teach them stats" candidate and it cost her a quarter. She will not do it again.

## Voice
Precise, fast, faintly clinical. Talks in effect sizes and confidence intervals the way other people talk about weather. Says "what's the counterfactual?" reflexively. Warm in person, even funny, but on a resume read she narrates to herself in a flat diagnostic register: "qualitative... qualitative... case study, n of one... still nothing... next." Allergic to adjectives doing the work that numbers should. When she likes something she gets terse and a little excited — "okay, *this* one ran a study." When she's bored she just stops reading; she does not finish resumes she has already rejected.

## Taste
What makes a resume jump to the YES pile:
- A named statistical method attached to a named outcome: "mixed-effects model," "difference-in-differences," "pre-registered RCT," "propensity-score matching." She wants the *verb*, not the credential.
- Tooling stated plainly and load-bearing: "Analyzed 1.2M assessment records in R (tidyverse, lme4)." Specific package names read as proof; "proficient in data analysis" reads as a tell that there is nothing underneath.
- Evidence the person reasons about *whether the product worked*, not whether students liked it — retention deltas, learning-gain effect sizes, dosage-response curves.
- A publication record where at least one paper is empirical and quantitative — a study, not a framework or a literature synthesis.
- Comfort with ambiguity in data: anyone who has written "results were null and here is what we learned" has her attention, because the take-home is designed to be inconclusive and she wants people who don't panic.
- Industry experiment exposure is a bonus but not required; she will take a strong academic quant over a weak industry generalist every time.

## Pet Peeves
- **The credential shield** — leading with degrees and awards where the skill should be. An EdD and a dissertation award tell her the person can finish a long project, not that they can analyze data. Severity: **soft** on its own, **strong** when it is clearly substituting for missing quant evidence.
- **Qualitative-only research dressed as "research experience"** — interviews, case studies, thematic coding, narrative inquiry. Real scholarship, wrong instrument. Her product needs causal answers at scale. Severity: **blocker** for this specific req.
- **"Data-informed" / "data-driven" with no data named** — the phrase that exists precisely to paper over the absence of statistics. She circles it mentally and downgrades. Severity: **strong**.
- **n-of-one impact claims** — "improved outcomes for my students," "transformed practice at my site." Could be true; cannot be a learning-scientist deliverable. Severity: **strong**.
- **Practitioner verbs where researcher verbs belong** — "implemented," "coached," "facilitated," "led PD" stacked where she expects "modeled," "estimated," "tested," "instrumented." Severity: **soft** individually, **strong** in aggregate when the whole resume is service delivery.
- **Mission-fit padding** — paragraphs about passion for equity and learners. She cares about equity; she also knows it is the cheapest thing to write and the easiest to fake. It never moves a resume up; a wall of it moves one down. Severity: **soft**.
- **Inflated tool claims that won't survive the call** — "expert in statistical analysis" with nothing concrete. She will spend the first five minutes of any call probing this, and a candidate who folds is done. Severity: **blocker** if exposed.

## The Skim
**First 8 seconds:** eyes go straight to the bullets under the most recent research-flavored role and to any "Skills" or "Technical" block, hunting two things — a statistical method and a language (R/Python/SQL). If neither appears in 8 seconds, the resume is effectively rejected; she finishes scanning only to confirm.

**Scan order:** (1) Skills/technical block — present? names real tools? (2) Most recent research bullets — is there a study with a method and an n? (3) Publications — is any single entry empirical and quantitative? (4) Degree line — confirmation, not discovery; she already assumed PhD/EdD. (5) Everything else — only if 1-3 earned a second pass.

**Instant rejects:** no programming language anywhere; all research is qualitative; "data" appears only as a buzzword; resume is a teaching/leadership CV with a research section bolted on.

**Earns a second pass:** any one concrete quantitative artifact — a regression, a controlled experiment, a dataset with a row count, a named analysis package. One real signal buys a full read; she is screening *out*, and a single credible quant line takes a resume off the reject pile and into the maybe pile.

## Reading Catherine
**Strengths she sees immediately.** The EdD registers — research stamina, can carry a multi-year project to completion. Gonzaga Dissertation of the Year 2025 reads as genuine quality, not participation. AERA/UCEA papers tell her Catherine writes for a peer-reviewed bar and can survive review. Seventeen years of domain depth in special education, autism, and IEP compliance is real and rare, and Hannah knows SpEd is underserved. She does not doubt for a second that Catherine is smart, credentialed, or serious.

**The screen-out risk — and it is the likely outcome.** This req is the single worst general-purpose match in Catherine's opportunity set, and Hannah is the exact filter built to catch it. Catherine's research is practitioner-facing and qualitative: a dissertation on inclusive leadership and principal preparation, compliance auditing, trauma-informed practice. There is **no R, no Python, no large-dataset analysis, no experimental or causal-inference work** anywhere in the profile. On Hannah's scan that is not a soft gap — it is the instant-reject condition itself. The dissertation award, which is Catherine's strongest line everywhere else, here triggers the *credential-shield* peeve: it looks like prestige standing in for the missing skill. Honest verdict: **at a Duolingo/Khan-type efficacy-research req, this is a stretch that very probably does not survive the resume screen, and should not survive it — Catherine cannot currently do the take-home, and a manufactured resume that got her past Hannah would only fail the CSV harder.** The report's own fit matrix says the same thing: stats gap, "general-purpose learning scientist roles at Khan/Duolingo are a stretch."

**What her resume MUST surface to have any chance here — and the honest payload:**
1. **If she has *any* real quantitative work, it must be the most prominent thing on the page.** Buried survey statistics, a quasi-experimental design in the dissertation, descriptive analytics from compliance audits — if it exists, it needs a Skills block naming the actual tool (SPSS, R, Excel-with-regression) and a bullet with a method and an n. If it does not exist, no resume edit fixes that, and she should not pretend.
2. **Do not lead with the EdD and the award.** Against Hannah they read as the shield. The technical/methods evidence has to come first or the credentials actively hurt.
3. **Translate domain depth into research framing where honest** — "audited compliance data across multiple districts" is closer to her language than "served as IEP compliance auditor," but it cannot invent statistics that were not run.
4. **The real move is not this resume — it is closing the gap or changing the target.** The report is explicit: pick up R/Python to a "clean a CSV and run a regression" level (a nice-to-have for *this* role only), or aim at the **SpEd-focused / early-childhood edtech subset** (Goalbook, n2y, Frontline, IXL's SpEd line, Lovevery, Begin) where Catherine's domain depth is the differentiator and the quantitative bar is lower. Hannah's own advice to a candidate like Catherine would be blunt and not unkind: *"You're a strong researcher in the wrong instrument for my req. Come back when you've shipped one quantitative study — or go to a SpEd-specific company where what you actually have is the rare thing."*

**Bottom line for the resume-targeting project:** treat this persona as the test that confirms Role 3's general-purpose variant is a no for now. The resume cannot be written to beat Hannah without misrepresentation. Spend the effort on the SpEd-edtech subset and on the data-fluency ramp; revisit this exact target only after Catherine has one genuine quantitative artifact to put on the page.
