# Devang Rao — Eng & Research Lead, Model Behavior / Education Evals

## Description
The technical lead who owns a frontier lab's education-evals pod — a former ML research engineer who quietly became the person responsible for whether the model behaves well in K-12 contexts, and who now has one headcount and forty resumes to read himself.

## The Position
Devang runs a six-person sub-team inside a larger model-behavior org at a frontier lab (think Anthropic-adjacent: Seattle hub, hybrid 3 days/week). His team designs evals for how the model handles education contexts — age-appropriate content, accessibility, safety in tutoring scenarios — and writes the specs that human-data contractors execute against. The req he's hiring for is a senior IC "education domain lead": the person who turns "should the model handle a 7-year-old with an IEP differently" into a written, testable spec.

Comp band is real: $160–220k base plus equity that everyone around the table believes is worth more than the base. The team's culture is engineer-dense and document-driven — decisions live in design docs, not meetings; "show me the spec" is the recurring phrase. Devang is the only person on the pod with no CS degree, which is exactly why he's protective of the education hire: he needs someone who can survive a room of researchers without him translating, and who makes the whole pod *more* rigorous, not less.

He reads the shortlist himself — the recruiter hands him eight to twelve resumes and he reads them at his desk between meetings. A "yes" from Devang means: *this person can be handed an ambiguous behavioral question and return a structured artifact, and I can defend hiring a non-engineer to the org because this person is visibly the rigorous kind of education expert.* He is hiring exactly one. He is not optimizing for the best educator; he is optimizing for the educator who most reduces his own translation burden.

## Voice
Precise, compressed, allergic to filler. Talks in claims and counterexamples: "What's the failure mode?" "Give me the eval, not the philosophy." Uses engineering vocabulary unselfconsciously — spec, rubric, ground truth, edge case, false positive, taxonomy, coverage. Warm but fast; he'll spend ninety seconds being genuinely curious about your work and then pivot hard to "okay, but how would you operationalize that." When he likes a resume he says so flatly — "this one's real" — and when he doesn't he just sets it down. Writes in bullets. Distrusts adjectives, especially "passionate," "innovative," and "transformational."

## Taste
What jumps a resume to the YES pile for Devang:
- **A public artifact he can click.** An essay, an eval design, a talk, a Substack — anything that proves the candidate has *already thought in writing* about AI and their domain. He will open the link. If it's good, the resume is basically done.
- **Specs and rubrics in the work history.** "Authored multi-district IEP compliance audit framework" reads to him almost exactly like "designed an eval suite." Compliance auditing *is* structured assessment against a written standard — that's the whole job. He notices when a candidate has built a repeatable, criteria-based instrument.
- **A genuinely scarce domain.** Special education, IDEA compliance, complex autism, accessibility — he gets zero qualified candidates with this and knows the model is weakest exactly here. A defensible, narrow expertise beats "AI in education" generalism every time.
- **Numbers and scope.** "60-person PD cohorts," "multi-site," "17 years," "Dissertation of the Year." Concrete scale tells him the person operated a system, not just a classroom.
- **Evidence of cross-functional survival.** Anything showing the candidate held their own with people who speak a different professional language — researchers, lawyers, engineers, district counsel.

## Pet Peeves
- **Mission-statement prose** — paragraphs about passion for equity and transforming learning with no artifact or instrument underneath. Severity: strong.
- **"Familiar with AI" with nothing to click** — claiming AI engagement with no link, no piece, no eval. He reads it as a tell that the person has *not* actually used the tools. Severity: blocker.
- **Org-chart resume** — title progression and reporting lines presented as the achievement, with no description of what was actually built or measured. Severity: strong.
- **Adjective inflation** — "transformational leader," "innovative practitioner," "passionate advocate." Each one lowers his trust a notch. Severity: soft.
- **Undifferentiated education generalism** — "AI in education consultant" with no narrow edge. He's seen forty of those this cycle. Severity: strong.
- **Credential pile with no operating verbs** — EdD, certifications, awards listed, but the bullets are duties ("responsible for...") not built things ("designed...," "audited...," "authored..."). Severity: strong.
- **Curriculum/pedagogy jargon used as a wall** — terms like "scaffolded differentiation" deployed to sound expert rather than to communicate. He can't tell rigor from fog, so he defaults to skeptical. Severity: soft.

## The Skim
First 8 seconds: he reads the headline/summary line and the most recent role title, and his eyes go straight to any URL. Scan order after that: (1) is there a clickable artifact, (2) does any bullet contain the words *framework / rubric / spec / audit / standard*, (3) what's the actual domain — is it scarce, (4) scope numbers, (5) credentials last, as a tiebreaker only.

Instant rejects: a summary paragraph that's all mission and no instrument; "AI" claimed with nothing to verify; a resume that's entirely K-12 classroom duties with no systems-level or assessment-design work visible.

Earns a second pass: a live link he wants to open; the phrase "compliance audit framework" or equivalent within the first third of the page; a narrow domain (special education, autism, accessibility) stated plainly in the headline rather than buried.

## Reading Catherine
Devang's honest reaction to Catherine's actual profile:

**What he sees and likes.** The IEP/IDEA compliance auditing stops him cold — in the good way. Multi-site, multi-district compliance auditing is, structurally, exactly the work his team does: assessing artifacts against a written standard with a repeatable instrument. He will mentally translate "compliance auditor" to "eval designer" within seconds. The special-education / complex-autism / PDA niche is the scarcest thing on his shortlist; he knows the model is weakest in precisely these contexts and that he never sees candidates who can speak IDEA and model behavior in the same sentence. The EdD, AERA papers, and Dissertation of the Year register as proof she can produce rigorous written work and survive peer scrutiny — he reads research output as spec-writing stamina. 17 years and 60-person PD cohorts give him the scope signal. On paper, the rigor is there.

**Where he screens her out.** Two real risks. First and most dangerous: **the missing public AI artifact.** Per Section 4 of the report, there is currently *nothing* clickable that shows Catherine has thought about AI. For Devang this isn't a soft gap — it's close to a blocker, because his single biggest fear in hiring a non-engineer is hiring someone who *talks about* AI but hasn't *used* it. With no artifact, he has no way to distinguish her from the generalists, and he'll set the resume down. Second: her line title is still "classroom teacher" (Section 0). If the resume leads with the teaching job and buries the consulting/auditing/research, his 8-second skim lands on "K-12 teacher" and he never reaches the part that would have won him over. The "Education and Administration Leadership Expert" headline (Section 4) tells him nothing — leadership headlines read to him as org-chart ambition, not rigor.

**What her resume MUST surface to survive Devang's skim:**
- **A clickable artifact, above the fold.** Even one solid piece — "Five Compliance Questions Every District Should Ask Before Buying AI Tools" or an AI-evals-flavored essay on PDA autism — turns the resume from reject to YES. Without it she likely does not clear the skim. This is the single highest-leverage change.
- **Lead with the compliance-audit framework, not the classroom.** The headline and first bullet must put "authored multi-district IEP/IDEA compliance audit framework" front and center. Frame it in instrument language: criteria, standard, repeatable assessment across sites. That sentence is the one that makes Devang say "this one's real."
- **State the narrow domain plainly in the headline.** "Special education / complex autism / accessibility" — concrete and scarce — beats any "leadership expert" framing. Drop "Administration Leadership Expert" entirely for this target.
- **Convert duties to built things.** Every bullet should start with a verb of construction or assessment — *designed, authored, audited, built, established* — not "responsible for." Surface the consulting and OSPI contract work as evidence she operates systems independently.
- **Kill the adjectives.** Cut "passionate," "transformational," "innovative." Devang counts them against her.

If she does the first two of these, Devang moves her resume to the yes pile and is prepared to defend the hire to a room of engineers — because she'll read as the rigorous kind of education expert, which is the only kind he's allowed to hire.
